### Example 1: A cylinder

**A can has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume and surface area.**

1. **Volume = area of the base × height.** V = πr²h = π × 3² × 10 = 90π ≈ 282.7 cm³.
   *Say:* "Are we filling it or covering it?"
2. **Surface area = two circles + the side.** The side unrolls into a rectangle, 2πr long and h tall.
3. SA = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2π(9) + 2π(3)(10) = 18π + 60π = 78π ≈ 245.0 cm².

**Common slip:** using the diameter (6) instead of the radius (3).

### Example 2: A cone is one third of a cylinder

**A cone has radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. Find its volume.**

1. **A cone holds exactly one third of the cylinder with the same base and height.** V = ⅓πr²h.
2. **Calculate.** V = ⅓ × π × 9 × 4 = 12π ≈ 37.7 cm³.
   *Say:* "How many cones of water would fill the matching cylinder?" (Three.)

### Example 3: A grain bin

**A grain bin is a cylinder (radius 3 m, height 6 m) with a cone roof (radius 3 m, height 2 m). How much does it hold?**

1. **Split it into shapes you know.** Cylinder + cone.
2. **Cylinder:** π × 9 × 6 = 54π. **Cone:** ⅓ × π × 9 × 2 = 6π.
3. **Add:** 60π ≈ 188.5 m³.

**For surface area,** the circle where the cone sits on the cylinder is hidden inside, so it isn't counted.
