### Example 1: Adding with a negative

**Calculate −2/3 + 1/4.**

1. **Find a common denominator.** 3 and 4 both go into 12: −8/12 + 3/12.
   *Say:* "What number do 3 and 4 both divide into?"
2. **Add the numerators.** −8 + 3 = −5, so the answer is −5/12.
3. **Check with a quick estimate.** −2/3 is about −0.67 and 1/4 is 0.25, so the answer should be about −0.4. −5/12 is about −0.42. ✓

**Common slip:** adding tops and bottoms to get −1/7.

### Example 2: Dividing by a fraction

**Calculate −3/4 ÷ 1/2.**

1. **Decide the sign first.** A negative divided by a positive is negative.
2. **Multiply by the flipped second fraction.** 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = 3/2.
3. **Answer:** −3/2, or −1½.
   *Say:* "How many halves fit into three-quarters?" (One and a half.)

**Common slip:** flipping the first fraction instead of the second, or expecting the answer to be smaller.

### Example 3: Order of operations

**Calculate −2 + 3 × (−1/2)².**

1. **Exponent first.** (−1/2)² = (−1/2) × (−1/2) = 1/4. Negative times negative is positive.
2. **Then multiply.** 3 × 1/4 = 3/4.
3. **Then add.** −2 + 3/4 = −8/4 + 3/4 = −5/4, or −1¼.

**Common slip:** getting −1/4 for (−1/2)².
