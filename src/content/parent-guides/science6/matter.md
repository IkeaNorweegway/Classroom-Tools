### Example 1: Particles in ice, water and steam

**Describe the particles in ice, liquid water and steam.**

1. **Ice (solid):** particles packed tightly in a pattern, jiggling in place.
2. **Water (liquid):** particles still close, but sliding past each other.
3. **Steam (gas):** particles far apart, moving fast in all directions.
   *Say:* "What happens to the particles as we add heat?" (They move faster and spread out.)

### Example 2: Why does a can of pop burst in the freezer?

1. **As water cools, its particles slow down** and most things shrink.
2. **But water is unusual:** when it freezes, its particles lock into a pattern that takes up more space.
3. **So the ice expands** and pushes on the can until it splits.
   *Say:* "Does that explain why ice floats?" (Yes: ice takes up more space for the same mass.)

**Common slip:** thinking the pop got heavier when it froze. The mass didn't change; the space it takes up did.
