### Example 1: Circumference

A bike wheel has a radius of 33 cm. How far does it roll in one turn?

1. **Get the diameter.** One turn is the distance around. That needs the diameter: 2 × 33 = 66 cm.
2. **Multiply by 3.14.** 3.14 × 66 = 207.24, about 207.2 cm.
   *Say:* "Is that a bit more than 3 diameters? 3 × 66 is 198. Yes."

**Common slip:** doing 3.14 × 33. That only goes half way around.

### Example 2: Area

A pizza is 30 cm across. How much cheese covers it?

1. **Get the radius.** Area needs the radius: 30 ÷ 2 = 15 cm.
2. **Radius × radius, then × 3.14.** 15 × 15 = 225. 3.14 × 225 = 706.5 cm².
   *Say:* "Is this the edge or the inside? So which units?"

**Common slip:** doing 3.14 × 30 × 30 = 2826. That is 4 times too big.

### Example 3: Diameter from circumference

A tape around a tree reads 157 cm. How wide is the tree?

1. **Undo the × 3.14.** Diameter = 157 ÷ 3.14 = 50 cm.
2. **Check.** 3.14 × 50 = 157.
   *Say:* "Does the answer times 3.14 give back the circumference?"

**Common slip:** dividing by 2 instead of 3.14, or giving the radius when the question asks for the diameter.
