### Example 1: Find 6² and √36

1. **Picture a square.** 6² means a square of tiles, 6 on each side.
2. **Multiply.** 6 × 6 = 36. So 6² = 36.
3. **Go backwards.** √36 asks: what number times itself makes 36? 6 × 6 = 36, so √36 = 6.
   *Say:* "Is the 2 telling you to times by 2, or to use two 6s?"

**Common slip:** writing 6² = 12.

### Example 2: Find 4³ and ∛64

1. **Picture a cube.** 4³ means a cube of small blocks, 4 on each edge.
2. **One layer at a time.** One layer is 4 × 4 = 16. There are 4 layers: 16 × 4 = 64.
3. **Go backwards.** ∛64 asks: what number, used three times, multiplies to 64? 4 × 4 × 4 = 64, so ∛64 = 4.
   *Say:* "How many blocks along one edge?"

**Common slip:** writing 4³ = 12 or ∛64 = 21.

### Example 3: A square garden has an area of 49 m². How much fence goes around it?

1. **Find the side.** √49 = 7, so each side is 7 m.
2. **Find the fence.** A square has 4 equal sides: 4 × 7 = 28 m.
3. **Check the units.** A fence is a length, so m. The area was m².
   *Say:* "Is this question about the fence or the grass inside?"

**Common slip:** answering 49 m, the area, instead of the fence.
