title: Volume
plainSummary: "Your child is learning to measure the space inside 3-D objects. A prism, like a box or a tent, has two matching ends called bases. A cylinder, like a can or a grain bin, has two circle bases. First they learn to find the bases, even when the object is lying on its side. Then they fill a box with small cubes: one layer covers the bottom, and the layers stack up to the top. That gives volume = length × width × height, in cubic units like cm³. The same idea works for any prism or cylinder: volume = area of the base × height. Last, they work backwards: if they know the volume and the base area, they divide to find the height."

### Example 1: Volume of a box

A juice box is 6 cm long, 4 cm wide and 10 cm tall. How much does it hold?

1. **One layer.** Cubes that cover the bottom: 6 × 4 = 24.
2. **Stack the layers.** There are 10 layers: 24 × 10 = 240 cm³.
   *Say:* "Is the answer in cm² or cm³? Why?"

**Common slip:** writing 240 cm². Volume counts cubes, so it is cm³. (1 cm³ holds 1 mL, so the box holds about 240 mL.)

### Example 2: A grain bin (cylinder)

A grain bin is 6 m across and 5 m tall. Find its volume.

1. **Find the radius.** Half of 6 m is 3 m.
2. **Base area.** 3.14 × 3 × 3 = 28.26 m².
3. **Times the height.** 28.26 × 5 = 141.3 m³.
   *Say:* "Is 6 m the radius or the diameter?"

**Common slip:** using 6 as the radius. That gives 565.2 m³, which is 4 times too big.

### Example 3: Working backwards

A cooler holds 24 000 cm³. Its bottom is 40 cm by 30 cm. How deep is it?

1. **Base area.** 40 × 30 = 1200 cm².
2. **Divide.** Height = volume ÷ base area = 24 000 ÷ 1200 = 20 cm.
   *Say:* "Check it: does 1200 × 20 give back 24 000?"

**Common slip:** dividing the wrong way (1200 ÷ 24 000), which gives a tiny number that can't be a cooler's depth.
