### Example 1: Probability four ways

A bag has 4 red counters and 1 blue counter. Find the probability of drawing red.

1. **Count all the outcomes.** 4 + 1 = 5 counters, each with the same chance.
2. **Count the ones you want.** 4 are red.
3. **Write it four ways.** Ratio 4:5. Fraction 4/5. Decimal 4 ÷ 5 = 0.8. Percent 80%.
   *Say:* "Can a probability ever be more than 1? Why not?"

**Common slip:** writing 4:1 (red to blue). A probability compares the red ones to all of them: 4:5.

### Example 2: An experiment

Your child flips a coin 20 times and gets 12 heads.

1. **Experimental probability.** Times it happened ÷ number of trials: 12/20 = 0.6 = 60%.
2. **Theory.** Heads is 1 of 2 equally likely outcomes: 1/2 = 50%.
3. **Compare.** Close, but not the same. With 200 flips it would likely be closer to 50%.
   *Say:* "If you flipped 5 heads in a row, would tails be more likely next?"

**Common slip:** thinking the experiment was done wrong because it isn't exactly 10 heads.

### Example 3: Is the game fair?

Ava wins on a roll of 1 or 2. Sam wins on 3, 4, 5 or 6.

1. **Ava's chance.** 2 of 6 faces: 2/6 = 1/3.
2. **Sam's chance.** 4 of 6 faces: 4/6 = 2/3. Not the same, so the game is not fair.
3. **Fix it.** Ava wins on 1, 2 or 3. Sam wins on 4, 5 or 6. Now each has 1/2.
   *Say:* "Who would you rather be in the first game? Why?"

**Common slip:** calling it fair because each player has some numbers.
