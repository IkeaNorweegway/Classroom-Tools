When two lines cross, angles across from each other are equal and angles side by side on a straight line add to 180°. ### Example 1: Two lines cross

Two lines cross. One angle is 40°. Find the other three.

1. **Beside it on the line.** 180° − 40° = 140°.
2. **Across from each angle.** Opposite angles are equal: 40° across from 40°, and 140° across from 140°.
   *Say:* "Which angles sit on the same straight line? Which ones are across from each other?"

**Common slip:** writing 140° for the angle across from 40°. Across means equal.

### Example 2: Are the lines parallel?

A road crosses two lines. At the first line the angle is 70°. At the second line, in the same spot, it is 65°.

1. **Find the matching angles.** Both are in the same spot (say, top right) at each crossing.
2. **Compare them.** 70° and 65° are not equal. So the lines are not parallel.
   *Say:* "If the lines were parallel, would the road meet them at the same angle?"

**Common slip:** saying the lines are parallel because they look like it.

### Example 3: Matching parts

△ABC ≅ △RST. AB = 6 cm and ∠C = 80°. Find RS and ∠T.

1. **Match by place in the name.** A goes with R, B with S, C with T.
2. **Copy the measures.** RS matches AB, so RS = 6 cm. ∠T matches ∠C, so ∠T = 80°.
   *Say:* "Which letter is in the same place in the other name?"

**Common slip:** matching letters by the alphabet or by how the shapes look, not by their order.
