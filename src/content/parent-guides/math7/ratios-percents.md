Equal ratios come from multiplying or dividing both numbers by the same number: 2:5 = 4:10. They use this to find missing amounts and to tell which mix is stronger. A percent is a ratio out of 100. ### Example 1: Find the missing number in 3:5 = 12:?

1. **Find the factor.** 3 became 12. That is × 4.
2. **Do the same to the other number.** 5 × 4 = 20.
3. **Check.** 12:20 ÷ 4 = 3:5. ✓
   *Say:* "What did we multiply the first number by? Did we do the same to the second?"

**Common slip:** adding instead: 3 + 9 = 12, so 5 + 9 = 14.

### Example 2: Find 35% of 80

1. **Find 25%.** 80 ÷ 4 = 20.
2. **Find 10%.** 80 ÷ 10 = 8.
3. **Add.** 35% = 25% + 10%, so 20 + 8 = 28.
   *Say:* "Which easy percents add up to 35%?"

**Common slip:** taking 35% as "35 less" and answering 45.

### Example 3: A $60 jersey is 25% off. Add 5% GST.

1. **Find the discount.** 25% of $60 = $60 ÷ 4 = $15. The sale price is $60 − $15 = $45.
2. **Find the GST.** 10% of $45 = $4.50. 5% is half of that: $2.25.
3. **Add.** $45 + $2.25 = $47.25.
   *Say:* "Is the tax on the old price or the sale price?"

**Common slip:** working out the GST on $60 instead of the sale price.
