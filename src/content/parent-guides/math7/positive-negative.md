### Example 1: Opposite and absolute value of −3.5

1. **Find it on the number line.** −3.5 is 3.5 steps left of 0.
2. **The opposite** is the same distance on the other side: 3.5.
3. **The absolute value** is the distance from 0: |−3.5| = 3.5.
   *Say:* "How many steps is it from 0? Can a distance be negative?"

**Common slip:** writing |−3.5| = −3.5.

### Example 2: Change −3/8 to a decimal

1. **Ignore the sign for now.** 3/8 means 3 ÷ 8.
2. **Divide** (a calculator is fine): 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.
3. **Put the sign back:** −3/8 = −0.375.
   *Say:* "What does the fraction bar mean?"

**Common slip:** writing 3/8 as 3.8.

### Example 3: Order −1/2, 0.6, −0.75 and 1/4

1. **Change to decimals:** −0.5, 0.6, −0.75, 0.25.
2. **Picture a number line.** The negatives go on the left. −0.75 is further left than −0.5.
3. **Read left to right:** −0.75, −1/2, 1/4, 0.6.
   *Say:* "Which one is furthest left? That one is the smallest."

**Common slip:** putting −0.5 before −0.75 "because 0.5 is smaller than 0.75."
