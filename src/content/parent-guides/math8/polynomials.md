### Example 1: Name 4x³ − 2x + 1

1. **Count the terms.** 4x³, −2x and +1. Three terms: a trinomial.
2. **Find the highest power.** The powers are 3, 1 and 0. The highest is 3.
3. **Name it.** A cubic trinomial, degree 3.
   *Say:* "What is the biggest little number above an x?"

**Common slip:** counting the terms to get the degree. That works here by luck. For x³ + 1 it gives 2, but the degree is 3.

### Example 2: Simplify 2x² + 3x − x² + 4 − 5x

1. **Sort.** x² terms: 2x² and −x². x terms: 3x and −5x. Numbers: 4.
2. **Combine each group.** 2x² − x² = x². 3x − 5x = −2x.
3. **Write it highest power first.** x² − 2x + 4.
   *Say:* "Which sign belongs to the 5x? Did it move with it?"

**Common slip:** writing 2x² + 3x as 5x³. They are different kinds of terms.

### Example 3: Subtract (4x² − 3x + 1) − (x² + 2x − 6)

1. **Flip every sign in the second bracket.** It becomes −x² − 2x + 6.
2. **Remove the brackets.** 4x² − 3x + 1 − x² − 2x + 6.
3. **Combine.** 4x² − x² = 3x². −3x − 2x = −5x. 1 + 6 = 7. Answer: 3x² − 5x + 7.
   *Say:* "Did every term in the second bracket change its sign?"

**Common slip:** flipping only the x² term and getting 3x² − x − 5.
