plainSummary: "Your child is learning that fractions, decimals and integers all belong to one big family: the rational numbers. Any number that can be written as a fraction is rational, so 5 (which is 5/1), −0.75 and 2/3 all count. They learn that some fractions give decimals that stop (3/8 = 0.375) and some repeat forever (1/3 = 0.333…, written with a bar over the 3). They also add, subtract, multiply and divide negative fractions and decimals, and estimate to check that an answer makes sense."

### Example 1: Write 2/11 as a decimal

1. **Divide 2 by 11** (a calculator is fine): 0.181818…
2. **Spot the repeat.** The pair "18" keeps coming back.
3. **Write it with a bar** over the 18: 0.1̅8̅.
   *Say:* "Which digits repeat? Does the bar go over all of them?"

**Common slip:** rounding to 0.18 and calling it exact.

### Example 2: Add −1/2 + 1/3

1. **Find a common denominator.** Halves and thirds both make sixths: −1/2 = −3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6.
2. **Add the tops:** −3 + 2 = −1. Keep the 6.
3. **The answer is −1/6.**
   *Say:* "Are halves and thirds the same size? What can we change them into?"

**Common slip:** adding tops and bottoms to get 0/5.

### Example 3: Estimate, then work out −4.87 × 2.1

1. **Estimate with friendly numbers:** −5 × 2 = −10.
2. **Find the sign.** One negative, so the answer is negative.
3. **Calculate:** −4.87 × 2.1 = −10.227. It is close to −10, so it makes sense.
   *Say:* "What's your guess first? Is the real answer close to it?"

**Common slip:** forgetting the negative sign on the answer.
