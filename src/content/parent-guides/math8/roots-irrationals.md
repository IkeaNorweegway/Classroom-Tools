### Example 1: Is 0.09 a perfect square?

1. **Write it as a fraction:** 0.09 = 9/100.
2. **Check the top and bottom.** 9 = 3 × 3 and 100 = 10 × 10. Both are perfect squares.
3. **So yes:** 0.09 = 0.3 × 0.3.
   *Say:* "Can you write it as a fraction? Are the top and bottom both perfect squares?"

**Common slip:** saying 0.9 is a perfect square too. 0.9 = 9/10, and 10 is not a perfect square.

### Example 2: Solve x² = 49

1. **Ask which numbers times themselves give 49.** 7 × 7 = 49.
2. **Don't forget the negative.** (−7) × (−7) = 49 too.
3. **So x = 7 or x = −7**, written x = ±7.
   *Say:* "Is there another number that works? What about a negative?"

**Common slip:** writing √49 = ±7. √49 means just the positive root, 7.

### Example 3: Estimate √30

1. **Find the perfect squares around 30:** 25 = 5² and 36 = 6². So √30 is between 5 and 6.
2. **Decide which is closer.** 30 is near the middle, a bit closer to 25. Guess 5.5.
3. **Check by squaring:** 5.5 × 5.5 = 30.25. Very close! A calculator gives 5.477…
   *Say:* "Which two perfect squares is it between? Square your guess to check."

**Common slip:** guessing half of 30 (15). 15 × 15 = 225, far too big.
