title: Proportional Reasoning
plainSummary: "Your child is learning to compare amounts by multiplying and dividing instead of adding and subtracting. If one person has 12 cards and another has 4, the first has 3 times as many, and the second has 1/3 as many. In a ratio like 2 cups of juice to 5 cups of water, the number that turns the first amount into the second (here 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5) is called the constant of proportionality, k. With k, your child finds missing amounts: gas costs, map distances, recipe sizes. They also learn two kinds of relationships. In direct proportion, both amounts grow together (more litres, more cost). In inverse proportion, one goes up as the other goes down (more workers, fewer hours)."

### Example 1: Find k and use it

A recipe uses 4 cups of flour for 6 cups of milk. How much milk for 10 cups of flour?

1. **Find k.** k = milk ÷ flour = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. Each cup of flour needs 1.5 cups of milk.
2. **Multiply.** 1.5 × 10 = 15 cups of milk.
3. **Check.** 15 ÷ 10 = 1.5, the same k.
   *Say:* "What do you multiply the flour by to get the milk?"

**Common slip:** adding instead: "flour went up 6, so milk goes up 6," which gives 12 cups.

### Example 2: A map scale

A map uses 1 cm : 5 km. A lake is 60 km long. How long is it on the map?

1. **k = 5.** Each map centimetre is 5 real kilometres.
2. **You know the real length, so divide.** 60 ÷ 5 = 12 cm.
3. **Check.** 12 × 5 = 60 km.
   *Say:* "Are you going from map to real, or from real to map?"

**Common slip:** multiplying 60 × 5 = 300 cm, a map 3 m long.

### Example 3: More workers, less time

2 people shovel the rink in 6 h. How long would 4 people take?

1. **Find the total work.** 2 × 6 = 12 person-hours.
2. **Share it.** 12 ÷ 4 = 3 h.
3. **Make sense of it.** Twice the people, half the time. This is inverse proportion.
   *Say:* "Will more people take more time or less time?"

**Common slip:** answering 12 h, as if more people made the job longer.
