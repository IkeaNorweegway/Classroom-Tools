### Example 1: Is 36 a perfect square?

1. **Try to make a square.** 36 tiles can make 6 rows of 6.
2. **Write it.** 6² = 6 × 6 = 36, so √36 = 6.
   *Say:* "What times itself makes 36?"
3. **Try 12.** No two equal numbers multiply to 12 (3 × 4, 2 × 6), so 12 is not a perfect square.

**Common slip:** saying √36 = 18 (half). Ask your child to multiply 18 × 18 to see it's far too big.

### Example 2: Estimate √50

1. **Find the perfect squares on each side.** 49 < 50 < 64.
2. **Take their roots.** √49 = 7 and √64 = 8. So √50 is between 7 and 8.
3. **Which is closer?** 50 is only 1 away from 49, so √50 is just over 7.
   *Say:* "Which perfect square is it closer to?"
4. **Check with a calculator.** √50 ≈ 7.07.

**Common slip:** writing 7.5 every time ("halfway"). It is only halfway if the number is halfway between the two squares.
