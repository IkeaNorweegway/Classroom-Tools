A histogram shows measured data, like commute times, grouped into equal intervals with the bars touching. ### Example 1: Name the shape

Skiers wait for a lift: most wait 0–4 minutes, fewer wait 5–9, and only one or two wait 20 minutes or more.

1. **Find the peak.** It is on the left: most waits are short.
2. **Find the tail.** The few long waits stretch out to the right.
3. **Name it by the tail.** Skewed right.
   *Say:* "Which way does the long, thin part point?"

**Common slip:** calling it skewed left because the peak is on the left.

### Example 2: Pick the best average

Five houses on one street sold for $310 000, $340 000, $350 000, $365 000 and $1 500 000.

1. **Mean.** Add and divide by 5: $2 865 000 ÷ 5 = $573 000.
2. **Median.** The middle value in order: $350 000.
3. **Choose.** Four of the five houses cost less than the mean. The median is the better typical price.
   *Say:* "Which number is closer to most of the houses?"

**Common slip:** reporting the mean of $573 000 as a "typical" house.

### Example 3: Draw and read a circle graph

Of 240 students, 40% take the bus.

1. **Angle.** 0.40 × 360° = 144°. Draw that angle at the centre with a protractor.
2. **Count.** 0.40 × 240 = 96 students.
3. **Going back.** A 90° slice is 90 ÷ 360 = 25% of the circle, or 60 of the 240 students.
   *Say:* "What does the whole circle stand for?"

**Common slip:** multiplying the percent by 100 instead of 360 to get the angle.
