title: Independent & Dependent Events
plainSummary: "Your child is learning to find the chance of two things happening together, like flipping heads and rolling a 6. First they list every possible outcome, called the sample space, with a list, a table or a tree diagram. A coin and a die give 2 × 6 = 12 outcomes. Then they sort events into two kinds. Independent events don't affect each other: a coin can't change a die. For those, they multiply the chances: 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12. Dependent events do affect each other: if you pull two socks from a drawer and keep the first, the second pull has one fewer sock. For those, the second chance changes, and a tree diagram keeps track. Answers are written as fractions, decimals and percents."

watchFor:
  - "Adding instead of multiplying. The chance of heads AND a 6 is 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12, not 1/2 + 1/6."
  - "Counting outcomes by adding. A coin and a die give 2 × 6 = 12 outcomes, not 8."
  - "Using the same chance twice when nothing is put back. With 3 red and 2 blue, two reds is 3/5 × 2/4, not 3/5 × 3/5."
  - "Thinking a result is \"due\". After 4 tails in a row, heads is still 1/2. Coin flips are independent."
  - "Assuming two days of weather are always independent. Multiply only when the problem says so."

### Example 1: A coin and a die

Find the chance of flipping heads and rolling a 6.

1. **Independent?** Yes. The coin can't change the die.
2. **Multiply.** 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.
3. **Check by counting.** There are 2 × 6 = 12 outcomes. Only one is heads-and-6.
   *Say:* "Should needing two things to happen make the chance bigger or smaller?"

**Common slip:** adding 1/2 + 1/6 = 4/6. That makes the chance bigger, which can't be right.

### Example 2: Two socks from a drawer

A drawer has 4 black and 2 white socks. Pull out two in the dark. Find the chance both are black.

1. **First sock.** 4 black of 6: 4/6.
2. **Second sock.** One black sock is out. 3 black of 5 are left: 3/5.
3. **Multiply.** 4/6 × 3/5 = 12/30 = 2/5 = 0.4 = 40%.
   *Say:* "How many socks are left after the first pull? How many are black?"

**Common slip:** using 4/6 for both pulls, as if the first sock went back in.

### Example 3: Weather two days in a row

The forecast says 40% chance of snow Monday and 50% Tuesday. The question says to treat the days as independent.

1. **Write as decimals.** 0.4 and 0.5.
2. **Multiply.** 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2, a 20% chance of snow both days.
   *Say:* "Could a snowy Monday make Tuesday more likely to be snowy too?"

**Common slip:** adding the percents to get 90%.
