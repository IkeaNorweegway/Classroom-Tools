### Example 1: Find the scale factor

A rectangle is 4 cm by 2 cm. Its copy is 10 cm by 5 cm. Find the scale factor.

1. **Divide copy by original.** 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5.
2. **Check the other side.** 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5. The same, so the shapes are similar and the scale factor is 2.5.
   *Say:* "What number do you multiply 4 by to get 10? Does the same number work for 2 and 5?"

**Common slip:** subtracting (10 − 4 = 6) instead of dividing.

### Example 2: A trail map

A map of Edmonton's river valley uses 1 cm : 2 km. Two parks are 4.5 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they really?

1. **Read the scale.** Each centimetre on the map stands for 2 km.
2. **Multiply.** 4.5 × 2 = 9 km.
   *Say:* "How many groups of 2 km are there?"

**Common slip:** adding 4.5 + 2 instead of multiplying.

### Example 3: Enlarge on a grid

Enlarge the point (3, 1) from (0, 0) with a scale factor of 2.

1. **Multiply both numbers.** (3 × 2, 1 × 2) = (6, 2).
2. **Check with a line.** The line from (0, 0) through (3, 1) also passes through (6, 2).
   *Say:* "What happens to each number? Does the new point lie on the same line from (0, 0)?"

**Common slip:** adding 2 to each number to get (5, 3). That slides the point; it does not enlarge.
