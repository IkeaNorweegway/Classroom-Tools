plainSummary: "Your child is learning how much material it takes to cover a 3-D object, like wrapping paper for a box or the label on a can. This is surface area, measured in square units such as cm² or m². The main idea is the net: cut a box along its edges, lay it flat, and add up the areas of all the faces. They do this for boxes, triangular prisms (like a tent), pyramids and cylinders (like a can). They also draw objects built from cubes as flat views from the front, top and side, and as a single 3-D-looking picture on dot paper. They use π ≈ 3.14 and round to one decimal place."

### Example 1: Wrap a gift box

A box is 30 cm long, 20 cm wide and 10 cm tall. How much paper covers it?

1. **Find the three different faces.** Front: 30 × 10 = 300 cm². Side: 20 × 10 = 200 cm². Top: 30 × 20 = 600 cm².
2. **Each face has a matching face across from it, so double.** 2 × (300 + 200 + 600) = 2200 cm².
   *Say:* "How many faces does the box have? Did we count all six?"

**Common slip:** multiplying 30 × 20 × 10 = 6000. That is the volume, the space inside, not the paper outside.

### Example 2: A pyramid

A pyramid has a square base 6 cm on each side. Its slant height is 5 cm. Find its surface area.

1. **The base.** 6 × 6 = 36 cm².
2. **One triangle face.** ½ × 6 × 5 = 15 cm². There are 4, so 60 cm².
3. **Add.** 36 + 60 = 96 cm².
   *Say:* "Which height lies on the triangle face?"

**Common slip:** using the pyramid's height (the line straight down the inside) instead of the slant height on the face.

### Example 3: A soup can

A can has a radius of 4 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find its surface area.

1. **The two circles.** 3.14 × 4 × 4 = 50.24 cm² each, so 100.48 cm².
2. **The label.** It is a rectangle as long as the circumference: 2 × 3.14 × 4 = 25.12 cm. Its area is 25.12 × 10 = 251.2 cm².
3. **Add and round.** 100.48 + 251.2 = 351.68, about 351.7 cm².
   *Say:* "If you unrolled the label, how long would it be?"

**Common slip:** making the label 8 cm long (the width of the can) instead of 25.12 cm.
