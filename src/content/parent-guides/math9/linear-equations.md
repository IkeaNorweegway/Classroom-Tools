### Example 1: An equation with brackets

**Solve 3(x − 2) = 2x + 5.**

1. **Expand the brackets.** The 3 multiplies both terms inside: 3x − 6 = 2x + 5.
   *Say:* "What does the 3 multiply?"
2. **Get the x terms on one side.** Subtract 2x from both sides: x − 6 = 5.
3. **Undo the −6.** Add 6 to both sides: x = 11.
4. **Check.** Left side: 3(11 − 2) = 27. Right side: 2(11) + 5 = 27. Both sides match.

**Common slip:** writing 3(x − 2) as 3x − 2, which multiplies only the first term.

### Example 2: An inequality with a negative

**Solve −2x + 7 > 15 and show it on a number line.**

1. **Subtract 7 from both sides:** −2x > 8.
2. **Divide both sides by −2.** Dividing by a negative number flips the sign: x < −4.
   *Say:* "What did you divide by? Was it negative?"
3. **Test a number.** Try x = −5, which should work: −2(−5) + 7 = 17, and 17 > 15. ✓ Try x = 0, which shouldn't: 7 > 15 is false. ✓
4. **Number line.** Open circle at −4 (because −4 itself doesn't work), with an arrow pointing left.

**Common slip:** writing x > −4. Testing a number catches this every time.

### Example 3: From words to an inequality

**A farmers' market booth costs $60 for the day. Carrots sell for $4 a bag. How many bags must be sold to make a profit?**

1. **Say what the letter means.** Let b be the number of bags sold.
2. **Write it.** Money in must be more than the booth cost: 4b > 60.
3. **Solve.** Divide both sides by 4: b > 15.
4. **Answer in words.** You can't sell part of a bag, so at least **16 bags**.
   *Say:* "Does 15 bags make a profit, or just break even?"

**Common slip:** answering "15". Selling 15 bags only pays for the booth.
