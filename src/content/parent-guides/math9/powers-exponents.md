### Example 1: Multiplying powers

**Simplify 2³ × 2⁴.**

1. **Write it out.** (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2 × 2)
2. **Count the 2s.** There are seven, so the answer is 2⁷.
   *Say:* "How many 2s are being multiplied altogether?"
3. **See the shortcut.** Same base, multiplying: add the exponents, 3 + 4 = 7.
4. **Value:** 2⁷ = 128.

**Common slip:** writing 2¹² (multiplying the exponents) or 4⁷ (multiplying the bases).

### Example 2: Why 5⁰ = 1

**Show that 5⁰ = 1.**

1. **Divide a power by itself.** 5³ ÷ 5³ = 125 ÷ 125 = 1.
2. **Use the dividing shortcut.** Same base, dividing: subtract the exponents, 5³ ÷ 5³ = 5⁰.
3. **Both answers must be equal,** so 5⁰ = 1.
   *Say:* "What do you get when you divide any number by itself?"

### Example 3: Brackets and negatives

**Evaluate (−2)⁴ and −2⁴.**

1. **With brackets** the negative is part of the base: (−2)(−2)(−2)(−2) = 16.
2. **Without brackets** the exponent only touches the 2: −(2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = −16.
   *Say:* "What exactly is the exponent attached to?"

**Common slip:** giving the same answer for both.
