### Example 1: Estimating a square root

**Estimate √30.**

1. **Find the perfect squares on either side.** 25 = 5² and 36 = 6², so √30 is between 5 and 6.
   *Say:* "Which two perfect squares is 30 between?"
2. **Decide which it's closer to.** 30 is 5 away from 25 and 6 away from 36, so the answer is a little under halfway: about 5.5.
3. **Check by squaring.** 5.5 × 5.5 = 30.25, which is very close. A calculator gives √30 ≈ 5.48.

### Example 2: A ladder against a wall

**A 6 m ladder leans against a wall with its foot 2 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?**

1. **Sketch it and find the hypotenuse.** The ladder is across from the right angle, so it is the hypotenuse: c = 6.
   *Say:* "Which side is across from the right angle?"
2. **Write the theorem.** a² + 2² = 6², so a² + 4 = 36.
3. **Solve.** a² = 32, so a = √32 ≈ 5.66 m.

**Common slip:** adding 2² + 6² = 40 and getting about 6.32 m. That's longer than the ladder, which can't be right.

### Example 3: Square roots of fractions and decimals

**Find √(49/64) and √0.36.**

1. **Fractions:** take the root of the top and the bottom. √49 = 7 and √64 = 8, so √(49/64) = 7/8.
2. **Decimals:** 0.36 = 36/100, so √0.36 = 6/10 = 0.6.
3. **Check:** 7/8 × 7/8 = 49/64 ✓ and 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36 ✓

**Common slip:** saying √0.36 = 0.06. Checking by squaring catches it.
