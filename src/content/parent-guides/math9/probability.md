### Example 1: Independent events

**What is the probability of flipping heads and rolling a 6?**

1. **The coin doesn't affect the die,** so the events are independent.
   *Say:* "Does the coin change anything about the die?"
2. **Multiply.** P(heads) × P(6) = 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.
3. **Check with a table.** 2 coin results × 6 die results = 12 outcomes, and only 1 is heads-and-6. ✓

### Example 2: Dependent events

**A bag has 3 red and 2 blue marbles. Two are drawn without putting the first back. What is the probability both are red?**

1. **First draw:** 3 red out of 5, so 3/5.
2. **Second draw:** now there are 2 red left out of 4, so 2/4.
   *Say:* "What's left in the bag after the first one comes out?"
3. **Multiply.** 3/5 × 2/4 = 6/20 = 3/10.

**Compare:** if the first marble went back in, it would be 3/5 × 3/5 = 9/25. Not replacing changes the answer.

### Example 3: Experiment versus theory

**A die is rolled 30 times and lands on 6 eight times. Is the die unfair?**

1. **Experimental probability:** 8/30 ≈ 27%.
2. **Theoretical probability:** 1/6 ≈ 17%.
3. **30 rolls is a small experiment,** so results like this happen by chance. Many more rolls would be needed to judge the die.
