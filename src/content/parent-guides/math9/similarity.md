### Example 1: Using a scale factor

**A triangle has sides 3, 4 and 5 cm. A similar triangle has a shortest side of 9 cm. Find its other sides.**

1. **Match the shortest sides and find the scale factor.** 9 ÷ 3 = 3.
   *Say:* "How many times bigger is the new triangle?"
2. **Multiply every side by 3.** 4 × 3 = 12 cm and 5 × 3 = 15 cm.

### Example 2: Measuring a tree with a shadow

**A 1 m stick casts a 1.5 m shadow. At the same time, a tree casts a 12 m shadow. How tall is the tree?**

1. **The sun makes the same angle for both,** so the stick and its shadow form a triangle similar to the tree and its shadow.
2. **Set up a proportion.** height ÷ shadow is the same for both: h/12 = 1/1.5.
3. **Solve.** h = 12 ÷ 1.5 = 8 m.
   *Say:* "Is the tree taller than its shadow, or shorter? Does the stick match?"

### Example 3: A map scale

**A map has a scale of 1 : 50 000. Two towns are 3 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they really?**

1. **Each map centimetre stands for 50 000 real centimetres.** 3 × 50 000 = 150 000 cm.
2. **Convert.** 150 000 cm = 1500 m = 1.5 km.

**Common slip:** forgetting to convert centimetres to kilometres.
