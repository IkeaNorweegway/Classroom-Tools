### Example 1: Reading rock layers

**A canyon wall has layer A at the bottom, B in the middle and C on top. Which is oldest?**

1. **Layers build up from the bottom,** like a stack of papers.
2. **So A is oldest and C is youngest.**
   *Say:* "Which layer had to be there first?"
3. **Fossils follow the same rule:** a fossil in layer A is older than one in layer C.

**Common slip:** thinking the top layer is oldest because it's been exposed the longest.

### Example 2: Where plates meet

**What happens at the three kinds of plate boundaries?**

1. **Pulling apart (divergent):** magma rises and makes new crust, like the ridge in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean.
2. **Pushing together (convergent):** one plate can slide under another, causing earthquakes and volcanoes, or the crust crumples into mountains like the Rockies.
3. **Sliding past (transform):** plates grind sideways, causing earthquakes.
   *Say:* "Use your hands: show me pulling apart, pushing together and sliding past."

### Example 3: Around the rock cycle

**How could granite become quartzite?**

1. **Granite (igneous)** is weathered into sand.
2. **The sand** is buried in layers and pressed into **sandstone (sedimentary)**.
3. **Deep underground,** heat and pressure change it into **quartzite (metamorphic)**.
   *Say:* "What had to happen to change it each time?"
