### Example 1: What kind of structure?

**Classify an igloo, a dam and a power-line tower.**

1. **Igloo:** a thin, curved dome. **Shell structure.**
2. **Dam:** a huge, solid mass of concrete. **Solid structure.**
3. **Power-line tower:** metal pieces joined in triangles. **Frame structure.**
   *Say:* "Is it one solid piece, a skeleton, or a thin curved skin?"

### Example 2: Forces in a shelf

**A shelf full of books sags a little in the middle. What forces are acting on it?**

1. **Loads:** the books are a live load; the shelf's own weight is a dead load.
2. **Inside the shelf:** the top surface is squeezed (compression) and the bottom surface is stretched (tension).
   *Say:* "If it bent more, which side would get longer?"
3. **A fix:** a thicker shelf, a support in the middle, or a stiffer material.

### Example 3: A lever

**A 150 N push on a crowbar lifts a 600 N rock. What is the mechanical advantage?**

1. **Formula:** mechanical advantage = output force ÷ input force.
2. **Calculate:** 600 ÷ 150 = 4. The crowbar multiplies the push by 4.
3. **The trade-off:** your end moves about 4 times farther than the rock does.
   *Say:* "Did the crowbar make energy, or just change the force?"

**Common slip:** dividing the wrong way and getting 0.25.
