### Example 1: The spoon puzzle

**A metal spoon and a wooden spoon sit on the same table. Why does the metal one feel colder?**

1. **They are the same temperature.** They've both been in the same room for hours.
2. **Your hand is warmer than both,** so heat flows from your hand into the spoon.
3. **Metal is a good conductor.** It pulls heat away from your hand quickly, so your skin cools and it feels cold. Wood is an insulator, so less heat leaves your hand.
   *Say:* "Which one is taking heat out of your hand faster?"

**Common slip:** saying the metal "gives off cold".

### Example 2: Heat or temperature?

**A cup of boiling water (100 °C) and a bathtub of warm water (40 °C). Which has the higher temperature? Which has more heat energy?**

1. **Temperature:** the cup. Its particles are moving faster.
2. **Heat energy:** the bathtub. It has far more water, so far more moving particles in total.
3. **Test it:** the bathtub would melt much more ice.
   *Say:* "Which one has more particles moving?"

### Example 3: Which way did the heat travel?

**Name the heat transfer: (a) a pot handle burns your hand, (b) warm air from a heater rises across the room, (c) you feel the warmth of a campfire on your face.**

1. **(a) Conduction.** Heat passes through the metal by touch, particle to particle.
2. **(b) Convection.** Warm air rises, cool air sinks, and the air circulates.
3. **(c) Radiation.** Heat travels as invisible rays straight to your face.
   *Say:* "Did it need touching, moving air, or neither?"
