### Example 1: Reading a food web

**In a food web, arrows go from grass to grasshopper and from grasshopper to frog. What do the arrows mean?**

1. **Each arrow shows energy moving from the food to the eater.** The grasshopper eats grass; the frog eats the grasshopper.
   *Say:* "Who is eating whom?"
2. **Name the roles.** Grass is the producer. The grasshopper is a primary consumer. The frog is a secondary consumer.
3. **Trace it back.** The energy started with the Sun, which the grass used to make food.

**Common slip:** drawing arrows from the eater to the food.

### Example 2: Why are there so few hawks?

**Grass captures 10 000 units of energy. About 10% passes to each next level. How much reaches each level?**

1. **Grasshoppers** get about 10% of 10 000 = 1000 units.
2. **Frogs** get about 10% of 1000 = 100 units. **Snakes** get about 10 units.
3. **Where does the rest go?** It's used for living (moving, growing, keeping warm) and lost as heat.
   *Say:* "So why are there fewer snakes than grasshoppers?"

### Example 3: When one species disappears

**What might happen if all the frogs in a pond disappeared?**

1. **Follow the arrows into the frog:** grasshoppers aren't eaten as much, so there may be more of them.
2. **Then the arrows out of the frog:** snakes lose food, so there may be fewer snakes.
3. **Keep going:** with more grasshoppers, more plants get eaten.
   *Say:* "What else is connected to that?"

**Common slip:** stopping after one effect. Changes ripple through the whole web.
