### Example 1: Photosynthesis in words

**What does a plant need to make food, and what does it produce?**

1. **Inputs:** carbon dioxide (from the air) and water (from the soil).
2. **Energy:** sunlight, captured by green chlorophyll in the leaves.
3. **Outputs:** sugar (the plant's food) and oxygen.
   *Say:* "What goes in, and what comes out?"
4. **Written as a word equation:** carbon dioxide + water → (light) → sugar + oxygen.

**Common slip:** saying plants take in oxygen and give off carbon dioxide during photosynthesis. That's the reverse.

### Example 2: Water up, sugar around

**How do water and sugar move through a plant?**

1. **Roots** absorb water and minerals from the soil.
2. **Xylem** (tiny tubes) carries the water up the stem to the leaves.
3. **Leaves** use the water to make sugar.
4. **Phloem** (other tubes) carries the sugar to the roots, fruits and new growth.
   *Say:* "Which way does the water go? Which way does the sugar go?"

### Example 3: Seeds or cuttings?

**Compare growing a sunflower from seed with growing a strawberry from a runner.**

1. **Seeds** come from flowers, after pollination. Each seed is a little different from its parents, and seeds can spread far.
2. **Runners and cuttings** grow an exact copy of the parent plant, and grow faster.
   *Say:* "Which way gives more variety? Which way is faster?"
3. **Farmers use both:** seeds for crops like wheat and canola, and cuttings or tubers for plants like potatoes.
