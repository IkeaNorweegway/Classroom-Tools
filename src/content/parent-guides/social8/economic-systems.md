### Example 1: The three economic questions

**A student runs a lemonade stand. How does it answer the three economic questions?**

1. **What to produce?** Lemonade.
2. **How to produce it?** By hand, with lemons, sugar and water.
3. **For whom?** Customers who are willing to pay.
   *Say:* "Who made those decisions?" (The student did. That's how a market economy works.)

### Example 2: Which system is it?

**Name the system: (a) the government tells each factory how many cars to make, (b) businesses compete and set their own prices, (c) private stores plus government-paid health care.**

1. **(a) Command economy:** the government decides.
2. **(b) Market economy:** businesses and buyers decide.
3. **(c) Mixed economy:** a blend, like Canada.
   *Say:* "Who's making the decision in each one?"

### Example 3: Surplus, deficit and debt

**Imagine a government collects $50 billion in a year and spends $54 billion. The next year it collects $56 billion and spends $53 billion.**

1. **Year 1:** spending is more than income, so it's a **deficit** of $4 billion. The government borrows, so its **debt** grows by $4 billion.
2. **Year 2:** income is more than spending, so it's a **surplus** of $3 billion. It could use that to pay down some debt.
   *Say:* "Is that one year's gap, or everything owed so far?"

**Common slip:** calling the total amount owed "the deficit".
