### Example 1: Turning a concern into action

**Your child is bothered by litter in the local park. What could they do?**

1. **Name the issue clearly:** litter is building up in the park.
2. **Find out who decides:** the city looks after parks.
3. **List options:** organise a clean-up, ask the city for more bins, write to a city councillor, or make posters.
   *Say:* "Which of these could you actually start this month?"
4. **Pick one and plan the steps:** who to ask, what to bring, when to do it.

### Example 2: From global to local

**How could a drought in another part of the world affect people in Canada?**

1. **Prices:** if crops fail, some foods cost more here.
2. **Help:** Canadian charities and the government may send aid, sometimes asking for donations.
3. **People:** some families may have to leave and could settle in Canada.
   *Say:* "Who here might notice it first?"

### Example 3: Who can vote?

**Can each person vote in a federal election? (a) a 17-year-old citizen (b) a 30-year-old permanent resident who isn't a citizen yet (c) a 19-year-old citizen**

1. **The rules:** a Canadian citizen, at least 18, who can prove who they are and where they live.
2. **(a) No:** not 18 yet. **(b) No:** not yet a citizen. **(c) Yes.**
   *Say:* "Which rule does each person miss?"
3. **But all three can volunteer, advocate and take part in other ways.**
