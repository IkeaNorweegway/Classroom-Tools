### Example 1: The road to Confederation

**Put these in order and say what each did.**

1. **Charlottetown Conference (1864):** leaders agreed to talk about a bigger union.
2. **Quebec Conference (1864):** they wrote the plan, the 72 Resolutions, including how power would be shared.
3. **London Conference (1866–67):** they finalized the plan with Britain.
4. **British North America Act (July 1, 1867):** the plan became law and Canada was created.
   *Say:* "Why do you think it took three meetings and three years?"

### Example 2: The three branches

**Match each part to its branch.**

1. **Executive (carries out laws):** the Crown, represented by the Governor General, and the prime minister and Cabinet.
2. **Legislative (makes laws):** Parliament, which is the House of Commons (elected MPs) and the Senate (appointed senators).
3. **Judicial (interprets laws):** the courts.

**Common slip:** saying the Senate is elected like the House of Commons.

### Example 3: Federal or provincial?

**Who is responsible: Ottawa or Alberta?**

1. **Money and banking:** federal.
2. **Schools:** provincial.
3. **The army and navy:** federal.
4. **Hospitals:** provincial.
   *Say:* "Why might it make sense for each province to run its own schools?"
