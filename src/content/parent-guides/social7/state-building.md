### Example 1: Why join together?

**Give two reasons the colonies agreed to Confederation in 1867.**

1. **Fear of the United States.** After the American Civil War (1861–65) the U.S. had a huge army, and some Americans talked about taking over British North America.
2. **Strength together.** Joined as one country, the colonies could build a railway, defend themselves and trade more easily.
   *Say:* "Why might a small colony feel safer as part of a bigger country?"

**Common slip:** saying Canada became independent from Britain in 1867. It became a Dominion that ran its own affairs at home.

### Example 2: The Red River Conflict

**Explain the Red River Conflict from two points of view.**

1. **The Métis at Red River:** in 1869 Canada prepared to take over the land where they lived without asking them. Louis Riel formed a provisional government to protect their land and rights.
2. **The Canadian government:** it had arranged to take over Rupert's Land and wanted to control the West before the United States could.
3. **The result:** Manitoba joined Canada in 1870, and the government promised Métis land through a system called scrip, which many Métis did not end up keeping.
   *Say:* "Was Riel defending his people or rebelling against Canada? What evidence supports each side?"

### Example 3: Reading a timeline of new provinces

**Put these in order: Alberta, Manitoba, British Columbia, Newfoundland and Labrador.**

1. Manitoba (1870), British Columbia (1871), Alberta (1905), Newfoundland and Labrador (1949).
2. **Ask why:** Alberta and Saskatchewan became provinces in 1905 because enough settlers had arrived to need their own governments.
