The area of a rectangular garden with an unknown width isn't a number you can calculate yet — it's an expression, like (x + 6)(x + 2). Multiplying that out is exactly what this unit is about, and it turns out to matter for a lot more than gardens: engineering, geometry, and finance problems all rely on the same skill.

This unit builds from representing polynomials to operating on them with confidence.

## What you will explore

You will represent polynomials using **algebra tiles** and **area models**, which make an abstract expression like x² + 5x + 6 into something you can literally see as a rectangle's area — a picture that makes multiplying binomials, like (x + 2)(x + 3), much easier to understand rather than just memorize.

From there, you will practice adding and subtracting polynomials (paying close attention to distributing a negative sign correctly — a classic place for errors) and multiplying a binomial by a binomial, connecting each expansion back to the area model that explains why the steps work. You will also apply these skills to real problems: finding the area of a composite shape, or the remaining area after a smaller region is removed from a larger one.

## By the end of this unit

You will be able to add, subtract, and multiply polynomials confidently, explain what each term in an expanded expression represents using the area model, and set up and simplify a polynomial expression to solve a real area or measurement problem.
