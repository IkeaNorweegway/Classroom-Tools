2⁵ isn't "2 times 5" — it's 2 multiplied by itself 5 times, and that one distinction is the seed of an entire set of rules that let you simplify expressions that would otherwise take forever to expand by hand.

This unit builds each exponent law from first principles — by actually expanding powers and counting factors — so that the rules make sense rather than needing to be memorized blind.

## What you will explore

You will discover the exponent laws the way they were discovered historically: by expanding powers and noticing the pattern. Multiplying powers with the same base (aᵐ × aⁿ) means combining all the factors together, which is why the exponents *add*. Dividing powers with the same base means factors cancel, which is why the exponents *subtract* — and that subtraction is also what explains why any nonzero number to the power of zero equals 1, and why a negative exponent means "move to the other side of the fraction," not "the answer is negative."

You will also work through **power of a power** (multiply the exponents) and **power of a product or quotient** (the exponent distributes to everything inside the brackets), always checking new rules against the "expand it and count" method so that a forgotten rule can always be rebuilt rather than guessed.

## By the end of this unit

You will be able to simplify expressions involving multiple exponent laws at once, explain why each law works by connecting it back to repeated multiplication, and correctly handle zero and negative exponents without treating them as special exceptions to memorize separately.
