Two data points scattered on a graph don't tell you anything. A hundred of them, with a clear trend running through them, can tell you whether ice cream sales really do rise with temperature — or whether a claim in an advertisement is backed by real evidence at all.

This unit is about reading data critically: not just making a graph, but asking whether the data behind it is trustworthy in the first place.

## What you will explore

You will construct and interpret **scatter plots**, identifying whether two variables show a **positive**, **negative**, or **no correlation**, and sketching a **line of best fit** to describe the trend. You will also learn to spot **outliers** — data points that don't fit the pattern — and think about whether an outlier should be explained, excluded, or taken as evidence the pattern itself is more complicated than it first appeared.

Just as importantly, you will critically evaluate how data was collected in the first place: whether a sample was large enough and representative, whether survey questions were worded in a way that could bias the results, and whether correlation between two variables actually implies that one causes the other. A strong trend line does not automatically mean a strong argument.

## By the end of this unit

You will be able to create a scatter plot from data, describe the type and strength of correlation shown, and evaluate a real data set or survey for potential bias, unreliable sampling, or claims that overstate what the data actually supports.
