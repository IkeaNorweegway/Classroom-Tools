You cannot walk up to a grain elevator with a tape measure and find its height directly — but you can measure its shadow, measure the shadow of a metre stick standing next to it, and use similar triangles to calculate the elevator's height exactly. This is indirect measurement, and it has been used for real surveying and engineering for thousands of years.

This unit is about recognizing similarity and putting it to work.

## What you will explore

You will learn what makes two shapes **similar** — the same shape, different size, with corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides in the same ratio — and how to verify similarity using the **AA**, **SSS**, and **SAS** criteria for triangles. From there, you will calculate and apply **scale factor**, the single number that relates every corresponding length between two similar shapes, and use it to find missing side lengths.

You will also connect similarity to real measurement problems: two objects casting shadows at the same time form similar triangles with the sun's angle, which is exactly how you can find the height of a building, a tree, or a grain elevator without ever touching it. You will practice setting up these problems correctly, matching corresponding sides between the small triangle and the large one.

## By the end of this unit

You will be able to determine whether two triangles are similar and by which criterion, calculate an unknown side using scale factor, and set up and solve an indirect measurement problem using similar triangles formed by shadows or scale diagrams.
