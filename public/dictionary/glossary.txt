# ───────────── SCIENCE: matter and energy ─────────────
matter | Anything that takes up space and has mass. Solids, liquids and gases are all matter. | A rock, water and the air in a balloon are matter.
particle | A very tiny piece of matter, too small to see. | Water is made of tiny particles.
particle model | An idea that says all matter is made of tiny particles that are always moving, with spaces between them. | The particle model explains why a gas spreads out to fill a room.
atom | The smallest building block of an element. Everything is made of atoms. | A water molecule has three atoms.
molecule | Two or more atoms joined together. | A water molecule is two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.
element | A pure substance made of only one kind of atom. | Gold, oxygen and iron are elements.
compound | A substance made of two or more different elements joined together. | Water is a compound of hydrogen and oxygen.
mixture | Two or more things put together that can be separated again. | Salad and trail mix are mixtures.
pure substance | Matter that is made of only one kind of material. | Distilled water is a pure substance.
solid | A state of matter that keeps its own shape. Its particles are packed close and only jiggle in place. | Ice, a pencil and a rock are solids.
liquid | A state of matter that flows and takes the shape of its container. | Water and juice are liquids.
gas | A state of matter that spreads out to fill any space it is in. Its particles are far apart. | The air in a tire is a gas.
state of matter, states of matter | One of the forms matter can take: solid, liquid or gas. | Water can be in the solid state (ice) or the liquid state.
phase change | When matter changes from one state to another, like ice turning to water. | Melting and freezing are phase changes.
melting | Changing from a solid to a liquid when heated. | An ice cube melting into water.
freezing | Changing from a liquid to a solid when cooled. | Water freezing into ice.
evaporation | Changing from a liquid to a gas at the surface of the liquid. | A puddle drying up on a sunny day.
condensation | Changing from a gas to a liquid when cooled. | Water drops forming on a cold glass.
boiling | When a liquid is heated so much that bubbles of gas form all through it. | Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.
sublimation | Changing straight from a solid to a gas, without becoming a liquid first. | Dry ice turns into a cloud of gas.
density | How much mass is packed into a certain amount of space. | A rock is denser than a sponge of the same size.
mass | How much matter is in an object. It is measured in grams or kilograms. | A bowling ball has more mass than a tennis ball.
volume | How much space something takes up. | The volume of a juice box is about 200 millilitres.
weight | How strongly gravity pulls on an object. | You would weigh less on the Moon than on Earth.
temperature | A measure of how hot or cold something is. It tells how fast the particles are moving. | Room temperature is about 21 degrees Celsius.
thermometer | A tool that measures temperature. | A thermometer showed -5 degrees Celsius outside.
celsius | The temperature scale used in Canada. Water freezes at 0 degrees and boils at 100 degrees. | It is 20 degrees Celsius today.
heat | Energy that moves from something warmer to something cooler. | Heat flows from a hot cup into your cold hands.
thermal expansion | When matter gets bigger because it is heated. | Bridges have gaps so they can grow in summer heat.
contraction | When something gets smaller, such as when it cools. | Metal rails contract on a cold winter night.
expansion | When something grows bigger or spreads out. | The expansion of a heated gas can pop a balloon.
conduction | Heat moving through a solid when particles bump into each other. | A metal spoon gets hot in a pot of soup.
convection | Heat moving through a liquid or gas as the warm part rises and the cool part sinks. | Warm air rising above a heater.
radiation | Energy that travels as waves, even through empty space. | Heat from the Sun reaches Earth by radiation.
insulator | A material that does not let heat or electricity move through it easily. | Foam and wool are good insulators.
conductor | A material that lets heat or electricity move through it easily. | Copper wire is a good conductor.
energy | The ability to make something move or change. | Food gives your body energy.
kinetic energy | The energy an object has because it is moving. | A rolling ball has kinetic energy.
potential energy | Stored energy that an object has because of where it is or how it is held. | A book on a high shelf has potential energy.
thermal energy | The energy of the moving particles in matter. It is what we feel as heat. | Hot soup has more thermal energy than cold soup.
light energy | Energy that we can see. | The Sun gives off light energy.
chemical energy | Energy stored in food, fuel and batteries. | A banana has chemical energy.
electrical energy | The energy of moving electric charges. | Electrical energy lights a lamp.
sound energy | Energy that travels as vibrations we can hear. | A drum gives off sound energy.
energy transformation | When energy changes from one form to another. | A toaster changes electrical energy into heat.
conservation of energy | The rule that energy is never made or destroyed. It only changes form. | The energy in a falling ball becomes sound and heat when it lands.
renewable | Something that can be used over and over because nature makes more of it. | Wind and sunlight are renewable.
nonrenewable, non-renewable | Something that will run out because it takes millions of years to form. | Coal and oil are nonrenewable.
fossil fuel | A fuel made from the remains of ancient plants and animals. | Coal, oil and natural gas are fossil fuels.
fuel | A material that is burned to make heat or power. | Gasoline is a fuel for cars.
solar | Having to do with the Sun. | A solar panel turns sunlight into electricity.
hydroelectric | Electricity made from the energy of moving water. | A dam can make hydroelectric power.
turbine | A machine with blades that spin when wind, water or steam pushes on them. | Wind turbines spin to make electricity.
generator | A machine that changes movement into electricity. | A turbine spins a generator.
circuit | A path that electricity can travel around, usually in a loop. | A circuit has a battery, wires and a bulb.
battery | A device that stores chemical energy and changes it into electrical energy. | A battery powers a flashlight.
natural resource | Something from nature that people use. | Water, trees and oil are natural resources.
resource | Something useful that people can use or get value from. | Water is an important resource.
conserve, conservation | To use less and protect something so it lasts. | We conserve water by turning off the tap.
efficiency | How well something uses energy without wasting it. | An LED bulb has high efficiency.
efficient | Doing a job well without wasting energy, time or materials. | A bike is an efficient way to travel.

# ───────────── SCIENCE: forces and machines ─────────────
force | A push or a pull on an object. | Kicking a ball is a force.
gravity | The force that pulls objects toward each other. On Earth it pulls things toward the ground. | Gravity makes a dropped pencil fall.
friction | A force that slows down things that rub together. | Friction stops your shoes from slipping.
air resistance | Friction from the air that slows down a moving object. | A parachute uses air resistance to slow a fall.
balanced forces | Forces that are equal and opposite, so the object does not change how it moves. | Two teams pulling a rope equally.
unbalanced forces | Forces that are not equal, so the object speeds up, slows down or changes direction. | One team pulling harder wins the tug of war.
net force | The total of all the forces acting on an object. | If you push 5 N and friction pushes back 2 N, the net force is 3 N.
newton | The unit used to measure force. Its short form is N. | Lifting a small apple takes about one newton.
motion | Movement. When something changes its position. | The motion of a swing.
speed | How fast something moves. It is distance divided by time. | A car going 50 kilometres each hour.
velocity | Speed in a certain direction. | A car going 50 km/h north.
acceleration | How quickly the speed or direction of something changes. | A car speeding up at a green light.
inertia | The tendency of an object to keep doing what it is doing, staying still or moving. | You lurch forward when a bus stops suddenly because of inertia.
magnet | An object that attracts iron and some other metals. | A magnet sticks to a fridge.
magnetic | Able to be pulled by a magnet, or acting like one. | Iron is magnetic. Wood is not.
attract | To pull something closer. | Opposite poles of magnets attract.
repel | To push something away. | Two north poles repel each other.
lever | A bar that turns on a point to help lift or move a load. | A seesaw is a lever.
fulcrum | The point a lever turns on. | The middle of a seesaw is its fulcrum.
pulley | A wheel with a rope that helps lift things. | A flagpole uses a pulley.
wheel and axle | A wheel fixed to a rod that turns together with it. | A car wheel and axle.
inclined plane | A flat surface that slopes, making it easier to lift something up. | A ramp is an inclined plane.
simple machine | A tool with few or no moving parts that makes work easier. | Levers, ramps and pulleys are simple machines.
machine | A tool that makes a job easier by changing the size or direction of a force. | A wheelbarrow is a machine.
load | The object that a machine moves or lifts. | The load on the seesaw was a heavy backpack.
effort | The force you put in to move a load. | A long handle needs less effort.
buoyancy | The upward push of a liquid or gas on an object in it. | Buoyancy helps a boat float.
float | To stay on top of a liquid or in the air. | A cork floats on water.
sink | To go down below the surface of a liquid. | A coin sinks in water.
pressure | How much force is pushing on a certain area. | Deep in the ocean the water pressure is very high.
structure | Something built from parts that are put together. | A bridge is a structure.
stable | Steady and not likely to fall over or change. | A wide base makes a tower stable.
stability | How steady and hard to tip over something is. | A low centre of mass adds stability.
compression | A squeezing force that pushes material together. | A column is under compression.
tension | A stretching force that pulls material apart. | A rope is under tension in a tug of war.

# ───────────── SCIENCE: living systems ─────────────
living thing | Something that grows, needs food and energy, and can make more of its kind. | A tree, a fish and a mushroom are living things.
organism | Any living thing. | A bee is an organism.
cell | The smallest living unit. All living things are made of cells. | Your body is made of trillions of cells.
tissue | A group of cells that do the same job. | Muscle is a tissue.
organ | A body part made of tissues that does a special job. | The heart is an organ.
organ system | A group of organs that work together. | The stomach and intestines are part of the digestive system.
ecosystem | All the living and nonliving things in one area and how they work together. | A pond with its fish, plants, water and rocks is an ecosystem.
habitat | The place where a plant or animal lives and finds what it needs. | A beaver's habitat is a stream or pond.
community | All the different living things in one area. | The plants and animals of a meadow make a community.
population | All the living things of one kind in one area. | The population of deer in a forest.
species | A group of living things that can have babies together. | A wolf is one species. A coyote is another.
biotic | Living, or made by living things. | Plants and animals are biotic parts of an ecosystem.
abiotic | Not living. | Sunlight, water and rocks are abiotic.
biodiversity | The number of different kinds of living things in an area. | A rainforest has high biodiversity.
producer | A living thing, such as a plant, that makes its own food. | Grass is a producer.
consumer | A living thing that eats other living things for energy. | A rabbit is a consumer.
primary consumer | An animal that eats plants. | A deer is a primary consumer.
secondary consumer | An animal that eats primary consumers. | A fox that eats a rabbit.
tertiary consumer | An animal that eats secondary consumers. | A hawk that eats a snake.
herbivore | An animal that eats only plants. | A cow is a herbivore.
carnivore | An animal that eats only other animals. | A wolf is a carnivore.
omnivore | An animal that eats both plants and animals. | Bears and people are omnivores.
predator | An animal that hunts and eats other animals. | A lynx is a predator.
prey | An animal that is hunted and eaten by another animal. | A hare is prey for a lynx.
apex predator | A predator at the top of the food chain that nothing else hunts. | A grizzly bear is an apex predator.
decomposer | A living thing that breaks down dead plants and animals into nutrients. | Mushrooms and bacteria are decomposers.
scavenger | An animal that eats animals that are already dead. | A raven can be a scavenger.
food chain | A line that shows who eats whom, and how energy passes along. | Grass, then a rabbit, then a fox is a food chain.
food web | Many food chains linked together to show all the feeding links in an ecosystem. | A food web shows that a fox can eat many different foods.
energy pyramid | A diagram that shows less energy is available at each step up a food chain. | The bottom of the energy pyramid is the producers.
photosynthesis | How plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make food and oxygen. | Leaves do photosynthesis.
chlorophyll | The green material in plants that captures sunlight. | Chlorophyll makes leaves green.
glucose | A type of sugar that plants make and living things use for energy. | Plants make glucose during photosynthesis.
oxygen | A gas in the air that most living things need to breathe. | We breathe in oxygen.
carbon dioxide | A gas that animals breathe out and plants use to make food. | Cars give off carbon dioxide.
respiration | How living things use oxygen to get energy from food. | Cells do respiration.
nutrient | A material that living things need to grow and stay healthy. | Plants take nutrients from the soil.
adaptation | A feature or behaviour that helps a living thing survive in its habitat. | Thick fur is an adaptation to cold.
survive | To stay alive. | Camels survive in the desert because they save water.
extinct | When a kind of living thing has died out and none are left. | The dodo bird is extinct.
endangered | In danger of becoming extinct. | The whooping crane was once endangered.
invasive | Describes a species that is brought to a new place and harms it. | Zebra mussels are an invasive species.
native | Naturally living in a place, not brought from somewhere else. | The prairie crocus is native to Alberta.
pollination | Moving pollen from one flower to another so seeds can form. | Bees help with pollination.
pollen | A fine powder made by flowers that helps make seeds. | Bees carry pollen on their legs.
germination | When a seed starts to grow into a new plant. | Water starts germination.
seed | The part of a plant that can grow into a new plant. | An apple seed.
root | The part of a plant that holds it in the ground and takes in water. | Carrots are roots.
stem | The part of a plant that holds it up and carries water to the leaves. | A celery stalk is a stem.
leaf | The part of a plant that makes food using sunlight. | Maple leaves.
reproduction | Making new living things of the same kind. | Seeds are part of plant reproduction.
heredity | Passing features from parents to their young. | Eye colour comes from heredity.
trait | A feature or characteristic of a living thing. | Hair colour is a trait.
life cycle | The stages a living thing goes through from birth to making young. | A butterfly's life cycle has four stages.
migration | Moving from one place to another, often each year. | Geese make a yearly migration south.
hibernate | To spend the winter in a deep, sleep-like rest. | Bears hibernate in winter.
boreal forest | A large forest of evergreen trees across northern Canada. | Spruce and pine grow in the boreal forest.
grassland | A large, open area covered mostly with grasses. | The Prairies are grassland.
wetland | Land that is covered with water for much of the year. | Marshes and swamps are wetlands.
biome | A large region with a certain climate and certain plants and animals. | Tundra and desert are biomes.
tundra | A cold, treeless region with frozen ground. | Caribou live on the tundra.
watershed | An area of land where all the water drains to the same river or lake. | The Bow River watershed.
pollution | Harmful materials added to the air, water or land. | Garbage in a river is pollution.
bacteria | Tiny living things made of one cell. Some are helpful and some cause illness. | Bacteria help us digest food.
fungus, fungi | A living thing such as a mushroom or mould that feeds on dead material. | Mushrooms are fungi.
microscope | A tool that makes very small things look bigger. | We looked at onion cells under a microscope.
hypothesis | A smart guess about what will happen, that you can test. | My hypothesis is that plants grow faster with more light.

# ───────────── SCIENCE: climate, Earth and space ─────────────
weather | What the air outside is like at a certain time and place. | Today's weather is rainy and cool.
climate | The usual weather of a place over many years. | Edmonton has a cold climate.
atmosphere | The layer of gases around Earth. | The atmosphere holds the air we breathe.
greenhouse effect | When certain gases in the air trap heat from the Sun and keep Earth warm. | Without the greenhouse effect Earth would be frozen.
greenhouse gas | A gas in the air, such as carbon dioxide, that traps heat. | Methane is a greenhouse gas.
global warming | The slow rise in Earth's average temperature. | Global warming is melting glaciers.
climate change | Long-term changes in Earth's weather patterns, mostly from rising temperatures. | Climate change is causing more wildfires.
carbon footprint | The amount of greenhouse gas that a person or group puts in the air. | Biking to school lowers your carbon footprint.
carbon cycle | How carbon moves between air, living things, water and the ground. | Plants take part in the carbon cycle.
emission | Gas or other material sent out into the air. | Car emissions add to air pollution.
water cycle | How water moves between the ground, the air and the sky, over and over. | Evaporation and rain are parts of the water cycle.
precipitation | Water that falls from clouds, such as rain, snow, sleet or hail. | The precipitation today was snow.
runoff | Water that flows over the land into streams and lakes. | Melting snow becomes runoff.
glacier | A huge, slow-moving mass of ice. | The Athabasca Glacier is in the Rockies.
drought | A long time with very little rain. | The drought dried up the crops.
erosion | The wearing away of rock and soil by wind, water or ice. | The river caused erosion of its banks.
weathering | The breaking of rock into smaller pieces by wind, water, ice or plants. | Frost cracks rock by weathering.
sediment | Small pieces of rock, sand and soil carried and dropped by water or wind. | Sediment settles on the bottom of a lake.
fossil | The remains or imprint of a living thing from long ago, kept in rock. | A dinosaur bone fossil.
mineral | A solid material found in nature that is not alive. | Quartz is a mineral.
rock cycle | How rocks slowly change from one kind to another. | Melting and cooling are part of the rock cycle.
planet | A large round object that goes around a star. | Earth is a planet.
star | A huge ball of hot gas that gives off light. | The Sun is a star.
moon | A natural object that goes around a planet. | Earth has one Moon.
solar system | The Sun and everything that travels around it. | Our solar system has eight planets.
orbit | The path an object takes around another object in space. | Earth's orbit around the Sun takes one year.
rotation | Spinning around a centre. In math, turning a shape around a point. | Earth's rotation gives us day and night. A 90-degree rotation turns a shape a quarter turn.
revolution | One full trip of an object around another. | Earth makes one revolution around the Sun each year.
axis | The imaginary line an object spins around. In math, a number line on a graph, like the x-axis. | Earth's axis is tilted. The x-axis goes across.
galaxy | A huge group of stars, gas and dust. | Our galaxy is called the Milky Way.
telescope | A tool that makes faraway things look closer. | We used a telescope to see Saturn's rings.
satellite | An object that orbits a planet. It can be natural or made by people. | A weather satellite takes pictures of clouds.
space probe | A spacecraft with no people on board that explores space. | A space probe sent back pictures of Mars.
asteroid | A rocky object that orbits the Sun, smaller than a planet. | Many asteroids are in a belt between Mars and Jupiter.
comet | A ball of ice and dust that glows with a tail when near the Sun. | Halley's comet.
eclipse | When one object in space blocks the light from another. | In a solar eclipse the Moon covers the Sun.
season | One of the four parts of the year, each with its own weather. | Winter is the coldest season.

# ───────────── SCIENCE: investigating ─────────────
experiment | A fair test that is done to answer a question. | We did an experiment to see which paper towel soaks up the most.
variable | Something that can change. In an experiment, a thing you can change or measure. In math, a letter that stands for a number. | The amount of water was a variable. In 3x, x is the variable.
independent variable | The one thing you change on purpose in an experiment. | If you test heights of a ramp, the height is the independent variable.
dependent variable | The thing you measure to see the effect of the change. | The distance the car rolled.
controlled variable | Something you keep the same so the test is fair. | Use the same ball each time.
fair test | A test where only one thing is changed and everything else stays the same. | Using the same cup size makes the test fair.
observation | Something you notice using your senses. | The observation was that the ice melted in 5 minutes.
measure | To find out the size, amount or length of something with a tool. | Measure the length with a ruler.
data | Facts and numbers collected in an investigation. | We recorded the data in a table.
evidence | Facts or information that show something is true. | Footprints are evidence that an animal was here.
prediction | A guess about what will happen, based on what you know. | My prediction is that the heavier ball will fall faster.
predict | To say what you think will happen, using what you already know. | Predict what will happen to the ice.
inference | An idea you work out from clues and what you already know. | Wet ground leads to the inference that it rained.
infer | To work something out from clues, even if it isn't told directly. | We can infer that it was cold from the ice on the pond.
model | A drawing, object or idea that helps explain how something works. | A globe is a model of Earth.
calibration | Setting a measuring tool so it gives the right reading. | Calibration of the scale means checking it reads zero when empty.
accurate | Correct, with no mistakes. | An accurate measurement is very close to the real value.
precise | Very exact and detailed. | A ruler with millimetres is more precise than one with centimetres.
reliable | Able to be trusted to give the same results each time. | Repeating the test makes the results more reliable.
bias | Favouring one side unfairly, without good reasons. | A story that tells only one side shows bias.
source | A person, book or website where you get information. | A science book is a source.
primary source | Something made by someone who was there at the time. | A diary or photograph from the time.
secondary source | Something that is written later by someone who was not there. | A textbook is a secondary source.
reference point | Something you compare to in order to tell if an object has moved. | A tree is a reference point to see if a car moved.

# ───────────── SOCIAL STUDIES: government and democracy ─────────────
government | The people and groups that make laws and run a country, province or town. | The government of Alberta.
federal | Having to do with the government of the whole country. | Federal laws apply across Canada.
federalism | A way of governing where power is shared between a national government and regional governments. | Canada uses federalism.
federal government | The national government of Canada. It meets in Ottawa. | The federal government runs the military.
provincial government | The government of a province, such as Alberta. | The provincial government runs schools.
municipal | Having to do with a city, town or county. | The municipal government fixes local roads.
territory | An area of land that belongs to or is controlled by a government. In Canada, Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut are territories. | Nunavut is a territory.
province | One of the ten large regions that make up Canada, each with its own government. | Alberta is a province.
division of powers | How the powers of government are split between the federal and provincial governments. | Education is a provincial power by the division of powers.
democracy | A system of government in which the people vote to choose their leaders. | Canada is a democracy.
direct democracy | A system where people vote on laws themselves. | A town meeting where everyone votes.
representative democracy | A system where people elect others to make decisions for them. | Canada has a representative democracy.
dictatorship | A system where one person has all the power and is not chosen by the people. | A dictator rules a dictatorship.
dictator | A leader who has all the power and is not chosen by the people. | A dictator can make laws alone.
monarchy | A system of government with a king or queen as head of state. | Canada is a constitutional monarchy.
monarch | A king or queen. | The monarch is the head of state.
constitutional monarchy | A system where a king or queen is head of state but the laws and elected government hold the real power. | Canada is a constitutional monarchy.
oligarchy | A system where a small group of people holds all the power. | A few rich families rule an oligarchy.
anarchy | When there is no government or rules. | Anarchy means no one is in charge.
authority | The right or power to give orders or make decisions. | A teacher has authority in a classroom.
power | The ability to make decisions or control others. In math, a number written with an exponent. | The prime minister has political power. 2 to the power of 3 means 2 x 2 x 2.
law | A rule made by a government that everyone must follow. | It is the law to wear a seatbelt.
rule of law | The idea that everyone, even leaders, must obey the law. | Rule of law means no one is above the law.
constitution | The set of basic rules for how a country is run. | Canada's constitution was updated in 1982.
charter | A written document that lists rights and freedoms. | The Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms.
freedom | The power to act, speak or think without being controlled. | Freedom of speech.
responsibility | A duty or job that you should do. | Your responsibility is to do your homework.
citizen | A person who legally belongs to a country and has rights and duties there. | A Canadian citizen can vote at 18.
citizenship | Being a member of a country, with its rights and responsibilities. | Canadian citizenship.
civic | Having to do with citizens and their community. | Voting is a civic duty.
civic engagement | Taking part in your community and in decisions that affect everyone. | Volunteering is civic engagement.
election | A vote to choose a leader or a government. | The next election is in October.
vote | To choose a person or an idea by marking a ballot. | We vote for our class president.
ballot | The paper or form used to cast a vote. | She marked her ballot.
voter | A person who votes. | Every voter picks one candidate.
candidate | A person who is trying to be elected. | Each candidate gave a speech.
riding | An area of the country that elects one Member of Parliament. Also called a constituency. | Our riding elects one MP.
constituency | The area and people represented by one elected member. | The MP visited her constituency.
referendum | A vote by all the people on one important question. | A referendum on changing the flag.
suffrage | The right to vote. | Women won suffrage in Alberta in 1916.
majority rule | The idea that the choice with more than half the votes wins. | By majority rule, the vote passed.
minority rights | Rights that protect smaller groups so the majority cannot treat them unfairly. | Minority rights protect language groups.
parliament | The group of elected people who make the laws of a country. | The Parliament of Canada meets in Ottawa.
house of commons | The part of Parliament where elected Members of Parliament make laws. | The House of Commons has 338 seats.
senate | The part of Parliament whose members are appointed. It reviews and can change laws. | The Senate studied the new law.
senator | A member of the Senate. | Senators are appointed, not elected.
member of parliament, mp | A person elected to represent a riding in the House of Commons. | Our MP spoke in Ottawa.
prime minister | The leader of the federal government in Canada. | The prime minister leads the country.
premier | The leader of a province's government. | The premier of Alberta.
cabinet | A group of top ministers chosen by the leader to run government departments. | The health minister is in the cabinet.
minister | A member of the cabinet in charge of one part of government. | The minister of education.
governor general | The person who represents the monarch in Canada and signs bills into law. | The governor general gave royal assent.
royal assent | The final signature that turns a bill into a law. | The bill got royal assent.
bill | A plan for a new law that has not yet been passed. | The bill was debated in the House.
legislature | The group of elected people who make the laws of a province. | The Alberta Legislature is in Edmonton.
legislative branch | The part of government that makes laws. | Parliament is the legislative branch.
executive branch | The part of government that carries out and enforces the laws. | The prime minister and cabinet are the executive branch.
judicial branch | The part of government that explains the laws and decides court cases. | Judges are the judicial branch.
judiciary | All the judges and courts of a country. | The judiciary is independent.
judge | A person who decides cases in a court of law. | The judge listened to both sides.
court | A place where a judge decides legal cases. | The case went to court.
supreme court | The highest court in Canada. | The Supreme Court has the final say.
appeal | To ask a higher court to look at a decision again. | He appealed the ruling.
precedent | An earlier court decision that is used as a guide for later cases. | The judge followed a precedent.
criminal law | Laws about crimes and their punishments. | Theft is covered by criminal law.
