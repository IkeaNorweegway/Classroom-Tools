Answer Key

Ticket 1 — What Is an Ideology? Individualism vs. Collectivism

1. An ideology is a set of ideas about how society and government should work. Award the mark for any answer capturing "a set of beliefs/ideas about how society or government should be organized."

2. Individualism — the statement prioritizes each person's own choice and autonomy over free time, with no group decision involved. 1 mark for correct identification, 1 mark for a reasoning that references choice/autonomy.

3. Full marks (3): correctly states an ideology is the underlying set of ideas, a political party is the organization built around it, and gives a valid example (e.g., liberalism as an idea can exist in more than one party/country; a party can change platform while the idea persists). 2 marks: correct distinction, weak or missing example. 1 mark: only a vague gesture at "they're different."

Ticket 2 — Historical Roots

1. Plato believed the wisest people (philosopher-rulers) should lead, because they understand what is truly good better than untrained citizens.

2. Closer to the Athenian aristocrats — a small founding group retains control over the rules that matter most, even though other members get a vote on day-to-day items. 1 mark for correct identification, 1 mark for reasoning tied to "who controls the real power."

3. Full marks (3): explains that Aristotle worried a ruler with no legal limits could act unfairly or abuse power, and that shared, known laws protect everyone — including from the ruler. 2 marks: correct general idea, missing the "protects from abuse of power" mechanism. 1 mark: only restates "laws are important" with no reasoning.

Ticket 3 — The Political Spectrum

1. Left tends toward collectivism (more government involvement/group decision-making); right tends toward individualism (more individual choice, less government involvement).

2. Because left/right positions are relative to the political norms of each country — a policy that is far outside the norm in one country can be close to the mainstream in another. Award the mark for any answer identifying that the comparison point (the country's own political center) differs.

3. Full marks (3): explains the spectrum is relative, not fixed, gives a valid example (e.g., government-funded healthcare being centrist in Canada vs. labeled far-left elsewhere; or an idea once considered radical becoming mainstream over time), and connects it explicitly to "compared to what, and when." 2 marks: correct claim, example weak or missing. 1 mark: states "it's relative" with no explanation.

Ticket 4 — Four Ideologies

1. Communism, socialism, liberalism, conservatism (any order).

2. Socialism — community ownership/regulation of major industries alongside an existing elected government matches socialism's definition specifically (not communism, which requires a classless, stateless society with no government).

3. Full marks (3): correctly states socialism means the means of production are owned/regulated by the community, often alongside an active government and existing classes; communism is the further goal of a classless, stateless society with common ownership of everything. 2 marks: correct general distinction, imprecise on one definition. 1 mark: states they are different but cannot explain how.

Ticket 5 — Ideology Taken to Extremes

1. A totalitarian government holds complete control over political, social, and economic life, crushes opposition, and uses propaganda to control belief.

2. Any two of: one leader/party holds complete power; opposition and free press are shut down/controlled; propaganda controls public belief; state influence extends into schools, media, and family life.

3. Full marks (3): explains totalitarianism is a category/pattern of government (not a single closed historical event), that Nazi Germany is the historical case study but not the only possible case, and that treating it as "only historical" risks failing to recognize the same warning signs elsewhere or in the future. 2 marks: correct general claim, missing the "risk of not recognizing it again" reasoning. 1 mark: only states "it could happen again" with no supporting reasoning.
