Core Name the three states of matter.
Word Bank  solid · liquid · gas
Hint: Think about water in three different forms.

1. solid   2. liquid   3. gas

3
Core How are particles arranged in a solid? Fill in the blanks.
Word Bank  close together · fixed positions · vibrate · strong
Hint: A solid has a definite shape. Think about why.

In a solid, particles are packed close together. They are held in fixed positions by strong forces. They can vibrate but they do not move around.

4
Core How are particles arranged in a liquid? Fill in the blanks.
Word Bank  close together · flow · weaker · no fixed positions
Hint: A liquid can pour. Particles must be able to move past each other.

In a liquid, particles are still close together. But forces between them are weaker. Particles have no fixed positions. They can flow past each other.

5
Core How are particles arranged in a gas? Fill in the blanks.
Word Bank  far apart · spread out · very fast · very weak
Hint: Gas fills any container. Particles must move freely.

In a gas, particles are far apart. Forces between them are very weak. Particles move very fast and spread out to fill any space.

Core What happens to particles when you heat a substance? Fill in the blanks.
Word Bank  faster · more energy · farther apart · expand
Hint: Heating adds energy to particles.

When you heat a substance, particles gain more energy. They move faster. They spread farther apart. This makes the substance expand.

8
Core A student has 75 g of ice. The ice melts and becomes water. What is the mass of the water?
Word Bank  75 g · more than 75 g · less than 75 g
Hint: Particles do not disappear when matter changes state. Mass is conserved.

The mass of the water is .

Explain why:

Answer Mass = 75 g. Explanation: When ice melts, only the arrangement of particles changes — no particles are created or destroyed. Mass is conserved.

Section 2 — Temperature, Expansion, and Changes Questions 11–20
11
Core What happens to matter when it cools down? Fill in the blanks.
Word Bank  slower · energy · closer together · contract
Hint: Cooling is the opposite of heating.

When matter cools, particles lose energy. They move slower. They move closer together. This makes the substance contract.

12
Core A metal bridge is built in summer. In winter it gets very cold. What happens to the bridge? Choose the best answer and explain.
Word Bank  contracts (gets smaller) · expands (gets bigger) · stays the same size
Hint: Metal is matter. All matter responds to temperature changes.

The bridge .

Explain using particles:

Answer contracts (gets smaller). Explanation: Cold removes energy from particles → they slow down and move closer together → the metal takes up less space. Accept: particles slow/come closer → bridge shrinks.
13
Core You mix salt into water. The salt seems to disappear. A student says: "The salt is gone. Its mass disappeared too." Is this student correct? Circle your answer.
Word Bank  Yes, the student is correct · No, the student is wrong
Hint: Dissolving is a physical change. Particles are still there — just mixed in.

The student is: .

Explain:

Answer No, the student is wrong. Explanation: Dissolving is a physical change. The salt particles are still there — they are just spread out among the water particles. Mass is conserved.

Word Bank  rises · falls · stays the same
Hint: Particles expand when they get more energy.

The liquid level .

Why:

Answer rises. Why: Particles gain energy → move faster → spread farther apart → liquid expands → takes up more space → level in tube rises.
17
Core You add sugar to cold water and hot water. In which cup does the sugar dissolve faster? Fill in the blank and explain.
Word Bank  hot water · cold water · both the same
Hint: Faster-moving particles can break apart the sugar more quickly.

Sugar dissolves faster in .

Because:

Answer hot water. Because: Hot water particles move faster → collide more with sugar particles → break sugar apart faster → sugar dissolves more quickly.
18
Core You have muddy water. You want to separate the mud from the water. Name the best method and explain how it works.
Word Bank  filtration · evaporation · distillation · filter · pores · too big
Hint: One method uses a material with tiny holes that let water through but not mud.

Best method:

How it works:

Answer filtration. How: A filter has tiny pores. Water particles are small enough to pass through. Mud particles are too big → they stay on the filter.
19
Core You have salt water. You want to get the salt out. Name the best method and explain how it works.
Word Bank  evaporation · filtration · dissolving · water evaporates · salt stays behind
Hint: Salt does not evaporate when water does.

Best method:

How it works:

Answer evaporation. How: Water particles gain enough energy to escape as gas (evaporate). Salt particles do not evaporate → they are left behind in solid form.
20
Core True or False? Particles in a solid never move at all. Circle your answer and explain.
Word Bank  True · False · vibrate · always moving · back and forth
Hint: Key Idea 2 of the particle model says particles are always moving.

True  /  False (circle one)

Explain:

Answer False. Explain: Particles in a solid always vibrate (move back and forth) in fixed positions. They never stop moving completely — they just cannot move from place to place.

Answer Boiling means: particles at and below the surface gain enough energy to escape as gas (evaporation happening rapidly throughout the liquid, not just at the surface). Accept: fast evaporation / particles gain enough energy to become gas / bubbles of gas form inside the liquid.

Word Bank  expands · more space · pushes · crack widens · freeze-thaw weathering

Stretch Zone — Challenge Questions
S1
Design an investigation. You want to find out if temperature affects how fast sugar dissolves. Name the variable you change, the variable you measure, and two variables you keep the same.

Variable I change:

Variable I measure:

Two things I keep the same:

Answer Change: temperature of water (e.g., cold / room temp / hot). Measure: time for a set amount of sugar to dissolve. Keep same: amount of sugar, amount of water, type of sugar, stirring (or no stirring). Accept any two controlled variables.
S2
Most liquids contract (get smaller) when they freeze. Water does the opposite — it expands. Why might this property be important for life on Earth?
Answer Ice floats → insulates water below → lakes and rivers do not freeze solid → aquatic life survives through winter. If ice sank, lakes would freeze solid from the bottom up → most aquatic life would die. Accept any response capturing: ice floats → insulation → life survives.
S3
Roads have small gaps built into them every few metres. Explain why engineers add these gaps. Use the word "expansion" in your answer.
Answer In summer, road material heats up → particles spread farther apart → material expands. Without gaps, sections would push against each other and buckle or crack. Gaps give the material room to expand safely.
S4
A scientist claims: "All substances expand when heated." Is this always true? Use water as a counter-example to challenge this claim.
Answer Not always true. Water between 0°C and 4°C actually contracts as it warms — this is the opposite of normal expansion behavior. This counter-example disproves the claim that ALL substances always expand when heated. Accept any clear explanation of water's unusual behavior near 0°C.
S5
Dry ice is solid carbon dioxide. It goes straight from solid to gas — it does not become liquid first. What is this phase change called? Why might dry ice be useful in food storage?

Phase change name:

Useful because:

Answer Phase change name: sublimation. Useful because: Dry ice stays very cold without creating liquid water (no mess, no soaking). It keeps food frozen while evaporating cleanly as gas.
