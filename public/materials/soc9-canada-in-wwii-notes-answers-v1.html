Section 1: Declaring War Independently

Learning target: I can analyze how Canada's decision to declare war independently in 1939 demonstrated its growing sovereignty.

Guided Notes

Britain declared war on Germany on September 3, 1939. Canada did not automatically join — its own Parliament debated the question and declared war independently about a week later, on September 10, 1939.

This delay was small militarily but enormous symbolically: it represented a significant step forward in Canada's sovereignty and identity, showing that the independence gained through the Statute of Westminster (1931) was now being exercised in a moment that truly mattered.

Section 2: Canada's Military Contributions

Learning target: I can use evidence from key battles to explain Canada's contribution to Allied success.

Guided Notes

More than one million Canadians joined the armed forces during the war — allowing the country to build one of the world's largest navies and air forces by 1945, a remarkable achievement for a nation of just over 11 million people.

Match each battle or campaign to its year:

Canadian forces earned recognition on the battlefield and were highly regarded by their Allies. Their independent role in these campaigns contributed to Canada's emergence as a international nation with a distinct international role, and helped transform Canada into a more prominent international actor, strengthening its global reputation and influence.

Section 3: Diverse Contributions to the War Effort

Learning target: I can explain the significance of contributions by groups such as Chinese Canadians and Cree code talkers to the war effort.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own words
Force 136 
Code talker 

Guided Notes

Canada's diverse populations and communities made important contributions to the Second World War — often while being denied the full rights of citizenship at home. Chinese Canadians, including those in Force 136, served despite decades of discriminatory laws that had restricted their rights in Canada.

Cree code talkers were First Nations and Métis soldiers who developed and contributed a military coding system based on the Cree language. This system was invaluable to communications during the war, because it was virtually impossible for the enemy to decode.

Source for analysis
