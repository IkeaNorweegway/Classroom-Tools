ReadPhotosynthesis in a Nutshell

Photosynthesis is how a plant makes its own food. In the green parts of a plant (mostly the leaves), tiny chloroplasts use green chlorophyll to capture light energy. The plant uses that energy to turn water (pulled up from the roots through the xylem) and carbon dioxide (taken in from the air) into glucose, a sugar that feeds the whole plant. Oxygen is released into the air as a by-product.
light energy + water + carbon dioxide → glucose + oxygen

ModelWhat Every Sketch Needs to Show

This map shows the parts and labels every plant sketch needs. Your drawings will look like the real plant. They don't need to be art. They need to be clear and labelled.

Word bank: plant systems and parts
root system shoot system roots stem (a tree's stem = trunk) branches leaves (needles and grass blades are leaves) flower seeds / fruit / cones xylem (water up) phloem (food around)
Every sketch needs:
  1. A ground line. It's already printed in each drawing box.
  2. The shoot system above the ground, drawn from what you see.
  3. The root system below the ground, drawn as a dashed prediction.
  4. A bracket and label for both systems.
  5. At least 4 parts labelled from the word bank.
  6. Shade green every part that does photosynthesis and add the sun mark .
  7. Photosynthesis arrows: light in, CO₂ in, O₂ out, and water up from the roots.
light gases (CO₂ in, O₂ out) water predicted roots photosynthesis happens here

No coloured pencils? Use a plain pencil and write "light", "CO₂", "O₂", and "water" on your arrows.
