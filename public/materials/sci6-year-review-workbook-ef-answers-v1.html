Stretch Zone — Cross-Unit Challenge Questions
S1
Matter + Forces: A bridge cable stretches under the weight of traffic. Use the particle model to explain why the cable gets longer under tension. Then explain what would happen if the force exceeded the elastic limit.
Answer Particle model: tension pulls particles farther apart → spacing between particles increases → the cable gets longer. If force exceeds the elastic limit: plastic deformation occurs — particles are displaced permanently, the cable does not return to its original length and may eventually snap. Award marks for: particle-level explanation of elongation AND correct use of elastic limit to explain failure.
S2
Living Systems + Climate: A wetland ecosystem stores large amounts of carbon in peat (dead plant matter). If the wetland dries out due to climate change, the peat decomposes and releases CO₂. Draw the cascade of effects — starting with climate change and ending with more climate change.
Sketch or describe the feedback loop here.
Answer Feedback loop: climate change → temperatures rise → wetland dries out → peat decomposes → bacteria release CO₂ → more CO₂ in atmosphere → more warming → more wetlands dry. This is a positive feedback loop — the effect (warming) amplifies the cause (warming). Award marks for: four linked steps in the loop AND identifying it as a positive feedback.
S3
Energy Resources + Climate: Alberta is a major producer of oil and gas. But climate change threatens Alberta's agriculture and water supply. How should Alberta balance these two realities? Give one argument on each side. Then state your own position.
Answer Pro-oil: Alberta's economy, tax revenues, and many jobs depend on the oil industry; an abrupt phase-out would cause significant economic hardship and instability. Pro-action: climate change threatens Alberta's water supply, agriculture, and long-term economic viability; delayed action increases future costs and risks. Accept any position stated with one argument on each side. No single right answer.
S4
Space + Energy Resources: Solar satellites in orbit could collect solar energy 24 hours a day and beam it to Earth as microwaves. Evaluate this idea using at least three of the five selection factors for energy.
Answer Using three selection factors: Availability — 24-hour sunlight in orbit (no weather or day/night cycle) → highly reliable. Environmental impact — no CO₂ during operation, but launch creates emissions. Cost — extremely expensive to build and launch; microwave transmission infrastructure on Earth required. Efficiency — very high in orbit (no atmosphere filtering). Reliability — technically reliable but vulnerable to microwave transmission losses and orbital debris. Award marks for applying at least three named factors with correct reasoning.
S5
All Units — Indigenous Knowledge: Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) has contributed to our understanding of ice, ecosystems, climate, energy, and astronomy. Choose two units. For each, explain how TEK complements scientific knowledge.
Answer Example response (Matter + Climate): Indigenous knowledge of ice — reading ice thickness, colour, and texture to assess safety — connects directly to particle model (density, particle spacing). In climate: Indigenous river observations (ice break-up timing, water levels) complement instrumental climate records by providing long-term data from before instruments existed. Accept any two well-explained unit connections using TEK. Award marks for naming a specific TEK contribution in each unit and explaining how it connects to the science.
S6
All Units — Big Picture: Choose the one idea from Grade 6 Science that you think is most important for people to understand. Explain your choice. Connect it to at least two units.
Answer Open response. Accept any well-reasoned choice. Award marks for: (1) identifying one idea, (2) explaining why it is important, (3) connecting it to at least two units. Example: "Everything is connected — removing one species can change a river (Living Systems + Climate + Forces); burning fossil fuels affects ecosystems, climate, and even space weather monitoring." No single right answer.
