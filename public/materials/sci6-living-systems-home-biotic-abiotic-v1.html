Living and Non-living Factors

One skill: sorting biotic and abiotic factors and explaining how they connect · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 1: Biotic and Abiotic — What Makes Up an Ecosystem?

NameDate

For the adult: Biotic = living (or once living). Abiotic = non-living, like sunlight, water, soil and temperature.

Worked example

Worked example

List the biotic and abiotic parts of a pond and how they connect.

  1. Biotic: fish, frogs, algae, cattails.
  2. Abiotic: water, sunlight, temperature, rocks.
  3. Connection: sunlight lets algae make food, and fish eat the algae.

Your turn

1. Biotic means . Abiotic means .

2. Sort into biotic (B) and abiotic (A): sunlight frog soil mushroom water grass

3. How does an abiotic factor, like temperature, affect living things in a pond?

4. What do plants need for photosynthesis, and what do they make?

5. Are fallen leaves biotic or abiotic? Explain your thinking.

6. Choose a place near your home. List three biotic and three abiotic factors.
