Notebook key words

Push factor

A condition at home that drives someone to leave, such as war or persecution

Pull factor

A condition at the destination that draws someone there, such as safety or jobs

Displaced person

A refugee left homeless or unable to safely return home

Ask: for a Holocaust survivor arriving in 1948, what was the push, and what was the pull? Most people experience both at once. Students copy the three key words into their notebooks.

Notes · Section 1: Push and pull factors

Fill in the blanks

A push factor is a condition in a person’s home country that makes them want to (1) __________.

A pull factor is a condition in the destination country that makes it (2) __________.

After the Second World War (1945–51), displaced persons arrived in Canada: refugees, including (3) __________, who had lost their homes, families, and countries during the war.

Answers

1. leave

2. attractive (or appealing)

3. Holocaust survivors

Give students a minute to fill the three blanks from memory, then click to show the answers. For blank 3, accept other refugees who could not return home, but name Holocaust survivors.

Growth is never free

Before: one wilderness

After: land divided into parcels

Pollution

Air and water pollution

Land consumption

Farmland and wilderness built over

Land division

Large areas split into parcels

Wildlife migration

Roads and fences block animal routes

Common misconception

The environmental costs came later, with modern industry. In fact, pollution, land division, and habitat loss were already visible in the 1950s and 60s.

Notebook key word

Urbanization

The process by which more of a population comes to live in cities rather than rural areas

Ask students which of the four impacts they can see near them today. Then ask whether the misconception held because the costs were less discussed at the time. Students copy the key word into their notebooks.
