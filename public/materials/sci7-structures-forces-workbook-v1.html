Q3 ★★ Extended Evaluate — LO 7-4-02
West Edmonton Mall has a multi-level parking structure. A heavy delivery truck parks on the top level. Its mass is about 8,000 kg. It parks right above a ground-floor support column. That night, a strong windstorm hits Edmonton.

(a) What type of load is the delivery truck? Explain why it is this load type and not a dead load.

(b) The windstorm also puts force on the parking structure. What load type is wind? Explain why it is more damaging than a live load of the same size. Give one other example of this load type. Think of one a parking structure in Edmonton might face.

(c) Look at the concrete column under the truck. Where is compression highest? This load pushes straight down. Would tension show up anywhere in the column? Concrete is weak in tension. Name one design feature that fixes this. Explain how it works.

Q4 ★ Core Apply / Analyze — LO 7-4-03
A round grain silo on a Peace River farm stores 50,000 kg of grain. The silo walls are clearly thicker at the base than near the top.

(a) A full silo is heavier than an empty one. But a full silo is more stable against toppling. This seems strange at first. Use centre of gravity to explain why it makes sense.

(b) The stored grain pushes outward on the round walls. This is called lateral pressure. It acts a lot like a liquid would. Name the internal force this creates in the silo walls. Explain why this force is greatest at the base. Explain why the base walls must be thickest.

(c) A strong wind pushes sideways on the silo. Would a full silo or an empty silo topple more easily? Back up your answer. Use both centre of gravity and base of support.

Questions 7 – 12
Q7 ★★ Extended Evaluate — LO 7-4-01
The TELUS World of Science Edmonton has a geodesic dome. It is a near-sphere built from triangulated frame panels. A nearby hall has the same floor area. But it is a normal rectangular box building.

(a) A geodesic dome is a combination structure (frame + shell). Name the frame part and the shell part in its design. Explain how they work together. They send load down to the foundation.

(b) The dome encloses the same space as the box building. But it uses much less material. Weigh this trade-off. Name one structural advantage the dome has over the box. Name one practical downside. Then say which design fits a science centre better, and why.

(c) Why is the triangle the strongest 2D shape for frame structures? Think about rigidity. This means the shape holds its form. Compare a sideways force on a triangle panel to the same force on a square panel. Use the same material for both.

Q8 ★★ Extended Create / Evaluate — LO 7-4-03, 7-4-05
Parks Canada wants to build a footbridge for hikers. It crosses a fast-moving creek in Banff National Park. The bridge must span 8 m. It must hold up to 20 hikers at once. It must survive heavy snow in winter. It must handle spring flooding. It must fit in with nature nearby. All building materials must be carried in by pack horse. No piece can weigh more than 80 kg.

(a) List all the loads this bridge must be built for. Sort each one as dead, live, or dynamic. Explain what makes the dynamic loads extra tricky in the mountains.

(b) Which structure type would you pick: solid, frame, shell, or combination? Back up your choice with three kinds of reasons: structural, material, and travel reasons. One reason alone is not enough.

(c) Which way of failing worries you most for your design? Name one design feature that would lower that risk. Explain how it works.

Q10 ★★ Extended Error Analysis Evaluate — LO 7-4-04
Student Claim
"Simple machines reduce the amount of work you have to do — that's the whole point. If a lever has a mechanical advantage of 4, you do four times less work than without it."

(a) What exactly is wrong with this claim? Write the formula for work. What does mechanical advantage actually change? What can it not change?

(b) Use numbers to prove the claim wrong. A lever has a mechanical advantage of 4. You apply 25 N of effort over 2 m. Calculate: (i) the load force lifted, (ii) the distance the load moves, (iii) the work you do (effort side), (iv) the work done on the load. What do these four numbers show?

Work space

(c) Simple machines cannot reduce work. So why do we use them? Give two separate reasons. They must differ from each other. Each must tie to what a simple machine actually does.

(a) Calculate the mechanical advantage of this pulley system. Calculate the effort force the rigger must apply. Assume there is no friction.

S1 Synthesis — Structures + Forces + Materials

The Lethbridge Viaduct (CPR High Level Bridge) is a steel trestle bridge. It is one of the longest railway bridges in Canada. It spans a wide valley 96 m above the Oldman River. It carries fully loaded freight trains.

Look at the Lethbridge Viaduct in three ways. (a) Name its structure type. Explain why engineers chose a frame trestle, not a solid or shell structure, for this span and height. (b) Name and explain at least four different loads it faces. Include at least one dynamic load tied to its location and use. (c) Name two material properties that matter most for the steel parts. Explain why a tension failure would look different from a compression failure on a railway bridge.

S2 Systems Thinking — Compound Machine + Energy

A compound machine combines a wheel and axle with a movable pulley system. The wheel has a radius of 0.4 m. The axle has a radius of 0.05 m. The axle connects to a movable pulley system. That system has 3 rope segments holding the load.

(a) Calculate the mechanical advantage of the wheel and axle alone. (b) Calculate the mechanical advantage of the pulley system alone. (c) Calculate the overall mechanical advantage of the combined system. (d) An effort force of 20 N is applied at the wheel rim. In theory, what is the biggest load the system could lift? Ignore friction. (e) In practice the system lifts only 85% of that load. Where did the "missing" energy go? Show that energy is still conserved.

S3 Design Critique — Cross-Unit Reasoning

An engineering student makes this claim: "A concrete dome shell needs no internal support columns, because the dome shape sends all loads down to the foundation ring as compression. So concrete is the ideal material for all dome structures."

Judge this claim in full. Your answer should do four things. (a) Say what is correct. Explain the compression-redirection idea correctly. (b) Find at least two errors or overstatements in the claim. (c) Describe a realistic case where a concrete dome would still fail, despite this compression idea. Think carefully about load type and scale. (d) Suggest one design change that would fix the biggest weakness you found. Explain how it works.
