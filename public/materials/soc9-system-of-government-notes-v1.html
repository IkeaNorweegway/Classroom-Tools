Section 2: How Laws Are Made

Learning target: I can interpret how government tools such as regulations and orders in council shape decisions beyond Parliament.

Guided Notes

Laws are created through a formal legislative process. Put these six steps in order by numbering them 1–6:

OrderStep
 Third reading (final approval)
 First reading (introduction)
 Royal assent by the Crown
 Bill drafting
 Second reading (debate)
 Committee stage (detailed review)
 Approval by the Senate

This formal process ensures laws are debated, reviewed, and approved by multiple institutions before being enacted. But it is not the only way governments make decisions — they also act through orders in council, regulations and directives, and ministerial orders/decisions, which shape policy without going through the full legislative process every time.

Common misconception
"All government rules are made the same way, through Parliament passing a full law."

Much of what governments actually do day-to-day happens through regulations, orders in council, and ministerial decisions — faster tools that operate under the authority of laws Parliament has already passed, without requiring a brand-new bill each time.
