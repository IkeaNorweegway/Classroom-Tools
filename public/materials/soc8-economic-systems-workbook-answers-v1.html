1 ★ Core

A town council has enough money to build a new hockey rink OR a new library this year — not both. This is the same choice from your notes.

  1. Name the scarce resource being divided here.
  2. Sentence starter: “The opportunity cost of building the library is  .” Complete the sentence, assuming the council chooses to build the rink.
  3. Which economic question is the town answering here? Circle one: What  /  How  /  For whom
Answer

(a) The scarce resource is money (the town's limited budget) — accept "funding" or "resources."

(b) Completed sentence: "The opportunity cost of building the library is the hockey rink (or: not having the rink)."

(c) What to produce — the town is deciding which good/service (rink or library) to produce with its limited resources.

2 ★ Core

A student says: “My family's taxes basically just pay for the road we drive on. That's really the main thing taxes are for.”

  1. Is this student's claim accurate? Circle one: Yes  /  No
  2. Name three OTHER things tax dollars pay for that this student is missing.
    Word bank healthcare   education   social programs   public safety   infrastructure   roads
  3. In 1–2 sentences, explain why thinking taxes fund "just one thing" is a misunderstanding of government revenue.
Answer

(a) No — the claim is not accurate.

(b) Any three of: healthcare, education, social programs, public safety, infrastructure (beyond just roads).

(c) Model answer: Taxes fund a broad basket of services, not one visible thing. If taxes only paid for roads, there would be no funding for schools, hospitals, or emergency services, which clearly exist and are paid for by tax revenue too.

3 ★ Core

A student says: “The United States is a pure market economy — the government has zero involvement in the economy at all.”

  1. Is this claim accurate? Circle one: Yes  /  No
  2. Name one example of government involvement in the U.S. economy that proves this student wrong.
    Word bank public schools   Social Security   a public park   a public hospital   a government office
  3. Where would you place the United States on the economic spectrum — closer to the market end, the middle, or the command end? Explain in one sentence.
Answer

(a) No — the claim is not accurate.

(b) Any one of: public schools, Social Security, a public park, a public hospital, a government office/agency.

(c) Closer to the market end, but not at the extreme — the U.S. leans strongly market but still has real government programs, which is why it is not "pure."

4 ★★ Extended

A student says: “Our country has $30 billion in debt this year, so that means this year's deficit was $30 billion.”

  1. Explain what is wrong with this statement.
  2. If the country's debt was $22 billion at the start of the year and is $30 billion at the end of the year, what was this year's deficit?
  3. Could a country have a large debt but a small deficit this year? Explain how that is possible.
Answer

(a) The statement confuses debt (the total accumulated amount owed) with deficit (the gap in just one year). $30 billion is described as "debt," which is a running total — it is not automatically the same number as this year's deficit.

(b) This year's deficit = $30 billion − $22 billion = $8 billion.

(c) Yes. A country could have a large debt built up from many past years, but if this year's revenue and spending are close to equal, this year's deficit could be small (or the country could even run a small surplus while still carrying a large total debt).

5 ★ Core

Look at this partly filled table for a country's economy.

MeasureThis yearLast year
GDP growth4%2%
Unemployment rate6%6%
CPI change3%3%
  1. Which measure improved compared to last year?
  2. Which two measures stayed the same?
  3. Based on GDP alone, is it fair to say the economy is "doing better" this year? Why or why not?
Answer

(a) GDP growth improved (2% → 4%).

(b) Unemployment rate and CPI change stayed the same.

(c) Not necessarily — GDP growth alone doesn't tell the whole story. Since unemployment didn't improve and inflation (CPI) didn't change, the benefits of that GDP growth may not be reaching everyone, or prices may be rising at the same steady rate as before. Accept any answer that references needing more than one measure.

6 ★★ Extended

A farmer has one field. She can plant wheat or plant corn, but not both this season. Wheat sells for less per bushel than corn this year, but corn needs more water, and her region is in a drought.

Evaluate: should the farmer plant wheat or corn? Use scarcity and opportunity cost to justify your recommendation, and name one thing she gives up either way.

Answer

Accept either wheat or corn as the recommendation, provided the reasoning is sound. Strong answers note: scarcity forces a choice because she has only one field; if she plants corn, she risks lower yield or crop failure due to drought and needs more water (a resource that may also be scarce); if she plants wheat, she gives up the higher price corn would bring. The opportunity cost is whichever crop she does not plant. Full marks require naming the opportunity cost explicitly.

7 ★ Core

Below is a blank economic spectrum. Three countries are described. Write each country's letter on the line where you think it belongs.

Write A, B, or C directly on the line where each country belongs.

Country A: Private businesses compete freely; government runs almost no programs.
Country B: The government owns almost all major industries and sets most prices.
Country C: Private businesses compete, but the government also runs public healthcare and education.

Hint Which country is closest to "individuals decide everything"? Which is closest to "government decides everything"?

Explain one of your placements in a full sentence:

Answer

Country A → near the market end. Country B → at the command end. Country C → in the middle (mixed). Explanation should correctly connect the placement to who makes most decisions (individuals/businesses vs. government) for the chosen country.

8 ★★ Extended

A province must choose between spending an extra $500 million on new hospitals or on new highways this year — it cannot fully fund both.

Evaluate this decision: what would you recommend, and what is the opportunity cost of your recommendation? Use reasoning, not just an opinion.

Answer

No single correct recommendation. Full marks require: a clear choice (hospitals or highways), a named opportunity cost (the other option, or what it would have funded), and reasoning connected to trade-offs (e.g., healthcare need vs. economic/transport benefit) rather than just a preference.

9 ★ Core

A province runs a deficit this year because a major flood destroyed roads and homes, and the government spent extra money on emergency repairs right away.

  1. Was this deficit a sign of poor money management? Circle one: Yes  /  No
  2. Explain your answer in 1–2 sentences.
    Hint Think about why the deficit happened and what the money was used for.
Answer

(a) No.

(b) Model answer: This deficit happened because of an emergency (a flood) and the money was used to help people and rebuild right away — a planned, reasonable response, not careless spending. A deficit is not automatically bad management; it depends on the reason and how the money is used.

10 ★★ Extended

One year after the Country A / Country B comparison from your notes, new data comes in:

Country ACountry B
GDP growth1%4%
Unemployment rate7%4%
CPI change (inflation)5%2%

Compare this to the original numbers from your notes. What changed for each country? Which country's economy looks stronger now, and which measure changed your mind the most?

Answer

Country A got worse on all three measures (GDP growth down from 3% to 1%, unemployment up from 5% to 7%, CPI/inflation up from 2% to 5%). Country B improved on all three (GDP growth up from 1% to 4%, unemployment down from 9% to 4%, CPI change down from 6% to 2%). Country B now looks stronger. Accept any answer that correctly identifies the shift and names a specific measure (most students will point to unemployment or the full reversal in GDP growth) as the most convincing.

11 ★★ Extended

Look at the economic spectrum diagram from your notes, showing Hong Kong, Canada, China, and North Korea placed along the line.

A student claims: “Since Canada and China are both closer to the middle than the extreme ends, they must be almost the same type of economy.”

Evaluate this claim. Is it accurate? Use specific differences between Canada and China (ownership, government role, main goal) to support your answer.

Answer

The claim is not accurate. Being at a similar distance from the extreme ends of the spectrum does not mean two economies are alike. Strong answers name at least one real difference: Canada has significant private business ownership and elected government changing hands, while China has much heavier state ownership of major industries and tighter government control overall, even though it now permits private enterprise. Position on a single spectrum line is a simplification — it does not capture every difference between two mixed economies.

12 ★★ Extended

A school has enough budget for either new sports equipment or new art supplies this year — not both. Using scarcity, the three economic questions, and opportunity cost, write a short recommendation to the school council. Your recommendation must name the opportunity cost of your choice.

Answer

No single correct recommendation. Full marks require: a clear choice (sports equipment or art supplies), correct identification of the opportunity cost (the other option), and a connection to at least one of the three economic questions (most naturally "what to produce").

Challenge Zone

Attempt after completing all previous questions. All Extended. No scaffolds.

S1 ★★ Extended

A country's data this year:

MeasureValue
GDP growth0.5%
Unemployment rate9%
CPI change1%
This year's budgetDeficit of $12 billion
Total debt$340 billion (was $328 billion last year)

The Prime Minister says: “Running this deficit was the right call — our economy needed the help.”

Using the economic measures above and what you know about deficits, evaluate whether this deficit seems like a reasonable choice or a risky one. Support your answer with at least two pieces of evidence from the table.

Answer

No single correct verdict. Strong answers use at least two pieces of evidence from the table — e.g., high unemployment (9%) and very low GDP growth (0.5%) suggest the economy genuinely needed support, which supports the Prime Minister's claim; but debt grew by $12 billion in one year and is already very large ($340 billion), which is a risk if this pattern continues for many years. Full marks require weighing evidence on both sides rather than picking one side without using the data.

S2 ★★ Extended

Design an economic plan for a new country.

  1. Decide where your country sits on the economic spectrum (market, mixed, or command) and explain why.
  2. Name two things your government will fund with tax revenue, and one thing it will leave mostly to private businesses.
  3. Explain one trade-off your country accepts by choosing this plan instead of a different point on the spectrum.
Answer

Assess for internal consistency rather than a single correct plan: the spectrum placement in (a) should logically match the funding choices in (b) (e.g., a country placed near the command end should describe more government-funded services; one placed near the market end should describe more privately run business). Part (c) must name a genuine trade-off (something given up by not choosing a different point on the spectrum), not just restate the plan.

S3 ★★ Extended

A country has been running a growing deficit for ten years straight, has rising unemployment, and its citizens are asking the government to cut taxes.

Using scarcity, government revenue and spending, and the difference between deficit and debt, write a paragraph explaining the trade-offs the government faces. Your paragraph should mention at least three ideas from this unit.

Answer

Look for a paragraph that connects at least three unit ideas correctly, for example: scarcity forces the government to choose between cutting taxes (less revenue) and continuing services (spending); a tax cut without a spending cut would increase the deficit further; a shrinking deficit or debt reduction might require politically unpopular choices (higher taxes or lower spending) that conflict with what citizens are asking for; a rising debt means more revenue going to interest payments over time, which is relevant given the ten-year pattern. Do not require agreement with any specific policy — assess for correct use of vocabulary and logical trade-off reasoning.
