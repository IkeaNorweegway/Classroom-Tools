Guided Notes

After WWII, immigration patterns shifted, with increasing numbers of immigrants arriving from non-European regions such as  ,  , and  . Combined with displaced persons and Holocaust survivors who migrated between 1945 and 1951, these new waves of immigration contributed to Canada's growing cultural diversity and reshaped its population and identity. The development of multiculturalism reflected government efforts to recognize and include diverse cultural groups within Canadian society.

Do not treat this era as only "progress"

At the very same time Canada was expanding rights and diversity, government policies such as residential schools and the Sixties Scoop caused long-term harm to Indigenous communities by separating children from their families and cultures. A full and honest picture of this period holds both realities at once.
