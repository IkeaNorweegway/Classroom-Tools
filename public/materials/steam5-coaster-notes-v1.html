Part 1: Energy & Motion

I can: use the word "energy" to explain why a marble moves when you let it go.

📌 New Word: potential energy

Stored energy. A marble held at the top of a ramp has potential energy just because of how high up it is.

📌 New Word: kinetic energy

The energy of moving. When you let the marble go, its potential energy turns into kinetic energy.

The higher the marble starts, the more potential energy it has to turn into motion.

Part 2: Friction & Control

I can: explain why a marble slows down, and name design choices that control its speed.

Common Mistake

"The marble slows down because it runs out of energy on its own."

Why that's wrong: Energy doesn't just disappear — something has to remove it. Picture two ramps, same height, same marble: one smooth and straight, one rough with turns. Same starting energy in both — but very different results at the bottom. If energy just "ran out" by itself, both marbles would act the same way.

📌 New Word: friction

Rubbing between the marble and the track. Friction (and bumping into turns) is what removes energy from the marble — turning it into heat and sound you can't easily see.

What's actually true: A rougher surface, a narrower channel, or a tighter turn all increase friction and slow the marble down on purpose. That's a design choice, not something that just happens.

A steeper section makes the marble go faster at that point. For this unit's goal — keeping the marble moving slowly without stopping — is steeper usually helpful or unhelpful?

Check Yourself — no peeking
  1. Name two design features that would slow a marble down without stopping it completely.
  2. A friend says "my coaster failed because the marble ran out of energy." Rewrite their sentence using the word "friction" so it's more accurate.

Part 3: The Design Cycle & Constraints

I can: name the steps of the design cycle, and explain what a constraint does to a design problem.

This unit, we name a process for the first time: the engineering design cycle.

Define → Brainstorm → Build → Test → Redesign → Share

📌 New Word: constraint

A limit or rule your design has to work inside — not a suggestion. Your coaster has two constraints that fight each other: it has to keep moving for a set time, and it can't stop completely. Solving one part (going slow) makes the other part (not stopping) harder.

📌 New Word: prototype

A first attempt you expect to test and change. It is not supposed to be perfect the first time.

📌 New Word: redesign

Changing your design based on what a test showed you.

Testing a prototype and finding out what's wrong with it is how the design cycle is supposed to work — not a sign you did something wrong.

Connection

Think of something you've built before (Lego, a fort, a drawing) where your first try didn't work the way you wanted. What did you change?

Check Yourself — no peeking
  1. List the six steps of the design cycle in order.
  2. Why are "keep moving" and "move slowly" two constraints that fight each other, instead of just one goal?
