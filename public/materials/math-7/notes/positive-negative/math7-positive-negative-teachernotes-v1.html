Teacher notes

Positive & Negative Numbers

Grade 7 Mathematics · Number · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcome 7N1 · Codes PN1–PN4

Overview

Big idea: every number has a place on one number line. Left is smaller, right is bigger. Absolute value is distance from 0, so it is never negative. Fractions and decimals are two names for the same point, and the sign can sit anywhere in a fraction: −a/b = a/(−b) = −(a/b).

Sequence: opposites and absolute value with integers (PN1) → the same ideas with negative fractions and decimals (PN2) → converting fractions ↔ decimals (PN3) → comparing and ordering mixed lists (PN4). PN3 comes before PN4 because ordering a mixed list needs one form.

Materials: a large floor number line (−5 to 5, marked in quarters and tenths between −1 and 1), a classroom thermometer or a picture of one, hundred grids, fraction strips, calculators for 3 ÷ 8 style divisions.

Depth ceiling: decimals that stop (terminating). 1/3 = 0.333… may appear as "keeps going", but no bar notation, no "repeating decimal" as a term and no rational/irrational talk (Grade 8). Integer operations are the next topic; do not teach adding or multiplying here.

Reading level: student pages are written at about Grade 3–4. Read word problems aloud when needed. The math words (opposite, absolute value, negative, simplest form) are the learning; teach them with the picture beside them.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Opposites and absolute value · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Read it for who still thinks −5 is warmer than −2.
SHOWThe number line as a road; the thermometer as a number line standing up. Then 4 and −4 with the two arcs.
SAY"Opposites are the same distance from 0, on different sides. Absolute value is that distance. A distance is never negative."
DOStudents stand on the floor line at −4 and 4 and count steps to 0. Then Worked Examples 1–3 (Calgary morning, |−2.5|, Leo's −$15.50 bank balance).
CUE"Read |−6| as 'the absolute value of negative six'." Have students say it aloud.
WATCH|−5| = −5. Use the misconception box before the Your turn.
DOExit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Fractions and decimals on the number line · 60 min
DOWarm-up: |−7|, the opposite of 3.5, and "which is colder, −12 °C or −9 °C?"
SHOWQuarters and tenths on both sides of 0. "The left side is a mirror of the right side."
DOWorked Examples 1–3 (−1.5, −3/4 and −1 1/4, stock changes). Students place cards on the floor line.
SAY"One negative sign puts the number left of 0. It does not matter if it is in front, on top or on the bottom."
WATCH−1.5 between −1 and 0. Ask "Is it more than one step from 0?"
CUEFor a/(−b), do not use division sign rules yet (that is the Integers topic). The argument is "one negative sign, so it's on the negative side." If a student asks about (−3)/(−4), say it is positive and that the Integers topic shows why.
DOExit ticket Section 2.
Lesson 3 — Fractions to decimals and back · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: place −0.75 on a line; |−1 1/2|; write 0.7 as a fraction.
SHOWThe hundred grid with 3/4 shaded (75 hundredths), then fraction strips 2/5 and 4/10.
SAY"Way 1: make 10, 100 or 1000 on the bottom. Way 2: the fraction bar means divide."
DOWorked Example 1 (3/4), Worked Example 2 (−3/8 stock change, calculator), Worked Example 3 (−0.35 m lake level, simplest form).
WATCH1/4 = 1.4. Ask where 1.4 sits on the number line.
CUEBenchmarks to memorize: 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/5, 1/10, 1/8. Quick-fire them at the start of the next three classes.
DOExit ticket Section 3. Quiz next class (PN1–PN3).
Lesson 4 — Comparing and ordering · 60 min
DOMid-unit quiz (25 min) first. Then the lesson.
SHOW−8 and −3 on the line and on two thermometers. Then 0.500 vs 0.125 in the place-value table.
SAY"Further left is smaller. For a mixed list: change to decimals, line up the places, put them on a line."
DOWorked Example 1 (−3/4 vs −5/8), Worked Example 2 (mixed list), Worked Example 3 (five Alberta towns one January morning). Then the Core worksheet; Challenge for early finishers.
WATCHStudents who compare −0.75 and −0.625 by size of digits only. Every comparison gets a quick sketch.
DOExit ticket Section 4.

Assessment

Mid-unit quiz (22 marks)

PN1 (Q1–3), PN2 (Q4–6), PN3 (Q7–9). Q9 is the explain-your-thinking item. Mark placement on number lines as correct if the point is within the right quarter or tenth.

Worksheets and exits

Core (★) covers PN1–PN4 with a picture on most questions. Challenge (★★) adds elevations above and below sea level, a number between two negatives, and "always, sometimes, never" reasoning. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it.

Data and contexts used

  • The Alberta temperatures in the notes and worksheets ("one January morning") are realistic but made up for practice.
  • Elevations: Dead Sea shore about −430 m (it drops about 1 m a year; check a current source), Death Valley (Badwater Basin) −86 m, Netherlands' lowest point about −7 m, Edmonton about 645 m, Lake Louise about 1750 m.
  • Stock changes in eighths: North American stock prices were quoted in fractions (eighths, then sixteenths) until decimal pricing in the late 1990s and early 2000s.

Connections

  • Integers (next topic): a number plus its opposite is 0 (a zero pair). Absolute value helps decide the sign of a sum.
  • Fractions & Decimals: converting and comparing are needed for × and ÷ of fractions and decimals.
  • Grade 8: rational numbers, repeating decimals with bar notation, and operations with negative fractions and decimals.
