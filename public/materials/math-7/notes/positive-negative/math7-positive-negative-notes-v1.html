Section 1 · Opposites and absolute value

Learning target: I can find the opposite and the absolute value of a number.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
positive number

opposite

A number line is like a road.

Numbers get bigger as you go right.

They get smaller as you go left.

A negative number is less than 0. A positive number is more than 0.

A thermometer is a number line standing up. Up is warmer. Down is colder.

Opposites

Opposites are the same distance from 0. They are on different sides.

Opposites show up in real life.

  • A gain of $5 and a loss of $5.
  • 10 m above sea level and 10 m below sea level.

The opposite of −7 is 7. The opposite of 7 is −7.

The opposite of 0 is 0.

Absolute value

The absolute value of a number is its distance from 0.

We write it with two straight bars: |−5|.

Say it: "the absolute value of negative five."

Absolute value is a distance

A distance is never negative.

So |−5| = 5 and |5| = 5.

Opposites have the same absolute value.

Fractions and decimals live between the whole numbers.

The left side of 0 is a mirror of the right side.

Tenths work the same way.

Worked Example 1 — Where is −1.5?

Step 1: −1.5 is past −1. It is between and .

So −1.5 is half way from −1 to −2.

Watch out — a common mistake

"−1.5 is between −1 and 0."

Why it is wrong: −1.5 is more than 1 step from 0. So it must be past −1.

What is true: −1.5 is half way from −1 to −2. The point between −1 and 0 is −0.5.

One sign, three places

A negative sign can sit in front, on top, or on the bottom.

It is still the same number.

−a/b = a/−b = −(a/b)

−3/4 = 3/−4 = −(3/4)

All three mean "three quarters, on the negative side."

Watch out — a common mistake

"−3/4, 3/−4 and −(3/4) are three different numbers."

Why it is wrong: Each one has just one negative sign. One negative sign puts the number left of 0.

What is true: All three are the same number. They sit at the same point.

Section 4 · Comparing and ordering

Learning target: I can compare and order positive and negative fractions and decimals.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
greater than (>)
less than (<)
least to greatest

The number line decides. Further left is smaller.

Watch out — a common mistake

"−8 is bigger than −3, because 8 is bigger than 3."

Why it is wrong: −8 is further from 0, but on the left side. Further left is smaller.

What is true: −8 < −3. Think: −8 °C is colder than −3 °C.

Watch out — a common mistake

"0.125 is bigger than 0.5, because it has more digits."

Why it is wrong: Compare the place values, not the number of digits.

NumberTenthsHundredthsThousandths
0.500500
0.125125

What is true: 0.500 > 0.125. Five tenths beats one tenth.
