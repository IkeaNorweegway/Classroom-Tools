Section 1 · Lines, rays, segments, angles, polygons

Learning target: I can tell apart lines, rays, segments, angles and polygons. I can name them with symbols.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
pointan exact spot. Name it with a capital letter.point A
linestraight, and goes on forever both ways. It has no ends.line CD
raystarts at one endpoint and goes on forever one wayray AC
segmentthe part of a line between two endpointssegment AB
endpointa point where a ray or a segment stopsA is the endpoint of ray AC
angletwo rays (or segments) that share an endpoint∠CAB
vertexthe shared endpoint of an angle. Also a corner of a polygon.A is the vertex of ∠CAB
polygona closed shape made of 3 or more segments. They meet only at their ends.△ABC

Ideas, not drawings

A line goes on forever. You can't draw forever! So a drawing is only a model. An arrowhead means "keeps going".

A segment is like a railway track between two stations. It stops at both ends.

A ray is like a flashlight beam. It starts at the flashlight and goes on and on.

An angle is two rays with the same endpoint. That point is the vertex.

Naming with symbols

Line CD: use any two points on the line. Draw ↔ over CD.

Ray AC: the endpoint comes FIRST. Draw → over AC.

Segment AB: use the two endpoints. Draw a bar over AB.

∠CAB: the vertex goes in the MIDDLE. If only one angle is at A, write ∠A.

△ABC: a triangle with vertices A, B and C.

Watch out — a common mistake

"A ray goes both ways." Or "A line has end points."

Why it is wrong: Count the arrowheads. A line has two. A ray has one. A segment has none.

What is true: A line goes forever both ways. A ray has one end. A segment has two ends.

Worked Example 1 — Name each object

Step 1: The first one has one endpoint, K. It is a ray. The endpoint goes first: ray KM.

Step 2: The second one has two endpoints. It is segment GH. Segment HG is fine too.

Step 3: The third one has two arrowheads. It is line EF. Line FE is fine too.

Worked Example 2 — Three names for one angle

Step 1: Find the vertex. The rays meet at S.

Step 2: Put S in the middle: ∠TSU or ∠UST.

Step 3: Only one angle is at S. So ∠S is fine too.

Explain it Why must the vertex go in the middle of an angle's name?
Answer The middle letter tells where the rays meet. ∠CAB has its vertex at A, but ∠ACB has its vertex at C.
Worked Example 3 — Polygon or not?

Step 1: Shape 1 is closed. It has 3 segments. They meet only at their ends. It is a polygon: a triangle.

Step 2: Shape 2 has a gap. It is not closed. It is not a polygon.

Step 3: The sides of Shape 3 cross in the middle. A polygon's sides meet only at their ends. So Shape 3 is not a polygon.

Step 4: Shape 4 is closed. It has 5 sides that meet only at their ends. It is a polygon: a pentagon.

Worked Example 4 — A roof truss

A roof truss is a triangle made of wood. Name the triangle, its sides and its angles.

Step 1: The triangle is △ABC.

Step 2: The sides are segments AB, BC and CA.

Step 3: The angles are ∠A, ∠B and ∠C. ∠A can also be called ∠CAB.

Your turn
  1. 1. Name this object.

Ray NP. N is the endpoint, so it goes first.

  1. 2. Give two names for this angle.

∠QRS and ∠SRQ. ∠R works too.

  1. 3. Draw segment XY. Then draw ray YZ. A segment from X to Y with no arrowheads. A ray that starts at Y and goes through Z.
  2. 4. Is a circle a polygon? No. A circle is not made of segments.
  3. 5. Zoe says ray AB and ray BA are the same. Is she right? No. Ray AB starts at A. Ray BA starts at B. They go opposite ways.

  2. Solve x + 70 = 180.
    Answer x = 110
  3. Find 3/4 of 20.
    Answer 15
  4. Is (1, 2), (1, 5) a function?
    Answer No. The input 1 has two outputs.

Section 2 · Parallel and perpendicular

Learning target: I can show parallel and perpendicular lines with marks and symbols.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
intersectcross or meet at a pointtwo roads meet at a corner
parallel (∥)lines that never meet. They stay the same distance apart.railway rails: m ∥ n
perpendicular (⊥)lines that meet at a right anglethe edges of a window pane: m ⊥ n
right anglean angle of exactly 90°. Mark it with a small square.the corner of a page
arrowheadsmatching marks on lines. They show the lines are parallel.one > on each rail

Railway tracks and window panes

The two rails of a railway track never meet. They are parallel.

The edges of a window pane meet at square corners. They are perpendicular.

Fold it

  1. 1. Fold a sheet of paper in half.
  2. 2. Fold it in half again. Line up the first crease with itself.
  3. 3. Open it up. Look at the two creases.
  4. 4. Check the corners with the corner of a page. All four are right angles.

The two creases are perpendicular.

Marks and symbols

Small square: a right angle (90°).

Matching arrowheads: the lines are parallel.

m ∥ n means "line m is parallel to line n".

m ⊥ n means "line m is perpendicular to line n".

Watch out — a common mistake

"Lines that look parallel are parallel."

Why it is wrong: AB and DC look parallel. But they have no arrowheads. Measure the gap at each end.

The gap is 2.6 cm on the left and 3 cm on the right. Far to the left, the lines meet.

What is true: Only matching arrowheads (or a measurement) show that lines are parallel.

Worked Example 1 — A window pane

Write the parallel and perpendicular sides with symbols.

Step 1: AB and DC both have one arrowhead. So AB ∥ DC.

Step 2: AD and BC both have two arrowheads. So AD ∥ BC.

Step 3: The small square at B shows a right angle. So AB ⊥ BC.

Worked Example 2 — Edmonton streets

In downtown Edmonton, avenues run east–west. Streets run north–south.

Step 1: Jasper Avenue and 102 Avenue never meet. So Jasper Ave ∥ 102 Ave.

Step 2: 109 Street meets Jasper Avenue at a right angle. So 109 St ⊥ Jasper Ave.

Step 3: 109 Street meets 102 Avenue at a right angle too. So 109 St ⊥ 102 Ave.

Explain it Why can't you tell two lines are parallel just by looking?
Answer They might lean a tiny bit and meet far away. You need arrowheads or a measurement.
Worked Example 3 — A quilt block piece

Which sides are parallel? Which are perpendicular?

Step 1: PQ and SR have matching arrowheads. So PQ ∥ SR.

Step 2: PS and QR have no arrowheads. They lean toward each other. They are not parallel.

Step 3: There are no small squares. So no sides are perpendicular.

Your turn
  1. 1. Line a and line b never meet. Write this with a symbol. a ∥ b
  2. 2. Write this with a symbol.

p ⊥ q

  1. 3. Name a side parallel to EF. Name a side perpendicular to EF.

HG is parallel to EF. FG and EH are perpendicular to EF.

  1. 4. The rungs of a ladder each meet the rails at a right angle. Are the rungs parallel to each other? Yes. They are all perpendicular to the same rail.
  2. 5. Ben sees two roads on a map. They look parallel. There are no arrowheads. Can he be sure? No. They may meet far away. He needs arrowheads or a measurement.
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. Name the vertex of ∠XYZ.
    Answer Y
  2. How many endpoints does a ray have?
    Answer One
  3. Find 180 − 47.
    Answer 133
  4. Solve 2x + 10 = 180.
    Answer 2x = 170, so x = 85.
  5. Find 20% of 45.
    Answer 10% is 4.5, so 20% is 9.
  6. ★ Stretch Can two lines be parallel AND perpendicular? Explain.
    Answer No. Perpendicular lines meet. Parallel lines never meet.

Section 3 · Angles where lines cross

Learning target: I can find angles where two lines cross. I can use angles to check if lines are parallel.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
opposite anglesangles across from each other where two lines cross. Also called vertically opposite angles.∠1 and ∠3
adjacent anglesangles side by side. They share a vertex and a ray.∠1 and ∠2
supplementarytwo angles that add to 180°40° and 140°
congruent (≅)exactly the same size. Congruent angles have the same number of degrees.∠1 ≅ ∠3
transversala line that crosses two or more linesa diagonal road across two avenues
corresponding anglesangles in the same spot at each crossing∠2 and ∠6 (both top right)

Try it with tracing paper

  1. 1. Draw two lines that cross. Number the angles 1 to 4.
  2. 2. Trace them on tracing paper.
  3. 3. Turn the tracing paper halfway around. Pin the centre with your pencil.
  4. 4. ∠1 lands on ∠3. ∠2 lands on ∠4.

A straight line makes a straight angle: 180°. ∠1 and ∠2 sit side by side on a line. So ∠1 + ∠2 = 180°.

Two lines cross

Opposite angles are congruent: ∠1 ≅ ∠3 and ∠2 ≅ ∠4.

Adjacent angles on a line are supplementary: they add to 180°.

Worked Example 1 — Find the missing angles

Step 1: ∠2 sits beside the 40° angle on a line. ∠2 = 180° − 40° = 140°.

Step 2: ∠3 is opposite the 40° angle. So ∠3 = 40°.

Step 3: ∠4 is opposite ∠2. So ∠4 = 140°.

Check: the four angles go all the way around. They add to 360°.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Opposite angles add to 180°."

Why it is wrong: Opposite angles are the same size. 40° + 40° is only 80°.

What is true: Opposite angles are equal. Angles side by side on a line add to 180°.

Worked Example 2 — Calgary's Peace Bridge

Steel bars on the Peace Bridge cross like an X. One angle is 115°.

Step 1: ∠1 is beside the 115° angle. ∠1 = 180° − 115° = 65°.

Step 2: ∠3 is opposite ∠1. ∠3 = 65°.

Step 3: ∠4 is opposite the 115° angle. ∠4 = 115°.

Explain it In Worked Example 2, why does ∠1 + 115° make 180°?
Answer They sit side by side on one straight line. A straight line is 180°.

A transversal

A transversal crosses two lines. It makes 8 angles: 4 at each crossing.

Corresponding pairs: ∠1 and ∠5, ∠2 and ∠6. Also ∠3 and ∠7, ∠4 and ∠8.

Corresponding angles

If m ∥ n, corresponding angles are congruent: ∠2 ≅ ∠6.

To check if two lines are parallel, measure two corresponding angles.

Equal: the lines are parallel. Not equal: the lines are not parallel.

Worked Example 3 — A diagonal road

Two avenues are parallel: m ∥ n. A diagonal road t crosses both. ∠2 = 55°.

Step 1: ∠6 corresponds to ∠2. The avenues are parallel. So ∠6 = 55°.

Step 2: ∠1 is beside ∠2 on line m. ∠1 = 180° − 55° = 125°.

Step 3: ∠5 corresponds to ∠1. So ∠5 = 125°.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Corresponding angles are always equal."

Why it is wrong: Here m and n are not parallel. ∠2 is 60°, but ∠6 is only 55°.

What is true: Corresponding angles are equal only when the lines are parallel.

Worked Example 4 — Are the rails parallel?

Measure ∠2 and ∠6 in each picture.

Step 1: ∠2 and ∠6 are corresponding angles.

Step 2: In Picture A, both are 70°. So m and n are parallel.

Step 3: In Picture B, they are 75° and 70°. So m and n are not parallel.

Your turn
  1. 1. Find the other three angles.
  1. 2. Two angles sit side by side on a line. One is 35°. Find the other. 180° − 35° = 145°
  2. 3. m ∥ n and ∠4 = 60°. Find ∠8 and ∠7.

∠8 corresponds to ∠4, so ∠8 = 60°. ∠7 is beside ∠8, so ∠7 = 180° − 60° = 120°.

  1. 4. Is m ∥ n? Use ∠1 and ∠5.

No. ∠1 and ∠5 correspond, but 115° ≠ 120°.

  1. 5. Raj says two opposite angles are 75° and 105°. Can he be right? No. Opposite angles are equal. 75° and 105° add to 180°, so they are adjacent.
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. Name the ray with endpoint B that goes through C.
    Answer Ray BC
  2. Write "p is perpendicular to q" with a symbol.
    Answer p ⊥ q
  3. Find −15 ÷ 3.
    Answer −5
  4. Solve 3x = x + 40.
    Answer 2x = 40, so x = 20.
  5. Find 2/3 × 9.
    Answer 6
  6. ★ Stretch Two lines cross. One angle is x°. Write the other three angles using x.
    Answer (180 − x)°, x° and (180 − x)°.

Section 4 · Congruent polygons

Learning target: I can match the parts of congruent polygons. I can write a congruence statement.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
congruent polygonssame size and same shape. One fits exactly on top of the other.two quilt triangles
corresponding verticesmatching cornersA ↔ R
corresponding sidesmatching sides. They are congruent.AB ≅ RS
corresponding anglesmatching angles. They are congruent.∠ABC ≅ ∠RST
hash markssmall ticks on sides. The same number of ticks means equal sides.one tick, two ticks
arcssmall curves in corners. The same number of arcs means equal angles.one arc, two arcs

Trace, then fit

  1. 1. Trace △ABC on tracing paper.
  2. 2. Slide it, turn it or flip it onto △RST.
  3. 3. Does it fit exactly? Then the triangles are congruent.

Hash marks and arcs show which parts match.

"Corresponding" means "matching". In Section 3, it matched angles on a transversal. Here it matches parts of two shapes.

Congruence statements

△ABC ≅ △RST says the triangles are congruent.

The letter order shows the match: A ↔ R, B ↔ S, C ↔ T.

Matching sides: AB ≅ RS, BC ≅ ST, CA ≅ TR.

Matching angles: ∠ABC ≅ ∠RST, and so on.

Four-sided shapes work the same way: ABCD ≅ RSTU.

Worked Example 1 — Read the marks

Write a congruence statement for these triangles.

Step 1: Match the angles by arcs. One arc: ∠A and ∠Y. Two arcs: ∠B and ∠X. Three arcs: ∠C and ∠Z.

Step 2: Match the sides by hash marks. AB ≅ YX. BC ≅ XZ. CA ≅ ZY.

Step 3: Write the letters in matching order: △ABC ≅ △YXZ.

Watch out — a common mistake

"The letter order in a congruence statement doesn't matter."

Why it is wrong: Mia writes △ABC ≅ △XYZ. That says A matches X. But ∠A has one arc. ∠X has two arcs.

What is true: The order shows the match. Pair the letters by their marks. Then write them in that order.

Worked Example 2 — Two quilt pieces

ABCD ≅ RSTU. One piece is flipped. Find ST, UR, ∠R and ∠U.

Step 1: The order shows the match. A ↔ R and B ↔ S. C ↔ T and D ↔ U.

Step 2: ST matches BC. So ST = 4 cm. UR matches DA. So UR = 5 cm.

Step 3: ∠R matches ∠A, so ∠R = 53°. ∠U matches ∠D, so ∠U = 127°.

Explain it In Worked Example 2, how did you know ST matches BC?
Answer S and T are 2nd and 3rd in RSTU. B and C are 2nd and 3rd in ABCD.
Worked Example 3 — Are they congruent?

Step 1: Pair A: both are 4 cm by 3 cm. All the angles are 90°. One is just turned. They are congruent.

Step 2: Pair B: one is 4 cm long. The other is 5 cm long. They are not congruent.

Worked Example 4 — Roof trusses

All the trusses on a roof are congruent: △ABC ≅ △DEF. Find DE, ∠E and ∠F.

Step 1: DE matches AB. So DE = 8 m.

Step 2: ∠E matches ∠B. So ∠E = 35°.

Step 3: ∠F matches ∠C. So ∠F = 110°.

Your turn
  1. 1. △PQR ≅ △KLM. Which side matches QR? Which angle matches ∠P? LM matches QR. ∠K matches ∠P.
  2. 2. Write a congruence statement. Start with △DEF.

△DEF ≅ △NLM

  1. 3. ABCD ≅ WXYZ. AB = 7 cm and ∠C = 80°. Find WX and ∠Y. WX = 7 cm and ∠Y = 80°.
  2. 4. Is a square with 5 cm sides congruent to a square with 6 cm sides? No. They have the same shape but not the same size.
  3. 5. Noor writes △ABC ≅ △RST. Kai writes △BCA ≅ △STR. Do they show the same match? Yes. Both match A ↔ R, B ↔ S and C ↔ T.

Put it all together

From memory — notes closed
  1. A road t crosses two railway rails, m and n. The rails are parallel. ∠2 = 50°.
    1. a) Find ∠1, ∠3 and ∠6. b) Why is ∠6 equal to ∠2?
    Answer a) ∠1 = 180° − 50° = 130°. ∠3 is opposite ∠2, so 50°. ∠6 corresponds to ∠2, so 50°.
    b) They are corresponding angles, and the rails are parallel.
  2. Name the ray, the segment and the angle.
    Answer Ray AB. Segment CD. ∠EFG (or ∠GFE or ∠F).
  3. △JKL ≅ △PQR. JK = 9 cm and ∠L = 40°. Find PQ and ∠R.
    Answer PQ = 9 cm. ∠R = 40°.
  4. Sam says two lines are parallel because they look parallel. What should he check?
    Answer Arrowheads, or a pair of corresponding angles where a transversal crosses. Equal angles mean parallel.
  5. Look back at the Pre-Knowledge Check. What would you change now?
    Answer Answers vary.
