Section 1 · Multiplying fractions

Learning target: I can multiply fractions with a picture and with a rule.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
productthe answer to a multiplicationthe product of 2 and 3 is 6
ofin math, "of" often means ×1/2 of 8 = 1/2 × 8 = 4
area modela square cut into rows and columns to show a product1/3 × 1/2 is 1 part out of 6
proper fractionthe top is smaller than the bottom. It is less than 1.3/4
simplest formthe top and bottom have no common factor but 14/8 in simplest form is 1/2

A whole number times a fraction

3 × 2/5 means 3 groups of 2/5.

3 × 2/5 = 2/5 + 2/5 + 2/5 = 6/5

So 3 × 2/5 = 6/5 = 11/5.

A fraction of a number

1/2 × 8 means "half of 8."

1/2 × 8 = 4. The answer is less than 8.

A fraction of a fraction

1/3 × 1/2 means "1/3 of 1/2."

  1. 1. Shade 1/2 of a square in columns (blue).
  2. 2. Shade 1/3 of the square in rows (yellow).
  3. 3. The overlap is the answer.

The square has 3 rows and 2 columns. That makes 3 × 2 = 6 parts.

The overlap is 1 × 1 = 1 part. So 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6.

Multiplying fractions

Multiply the tops. Multiply the bottoms.

a/b × c/d = (a × c)/(b × d)

Then write the answer in simplest form.

Worked Example 1 — Half a bannock recipe

Ava's bannock recipe uses 3/4 cup of milk. She makes half a recipe. How much milk does she need?

Step 1: Half of 3/4 means 1/2 × 3/4.

Step 2: Multiply the tops: 1 × 3 = 3.

Step 3: Multiply the bottoms: 2 × 4 = 8.

She needs 3/8 cup of milk.

Explain it Why is the bottom number 8 in Worked Example 1?
Answer The square is cut into 2 rows and 4 columns. That makes 2 × 4 = 8 equal parts.
Watch out — a common mistake

"Multiplying always makes a number bigger."

Why it is wrong: Times a proper fraction means take part of it. A part is smaller.

What is true: 1/2 × 8 = 4. And 1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8. Each answer is smaller.

Two proper fractions

When you multiply two proper fractions, the product is less than each one.

Worked Example 2 — Simplify first or last?

Find 2/4 × 2/3 two ways.

Way 1: Multiply first. 2/4 × 2/3 = 4/12. Simplify: 4/12 = 1/3.

Way 2: Simplify first. 2/4 = 1/2. Then 1/2 × 2/3 = 2/6 = 1/3.

Both ways give 1/3. Equal fractions give equal products.

Watch out — a common mistake

"To multiply, find a common denominator first. 1/2 × 1/3 = 3/6 × 2/6 = 6/6 = 1."

Why it is wrong: Common denominators are for + and −. For ×, multiply the tops and the bottoms.

What is true: 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6. Half of a third is a sixth.

Worked Example 3 — Pancakes, one and a half times

Leo's pancake recipe uses 2/3 cup of milk. He makes 11/2 batches. How much milk?

Step 1: Change the mixed number. 11/2 = 3/2.

Step 2: Multiply. 3/2 × 2/3 = 6/6.

Step 3: Simplify. 6/6 = 1. He needs 1 cup.

This time the answer is bigger than 2/3. 11/2 is more than 1.

Your turn

  1. 2. Find each product. Write it in simplest form.
  2. a) 3/5 × 1/2 = 3/10
  3. b) 3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12 = 1/2
  4. c) 4 × 2/3 = 8/3 = 22/3
  5. 3. There is 3/4 of a pan of bannock left. Noor eats 1/3 of it. What fraction of the pan does she eat?

1/3 × 3/4 = 3/12 = 1/4 of the pan

  1. 4. Ben says 1/2 × 1/4 is bigger than 1/4. Is he right?

No. Half of 1/4 is 1/8. That is less than 1/4.

Section 2 · Dividing fractions

Learning target: I can divide with fractions using groups, common denominators and reciprocals.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
quotientthe answer to a division6 ÷ 2 = 3. The quotient is 3.
divisorthe number you divide byin 3 ÷ 1/2, the divisor is 1/2
reciprocalthe fraction flipped: switch the top and the bottomthe reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2

How many groups fit?

3 ÷ 1/4 asks: how many 1/4s fit in 3?

Each whole has 4 quarters. 3 wholes have 3 × 4 = 12 quarters.

So 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Dividing always makes a number smaller."

Why it is wrong: Dividing by a small piece counts how many pieces fit. Lots of small pieces fit.

What is true: 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6. There are 6 halves in 3.

Worked Example 1 — Cutting rope

Raj has 6 m of rope. He cuts pieces that are 3/4 m long. How many pieces does he get?

Step 1: Ask: how many 3/4s fit in 6?

Step 2: Each metre has 4 quarters. So 6 m has 24 quarters.

Step 3: Each piece uses 3 quarters. 24 ÷ 3 = 8.

He gets 8 pieces.

Explain it Why is the answer to 6 ÷ 3/4 bigger than 6?
Answer Each piece is less than 1 m. So more than 6 pieces fit in 6 m.

A fraction shared by a whole number

Three friends share 1/2 of a pan of bannock. How much does each one get?

Each friend gets 1/3 of the half.

1/2 ÷ 3 = 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6 of the pan.

Dividing by 3 is the same as taking 1/3 of it.

Worked Example 2 — Sharing a pizza

Mia has 3/4 of a pizza. She shares it with 2 friends. All 3 get the same amount. How much does each one get?

Step 1: Write it: 3/4 ÷ 3.

Step 2: The picture has 3 quarters. Each person gets 1 quarter.

Step 3: Check with the rule: 1/3 × 3/4 = 3/12 = 1/4.

Each person gets 1/4 of the pizza.

Fraction ÷ fraction with the same bottom

3/4 ÷ 1/8: write both in eighths first.

3/4 = 6/8. Now ask: how many 1/8s fit in 6/8?

Six eighths hold 6 pieces of one eighth.

Same bottoms

When the bottoms match, divide the tops.

a/b ÷ c/b = a ÷ c

6/8 ÷ 2/8 = 6 ÷ 2 = 3

Worked Example 3 — Shelves from a board

Kai has a board 11/2 m long. Each shelf is 3/8 m long. How many shelves can he cut?

Step 1: Change the mixed number. 11/2 = 3/2.

Step 2: Make the bottoms match. 3/2 = 12/8.

Step 3: Divide the tops. 12/8 ÷ 3/8 = 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

He can cut 4 shelves.

Reciprocals

How many 1/4s make 1 whole? Four. So 1/4 × 4 = 1.

A number times its reciprocal is always 1.

Dividing by a fraction

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.

  1. 1. Keep the first number.
  2. 2. Change ÷ to ×.
  3. 3. Flip the divisor (the second number).

3 ÷ 1/4 = 3 × 4 = 12. That matches the picture of 12 quarters.

Worked Example 4 — 2/3 ÷ 1/6

Step 1: Keep 2/3. Change ÷ to ×. Flip 1/6 to 6/1.

Step 2: 2/3 × 6/1 = 12/3.

Step 3: Simplify. 12/3 = 4.

Check with a picture. How many sixths fit in 2/3?

Watch out — a common mistake

"To divide, flip the first fraction. 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 2/1 × 1/4 = 2/4."

Why it is wrong: Keep the first one. Flip only the one you divide BY.

What is true: 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/2 × 4/1 = 4/2 = 2. Two quarters fit in a half.

Your turn

  1. 2. Write the reciprocal.
  2. a) 3/5 → 5/3
  3. b) 7 → 1/7
  4. c) 1/2 → 2
  5. 3. Find 5/6 ÷ 1/6. 5 ÷ 1 = 5
  6. 4. Find 3/4 ÷ 1/2. 3/4 × 2 = 6/4 = 11/2
  7. 5. Four friends share 1/2 of a pizza. How much does each one get? 1/2 ÷ 4 = 1/8
  8. 6. Zoe wrote 2/3 ÷ 1/3 = 3/2 × 1/3 = 3/6. Fix her mistake.

She flipped the first fraction. Flip the divisor: 2/3 × 3 = 6/3 = 2.

Section 3 · Multiplying and dividing decimals

Learning target: I can multiply and divide decimals and check with an estimate.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
tenthone of 10 equal parts of 10.3 is 3 tenths
hundredthone of 100 equal parts of 10.06 is 6 hundredths
decimal placea digit after the decimal point2.45 has two decimal places
estimatea close answer with friendly numbers2.9 × 4 is about 3 × 4 = 12
equivalent divisiona division with the same quotient6 ÷ 0.5 = 60 ÷ 5

A decimal times a whole number

Mia buys 3 m of ribbon. It costs $1.25 for each metre.

3 × 1.25 = 1.25 + 1.25 + 1.25 = 3.75

She pays $3.75.

A decimal times a decimal

0.3 × 0.2 means 3 tenths of 2 tenths.

Decimals are fractions: 0.3 = 3/10 and 0.2 = 2/10.

3/10 × 2/10 = 6/100 = 0.06

Multiplying decimals
  1. 1. Multiply as if there were no decimal points: 3 × 2 = 6.
  2. 2. Count the decimal places in both numbers: 1 + 1 = 2.
  3. 3. Give the answer that many decimal places: 0.06.
  4. 4. Check with an estimate or a picture.
Watch out — a common mistake

"0.3 × 0.2 = 0.6"

Why it is wrong: 0.6 is bigger than both numbers. Part of a part must be smaller.

What is true: 3 × 2 = 6. There are two decimal places in all. So 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.06.

Worked Example 1 — Filling up with gas

Gas costs $1.45 per litre. Sam's dad buys 30 L. What does it cost?

Step 1: Estimate. 30 × $1.50 = $45.

Step 2: Multiply: 30 × 145 = 4350.

Step 3: There are 2 decimal places. So 30 × 1.45 = 43.50.

It costs $43.50. That is close to the estimate. ✓

Worked Example 2 — A rug

A rug is 2.4 m long and 1.5 m wide. Find its area.

Step 1: Split each side. 2.4 = 2 + 0.4. 1.5 = 1 + 0.5.

Step 2: Find the four parts. Add them: 3.6 m².

Step 3: Check with the rule. 24 × 15 = 360. Two decimal places: 3.60.

The area is 3.6 m².

Explain it In Worked Example 2, why are there four parts?
Answer Each part of the length times each part of the width. 2 parts times 2 parts make 4 parts.

A decimal shared by a whole number

Three friends share $4.50 equally.

4.50 ÷ 3 = 1.50. Each friend gets $1.50.

A whole number divided by a decimal

6 ÷ 0.5 asks: how many halves fit in 6?

Each whole has 2 halves. 6 wholes have 12 halves. So 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12.

Watch out — a common mistake

"6 ÷ 0.5 = 3"

Why it is wrong: 3 is half of 6. That is 6 ÷ 2, not 6 ÷ 0.5.

What is true: Count the jumps of 0.5. There are 12. So 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12.

Make an equivalent division

Multiply both numbers by 10. The quotient stays the same.

Think of money. $6 in 50¢ coins is 600¢ ÷ 50¢ = 12 coins.

Dividing by a decimal
  1. 1. Multiply both numbers by 10 (or 100). Stop when the divisor is a whole number.
  2. 2. Divide.
  3. 3. Check with an estimate.

For long decimals, use a calculator. Then check with an estimate.

Worked Example 3 — Bows from ribbon

Tess has 4.8 m of ribbon. Each bow uses 0.6 m. How many bows can she make?

Step 1: Write it: 4.8 ÷ 0.6.

Step 2: Multiply both by 10: 48 ÷ 6.

Step 3: Divide: 8. She can make 8 bows.

Explain it In Worked Example 3, why can we multiply both numbers by 10?
Answer Both get 10 times bigger. The number of groups that fit stays the same.
Worked Example 4 — Quarters

How many quarters ($0.25) make $3?

Step 1: Write it: 3 ÷ 0.25.

Step 2: Multiply both by 100: 300 ÷ 25.

Step 3: Divide: 12 quarters.

This is the same as 3 ÷ 1/4 in Section 2!

Your turn
  1. 1. Use the area model. Find 0.4 × 0.2.
  1. 2. Find 1.2 × 3. 3.6
  2. 3. Find 2.5 × 1.2. Fill in the parts.
  1. 4. Find 8 ÷ 0.5. 16
  2. 5. Find 1.8 ÷ 0.3. 18 ÷ 3 = 6
  3. 6. Omar says 0.5 × 0.5 = 2.5. Fix his mistake.

5 × 5 = 25. Two decimal places: 0.25. Half of a half is a quarter.

Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. Find 3/4 ÷ 1/4.
    Answer 3 ÷ 1 = 3
  2. Find 1/2 × 2/5.
    Answer 2/10 = 1/5
  3. Write 0.5 as a fraction. Then find 0.5 × 8.
    Answer 1/2. Half of 8 is 4.
  4. Find 3² × 0.1.
    Answer 9 × 0.1 = 0.9
  5. ★ Stretch 6 ÷ 0.5 is bigger than 6. Is 6 × 0.5 bigger or smaller than 6? Why?
    Answer Smaller. 6 × 0.5 = 3. Times a number less than 1 takes only part.

Section 4 · Order of operations

Learning target: I can evaluate an expression in the right order.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
expressionnumbers and signs like + and ×, with no = sign2 + 3 × 4
evaluatefind the value2 + 3 × 4 evaluates to 14
order of operationsthe rules for which step comes firstbrackets first, then × and ÷
brackets( ) around part of an expression. Do that part first.(2 + 3) × 4 = 20

Why we need an order

Mia is at the Calgary Stampede. She buys a $2 drink and 3 hot dogs at $4 each.

Her cost is 2 + 3 × 4.

Sam works left to right. 2 + 3 = 5, then 5 × 4 = 20.

Lily multiplies first. 3 × 4 = 12, then 2 + 12 = 14.

Mia pays $14. Lily is right. Everyone must get the same answer, so math has rules.

Order of operations
  1. 1. Brackets ( ).
  2. 2. Squares and cubes, like 3² and 2³.
  3. 3. × and ÷, left to right.
  4. 4. + and −, left to right.
Watch out — a common mistake

"Always work left to right. 2 + 3 × 4 = 5 × 4 = 20."

Why it is wrong: × comes before +, even when + is first in the line.

What is true: 2 + 3 × 4 = 2 + 12 = 14.

Watch out — a common mistake

"× always comes before ÷. 12 ÷ 2 × 3 = 12 ÷ 6 = 2."

Why it is wrong: × and ÷ are a team. Do them left to right, in the order you see them.

What is true: 12 ÷ 2 = 6. Then 6 × 3 = 18.

Worked Example 1 — With integers

Evaluate −3 + 4 × (−2).

Step 1: × first: 4 × (−2) = −8.

Step 2: Then +: −3 + (−8) = −11.

Worked Example 2 — Same numbers, different answers

Evaluate (5 − 2)² × 2 and 5 − 2² × 2.

Step 1: In the first one, brackets come first: 5 − 2 = 3. Square it: 9. Then × 2: 18.

Step 2: The second has no brackets. Square first: 2² = 4. Then 4 × 2 = 8. Then 5 − 8 = −3.

Explain it In Worked Example 2, why are the two answers different?
Answer The brackets change what we do first. With brackets, we subtract before we square.
Worked Example 3 — With fractions

Evaluate 1/2 + 1/2 × 1/4.

Step 1: × first: 1/2 × 1/4 = 1/8.

Step 2: Make the bottoms match: 1/2 = 4/8.

Step 3: Add: 4/8 + 1/8 = 5/8.

Worked Example 4 — Money at West Edmonton Mall

Noor buys 2 shirts at $12.50 each. She pays with $30. Find her change.

Step 1: Write it: 30 − 2 × 12.50.

Step 2: × first: 2 × 12.50 = 25.

Step 3: Then −: 30 − 25 = 5.

Her change is $5.00.
