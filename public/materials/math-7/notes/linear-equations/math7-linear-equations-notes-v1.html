Algebra tiles

A tall tile is x. A small square tile is 1. Red tiles are negative.

3x + 2 is three x tiles and two 1 tiles.

Like terms

Like terms have the same variable part. Add like terms by adding the coefficients.

2x + 3x is 2 tall tiles and 3 tall tiles. That is 5 tall tiles.

x-terms and numbers are not like terms. They stay apart.

Like terms

Add or subtract the coefficients. Keep the variable part the same.

4x + 2x = 6x. 7x − 3x = 4x. 5 + 2 = 7.

An x-term and a number do not combine: 4x + 2 stays 4x + 2.

Watch out — a common mistake

"2x + 3x = 5x²" and "3x + 2 = 5x."

Why it is wrong: 2x + 3x is 5 tiles that are all x. Adding does not make x². And 3x + 2 has two kinds of tiles. You can't count them as one kind.

What is true: 2x + 3x = 5x. 3x + 2 stays 3x + 2.

Zero pairs

A tile and its opposite make a zero pair. A zero pair adds to 0. You can take it away.

Worked Example 1 — Zero pairs

Simplify 3x + 4 − x − 6.

Step 1: Group like terms: 3x − x and 4 − 6.

Step 2: One x and one −x make a zero pair. 3x − x = 2x.

Step 3: Four + tiles and four − tiles make zero pairs. 4 − 6 = −2.

Step 4: The answer is 2x − 2.

Explain it In Worked Example 1, why can we take away the crossed-out tiles?

The distributive property

2(x + 3) means 2 groups of (x + 3).

Count all the tiles. There are 2 x tiles and 6 small tiles. So 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6.

Multiply BOTH terms by 2. 2 × x = 2x. 2 × 3 = 6.

Watch out — a common mistake

"2(x + 3) = 2x + 3."

Why it is wrong: The 2 multiplied only the x. Look at the 2 groups. Each group has 3 small tiles.

What is true: Multiply BOTH terms in the brackets. 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6.

Worked Example 2 — A negative number in front

Simplify −3(x − 2).

Step 1: 3(x − 2) is 3 groups of (x − 2). That is 3x − 6.

Step 2: The − sign makes the opposite. Flip every tile: −3x + 6.

Step 3: Check with the integer rules. −3 × x = −3x. −3 × (−2) = +6.

So −3(x − 2) = −3x + 6.

An equation is a balance

An equation is like a balance. Both sides weigh the same.

Take 3 blocks off BOTH sides. It stays level.

Keeping the balance

Do the same thing to both sides.

Add or subtract the same number, or the same x-term.

Multiply or divide both sides by the same number (not 0).

The new equation is an equivalent equation. It has the same solution.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Do it to one side only. 2x + 3 = 11, so 2x = 11."

Why it is wrong: The left side lost 3 blocks. The right side did not. Now the right side is heavier.

What is true: Take 3 from both sides. 2x + 3 − 3 = 11 − 3, so 2x = 8.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Move 2x across and keep its sign: 4x + 2x + 1 = 7."

Why it is wrong: Start with 4x + 1 = 2x + 7. The 2x left the right side. So 2x must come off the left side too. The term leaves one side as its opposite: −2x.

What is true: 4x − 2x + 1 = 2x − 2x + 7. So 2x + 1 = 7, 2x = 6 and x = 3. Check: 4(3) + 1 = 13 and 2(3) + 7 = 13. ✓

Verify a solution

Substitute the solution into the original equation. Work out each side. They must be equal.

Is x = 4 the solution of 5x + 2 = 3x + 10?

Left: 5(4) + 2 = . Right: 3(4) + 10 = . They match. ✓

Verify
  1. 1. Substitute the solution into the ORIGINAL equation.
  2. 2. Work out the left side. Work out the right side.
  3. 3. Equal? It is the solution. Not equal? Look for a mistake.

Checking each equivalent equation shows WHERE a mistake is.

Watch out — a common mistake

"x works in my last equation, so it must be right."

Why it is wrong: Leo solved 3(x + 2) = 2x + 9. His first step was 3x + 2 = 2x + 9. Then x = 7.

x = 7 works in 3x + 2 = 2x + 9. Both sides are 23. But check the original: 3(7 + 2) = 27 and 2(7) + 9 = 23.

What is true: The mistake is in the first step. 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6. Then 3x + 6 = 2x + 9, so x = 3. Check: 3(3 + 2) = 15 and 2(3) + 9 = 15. ✓
