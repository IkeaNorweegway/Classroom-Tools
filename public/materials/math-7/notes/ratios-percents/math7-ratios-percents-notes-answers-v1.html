Section 1 · Equivalent ratios

Learning target: I can make equal ratios by multiplying or dividing both terms.

What is a ratio?

Mia makes juice. She mixes 1 can of concentrate with 3 cans of water.

The ratio of concentrate to water is 1:3. Say "1 to 3."

The order matters. Water to concentrate is 3:1.

Terms can be whole numbers, decimals or fractions.

The first term can be smaller or bigger than the second.

Equivalent ratios

Multiply both terms by the same number. 1:3 = 4:12 (× 4).

Divide both terms by the same number. 4:12 = 1:3 (÷ 4).

The number you use is the factor.

Watch out — a common mistake

"To make an equal ratio, add the same number to both terms. 2:3 = 4:5."

Why it is wrong: Adding changes the mix. 2 batches of 2:3 need 6 cans of water, not 5.

What is true: 2:3 = 4:6. Multiply both terms by 2.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Ava's bracelet has 2 red beads to 3 blue beads. So 2 out of 3 beads are red."

Why it is wrong: 2:3 is part to part. The whole is 2 + 3 = 5 beads.

What is true: 2 out of 5 beads are red. Red is 2/5 of the bracelet.

Worked Example 1 — Juice for the team

Sam's juice mix is 2 cans of concentrate to 5 cans of water. Make 2, 3 and 4 batches.

Step 1: Multiply both terms by 2: 2:5 = 4:10.

Step 2: Multiply both terms by 3: 2:5 = 6:15.

Step 3: Multiply both terms by 4: 2:5 = 8:20.

Explain it Why must Sam multiply BOTH terms by the same number?
Answer So every batch has the same mix. If only one term changes, the juice tastes different.
Worked Example 2 — A smaller batch of bannock

Lily's big bannock recipe uses 12 cups of flour and 4 tsp of baking powder. She wants a smaller batch.

Step 1: The ratio of flour to baking powder is 12:4.

Step 2: Divide both terms by 2: 12:4 = 6:2.

Step 3: Divide both terms by 4: 12:4 = 3:1.

Dividing both terms is called partitioning. Multiplying both terms is iterating.

Worked Example 3 — Canola and wheat

Kai's farm has 4 ha of canola for every 6 ha of wheat. His neighbour has 6 ha of canola and 9 ha of wheat. Find the factor.

Step 1: Divide to find the factor. 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. Check the other term: 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5.

Step 2: Both terms use the same factor. So 4:6 = 6:9.

Step 3: The factor 1.5 is the same as 3/2. A factor can be a decimal or a fraction.

Explain it In Worked Example 3, why is the factor bigger than 1?
Answer The neighbour's numbers are bigger. Going from 4 to 6 means multiplying by more than 1.

Section 2 · Missing terms and comparing ratios

Learning target: I can find a missing term and compare two ratios.

Find a missing term

Sam's juice is 2:5. He has 8 cans of concentrate. How much water does he need?

2 × 4 = 8. So multiply 5 by the same factor: 5 × 4 = 20.

2:5 = 8:20. Sam needs 20 cans of water.

Worked Example 1 — Fans at Rogers Place

One section at Rogers Place has Oilers fans and Flames fans. The ratio is 5:2. There are 40 Flames fans. How many Oilers fans are there?

Step 1: The Flames term goes from 2 to 40. The factor is 40 ÷ 2 = 20.

Step 2: Use the same factor: 5 × 20 = 100.

Step 3: 5:2 = 100:40. There are 100 Oilers fans.

Worked Example 2 — When the factor isn't a whole number

Kai plants 4 bags of canola seed on 6 ha. How many hectares can 10 bags plant?

Bags4210
Hectares6315

Step 1: Divide both terms by 2: 4:6 = 2:3.

Step 2: Multiply both terms by 5: 2:3 = 10:15.

Step 3: 10 bags can plant 15 ha.

Explain it In Worked Example 2, why did we divide first?
Answer 4 does not go evenly into 10. Going down to 2 bags first makes the factor a whole number.

Compare with a common term

Juice A is 3:5. Juice B is 4:5 (concentrate to water). Which is stronger?

Both use 5 cans of water. B has more concentrate. B is stronger.

Comparing two ratios
  1. 1. Make the first terms match, or make the second terms match.
  2. 2. Then compare the other terms.
Watch out — a common mistake

"3:7 is stronger than 2:4, because 3 cans is more than 2 cans."

Why it is wrong: 3:7 also has more water. Make a common term first.

What is true: Both have 6 cans of concentrate. 2:4 has less water. So 2:4 is stronger.

Worked Example 3 — Which juice is stronger?

Noor's juice is 2:3. Omar's juice is 3:5. Which is stronger?

Step 1: Make the first terms match. 2 × 3 = 6 and 3 × 2 = 6.

Step 2: 2:3 = 6:9. 3:5 = 6:10.

Step 3: Same concentrate. Noor uses less water. Noor's juice is stronger.

Cross products: a shortcut

A ratio a:b can be written as a fraction: a/b.

Equivalent ratios make equal fractions: 2/3 = 4/6.

Multiply across: 2 × 6 = 12 and 3 × 4 = 12. They match!

Cross products

a/b = c/d is the same as a × d = b × c.

Use it to check two ratios. Use it to find a missing term.

Section 3 · Percents, including less than 1% and more than 100%

Learning target: I can find percents of numbers, even below 1% and above 100%.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
percenta ratio out of 100. % means "out of 100."45% = 45:100 = 45/100
benchmarka friendly percent you can use to build others1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%
100%one whole100% of 30 is 30

A percent is a ratio out of 100

45 out of 100 squares are shaded.

That is 45:100, or 45/100, or 0.45, or 45%.

To change a ratio to a percent, make the second term 100.

3 out of 4 seats are full. 3/4 is 75 out of 100. That is 75%.

You can also multiply by 100: 3/4 × 100 = 75. So 75% are full.

Worked Example 1 — Mini donuts at the Stampede

60 people wait in line at the Calgary Stampede. 35% of them buy mini donuts. How many people is that?

Step 1: Build 35% from benchmarks: 35% = 25% + 10%.

Step 2: 25% of 60 = 60 ÷ 4 = 15. 10% of 60 = 60 ÷ 10 = 6.

Step 3: Add them: 15 + 6 = 21. So 21 people buy mini donuts.

Explain it Why can we add 25% and 10% to get 35%?
Answer 25 out of 100 plus 10 out of 100 is 35 out of 100. The parts add up.
Worked Example 2 — 15% of $40

Find 15% of $40.

Step 1: 10% of $40 = $4.

Step 2: 5% is half of 10%: $2.

Step 3: 15% = 10% + 5% = $4 + $2 = $6.

Less than 1%

0.5% means half of 1%. It is half of one small square.

0.5% = 0.5/100 = 0.005.

To find 0.5% of 400: 1% of 400 = 4. Half of 4 is 2.

Watch out — a common mistake

"0.5% is the same as 50%." Or: "0.5% is the same as 0.5."

Why it is wrong: 0.5% is less than 1%. It is not even one small square of the grid.

What is true: 0.5% is half of 1%. As a decimal, 0.5% = 0.005. 0.5% of 400 is 2.

More than 100%

100% is one whole. 150% is one and a half wholes.

150% of 40 = 100% + 50% = 40 + 20 = 60.

Watch out — a common mistake

"A percent can't be more than 100%."

Why it is wrong: 100% is one whole. You can have more than one whole.

What is true: A plant was 30 cm tall. Now it is 200% of that height: 60 cm.

Worked Example 3 — 250% of 20

Zoe had 20 hockey cards. Now she has 250% as many. How many does she have?

Step 1: 250% = 100% + 100% + 50%.

Step 2: 20 + 20 + 10 = 50.

Step 3: She has 50 cards. That is more than 20, because 250% is more than one whole.

Solve a problem
Worked Example 1 — A hoodie on sale

An Oilers hoodie costs $60. It is 25% off. Find the sale price.

Step 1: The discount is 25% of $60 = $60 ÷ 4 = $15.

Step 2: Sale price: $60 − $15 = $45.

Step 3: Check: 100% − 25% = 75%. 75% of $60 is $45. ✓

Worked Example 2 — GST on a cap

A Flames cap costs $28. Add 5% GST. Find the total.

Step 1: 10% of $28 = $2.80.

Step 2: 5% is half of 10%: $1.40.

Step 3: Total: $28 + $1.40 = $29.40.

Worked Example 3 — Ticket prices go up

An Oilers ticket cost $80. The price goes up by 10%. Find the new price.

Step 1: 10% of $80 = $8.

Step 2: Up means add: $80 + $8 = $88.

Step 3: The new price is 110% of the old price.

If the price went down 10%, it would be $80 − $8 = $72.

Watch out — a common mistake

"20% off, then add 20%, gets you back to the start."

Why it is wrong: The second 20% is 20% of a smaller number.

What is true: $100 − $20 = $80. Then 20% of $80 is $16. $80 + $16 = $96, not $100.

Worked Example 4 — Bannock for more people

Tess's bannock for 6 people uses 3 cups of flour. How much flour for 10 people?

Step 1: Find the flour for 2 people: 6:3 = 2:1 (÷ 3).

Step 2: Multiply by 5: 2:1 = 10:5.

Step 3: She needs 5 cups of flour.

Explain it In Worked Example 4, why did Tess find the flour for 2 people first?
Answer 6 does not go evenly into 10. 2 goes into both 6 and 10.
Worked Example 5 — The better buy

A 2 L jug of apple juice costs $3.00. A 3 L jug costs $4.20. Which is the better buy?

Step 1: Find the unit rate. $3.00 ÷ 2 = $1.50 per litre.

Step 2: $4.20 ÷ 3 = $1.40 per litre.

Step 3: The 3 L jug costs less for each litre. It is the better buy.

Worked Example 6 — Changing units

1 m = 100 cm. The ratio of metres to centimetres is 1:100. Change 250 cm to metres.

Step 1: 250 cm ÷ 100 = 2.5.

Step 2: 250 cm = 2.5 m.

1 L = 1000 mL too. So 750 mL = 750 ÷ 1000 = 0.75 L.

Put it all together

From memory — notes closed
  1. Juice A is 2 cans of concentrate to 7 cans of water. Juice B is 3:10.
    1. a) Make three equivalent ratios for Juice A.
    2. b) Which juice is stronger?
    Answer a) 4:14, 6:21, 8:28 (any × both terms).
    b) Make the first terms match: 2:7 = 6:21 and 3:10 = 6:20. B has less water. B is stronger.
  2. A section at Rogers Place has 400 seats. 0.5% of the seats are broken.
    1. a) How many seats are broken?
    2. b) The next section holds 150% as many seats. How many seats is that?
    Answer a) 1% of 400 = 4. Half of 4 is 2. So 2 seats are broken.
    b) 150% of 400 = 400 + 200 = 600 seats.
  3. A $90 jacket is 20% off. Then 5% GST is added. Find the total.
    Answer 20% of $90 = $18. $90 − $18 = $72. GST: 10% of $72 = $7.20, so 5% = $3.60. Total $75.60.
  4. Bannock for 8 people uses 4 cups of flour. How much for 6 people? Show it two ways.
    Answer Ratio table: 8:4 = 2:1 = 6:3. 3 cups.
    Cross products: 8/4 = 6/?. 8 × ? = 4 × 6 = 24. ? = 3 cups.
  5. Ben says 120% of 50 is less than 50. Explain his mistake.
    Answer 120% is more than one whole. 100% of 50 is 50, and 20% more is 10. So 120% of 50 is 60.
