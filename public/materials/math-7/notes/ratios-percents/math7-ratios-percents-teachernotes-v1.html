Teacher notes

Ratios & Percents

Grade 7 Mathematics · Number · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcome 7N5 · Codes RP1–RP4

Overview

Big idea: equivalent ratios are multiplicative. You make them by multiplying both terms by the same factor (iterating) or dividing both terms by the same factor (partitioning), never by adding the same number. Every later skill uses that one idea: a missing term is found with the factor, two ratios are compared once one term matches, and a percent is just a ratio whose second term is 100. Percent of a number is built from benchmarks (50%, 25%, 10%, 5%, 1%), which also gives percents below 1% (half of 1%) and above 100% (one whole plus a part). Real problems (discounts, GST, increase and decrease, recipes, unit rates, unit changes) are all the same move in context.

Sequence: ratio as part-to-part and part-to-whole, terms that are whole numbers, decimals and fractions, first term smaller or bigger; ratio tables, tape diagrams and double number lines; iterating and partitioning; the factor as a whole number, a decimal or a fraction (RP1) → missing terms with the factor, going down to a "unit" ratio when the factor isn't whole, adding two equivalent ratios, comparing with a common first or second term, and only then cross products a/b = c/d ⇔ ad = bc (RP2) → percent as a ratio out of 100, ratio to percent (make the second term 100, or × 100), benchmarks, combining benchmarks (35% = 25% + 10%), 0.5% and other percents below 1%, 150% and 250% (RP3) → discounts, GST 5%, percent increase and decrease, the "20% off then 20% on" trap, scaling recipes, unit rates and best buys, cm ↔ m and mL ↔ L (RP4).

Materials: two-colour counters or linking cubes (ratio models), a can of frozen juice concentrate and a jug (the 1:3 opener; or just the empty can), hundred grids (paper or a projected grid), strips of paper for tape diagrams, a metre stick, a 1 L measuring jug and a 250 mL cup, store flyers or receipts showing GST, calculators for RP4 unit rates.

Depth ceiling: two-term ratios only. Tables, tapes and double number lines come before ad = bc; ad = bc is a checking and finding tool, not an algebra lesson (students find ? from "4 × ? = 60" by a fact, not by formal solving). Percent of a natural number by benchmarks; percents below 1% and above 100%. GST 5%, no PST in Alberta. Do not use "constant of proportionality" or k (Grade 8): say "the factor" or "times ". Finding a percent change from two amounts appears only as a ★★ question.

Reading level: student pages are written at about Grade 3–4. The math words (ratio, term, part-to-part, part-to-whole, equivalent ratios, ratio table, factor, iterating, partitioning, unknown term, common term, cross products, percent, benchmark, discount, sale price, GST, percent increase, percent decrease, unit rate, scale) are the learning; teach each with a picture on the board.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Equivalent ratios · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1 (equal fractions) and Q2 (red to blue) predict RP1; Q4 and Q5 preview percents and benchmarks.
SHOWThe juice can and jug. 1 can of concentrate, 3 cans of water. Draw the tape: 1 box and 3 boxes. "Concentrate to water is 1:3. Water to concentrate?"
SAY"A ratio compares two amounts. Order matters. Terms can be whole numbers, decimals or fractions, and the first term can be bigger." Read the ratio table of kinds.
DOTwo batches. Students build 2:6 with cubes, then fill the 1:3 ratio table to 4:12. Draw the double number line and the × 4 arrow.
WATCHAdding to both terms. First misconception box: one batch of 2:3, then two batches, 4:6. "Which one is 4:5? Nobody made that."
WATCHPart-to-whole. Second misconception box (the bracelet): count all 5 beads.
DOWorked Example 1 (Sam's 2:5 juice, iterating to 8:20). Worked Example 2 (12:4 bannock down to 3:1, partitioning). Name the two moves: iterating and partitioning.
CUEWorked Example 3 (canola 4:6 → 6:9): find the factor by dividing new ÷ old, and check it on BOTH terms. The factor 1.5 is the same as 3/2.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Missing terms and comparing ratios · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: 3:4 = 9:?, 12:18 ÷ 6, 2/3 × 6, 12 ÷ 0.5.
SHOWSam's 2:5 juice on a double number line. "He has 8 cans of concentrate. What do we multiply by? Do the same to the water."
DOWorked Example 1 (Rogers Place fans, 5:2 with 40 Flames fans: tape with 20 in each box). Worked Example 2 (canola: 4 bags to 6 ha, 10 bags). Go down to 2:3 first.
SAY"If the factor isn't a whole number, go down to a smaller ratio first, then up."
DOAdding ratios: 2:3 + 4:6 = 6:9. "One batch-pair plus another batch-pair is still the same mix."
SHOWTwo juice tapes, 3:5 and 4:5. "Same water. Which tastes stronger?"
WATCHComparing first terms only. Misconception box: 3:7 vs 2:4, rebuilt as 6:14 and 6:12.
DOWorked Example 3 (Noor 2:3 vs Omar 3:5, matched at 6). Then cross products: 2/3 = 4/6 and 2 × 6 = 3 × 4. Show the flow chart finds 15 again.
CUECross products are a check, not the first move. Students write the table first.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2. Quiz next class (RP1–RP2).
Lesson 3 — Percents, including less than 1% and more than 100% · 60 min
DOMid-unit quiz (25 min) first. Then the lesson.
SHOWA hundred grid with 45 shaded. "45 out of 100: 45:100, 45/100, 0.45, 45%."
SAY"A percent is a ratio out of 100. To change 3 out of 4 to a percent, make the second term 100: 75 out of 100."
DOThe benchmark table for 80. Students say how to get each one (÷ 2, ÷ 4, ÷ 10, half of 10%, ÷ 100).
DOWorked Example 1 (Stampede line: 35% of 60 = 25% + 10% = 21) and Worked Example 2 (15% of $40 = 10% + 5% = $6).
SHOWZoom into one square of the hundred grid and shade half: 0.5%. "Find 1% first. Then halve it."
WATCH0.5% read as 50% or 0.5. Misconception box: the half-shaded grid is 50%; 0.5% is half of ONE square.
SHOWTwo grids with 150 shaded. "100% is one whole. 150% is one and a half wholes."
WATCH"A percent can't be over 100%." Misconception box with the plant at 200%. Worked Example 3 (250% of 20 hockey cards = 50).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3.
Lesson 4 — Percent and ratio problems · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: 25% of 48, 0.5% of 400, 5:2 = ?:10, 150% of 30.
SHOWThe problem flow chart: read what is compared, draw a table, tape or number line, solve, check.
DOWorked Example 1 (hoodie, 25% off $60 = $45, checked as 75% of $60). Worked Example 2 (GST on a $28 cap: 10% then half = $1.40, total $29.40).
SAY"Alberta has GST, 5%, and no provincial sales tax. Find 10% and halve it."
DOWorked Example 3 (Oilers ticket $80 up 10% = $88; down 10% = $72).
WATCH"20% off then 20% on." Misconception box: $100 → $80 → $96. Ask "20% of what?"
DOWorked Example 4 (bannock for 6 → 10 people via 2:1), Worked Example 5 (2 L for $3.00 vs 3 L for $4.20: $1.50 vs $1.40 per litre), Worked Example 6 (250 cm = 2.5 m; 750 mL = 0.75 L).
CUECalculators are fine for unit rates like $4.20 ÷ 3, but students say the unit ("dollars per litre") and estimate first.
DOCore worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4.

Assessment

Mid-unit quiz (23 marks)

RP1 (Q1–4), RP2 (Q5–9). Given at the start of Lesson 3, after equivalent ratios and missing terms. Q9 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need a common term (12:20 vs 12:21, or a common second term 21:35 vs 20:35) and a conclusion, not just "A has less water." Accept unit-ratio reasoning (water per can of concentrate: 1.67 vs 1.75) as an equivalent method. On Q2 accept any valid route to 15 (× 2.5, or 2:3 × 5).

Worksheets and exits

Core (★) covers RP1–RP4 with a double number line, tape, percent bar, hundred grid, receipt or flow chart on most questions. Challenge (★★) adds part-part-whole totals (2:5 juice, 21 cans), ordering three ratios, cross products with decimals, 150% then 0.5% in one problem, best buys, a percent change found from two prices, and recipe scaling with fractions and mL ↔ L. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it.

Data and contexts used

  • Juice concentrate at 1:3 is the usual frozen-juice instruction (1 can concentrate to 3 cans water). The 2:5, 3:5 and 4:5 mixes are made up for comparison.
  • Rogers Place seating ratios (5:2, 3:2, 4:3 Oilers to Flames fans; a 300- or 400-seat section) are made up. Rogers Place seats about 18 000 for hockey.
  • Oilers and Flames ticket, hoodie, cap and jersey prices ($80 ticket, $60/$80 hoodie, $28/$40 cap, $60 jersey) are made-up round numbers, lower than real team-store prices.
  • Calgary Stampede mini-donut line (35% of 60 people) and ride counts (400 riders, 150%, 0.5% prize winners) are made up.
  • Canola and wheat hectares (4:6, 6:9) and 4 bags of seed for 6 ha are made up and not realistic seeding rates; they are there for the ratio.
  • Bannock amounts (12 cups flour : 4 tsp baking powder; 3 cups for 6 people) are made up and kept simple. Use family or community recipes if students bring them.
  • Apple juice prices ($3.00 for 2 L, $4.20 for 3 L) are made up.
  • GST is 5% in Alberta; there is no PST. That is current as of 2026.

Connections

  • Fractions & Decimals (topic 4): scaling a recipe by 1.5 or 3/2 is fraction and decimal multiplication; unit rates are decimal division. Retrieval in each section mixes these back in.
  • Positive & Negative Numbers (topic 1): fraction–decimal–percent links (1/4 = 0.25 = 25%; 0.5% = 0.005).
  • Probability (topic 12): probabilities are written as ratios, fractions, decimals and percents.
  • Circles (topic 9): C ÷ d ≈ 3.14 is a ratio that is the same for every circle.
  • Grade 8: proportional relationships and the constant of proportionality (k), similarity and scale factors, slope as a rate.
