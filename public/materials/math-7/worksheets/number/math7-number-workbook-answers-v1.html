Number

Strand Workbook — Answer Key

Answer key · Teacher copy
Every card says which topic and notes section must be taught before the question opens. The student copy has no topic labels: tell students which questions are open each day. Misconception tags mark the questions built to catch a common mistake.
1★ Core
Opens after: PN1 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 1

Use the number line.

  1. a) Find |−6|, |6| and |−2.5|.
  2. b) Name two numbers that are 4 steps from 0.
Answer a) |−6| = 6, |6| = 6, |−2.5| = 2.5. Each one is a distance from 0.
b) 4 and −4.
Misconception target

"|−5| = −5" or "absolute value makes it negative." Have the student count the steps from 0. A distance is never negative.

2★ Core
Opens after: IN1 — Integers, Section 1

At 7 am it is −5 °C in Calgary. By noon it is 2 degrees colder.

Sam writes (−5) + (−2) = 7. "Two negatives make a positive."

Use the number line. Find the right answer.

Answer (−5) + (−2) = −7. Start at −5 and move 2 to the left. It is −7 °C at noon.
"Two negatives make a positive" is a rule for × and ÷. It is not for adding.
Misconception target

"Two negatives make a positive, so −3 + (−4) = 7." Ask: did the temperature go up or down?

3★ Core
Opens after: SC1 — Squares & Cubes, Section 1

Mia says 6² = 12. Look at the squares of tiles.

  1. a) Fill in the number of tiles in each square.
  2. b) Is Mia right? Explain.
Answer a) 3² = 9, 6² = 36, 8² = 64.
b) No. 6² = 6 × 6 = 36. Mia did 6 × 2.
Misconception target

"5² = 10." Have the student draw the square and count the tiles.

4★ Core
Opens after: FD1 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 1

A pan has 3/4 of the bannock left. Ava eats 2/3 of what is left.

  1. a) Use the area model. Find 2/3 × 3/4.
  2. b) Is the answer more or less than 3/4? Why?
Answer a) 6 of 12 parts overlap. 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2 of the pan.
b) Less. Ava eats only part of the 3/4. Part of something is less than all of it.
Misconception target

"Multiplying always makes a number bigger." Point to the overlap: it sits inside the 3/4 strip.

5★ Core
Opens after: RP1 — Ratios & Percents, Section 1

Noor mixes 2 cans of concentrate with 3 cans of water.

  1. a) Write the ratio of concentrate to water.
  2. b) How many cans are in the jug?
  3. c) What fraction of the jug is concentrate?
Answer a) 2:3. b) 5 cans. c) 2/5.
Misconception target

"2:3 means 2 out of 3." Have the student count every box in the tape: 2 parts and 3 parts make 5.

6★ Core
Opens after: PN3 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 3

Kai writes 1/4 = 1.4. The grid shows 1/4.

  1. a) How many hundredths are shaded?
  2. b) Write 1/4 as a decimal.
  3. c) Is Kai right? Explain.
Answer a) 25 hundredths. b) 1/4 = 0.25.
c) No. 1/4 means 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25. 1.4 is more than 1, but 1/4 is less than 1.
Misconception target

"1/4 = 1.4." The fraction bar means divide. Ask: is 1/4 more or less than one whole?

7★ Core
Opens after: IN2 — Integers, Section 2

At night it is −3 °C. At noon it is 4 °C.

Ben says subtracting always gives a smaller number.

  1. a) Use the chips. Find 4 − (−3).
  2. b) Is Ben right? Explain.
Answer a) Add 3 zero pairs. Take away the 3 negative chips. 7 positive chips are left: 4 − (−3) = 7. Noon is 7 °C warmer.
b) No. 7 is bigger than 4. Taking away a negative makes it bigger: 4 − (−3) = 4 + 3.
Misconception target

"Subtracting always makes it smaller." The chip mat shows the answer growing.

8★ Core
Opens after: SC2 — Squares & Cubes, Section 2

Raj says √16 = 8.

  1. a) Find √16, √81 and √144.
  2. b) Check one answer with multiplication.
Answer a) √16 = 4, √81 = 9, √144 = 12.
b) 4 × 4 = 16 ✓. Raj took half of 16. But 8 × 8 = 64, not 16.
Misconception target

"√16 = 8" (taking half). Ask: what number times itself is 16? Check with the side of the square.

9★ Core
Opens after: FD2 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 2

Zoe has 4 cups of oats. One batch of muffins uses 2/3 cup.

Zoe thinks the answer must be less than 4.

  1. a) How many batches can she make? Use the picture.
  2. b) Why is the answer more than 4?
Answer a) 6 batches of 2/3 fit in 4 cups. 4 ÷ 2/3 = 6.
b) Each batch is less than 1 cup. So more than 4 batches fit.
Misconception target

"Dividing always makes a number smaller." Count the 2/3 pieces in the picture together.

10★ Core
Opens after: RP3 — Ratios & Percents, Section 3

200 people ride the Ferris wheel on Monday.

  1. a) Find 1% of 200.
  2. b) Find 0.5% of 200.
  3. c) Tuesday has 150% of Monday's riders. How many is that?
Answer a) 1% of 200 = 200 ÷ 100 = 2.
b) 0.5% is half of 1%. Half of 2 is 1 rider.
c) 100% is 200 and 50% is 100. So 150% = 200 + 100 = 300 riders.
11★ Core
Opens after: PN2 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 2

Mark each number on the number line.

  1. a) −1.5
  2. b) −3/4, 3/−4 and −(3/4)
  3. c) Lily says −1.5 is between −1 and 0. Is she right?
Answer a) −1.5 is half way from −1 to −2.
b) All three sit at the same point, −0.75. One negative sign anywhere gives the same number.
c) No. −1.5 is left of −1. It is between −2 and −1.
Misconception target

Two targets. "−1.5 is between −1 and 0": have the student count the quarter steps left from 0. "−3/4, 3/−4 and −(3/4) are three different numbers": they must land on one point.

12★ Core
Opens after: SC3 — Squares & Cubes, Section 3

Omar says 3³ = 9.

  1. a) How many small cubes make the big cube?
  2. b) Find ∛27 and ∛64.
  3. c) What did Omar do wrong?
Answer a) 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 small cubes.
b) ∛27 = 3, because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27. ∛64 = 4, because 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.
c) He did 3 × 3. That is one layer. 3³ means three 3s multiplied: three layers of 9.
Misconception target

"2³ = 6" or "∛27 = 9." Have the student build or count the layers. Watch for ∛27 = 9 in part b.

13★ Core
Opens after: FD3 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 3

Leo says 0.3 × 0.4 = 1.2. Use the hundred grid.

  1. a) How many small squares overlap?
  2. b) Find 0.3 × 0.4.
  3. c) How do you know Leo is wrong?
Answer a) 12 of 100 small squares.
b) 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12. (3 × 4 = 12, and there are two decimal places in all.)
c) Both numbers are less than 1. So the answer must be less than each one. 1.2 is more than 1.
Misconception target

"0.3 × 0.2 = 0.6." The overlap is hundredths, not tenths. Count the decimal places in both factors.

14★★ Extended
Opens after: IN3 — Integers, Section 3

Ava says (−2) × 5 = 10. "5 is the bigger number, and it is positive."

  1. a) Use the counters. Find (−2) × 5.
  2. b) Find (−2) × (−2) × (−2). Count the negatives first.
Answer a) −10. There is one negative. One is odd, so the answer is negative.
b) Three negatives: odd, so negative. (−2) × (−2) = 4, then 4 × (−2) = −8.
Misconception target

"The answer takes the sign of the bigger number" (for ×). That habit comes from adding. For × and ÷, count the negatives.

15★★ Extended
Opens after: FD1 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 1

Tess finds 2/5 × 3/4. She writes 8/20 × 15/20 = 120/20 = 6.

  1. a) Is 6 a sensible answer? Explain.
  2. b) Find the right answer.
  3. c) Do you need a common denominator to multiply?
Answer a) No. Both fractions are less than 1. So the answer must be less than 1.
b) Multiply tops: 2 × 3 = 6. Multiply bottoms: 5 × 4 = 20. 6/20 = 3/10.
c) No. Common denominators are for + and −. To multiply, multiply tops and multiply bottoms.
Misconception target

"You need a common denominator to multiply fractions." Tess also kept the denominator, as in adding. The area model shows 6 of 20 parts.

16★★ Extended
Opens after: RP2 — Ratios & Percents, Section 2

Omar's juice is 2 cans of concentrate to 5 cans of water. Mia's is 3 cans to 8 cans.

  1. a) Omar uses 8 cans of concentrate. How much water does he need?
  2. b) Whose juice is stronger? Make the first terms the same.
Answer a) 2 × 4 = 8, so 5 × 4 = 20 cans of water.
b) Omar: 2:5 = 6:15 (× 3). Mia: 3:8 = 6:16 (× 2). Same concentrate, but Omar uses less water. Omar's juice is stronger.
17★★ Extended
Opens after: FD2 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 2

Ben finds 3/4 ÷ 3/8. He writes 4/3 × 3/8 = 12/24 = 1/2.

  1. a) Use the picture. How many 3/8 pieces fit in 3/4?
  2. b) What did Ben do wrong? Fix his steps.
Answer a) 2 pieces.
b) He flipped the first fraction. Keep 3/4. Flip 3/8. 3/4 × 8/3 = 24/12 = 2.
Misconception target

"To divide, flip the first fraction." The picture gives 2, so 1/2 cannot be right.

18★★ Extended
Opens after: PN4 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 4

Morning lows: Edmonton −8 °C, Calgary −3 °C, Jasper −12.5 °C, Lethbridge 0.5 °C.

  1. a) Noor says −8 °C is warmer than −3 °C, because 8 > 3. Is she right?
  2. b) Order the four lows from coldest to warmest.
  3. c) Omar says 0.125 > 0.5 because it has more digits. Is he right?
Answer a) No. −8 is further left (lower) than −3. So −8 °C is colder: −8 < −3.
b) −12.5, −8, −3, 0.5.
c) No. Line up the place values: 0.500 > 0.125. 0.5 is bigger.
Misconception target

Two targets. "−8 is bigger than −3 because 8 > 3": point to the thermometer. "A longer decimal is bigger": add zeros so both have three places.

19★★ Extended
Opens after: FD4 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 4

Leo says 2 + 3 × 4 = 20.

  1. a) Find the right answer.
  2. b) Find (−6) + 8 ÷ (−2).
  3. c) Find 1/2 × (5 − 1) + 0.5.
Answer a) 3 × 4 = 12 first. 2 + 12 = 14.
b) 8 ÷ (−2) = −4 first. (−6) + (−4) = −10.
c) 5 − 1 = 4. 1/2 × 4 = 2. 2 + 0.5 = 2.5.
Misconception target

"Work left to right, always" (2 + 3 × 4 = 20). Have the student circle the × or ÷ before doing anything.

20★★ Extended
Opens after: SC4 — Squares & Cubes, Section 4

Kai says a square with 4 cm sides has area = perimeter. "Both are 16."

  1. a) Is he right? Use the units.
  2. b) A square garden has an area of 64 m². How much fence goes around it?
Answer a) No. The numbers match, but the units do not. Perimeter is 16 cm (a length). Area is 16 cm² (squares).
b) Side = √64 = 8 m. Fence = 4 × 8 = 32 m.
Misconception target

"Area and perimeter are the same thing." Perimeter is the fence (m). Area is the grass inside (m²).

21★★ Extended
Opens after: FD3 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 3

Mia has 4.5 m of ribbon. She cuts it into pieces 0.5 m long.

She says 4.5 ÷ 0.5 = 2.25.

  1. a) How many pieces does she get? Use the picture.
  2. b) Multiply both numbers by 10. Check your answer.
Answer a) 9 pieces. Each metre makes 2 pieces, and the half metre makes 1 more.
b) 4.5 ÷ 0.5 = 45 ÷ 5 = 9. Mia took half of 4.5. That is ÷ 2, not ÷ 0.5.
Misconception target

"6 ÷ 0.5 = 3." Ask: how many halves are in 4.5?

22★★ Extended
Opens after: RP1 — Ratios & Percents, Section 1

Lily's pancakes use 2 cups of flour for 3 eggs. For a bigger batch she writes 4:5.

  1. a) Is 4:5 equal to 2:3? Explain.
  2. b) Fill in the double number line.
Answer a) No. She added 2 to both. Equal ratios multiply both terms: 2:3 = 4:6 (× 2).
b) 4 cups: 6 eggs. 6 cups: 9 eggs.
Misconception target

"To make an equal ratio, add the same number to both terms." In 2:3 there are 1.5 eggs per cup. In 4:5 there are only 1.25.

23★★ Extended
Opens after: RP4 + FD3 — Ratios & Percents and Decimals

A jacket at West Edmonton Mall costs $80. It is 25% off. Then add 5% GST.

  1. a) Fill in the receipt.
  2. b) Sam says this is the same as 20% off. Check.
Answer a) 25% of 80 = 20, so the sale price is $60.00. 5% of 60 = 3, so GST is $3.00. Total $63.00.
b) No. 20% off $80 is $64.00. The 5% GST is taken of $60, not of $80.
24★★ Extended
Opens after: IN4 + FD4 — Integers and Order of Operations

The lows in Red Deer for 4 days were −9 °C, −5 °C, 2 °C and −4 °C.

  1. a) Write one expression for the mean. Use brackets.
  2. b) Find the mean low. Does it make sense?
Answer a) (−9 + (−5) + 2 + (−4)) ÷ 4.
b) The sum is −16. (−16) ÷ 4 = −4 °C. It sits between the lowest (−9) and the highest (2), so it makes sense.
Without brackets, only the −4 would be divided by 4.
