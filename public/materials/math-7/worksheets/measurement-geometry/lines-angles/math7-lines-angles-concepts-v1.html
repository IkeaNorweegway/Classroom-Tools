Lines, Angles & Congruence — Concept Reference

The key ideas on one page. Keep it beside you when you practise.

Lines, rays, segments, angles, polygons

Line CD: forever both ways. Ray AC: one endpoint, written first. Segment AB: two endpoints.

∠CAB or ∠A: the vertex goes in the middle. △ABC: a triangle.

A polygon is closed. Its 3 or more sides meet only at their ends.

Parallel and perpendicular

Matching arrowheads: parallel, m ∥ n. They never meet.

Small square: a right angle, m ⊥ n. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°.

Angles where lines cross

Opposite angles are congruent. Adjacent angles on a line add to 180°.

A transversal crosses two lines. Corresponding angles are in the same spot at each crossing.

Parallel lines: corresponding angles are equal. Equal corresponding angles show the lines are parallel.

Congruent polygons

Same size and same shape. Hash marks show equal sides. Arcs show equal angles.

△ABC ≅ △RST: A ↔ R, B ↔ S, C ↔ T. So AB ≅ RS and ∠ABC ≅ ∠RST.

MistakeFix
A ray goes both ways, or a line has endsA line has no ends. A ray has one. A segment has two.
Ray BA is the same as ray ABThe endpoint goes first. They point opposite ways.
The vertex can go anywhere in an angle's nameThe vertex goes in the middle: ∠CAB.
Lines that look parallel are parallelLook for arrowheads, or measure.
Opposite angles add to 180°Opposite angles are equal. Side by side on a line adds to 180°.
Corresponding angles are always equalOnly when the lines are parallel.
Letter order in △ABC ≅ △RST doesn't matterThe order shows the match: A ↔ R, B ↔ S, C ↔ T.
