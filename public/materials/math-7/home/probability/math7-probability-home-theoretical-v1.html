Finding a Probability

One skill: finding a probability as a fraction, decimal and percent · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 1: Outcomes, events and likelihood · Section 2: Theoretical probability

NameDate

For the adult: A probability is a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). When every outcome has the same chance: P = favourable outcomes ÷ all outcomes. It can be written as a fraction, a decimal or a percent: 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%.

Worked example

A bag has 3 red counters and 1 blue counter. Find P(red).

Step 1: All the counters: 3 + 1 = 4. Red ones: 3.
Step 2: P(red) = 3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 = 75%.

Your turn

2. Find P(roll a number less than 3). (Which faces match? How many faces in all?)

3. A bag has 2 red and 3 blue counters. Find P(blue) as a fraction, a decimal and a percent.

4. Impossible, unlikely, even chance, likely or certain? a) Roll a 0. b) Flip heads. c) Roll a number less than 6.

5. Ten cards are numbered 1 to 10. Find P(a card greater than 7).

6. Zoe says P(roll a 4) = 1/2, because "you roll a 4 or you don't." Is she right?

7. The chance of rain in Lethbridge is 0.6. Find P(no rain) as a percent.
