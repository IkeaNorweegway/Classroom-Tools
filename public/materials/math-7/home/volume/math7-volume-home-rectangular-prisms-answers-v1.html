Volume of a Box — Answers

One skill: volume of a box by layers, in cubic units · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the filled-in class notes: Section 1: Prisms and cylinders · Section 2: Volume by layers

For the adult: The most common slip is writing cm² for a volume. Volume counts cubes, so it is cm³. The second is thinking the base is always the face a box sits on. The bases are the two matching faces at opposite ends.

Answers

1. One layer: 3 × 2 = 6 cubes. 2 layers: 6 × 2 = 12 cm³.

2. 5 × 2 × 4 = 40 cm³.

3. One layer: 20 × 5 = 100. 30 layers: 100 × 30 = 3000 cm³.

4. 3 × 2 × 0.5 = 3 m³ of sand.

5. No. 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 is right, but volume counts cubes. It is 24 cm³.

6. A triangular prism. Its bases are the two triangles at the ends. It lies on a lateral face.

7. 50 × 20 × 30 = 30 000 cm³. 30 000 ÷ 1000 = 30 L.
