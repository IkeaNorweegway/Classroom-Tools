Samples & Statistics

Exit Tickets

For the teacher: print and cut. One ticket at the end of each section. Give 3–5 minutes. Read them before the next class and sort into three piles: got it, almost, not yet.

Exit ticket — Section 1 · Populations, samples and census

Name:Date:
Target: I can name the population for a question. I can say when to use a census or a sample. (ST1)

1. "How many hours do Grade 7 students in Lethbridge sleep?" Name the population.

2. Is the length of a walleye discrete or continuous data?

3. Mr. Lee wants the favourite lunch of his 24 students. Should he use a census or a sample? Explain how you know.

Exit ticket — Section 2 · Fair samples and random sampling

Name:Date:
Target: I can tell if a sample is fair. I can pick a sample at random two ways. (ST2)

1. A list has 50 names. You want a sample of 10. Find the jump. Start at 3 and list the picks.

2. Sam wants to know how many hours Grade 7 students play video games. He asks the 8 kids in his gaming club. Is his sample representative? Explain how you know.

Exit ticket — Section 3 · Mean, median, mode and range

Name:Date:
Target: I can find the mean, median, mode and range of a set of data. (ST3)

1. Find the mean, median, mode and range of 6, 2, 9, 4, 4.

2. Leo says the median is 9, because 9 is in the middle of the list. Explain how you know he is wrong.

Exit ticket — Section 4 · Comparing samples and drawing conclusions

Name:Date:
Target: I can compare two samples. I can use them to say something about the whole population. (ST4)

1. Two random samples of Grade 7 students give their number of pets. Find the mean of each sample.

2. Write a conclusion about all Grade 7 students.

3. The two means are different. Does that mean one sample is wrong? Explain how you know.
