Guiding Question: How can urbanization shift and shape a society?

Section 1: Patterns of Immigration — Push and Pull Factors

Blank / PromptExpected Answer
A push factor makes someone want to ___ their home countryleave
A pull factor makes a destination ___attractive / appealing
Displaced persons after WWII included ___Holocaust survivors (and other refugees who could not return home)
Postwar Canada had increasingly ___ demographicsurbanized
Canada's ___ reflected a more diverse populationurban centres / cities
A more diverse, ___ populationpluralistic

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. A push factor is a condition at home that drives someone to leave (e.g., war, persecution). A pull factor is a condition at the destination that draws them there (e.g., safety, jobs).
  2. Displaced persons were refugees — including Holocaust survivors — left homeless or unable to safely return home after WWII (1945–51); many were admitted to Canada.
  3. International migration made Canada's cities more urbanized and diverse/pluralistic than before the war.
  4. Sample: Push — persecution/loss of home; Pull — Canada offered safety and the chance to rebuild a life.

Section 2: Urbanization and the Growth of Industry

Blank / PromptExpected Answer

Growth of the ___ industry (Alberta)petroleum / oil and gas
Table: powers suburbs/factories via dammed riversHydroelectricity production
Table: drills/refines a resource central to Alberta's economyOil and gas exploration / petroleum industry
Table: builds roads, sewers, schools for a growing suburbPublic infrastructure development
Table: manufactures vehicles for a car-dependent populationAutomobile production

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. Urbanization is the process by which more of a population comes to live in cities rather than rural areas.
  2. Any three of: public infrastructure, oil and gas/petroleum, hydroelectric power, automobile and paper production.
  3. The petroleum industry created jobs, government revenue, and energy to support growing cities and industries.
  4. Sample: Renewable energy and urban planning fields are now responding to the pressures of urbanization the way oil and gas did in the 1950s — by trying to meet growing energy and infrastructure demand.

Section 3: Environmental Consequences of Urbanization

Blank / PromptExpected Answer
Farmland/wilderness converted into ___ and industrial sitessuburbs / urban development

Source analysis (sample): Population — postwar immigration and the baby boom made Canada's cities larger and more diverse. Economy — urbanization fed growth in oil and gas, hydro, and manufacturing. Environment — that same growth caused pollution, land division, and disrupted wildlife migration.

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. Pollution, land consumption, land division, wildlife migration changes.
  2. Sample (land division): As cities expand, large connected areas of farmland or wilderness get split into smaller parcels for roads and development, which can isolate ecosystems.
  3. Sample stance: Either position is acceptable if supported — e.g., "Yes, because the jobs and infrastructure improved quality of life for millions of postwar Canadians, even though environmental costs were real and serious."
