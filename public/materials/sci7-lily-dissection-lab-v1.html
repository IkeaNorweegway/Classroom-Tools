SafetyBefore You Touch the Lily

⚠️ Lab rules
  • Pollen stains. Lily pollen stains skin, clothes, and paper orange. Don't rub it. Lift it off with tape.
  • Allergies? Tell your teacher before you start. Gloves are available.
  • Don't eat any part of the lily.
  • Lilies are poisonous to cats, even the pollen. If you have a cat, don't take any lily parts home.
  • Cut away from your hand. Only your teacher cuts the ovary.
  • Wash your hands when you finish.

ReadThe Lily Trick

On most flowers the sepals are small and green. On a lily, the 3 sepals look almost exactly like the 3 petals. Botanists call all 6 of them tepals. You can still tell them apart. The sepals are the outer ring: they attach a little lower and further out, and they wrapped the outside of the bud. The petals are the inner ring: they are usually a little wider. All 6 tepals have a green nectar groove at the base.

ModelThe Parts of a Lily

Stamen = anther + filament. The male part. The anther makes pollen. A lily has 6.
Pistil = stigma + style + ovary. The female part. The sticky stigma catches pollen. The ovary holds ovules, which can become seeds.

Part ADissect: Outside In

Work from the outside of the flower to the centre. Lay each part on your tray in columns, like this model. Your tray should look like this when you finish.

StepsProcedure

  1. Look before you cut. Count the tepals and stamens. Find the pistil in the centre. Smell the flower.
  2. Pollen sample. Pollen Keeper: press a short piece of clear tape onto one anther and lift it off. Stick the tape in the pollen box on the next page.
  3. Sepals. Find the 3 tepals in the outer ring. Pull each one gently down and off at its base. Find the green nectar groove at the base. Lay them in column 1.
  4. Petals. Remove the 3 inner tepals. Compare their width to the sepals. Lay them in column 2.
  5. Stamens. Pinch each filament at its base and pull. Hold it by the filament, not the anther. Lay all 6 in column 3.
  6. Pistil. Only the pistil is left. Snip it off at the very bottom of the ovary. Lay it in column 4.
  7. Hand lens. Look closely at the stigma and one anther. Record on the next page.
  8. Teacher station. Bring your pistil to your teacher, who will cut a thin slice across the ovary. Look at the slice with the hand lens and sketch it.
  9. Keep everything. Don't throw anything away. You will press these parts in Part B.

Part BPress: The Archival Sandwich

Pressing flattens each part and pulls the water out of it slowly, so it dries flat instead of shrivelling. The parts sit between two sheets of acid-free archival paper. Blotter paper on the outside soaks up the water.

StepsBuild Your Press

  1. Choose what to press: 1 sepal, 1 petal, 2 stamens, the stigma and style (snip off the ovary), your teacher's ovary slice, and the leaf. Keep the rest as spares.
  2. Tap the anthers over scrap paper to shake off loose pollen. Loose pollen stains the archival paper.
  3. Lay down cardboard, then blotter, then one archival sheet. Spread the parts out so none touch or overlap. Smooth petals flat with a clean finger.
  4. Cover with the second archival sheet, then blotter, then cardboard.
  5. Write your group name in pencil on the top cardboard. Stack heavy books on top in a dry spot.
4.Predict: which part will press best, and which will be hardest to press? Explain using what you noticed about each part's thickness and how much water is inside it.

Part CMount and Label (About 2 Weeks Later)

Your parts are ready when they feel dry and papery, not cool or bendy. Scientists store pressed plants like this in a herbarium, a library of plant specimens. Some are over 200 years old.

  1. Plan first. Sketch your layout in the small sheet on the right. Put the parts in order, outside to inside, like your tray.
  2. Handle by the edges. Dry parts crack. Lift them with a flat paper strip, not your fingers.
  3. Attach each part to the acid-free card with tiny dots of archival glue or thin strips of archival tape. Never use regular tape.
  4. Label each part in pencil beside it: sepal, petal, stamen (anther + filament), stigma, style, ovary slice, leaf.
  5. Fill in the specimen label in the bottom-right corner of your card, using the fields below.
