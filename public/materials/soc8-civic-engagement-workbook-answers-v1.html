How this workbook works

Questions from across the unit are mixed together deliberately. Your job is to figure out what each question is asking before you answer it — that thinking is where the learning happens. Riverbend, the fictional town from your notes, shows up again here.

1★ Coreforms of civic engagement

Riverbend's town council is deciding whether to fix the cracked playground equipment at Riverbend Park — the same one Malik noticed in your notes. Name three things a 13-year-old could actually do about this.

Word bank volunteer   advocacy   rally   community initiative   ethical purchasing   petition   voting
Answer Any three of: volunteering, joining a community initiative, attending a rally, advocacy (letter/speaking at a meeting), starting or signing a petition, ethical purchasing (less directly relevant here but acceptable if justified). Voting is not acceptable — a 13-year-old cannot vote. Award full marks for any three non-voting forms with brief justification.
2★ Coreengaged citizenship

Read the story below. Label each sentence: Awareness, Decision-Making, or Action.

  1. Nadia hears her classmates complain that the water fountains at school are always broken.  
  2. She asks the office how repairs get approved and decides the school needs more than one working fountain.  
  3. She writes a short proposal and brings it to the student council meeting.  
Answer (a) Awareness — Nadia notices/hears about the problem. (b) Decision-making — she investigates the process and decides what she thinks should happen. (c) Action — she writes a proposal and brings it to council; she is doing something.
3★ Corevoting eligibility

Check each Riverbend resident against the four voter eligibility requirements. Circle Yes or No for “Can vote?” and give one reason.

Hint All four requirements must be met — citizen, 18+, proof of residence in the riding, proof of identity.
  1. Jordan, age 17, Canadian citizen, has ID, lives in the riding.
    Can vote?  Yes / No   Reason:  
  2. Priya, age 22, Canadian citizen, has ID, but her proof of residence is for a different riding where she used to live.
    Can vote?  Yes / No   Reason:  
Answer (a) Jordan: No — fails the age requirement (17, not yet 18). Meets citizenship, ID, and residence. (b) Priya: No — fails the proof-of-residence-in-this-riding requirement. Her documentation is for a different riding; she must update it before voting in her new riding.
4★★ Extendedglobal issues

A civil conflict overseas disrupts a major shipping route. Container ships must take a much longer path to reach North America. Explain what could happen next, step by step, ending with an effect on a Canadian shopper.

Fill in the final box: what does the Canadian shopper experience?
Answer Full chain expected: conflict disrupts the shipping route → ships take a longer route → shipping takes more time and costs more fuel/insurance → shipping companies raise their prices → the cost of importing goods rises → stores pass some of that cost on → Canadian shoppers pay more for the affected goods, or face shortages/delays. Award marks for a complete, logical chain ending in a concrete effect on a Canadian shopper — exact wording may vary.
5★ Coreforms of civic engagement

A student says: “My little brother is only 11. There's basically nothing he can do about the playground issue — he's just a kid.”

Name three forms of civic engagement from your notes that would NOT be blocked by his age.

One thing he could do is  , because it does not require being 18.
Answer Any three of: volunteering, joining a community initiative, attending a rally, advocacy, ethical purchasing (if relevant to a family purchase), signing/starting a petition. Each must include the reason "does not require being 18." [misconception target: civic engagement requires being 18+]
6★★ Extendedengaged citizenship

A classmate says: “I follow the news about climate change every day, so I'm definitely an engaged citizen.”

Evaluate this claim using the three-part framework. Is the classmate fully an engaged citizen? What, if anything, is missing?

Answer Not fully an engaged citizen. Following the news is awareness only — the first of three parts. To be fully engaged, the classmate also needs to decide what they think should be done (decision-making) and actually do something about it (action), such as advocacy, joining a group, or changing a purchasing habit. Full marks require naming the missing stage(s) by name.

7★ Corevoting eligibility

A new Riverbend resident, Deng, is 19 years old, a Canadian citizen, and has valid ID — but he moved into the riding only last week and has not yet updated his proof of residence.

Can Deng vote in an election happening tomorrow? Explain using the four requirements.

Answer No. Deng meets citizenship, age, and identity requirements, but he does not yet have valid proof of residence for his new riding. All four requirements must be met at the same time — missing even one (in this case, updated proof of residence) means he cannot vote in that riding's election yet.
8★★ Extendedforms of civic engagement

A student claims: “Riverbend's council votes on the playground next month. Since I can't vote in a real election until I'm 18, there's nothing I can do before then.”

Identify the error in this claim. Correct it using at least two forms of civic engagement that do not require voting or being 18.

Answer Error: The student assumes voting is the only way to influence the council's decision. This is false — the council vote happens next month, well before any election, and there are many non-voting forms available now. Correction (any two): advocacy (writing to or speaking at the council meeting), attending a rally, starting/signing a petition, joining a community initiative such as a youth advisory group. [misconception target: voting is the only meaningful form of civic engagement]

Categories economic disruption   poverty   drought and famine   human migration   civil unrest   war

  2. A country experiences years of armed conflict between groups fighting for control. Category:

10★★ Extendedengaged citizenship

Pick a real or made-up local issue (not the playground). Write out how you would move through all three stages: awareness → decision-making → action. Be specific — name what you would actually do at each stage.

Answer Answers will vary by chosen issue. Award marks for: a clearly stated issue; a specific, concrete awareness step (how they would learn about it); a specific decision (what they conclude should happen); and a specific, actionable action step (not just "do something" — must name an actual form of civic engagement). All three stages must be present and in order.

12★★ Extendedvoting eligibility

A student says: “I'm 17, a Canadian citizen, and I have valid ID, so I should be allowed to vote in the by-election happening in my riding next week.”

Explain why this student is still incorrect, even though they meet three of the four requirements.

Answer The student is missing the age requirement (must be 18+; they are 17). Meeting three of four requirements is not sufficient — Canadian voter eligibility requires all four at once, with no partial exceptions. This is the same reasoning used with Sofia and Liam in the notes.

Challenge Zone

★★ only. No scaffolds. Attempt after completing all Q1–Q12.

S1★★ Extendedglobal issues + forms

A drought overseas leads to a wave of migration, and some families resettle in Riverbend. A local group wants to help newcomer families settle in. Describe two different forms of civic engagement that could help, and explain which part of the engaged citizenship framework each one represents.

Answer Any two forms, each correctly matched to a framework stage. Example: (1) Advocacy — writing to the town council to ask for a welcome program — this is action. (2) Attending a community initiative meeting to learn what newcomer families actually need — this is awareness (if framed as information-gathering) or decision-making (if framed as deciding how to help). Award marks for internally consistent reasoning, not one fixed answer — the framework mapping should be logically defensible.
S2★★ Extendedvoting + engaged citizenship

A classmate says: “My vote won't matter — I'm just one person out of thousands.”

Evaluate this claim using the engaged citizenship framework and the idea of collective action. Do you agree or disagree? Justify your answer.

Answer Strong answers disagree with the claim, using collective action reasoning: one vote alone rarely decides an election, but every voter reasons this way, elections would have no votes at all — the claim only works if applied to one person and ignored for everyone else. Connect to the framework: voting is itself an action step that only matters after awareness and decision-making have happened; skipping it because "one vote doesn't matter" undermines the action stage for everyone collectively. Accept a well-justified "agree in part" answer if the student separates individual impact from collective impact accurately.
S3★★ Extendedmulti-concept design

Design a plan for how you and your classmates could research and respond to one real local issue in your own community. Your plan must use at least three different forms of civic engagement and must show all three stages of the engaged citizenship framework. Explain why you chose the forms you did.

Answer Award marks for: (1) a real, specific local issue named; (2) at least three distinct forms of civic engagement from the unit (not three variations of the same form); (3) all three framework stages represented across the plan; (4) a brief justification for why those specific forms were chosen for that specific issue (not generic). This is a design/create-level task — mark holistically using these four criteria rather than a single correct answer.
