Word Bank  sustainability · renewable · regrowth · balance · use what you need · traditional knowledge

The connection is:

Answer The connection is: both involve balance between use and regrowth. Word Bank  less CO₂ than coal · cleaner burning · not renewable · transition · bridge · to renewables · temporary
Hint: A transition fuel is used while renewable energy is being built up.

Answer It is called a transition fuel because: it emits less CO₂ than coal and burns cleaner, so it acts as a bridge to renewables. It is a temporary step — not a permanent solution — used while renewable energy infrastructure is being built. It is not renewable.

Stretch Zone — Challenge Questions
S1
A town is powered 100% by coal. They want to switch to 100% renewable energy in 10 years. List three challenges they would face and three strategies to solve them.
Answer Three challenges: cost of infrastructure replacement / reliability/storage gaps / job losses in coal industry. Three strategies: phased transition over time / invest in battery storage / retrain workers for renewable sector. Award marks for naming three challenges and three corresponding solutions — no single right answer.
S2
Some people argue that Alberta's economy depends on oil and gas, so we should not stop using them. Others argue that the climate crisis means we must act quickly. Write two sentences for each side of this debate. Then state your own position.
Answer Pro-oil: Alberta's economy and many jobs depend on oil revenues; abrupt phase-out would cause significant economic hardship. Pro-action: climate change threatens Alberta's water, agriculture, and long-term economic stability; delayed action increases future costs. Accept any clearly stated position with supporting reasoning on both sides.
S3
Hydrogen fuel cells produce electricity and release only water vapor. But making hydrogen currently requires energy — often from fossil fuels. Is hydrogen truly "clean" energy? Explain the full picture.
Answer Not necessarily. The fuel cell itself only emits water — it is clean at the point of use. But making hydrogen currently requires energy, often from fossil fuels ("grey hydrogen"). Only "green hydrogen" (made using renewable electricity) is truly clean. The full picture: the cleanliness depends entirely on the energy source used to produce the hydrogen.
S4
You are an energy advisor to the Alberta government in 2050. Recommend an energy mix for the province. Name at least two sources, explain why you chose them, and acknowledge one challenge of your plan.
Answer Accept any two sources justified with reasoning (e.g., solar + wind → low environmental impact, increasingly cost-competitive; or wind + geothermal for a fully renewable mix). Must acknowledge one challenge (e.g., storage, job transition, reliability). Award marks for: two sources named, selection factors applied to each, one honest challenge acknowledged.
