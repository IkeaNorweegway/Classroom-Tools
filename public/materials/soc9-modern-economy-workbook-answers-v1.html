Guiding Question: How did economic changes transform Canada into a modern economy?

Part A — Core Questions

Q1
AnswerAny three: overproduction of goods, declining consumer demand, collapse of global trade, financial instability.
Q2
AnswerPublic works, social welfare programs, deficit spending.
Q3
AnswerAny three: automobile manufacturing, petroleum and energy production, pulp and paper, hydroelectric power.
Q4
AnswerPeople sought affordable housing and improved living conditions outside crowded city centres.
Q5
AnswerIt expanded free trade, increasing the movement of goods, services, and investment between the two countries.

Part B — Extended Questions

Q6
Sample response The loan reflects deficit spending, and hiring workers for the community centre reflects public works. I think this kind of spending is a good idea during a real crisis. The cost of doing nothing — mass unemployment and business collapse — is usually worse than manageable future debt. This is the same logic that helped end the worst effects of the Great Depression.
Q7
Sample response Canada and the US are linked closely enough that a factory closure or new tariff in one country affects the other right away. I think the benefits are worth the risk for Canada. Trade access to a much bigger economy created real growth and jobs. But this comes with a cost: Canada has less control over its own economy when major decisions are made in Washington.
