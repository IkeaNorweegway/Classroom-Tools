Kinds of Numbers and Ordering

One skill: sorting rational numbers and putting them in order · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 1: Kinds of numbers · Section 2: Repeating decimals and the number line

NameDate

For the adult: A rational number is any number you can write as a fraction a/b. Integers count too: 5 = 5/1. To order numbers, change them all to decimals. Then the one furthest left on the number line is the smallest.

Worked example

Order from least to greatest: −3/4, −0.7, 1/3, 0.3
Step 1: Change to decimals: A = −0.75, B = −0.7, C = 0.333… (the 3 repeats), D = 0.3.

Step 2: Read left to right: −3/4, −0.7, 0.3, 1/3.

Your turn

1. Write each number as a fraction a/b. a) 7 b) −0.5 c) 1 1/2 (Put a whole number over 1.)

2. Put 6, −4 and 2/5 in the smallest box each one fits. (Is it a counting number? A whole number?)

3. Write 2/3 as a decimal. Does it end or repeat? (Divide 2 ÷ 3.)

4. Which is greater, −1/3 or −0.3? (−1/3 = −0.333…)

5. Find two numbers between 0.5 and 0.6.

6. Jasper had lows of −8 °C, −3.5 °C, 1 °C and −0.5 °C. Mark them on the line. Order them from coldest to warmest.

7. Leo says 0.333… and 0.3 are the same number. Is he right? Explain.
