Estimating Roots and Irrational Numbers

One skill: estimating square roots and telling rational from irrational numbers · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 3: Estimating square roots · Section 4: Irrational numbers and the real number line

NameDate

For the adult: To estimate √30, find the perfect squares on each side: 25 and 36. So √30 is between 5 and 6. Check a guess by squaring it. The root of a number that is not a perfect square never ends and never repeats. It is irrational. Calculator values like 1.41 for √2 are only close.

Worked example

Estimate √30 to one decimal place.
Step 1: 25 < 30 < 36, so 5 < √30 < 6.

Step 2: Try 5.5. Check: 5.5 × 5.5 = 30.25. Very close. √30 ≈ 5.5.

Your turn

1. Between which two whole numbers is √20? (Which perfect squares are on each side of 20?)

2. Estimate √50 to one decimal place. (Is 50 closer to 49 or to 64?)

3. Nia says √70 ≈ 8.5, because 70 is between 64 and 81. Is she right? (Find 8.5 × 8.5.)

4. A square garden has an area of 60 m². Find the side to the nearest tenth. How much fence goes around it? (Side = √60.)

5. Rational or irrational? √49, √7, 0.4545… (the 45 repeats), π

6. Order from least to greatest: √10, 3.1, π, 3.2. Mark each one on the line.

7. Jo says √2 = 1.41. Find 1.41 × 1.41. Is she right?
