The Constant of Proportionality

One skill: finding k for a ratio and using it to find a missing amount · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 2: The constant of proportionality · Section 3: Missing quantities

NameDate

For the adult: For a ratio a : b, the constant of proportionality is k = b ÷ a (second number ÷ first number). The order matters. Once you know k, multiply by k to go from the first amount to the second. Divide by k to go back.

Worked example

Lee pays $29.00 for 20 L of gas. Find k for litres : dollars. Then find the cost of 40 L.

Step 1: k = 29 ÷ 20 = 1.45. That is $1.45 for each litre (the unit rate).
Step 2: We know the first amount, 40 L. Multiply: 1.45 × 40 = $58.00.

Your turn

1. Find k for the ratio 3 : 12. (Second ÷ first: 12 ÷ 3.)

2. k = 1.5. Fill in the missing second terms. (Multiply each top number by 1.5.)

3. 30 L of gas costs $42.00. Find the unit rate. (Divide the dollars by the litres.)

4. Do 4 : 6 and 10 : 16 have the same k? (Find 6 ÷ 4 and 16 ÷ 10.)

5. A map has the scale 1 cm : 5 km. a) 9 cm on the map is how many km? b) A lake is 32 km long. How long is it on the map?

6. Bannock for 4 people uses 3 cups of flour. How much flour for 10 people?

7. Leo says k for the ratio 8 : 2 is 4. Is he right? Explain.
