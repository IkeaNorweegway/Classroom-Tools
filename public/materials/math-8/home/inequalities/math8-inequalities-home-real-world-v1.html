Writing and Checking Inequalities

One skill: checking a solution by substituting, and writing inequalities for real situations · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 3: Checking solutions · Section 4: Real-world inequalities

NameDate

For the adult: To check a number, put it in for the letter, work out each side, and ask "is this true?" True means it is a solution. For a real situation: "at least" or "minimum" is ≥, "at most", "no more than" or "maximum" is ≤. If only whole numbers make sense (people, tickets), only whole numbers count.

Worked example

A ride needs riders at least 122 cm tall. Write the inequality. Can a 118 cm rider go on?

Step 1: Let h = height in cm. "At least" means ≥. So h ≥ 122.
Step 2: Test 118: is 118 ≥ 122? No. The 118 cm rider cannot go on.

Your turn

1. Is x = 6 a solution of 3x − 5 > 12? (Put 6 in for x. Work out 3(6) − 5.)

2. Is x = −1 a solution of 2x + 7 ≥ 5? (2(−1) = −2.)

3. Write an inequality for the speed s on Highway 2. Is 112 km/h legal? (No faster than 110 means ≤.)

4. Which of 4, 6 and 7.5 are solutions of 0.5x < 3? (Test each one. Is 3 < 3 true?)

5. An elevator holds at most 1000 kg. Write the inequality and graph it. Is 980 kg safe? Is 1050 kg?

6. Bus tickets cost $3.50 each. Lin has $20. Write an inequality for n tickets. How many tickets can she buy?

7. Ali says 9 is a solution of 20 − x > 12. Check. Is he right?
