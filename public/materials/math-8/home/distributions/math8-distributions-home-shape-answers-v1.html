The Shape of Data — Answers

One skill: naming the shape of a distribution and choosing mean, median or mode · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the filled-in class notes: Section 2: Shapes of distributions · Section 3: Choosing mean, median or mode

For the adult: The most common slip is naming the skew by the bump instead of the tail (Q2). The second is calling the mean "typical" when one big value pulls it up (Q4: 5 of the 6 waits are below the mean of 8).

Answers

1. A: symmetric. B: skewed left (tail to the left). C: bimodal (two peaks).

2. No. The tail names the skew. If the peak is on the right, the tail goes left, so it is skewed left.

3. Mean: 20 ÷ 5 = 4. Median: 3. Mode: 3.

4. Mean: 48 ÷ 6 = 8 minutes. Median: (4 + 5) ÷ 2 = 4.5 minutes. The median is more typical: the 26 pulls the mean up.

5. a) mode (categories) b) mean c) median

6. The median. The tail on the left pulls the mean down.

7. Bimodal: one peak for kids, one for parents.
