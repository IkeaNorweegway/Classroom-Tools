The Shape of Data

One skill: naming the shape of a distribution and choosing mean, median or mode · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 2: Shapes of distributions · Section 3: Choosing mean, median or mode

NameDate

For the adult: Look at the outline of the bars. Count the peaks. Then look for a long, low tail. The tail names the shape: a tail to the right is skewed right. A long tail pulls the mean toward it. So for skewed data the median (middle value) is the better "typical" value. If the two halves match (symmetric), the mean is fine.

Worked example

Kids time their wait at a rink snack bar. Name the shape. Which average is best?

Step 1: One peak, on the left. Most waits are short.
Step 2: A few long waits make a tail to the right. The shape is skewed right.
Step 3: The tail pulls the mean up. Use the median.

Your turn

1. Name each shape. (Count the peaks. Then look for a tail.)

2. Leo says, "The tallest bar is on the right, so it is skewed right." Is he right? (Which way does the tail point?)

3. Find the mean, median and mode of 2, 3, 3, 4, 8. (Put them in order first.)

4. Bus waits (minutes): 3, 4, 4, 5, 6, 26. Find the mean and the median. Which is the better typical wait? (Mean: add, then divide by 6.)

5. Mean, median or mode? a) favourite hockey team b) heights of Grade 8 students (symmetric) c) house prices in Calgary (skewed right)

6. A histogram is skewed left. Which is bigger, the mean or the median?

7. A family skate has kids and parents. Predict the shape of everyone's heights.
