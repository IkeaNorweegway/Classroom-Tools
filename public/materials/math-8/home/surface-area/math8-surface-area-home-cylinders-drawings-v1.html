Cylinders and 3-D Drawings

One skill: surface area of cylinders, and top, front and side views · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 3: Surface area of cylinders · Section 4: Orthographic and isometric drawings

NameDate

For the adult: Unroll a can. You get two circles and a rectangle. The rectangle wraps once around the can, so its length is the circumference: 3.14 × diameter. SA = the two circles + the rectangle. Use π ≈ 3.14. For a cube building, a view shows only the squares you see from that side.

Worked example

A can has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find its surface area.

Step 1: Two circles: 2 × 3.14 × 3 × 3 = 56.52 cm².
Step 2: The rectangle: 18.84 × 10 = 188.4 cm².
Step 3: SA = 56.52 + 188.4 = 244.92. About 244.9 cm².

Your turn

1. A can is 10 cm across. How long is its label? (Around = 3.14 × diameter.)

2. Find the SA of this cylinder. (Two circles, then the rectangle: 2 × 3.14 × r × h.)

3. A water tank is 2 m across and 3 m tall. Paint covers its curved side only. How much area is painted? (Only the rectangle.)

4. Kim's can is 8 cm across. She cuts the label 8 cm long. Fix her mistake. (Does the label go across or around?)

5. Draw the top, front and right side views.

6. Build it from the views. How many cubes?
