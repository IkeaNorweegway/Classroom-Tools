Surface Area of Prisms and Pyramids — Answers

One skill: surface area of prisms and pyramids · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the filled-in class notes: Section 1: Polyhedrons and nets · Section 2: Surface area of prisms and pyramids

For the adult: The most common slip is using the pyramid's height (inside it) instead of the slant height (on the face), as in Q5. The second is finding the volume (8 × 2 × 3 = 48 cm³ for the example box) instead of the surface area. SA is in cm², volume in cm³.

Answers

1. One face: 4 × 4 = 16 cm². SA = 6 × 16 = 96 cm².

2. Front and back 5 × 3 = 15, sides 2 × 3 = 6, top and bottom 5 × 2 = 10. SA = 15 + 15 + 6 + 6 + 10 + 10 = 62 cm².

3. Base: 4 × 4 = 16 cm². Triangles: 4 × 1/2 × 4 × 6 = 48 cm². SA = 64 cm².

4. Ends: 2 × 1/2 × 6 × 4 = 24 m². Rectangles: 5 × 10 + 5 × 10 + 6 × 10 = 160 m². SA = 184 m².

5. Use the slant height, 13 cm, not the height. Base: 100 cm². Triangles: 4 × 1/2 × 10 × 13 = 260 cm². SA = 360 cm². (Using 12 cm gives 340 cm², which is wrong.)

6. Yes. The column of 4 wraps around. The two squares at the top fold to the left and right sides.
