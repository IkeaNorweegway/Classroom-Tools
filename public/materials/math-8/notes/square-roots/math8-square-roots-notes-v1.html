Watch out — a common mistake

"5² = 10, because the 2 means times 2."

Why it is wrong: The 2 is an exponent. It says how many 5s to multiply. It does not say "times 2."

What is true: 5² = 5 × 5 = 25. Draw it: a 5 by 5 square has 25 tiles, not 10.

Worked Example 1 — Is 36 a perfect square?

6 rows of 6 works. 6 × 6 = 36.

So 36 a perfect square.

Section 3 · Estimating square roots

Words to know

Not every number is a perfect square. But we can still estimate its square root.

Watch out — a common mistake

"√20 is about 10, because 10 is half of 20."

Why it is wrong: 10 × 10 = 100. That is way bigger than 20.

What is true: Use the perfect squares next to 20. 16 < 20 < 25, so √20 is between 4 and 5.

Worked Example 5 — Estimate √20

Step 1: Find the perfect squares on each side. 16 < 20 < 25

Step 2: Take their square roots. 4 < √20 < 5
