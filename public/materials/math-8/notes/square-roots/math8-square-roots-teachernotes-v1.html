Squares and Square Roots

Grade 8 Mathematics · Number · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcomes N1, N2 · Codes SQ1–SQ4

Overview

Big idea: a square number is an area; its square root is the side. Every lesson goes back to the tile picture.

Sequence: perfect squares (SQ1) → square roots (SQ2) → estimating (SQ3) → problems (SQ4). Quiz after Lesson 4. Exit ticket at the end of each lesson.

Materials: square tiles or grid paper, calculators (Lesson 3 only), the concept reference sheet.

Depth ceiling: whole numbers only, principal root only. Square roots of fractions and decimals wait for Grade 9.

Watch forWhat to do
5² = 10 (squaring as doubling)Build 5 × 2 and 5 × 5 with tiles side by side. Only one is a square.
√16 = 8 (root as half)Check every root by multiplying it by itself. Make "check it" a class habit.
√20 ≈ 10 or "halfway" (4.5 as a rule)Always name the two perfect squares first, then ask which is closer.
Dividing area by 4 to find a sideSeparate area (inside) from perimeter (around). Draw the square.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Perfect squares · 60 min
DOWarm-up: tiles on desks. "Make a square with 9 tiles. With 10. With 16." Students sort numbers into "makes a square" and "doesn't."
SHOWNotes Section 1 squares picture. Fill in 3 × 3 = 9 together.
SAY"Squared means the number times itself. The little 2 says use the number twice."
WATCHStudents writing 5² = 10. Use the misconception box before Worked Example 1.
CUEAfter Worked Example 2: "How did the rectangles prove 12 is not a perfect square?" Take two answers before students write.
DOTable of squares 1–15. Students keep it; it is used all unit.
DOExit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Square roots · 60 min
DORetrieval warm-up: 7², 9², 12², is 50 a perfect square?
SHOWThe 7 × 7 picture. "If I tell you the area is 49, what is the side?"
SAY"The square root undoes squaring. Area in, side out."
WATCH√16 = 8. Have the student check: 8 × 8. Let the check do the correcting.
CUEFactor pairs: "Which pair is special?" Prime factors: "Can you split these into two equal teams?"
DOExit ticket Section 2.
Lesson 3 — Estimating · 60 min
DOWarm-up: √25, √36, √49, and "Is there a whole number whose square is 30?"
SHOWThe number line from 3 to 6 with √16, √20, √25.
SAY"Find the perfect squares on each side first. Then ask: which one is it closer to?"
WATCH"Halfway" as a rule. √20 is not exactly 4.5 and √24 is not 4.5 either. Compare with a calculator.
DOCalculators for Worked Example 6. Discuss why the decimal keeps going (no formal "irrational" yet).
DOExit ticket Section 3.
Lesson 4 — Problems · 60 min
DOWarm-up from all three sections (interleaved): 13², √144, estimate √70.
SHOWThe quarter-section example. Ask first: "Is this a square-root problem or a squaring problem? How do you know?"
CUE"Does the answer make sense? Check side × side."
DOStart the Core worksheet. Fast finishers move to Challenge.
DOExit ticket Section 4. Quiz next class.

Assessment

Mid-unit quiz (16 marks)

Items map to SQ1–SQ4. Two items target the misconceptions (Q4: √64 = 32; Q6: estimating). Key is in `tests-quizzes/math-8/square-roots/math8-square-roots-quiz-v1.md`.

Exit tickets

One per section. Sort into three piles (got it / almost / not yet). Start the next class with the "almost" pile's question as the warm-up.

Connections

  • Pythagorean Theorem uses squares and square roots in every problem. Teach this topic first.
  • Grade 9: square roots of fractions and decimals, and the idea that some square roots never end.
  • Home practice: two parent sheets (perfect squares and roots, estimating) on the parent page.
