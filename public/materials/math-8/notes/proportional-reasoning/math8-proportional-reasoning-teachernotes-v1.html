Teacher notes

Proportional Reasoning

Grade 8 Mathematics · Number · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcome 8N5 · Codes PR1–PR4

Overview

Big idea: two amounts can be compared by subtracting (how many more) or by dividing (how many times as many). Grade 8 is about the second. The "times as many" number from one amount to the other is the constant of proportionality, k = b ÷ a for a ratio a : b, and it stays the same across every equivalent ratio. Knowing k turns every missing-quantity problem into one move: multiply down the arrow (b = k × a) or divide back up (a = b ÷ k). k goes by other names in context: unit rate (dollars per litre), scale factor (km per cm on a map) and, on a graph, slope. Direct proportion keeps b ÷ a fixed and graphs as a straight line through (0, 0); inverse proportion keeps a × b fixed (workers × hours) and graphs as a curve.

Sequence: additive vs multiplicative comparison, "times as many" both ways, 3 and 1/3 undo each other, the multiple as a decimal or fraction (PR1) → from the Grade 7 factor (across the ratio table) to k (down each column), k = b ÷ a, unit rate, testing whether ratios are equivalent by comparing k, the order of the ratio (PR2) → building equivalent ratios from k, the four-step "find k, multiply or divide, check" routine, gas, a 1 cm : 5 km map, bannock for a class, a made-up exchange rate, paint (PR3) → direct proportion as a table and a line through (0, 0) with k as slope, inverse proportion as worker-hours and a curve, speed and time for a fixed trip, sharing (PR4).

Materials: linking cubes or coloured tiles (for "times as many" blocks), grid paper, calculators, mini whiteboards, a road map of Alberta or a printed map with a 1 cm : 5 km scale, a pancake or bannock recipe card, chart paper. Optional: Desmos (desmos.com/calculator) to plot a table and see a line through (0, 0) beside the curve y = 12/x.

Depth ceiling: k as a whole number, decimal or simple fraction. Direct (b = k × a) and inverse (a × b = k) only; no "neither" relationships beyond a ★ table that is clearly not proportional. Slope is named, not calculated with rise ÷ run (that is Linear Functions, topic 7). Contexts: unit price, speed, recipes, maps, workers and time, sharing. Percent problems are Grade 7 and appear only in retrieval.

Reading level: student pages are written at about Grade 3–4 (F&P N–P). The math words (additive comparison, multiplicative comparison, multiple, times as many, proportional, constant of proportionality, k, unit rate, missing quantity, scale, scale factor, direct proportion, inverse proportion, origin, slope) are the learning; each has a picture beside it.

Notation: ratios are written a : b with spaces. k is always "second term ÷ first term" and the ratio tables show k as a down arrow "× k". Students should say which term they know before they calculate: "I know a, so I multiply" or "I know b, so I divide".

Watch forWhat to do
"12 is 8 times 4" (subtracting, then calling it "times")Build 4 and 12 with cubes. Ask "how many towers of 4 make 12?" Then ask "how many more?" Name both: 3 times as many, 8 more.
"Ben has 3 times fewer" or "−3 as many"Turn the blocks around: Ben is 1 block out of Ava's 3. Write the down arrow × 3 and the up arrow × 1/3 on the same diagram.
"k is the bigger number divided by the smaller"Insist on the order of the ratio. Write "first : second" over the numbers and k = second ÷ first. Rice : water and water : rice give 2 and 0.5.
Confusing the factor (across) with k (down)Draw both arrows on one ratio table in two colours. The factor changes from column to column; k is the same in every column.
Adding the same number to both terms (Grade 7 additive mix-up)Find k for both ratios. 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 but 5 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.83: not equivalent. Ask "does the bigger batch taste the same?"
Multiplying by k when b is known (60 km × 5 = 300 cm on the map)Estimate: a 300 cm map is 3 m wide. Ask "do I know the first term or the second?" before every calculation.
"More workers, more hours"Draw the worker-hour squares. 2 rows of 6 and 4 rows of 3 both make 12 squares. Ask "does the job get bigger when more people help?"
Calling every table "direct"Test both: divide b ÷ a in each column, then multiply a × b. Only one stays the same.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Times as many · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1–Q2 preview equivalent ratios from Grade 7, Q3 previews dividing both ways, Q4 decimal × and ÷ for k, Q5 a unit price, Q6 a fraction of an amount.
SHOWAva's 12 hockey cards and Ben's 4 as cube towers (or the block diagram). "How can we compare these? Give me two ways."
SAY"Subtracting tells you how many more. Dividing tells you how many times as many. In Grade 8 we use dividing."
DOPairs build 18 and 6, then 10 and 4, with cubes. Each pair writes both comparisons both ways on a whiteboard.
WATCH"12 is 8 times 4." Misconception box: 8 times 4 would be 32.
WATCH"Ben has 3 times fewer." Misconception box: going back undoes × 3.
CUEWorked Example 1 (canola 6 ha and wheat 15 ha: 2.5 and 0.4), Worked Example 2 (bus 60 and van 12: 5 and 1/5 = 0.2), Worked Example 3 (Leo runs 1.5 × 4 km = 6 km).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — The constant of proportionality · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: 21 ÷ 6; 3 : 5 = ? : 20; Mia ran 3 km and Jo ran 2.5 times as far; 0.75 × 8.
SHOWThe juice ratio table 2 : 5, 4 : 10, 6 : 15 with the Grade 7 factor arrow across. Then turn it: "How do you get from concentrate to water in each column?"
SAY"The number you multiply the first term by to get the second is k. It is the same in every column. That is what makes the ratios equivalent."
WATCH"k is always the bigger divided by the smaller." Misconception box: water : rice = 4 : 2 has k = 0.5.
CUEWorked Example 1 (20 L of gas for $29.00: k = 1.45, the unit rate), Worked Example 2 (pancake mixes: 4 : 6 and 6 : 9 have k = 1.5; 10 : 16 does not), Worked Example 3 (Ben : Ava and Ava : Ben give 3 and 1/3).
DOCard sort (10 min): groups sort 12 ratio cards into piles with the same k. One card in each set is a "trap" made by adding to both terms.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2. Quiz next class.
Lesson 3 — Missing quantities · 60 min
DOQuiz (25 min) first, on PR1–PR2. Then the lesson.
SHOWA ratio table with k = 1.5 and two empty second terms. "Pick any first term. What goes below it?"
SAY"Know the first term? Multiply by k. Know the second term? Divide by k. Then check by going the other way."
CUEWorked Example 1 (40 L at $1.45 per litre = $58.00), Worked Example 2 (map 1 cm : 5 km: 7.5 cm → 37.5 km and 60 km → 12 cm).
WATCHMultiplying when b is known. Misconception box: a 300 cm map.
CUEWorked Example 3 (bannock: 4 people use 3 cups, k = 0.75, 28 people need 21 cups).
WATCHAdding 2 cups for 2 more people. Misconception box.
CUEWorked Example 4 (a made-up rate of $0.75 US per Canadian dollar, both directions).
DOMap task (10 min): pairs measure three distances between towns on a 1 cm : 5 km map and find the real distances; then one real distance back to cm.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3.
Lesson 4 — Direct and inverse proportion · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: k for 8 : 6; 23 km on a 1 cm : 5 km map; 12 is how many times 4?; 2.5 × 1.45.
SHOWThe Edmonton–Calgary table at 100 km/h and its graph. "What do you notice about the points?" Draw the line through (0, 0).
SAY"When b ÷ a stays the same, it is direct proportion. The graph is a straight line through (0, 0), and k is its slope. You will use slope again in Linear Functions."
SHOWThe rink: 2 workers take 6 h. Draw 12 worker-hour squares. Rearrange them as 3 × 4, 4 × 3, 6 × 2.
WATCH"More workers, more hours." Misconception box: 4 workers take 3 h, not 12.
CUEWorked Example 1 (direct: 2.5 h → 250 km, 300 km → 3 h), Worked Example 2 (inverse: the same 300 km trip at 75, 60 and 50 km/h), Worked Example 3 (12 pizza slices shared).
DOSort (5 min): direct or inverse? Gas and cost, people sharing a gift, hours and pay, speed and time for one trip.
DOCore worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4.

Assessment

Mid-unit quiz (22 marks)

PR1 (Q1–4, Q10), PR2 (Q5–9). Given at the start of Lesson 3, after Sections 1–2 and before missing quantities (PR3) and direct and inverse proportion (PR4). Calculators allowed. Q4: full marks need both "20 is how many more" and 25 ÷ 5 = 5. Q5 b): accept 2/7 or 0.29. Q6 c): any ratio a : 1.5a. Q8: k ≈ 1.33 for 12 : 16 and ≈ 1.17 for 6 : 7 show why they are out; students need not give these. Q9 is the order-of-the-ratio item. Q10 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need "undo × 3 by dividing" or a numerical example such as 12 and 4.

Worksheets and exits

Core (★) covers PR1–PR4 with a diagram on most questions: shells both ways, the 20 − 5 = 15 mistake fixed with blocks, barley and canola, 0.75 times $40, k for 4 : 10 and 10 : 4, tea : milk with the order trap, which ratios share k with 3 : 4.5, a gas unit rate, a k = 0.6 ratio table, the 45 km lake on the map, soup for 10 people (the additive trap), and 4 workers painting a fence. Challenge (★★) adds pay as a direct proportion and its graph, a direct/inverse/neither table, Edmonton to Red Deer at different speeds, a made-up euro rate both ways, Pia and Tom's 49 cards, k for b : a, a two-coat paint job, and sharing a $90 gift. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it. Exit tickets: one per section, each ending with an "explain how you know" item.

Data and contexts used

  • Gas prices of $1.40 to $1.45 per litre are close to Alberta pump prices in 2025–26 but are rounded; $36.25 for 25 L ($1.45) and $56.00 for 40 L ($1.40) are made up to give clean unit rates.
  • Edmonton to Calgary is about 300 km by Highway 2 between city centres. The steady 100 km/h is a simplification: the limit is 110 km/h and real trips include stops.
  • Edmonton to Red Deer is about 150 km between city centres; rounded.
  • The map scale 1 cm : 5 km is a made-up but typical regional-map scale. Town and lake distances on it (7.5 cm, 60 km, 42 km, 45 km) are made up.
  • The exchange rates ($1 Canadian = $0.75 US; $1 Canadian = 0.6 euros) are made up and say so on the page. Real rates change daily.
  • The bannock, pancake, juice, trail mix and soup recipes are made up; the ratios are realistic.
  • Paint covering 8.5 m² per litre (and 10 m² per litre in the Challenge set) is realistic for one coat of interior paint; rounded.
  • Farm areas (6 ha canola, 15 ha wheat, 8 ha barley, 20 ha canola), the rink-shovelling times, volunteers, gift costs and card counts are made up.
  • A school bus holding 60 and a van holding 12 are close to typical seating; rounded.

Connections

  • Grade 7 Ratios and Rates: the factor across a ratio table and equivalent ratios are the starting point; k is the new idea.
  • Rational Numbers (topic 1): k is often a decimal or fraction; 3 and 1/3, 2.5 and 0.4 are reciprocals.
  • Solving Equations (topic 5): a = b ÷ k is solving k × a = b.
  • Linear Functions (topic 7): k is the slope m of a direct proportion; y = kx is y = mx + b with b = 0.
  • Similarity & Dilations (topic 8): scale factor is k for lengths of similar shapes.
