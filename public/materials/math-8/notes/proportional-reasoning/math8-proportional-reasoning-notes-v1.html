Section 1 · Times as many

Learning target: I can describe one amount as a multiple of the other, both ways.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
additive comparison
multiplicative comparison
multiple
times as many
as many as (a fraction)

Two ways to compare

Ava has 12 hockey cards. Ben has 4.

Subtract: 12 − 4 = 8. Ava has 8 more cards. This is an additive comparison.

Divide: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Ava has 3 times as many cards. This is a multiplicative comparison.

In Grade 8, we use the multiplicative comparison.

Turn it around

You can describe either amount as a multiple of the other.

Ben compared to Ava: 4 ÷ 12 = 4/12 = 1/3. Ben has 1/3 as many cards as Ava.

Ava compared to Ben: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Ava has 3 times as many cards as Ben.

The two numbers 3 and 1/3 flip each other.

Times as many

"A is how many times B?" Find A ÷ B.

12 ÷ 4 = 3, so 12 is 3 times 4.

4 ÷ 12 = 1/3, so 4 is 1/3 of 12.

The multiple can be a whole number, a decimal or a fraction.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Ava has 12 cards and Ben has 4. So Ava has 8 times as many."

Why it is wrong: 8 is how many MORE. That is subtracting. 8 times 4 would be 32 cards, not 12.

What is true: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Ava has 3 times as many. She also has 8 more.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Ava has 3 times as many as Ben. So Ben has 3 times fewer, or −3 as many."

Why it is wrong: Going back, you undo × 3. You divide by 3.

What is true: Ben has 1/3 as many as Ava. 12 × 1/3 = 4.

Worked Example 1 — Canola and wheat

Kai's farm has 6 ha of canola and 15 ha of wheat. Compare them both ways.

Step 1: Wheat compared to canola: 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5. Kai has 2.5 times as much wheat.

Step 2: Canola compared to wheat: 6 ÷ 15 = 0.4. Canola is 0.4 times the wheat.

Step 3: 0.4 = 4/10 = 2/5. So canola is 2/5 as much as the wheat.

Check: 2.5 × 6 = 15 ✓ and 0.4 × 15 = 6 ✓.

Explain it Why do 2.5 and 0.4 describe the same pair of fields?

Section 2 · The constant of proportionality

Learning target: I can find k for a ratio and use it to check equivalent ratios.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
proportional
constant of proportionality (k)
unit rate

The factor goes across. k goes down.

Sam's juice is 2 cans of concentrate to 5 cans of water. In Grade 7, you used a factor to make equal ratios.

Now look down each column. How do you get from concentrate to water?

5 ÷ 2 = 2.5. 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5. 20 ÷ 8 = .

Every column has the same down arrow. This number is the constant of proportionality, k.

Water = 2.5 × concentrate.

The constant of proportionality

For a ratio a : b, k = b ÷ a. So b = k × a.

k is the same for every equivalent ratio.

k can be a decimal or a fraction: k = 2.5 = 5/2.

Watch out — a common mistake

"k is always the bigger number divided by the smaller. For water to rice 4 : 2, k = 2."

Why it is wrong: k depends on the order of the ratio. k = second term ÷ first term.

What is true: For water : rice = 4 : 2, k = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. Rice = 0.5 × water. Check: 0.5 × 8 = 4 ✓. For rice : water = 2 : 4, k would be 2.

Lee pays $29.00 for 20 L of gas. Find k for litres : dollars.

Step 2: k = 29 ÷ 20 = .

k is the cost of ONE litre. That is why it is a unit rate.

Build equivalent ratios from k

Say k = 1.5. Pick any first term. Multiply it by 1.5 to get the second term.

2 : 3, 4 : 6, 5 : and 10 : all have k = 1.5.

Find a missing quantity

Down the arrow: multiply by k. Back up the arrow: divide by k.

Worked Example 2 — A map of Alberta

A map has the scale 1 cm : 5 km. So k = 5.

Step 1: Two towns are 7.5 cm apart on the map. Real distance: b = 5 × 7.5 = km.

Step 2: A lake is 60 km long. Map length: a = b ÷ k = 60 ÷ 5 = cm.

Step 3: Check: 5 × = 60 ✓.

Watch out — a common mistake

"The lake is 60 km long. On the map it is 60 × 5 = 300 cm."

Why it is wrong: 60 km is the second term (b). You go back up the arrow, so you divide.

What is true: a = 60 ÷ 5 = 12 cm. Check: 12 × 5 = 60 ✓. A 300 cm map would be 3 m wide!

Worked Example 3 — Bannock for the class

Bannock for 4 people uses 3 cups of flour. How much flour for a class of 28?

Step 1: people : flour = 4 : 3. k = 3 ÷ 4 = cups for each person.

Step 2: b = k × a = 0.75 × 28 = .

Step 3: The class needs cups of flour.

Watch out — a common mistake

"4 people use 3 cups. 6 people is 2 more people, so add 2 cups: 5 cups."

Why it is wrong: Adding keeps the difference the same, not k. 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75, but 5 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.83.

What is true: b = 0.75 × 6 = 4.5 cups. Check: 4.5 ÷ 6 = 0.75 ✓.

Section 4 · Direct and inverse proportion

Learning target: I can tell direct from inverse proportion and solve problems with each.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
direct proportion
inverse proportion
origin
slope

Direct proportion: a straight line through (0, 0)

The Bremner family drives from Edmonton to Calgary. That is about 300 km. They drive at 100 km/h.

Time (h)0123
Distance (km)0100

distance = 100 × time. So k = km per hour.

The points make a straight line through (0, 0). For each 1 h across, the line goes up km.

On a graph, k is the slope. You will use slope again in Linear Functions.

Inverse proportion: one goes up, the other goes down

2 workers shovel the outdoor rink in 6 h. That is 2 × 6 = 12 worker-hours of shovelling.

More workers share the same work. So each one works fewer hours.

Direct or inverse?

Direct: b ÷ a is the same every time. b = k × a. Both go up together. Straight line through (0, 0).

Inverse: a × b is the same every time. a × b = k. One goes up, the other goes down. A curve.

Watch out — a common mistake

"2 workers shovel the rink in 6 h. So 4 workers take 12 h."

Why it is wrong: That treats it as direct. More workers share the work, so the time goes DOWN.

What is true: 2 × 6 = 12 worker-hours. 4 workers take 12 ÷ 4 = 3 h. Doubling workers halves the time.

Worked Example 1 — Edmonton to Calgary

At 100 km/h, how far do you go in 2.5 h? How long do 300 km take?

Step 1: Direct proportion. k = 100 km per hour.

Step 2: In 2.5 h: b = 100 × 2.5 = km.

Step 3: For 300 km: a = 300 ÷ 100 = h.
