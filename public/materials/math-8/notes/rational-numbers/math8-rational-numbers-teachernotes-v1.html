Teacher notes

Rational Numbers

Grade 8 Mathematics · Number · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcome 8N1.1 · Codes RN1–RN4

Overview

Big idea: a rational number is any number that can be written as a ratio a/b of two integers with b ≠ 0. That one definition pulls integers, fractions, mixed numbers, terminating and repeating decimals into a single family, nested inside one another: natural numbers ⊂ integers ⊂ rationals. Every rational number has a place on the number line, and between any two of them there are endlessly many more. Division explains the decimals: a remainder that comes back forces the digits to repeat, and in simplest form only denominators built from 2s and 5s terminate. The Grade 7 integer rules then carry over unchanged to negative fractions and decimals, and the order of operations ties all four operations together, with estimation as the check on sign and size.

Sequence: natural numbers, integers and rational numbers as nested boxes, writing any integer or terminating decimal as a/b, sorting (RN1) → long division to decimals, bar notation, which fractions terminate, ordering on the number line, finding a number between two others (RN2) → common denominators with negative fractions (chips and the number line), subtracting as adding the opposite, × and ÷ with sign rules and the reciprocal (RN3) → order of operations with rational numbers, estimating with friendly numbers, multi-step problems: a mean temperature, a stock, a recipe (RN4).

Materials: two-colour counters (or chips) for zero pairs, number-line strips (−2 to 2 in tenths, and a "zoom" strip from 0.5 to 0.6 in hundredths), fraction strips or grid paper for the area model, calculators, mini whiteboards, a set of sorting cards (about 20 numbers: 12, −7, 3/8, 0, −1.2, 0.3, 51/4, −40, …) for RN1. Optional: Desmos or GeoGebra number line for zooming between 0.5 and 0.6.

Depth ceiling: set language (natural numbers, integers, rational numbers) but no set-builder notation, no ⊆, and no whole numbers as a separate set. Bar notation for repeating decimals; converting a repeating decimal back to a fraction is limited to the known cases (0.3 = 1/3, 0.6 = 2/3, 0.1 = 1/9) and is not taught as an algebraic method. Operations use fractions with small denominators and decimals to hundredths; a calculator is fine for messy decimals once students have estimated. Irrational numbers wait for the next topic (Square Roots & Irrational Numbers, SR4).

Reading level: student pages are written at about F&P N–P. The math words (natural number, integer, rational number, terminating decimal, repeating decimal, bar notation, between, common denominator, reciprocal, sign rules, order of operations, estimate, reasonable) are the learning; each has a picture or example beside it.

Notation: the notes write repeating decimals with a bar, 0.18, and also with dots, 0.1818…. The parent home sheets use only the dots, because the home renderer has no bar. Negative fractions are written −3/4 (sign in front), and students should know that −3/4 = (−3)/4 = 3/(−4).

"Integers aren't rational; only fractions are" (or the reverse)Write 5 = 5/1, −3 = −3/1, 0 = 0/1 on the board. Then return to the nested boxes: every number in an inner box also lives in the outer ones.
Putting 0 in the natural numbersCounting starts at 1. 0 is an integer and rational, not natural.
"0.333… is 0.3"Long division of 1 ÷ 3 on the board: the remainder 1 comes back every time. Then place both on the 0.29–0.34 zoom line. 0.3 = 3/10 stops; 1/3 is to its right.

Ordering negatives by their size (−0.6 < −0.75 "because 6 < 75")Change to the same number of decimal places and place both on the line. Further left is smaller, always.
"−1/2 + 1/3 = 0/5"Fraction strips: halves and thirds are different sizes. Common denominator first; chips for the signs (−3/6 + 2/6, two zero pairs).

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Kinds of numbers · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1 is integer comparison (temperatures), Q2 a fraction to a decimal, Q3–Q4 Grade 7 integer operations, Q5 adding unlike fractions, Q6 placing a negative decimal and a fraction on a line. Q3–Q5 predict who will struggle in Lesson 3.
SHOWThree number lines: dots at the counting numbers, then every integer, then points in the gaps (−3/2, −0.75, 1/2, 5/4). "Which of these lines has the most numbers on it?"
SAY"You count with natural numbers. Add 0 and the negatives and you have the integers. A rational number is any number you can write as a fraction of two integers, a over b, with b not zero. The word ratio is inside rational."
CUEThe nested-boxes picture. Then Worked Example 1 (5, −3, 0, 0.75, −2.5, 11/4 as a/b).
WATCH"Integers aren't rational" and "fractions aren't rational." Two misconception boxes.
DOCard sort in pairs: each card goes in the smallest box it fits. Discuss 0 and −40.
CUEWorked Example 2 (sort six numbers), Worked Example 3 (Jasper low temperatures: −8, −3.5, 1, −0.5 °C).
SAY"Why can't the bottom be 0? 6/0 would mean 6 ÷ 0, and nothing times 0 gives 6."
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Repeating decimals and the number line · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: write −1.25 as a fraction; is 0 rational?; −7 + 10; which is colder, −4.5 °C or −4 °C?
SHOWLong division of 3 ÷ 8 (it ends) beside 1 ÷ 3 (the remainder 1 keeps coming back).
SAY"If a remainder comes back, the digits repeat forever. We put a bar over only the digits that repeat."
CUEWorked Example 1 (2 ÷ 11 = 0.18), Worked Example 2 (5 ÷ 6 = 0.83: bar over the 3 only).
WATCH"0.333… is 0.3." Misconception box with the 0.29–0.34 zoom line.
DOThe "which fractions repeat?" table in pairs. Let them find the 2s-and-5s pattern before you name it. The explain box: 1 ÷ 7 must repeat within 6 digits because only remainders 1–6 are possible.
CUEWorked Example 3 (order five numbers with letters A–E on a line; −7/11 = −0.63).
WATCH"No number between 0.5 and 0.6." Misconception box; zoom strips.
CUEWorked Example 4 (a number between −3/4 and −2/3: −0.7).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2.
Lesson 3 — Operations with rational numbers · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: 2/3 with a bar; order −0.3, −1/3, −0.33; 1/2 + 1/3; (−4) × 3.
SAY"Nothing new about the rules. The Grade 7 integer rules work for fractions and decimals too."
SHOW−2.5 + 4 as a jump on the number line.
CUEWorked Example 1 (−1/2 + 1/3 with chips, each chip one sixth: two zero pairs leave −1/6).
WATCH"−1/2 + 1/3 = 0/5." Misconception box with the fraction strips.
CUEWorked Example 2 (Calgary: 2.5 °C drops 6.8 °C → −4.3 °C, the vertical jump).
SAY"For × and ÷, find the sign first, then the size. Same signs, positive. Different signs, negative."
CUEWorked Example 3 (−2/3 × 3/4 with the area model), Worked Example 4 (a stock drops $0.35 a day for 4 days), Worked Example 5 (−3/4 ÷ 1/2: how many halves fit, then the reciprocal).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3. Quiz next class (RN1–RN3).
Lesson 4 — Order of operations and estimating · 60 min
DOQuiz (25 min) first. Then the lesson.
SHOWThe flow chart: brackets, powers, × and ÷ left to right, + and − left to right.
CUEWorked Example 1 (−3 + 6 × (−0.5) = −6).
WATCHWorking left to right across levels. Misconception box (the −1.5 answer).
CUEWorked Example 2 ((1/2 − 3/4) × (−8) = 2).
SAY"Estimate first. Round to friendly numbers and decide the sign. If the calculator answer is far away, check again."
CUEWorked Example 3 (−4.87 × 2.1 ≈ −10), Worked Example 4 (Lethbridge mean: −3.5, 2.1, −6.4 → −2.6 °C), Worked Example 5 (stock at $12.40, three drops of $0.85, a rise of $1.10 → $10.95), Worked Example 6 (3/4 cup × 21/2 batches = 17/8 cups).
DOCore worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4. Unit synthesis to close the topic.

Assessment

Quiz (23 marks)

RN1 (Q1–3), RN2 (Q4–7), RN3 (Q8–9). Given at the start of Lesson 4, before order of operations and estimating (RN4). No item needs a calculator. Q3 (Ravi: 0 is not rational) is the explain item: the second mark needs 0 = 0/1 as a ratio of two integers. Q4c is −0.583: no mark for a bar over 583 or over 83. Q6: the decimals earn one mark and the order one; students who order negatives by digit size put −0.3 first. Q9 (Grande Prairie −14.5 °C, Lethbridge 3.5 °C): 18 °C warmer and a mean of −5.5 °C; give follow-through on the mean.

Worksheets and exits

Core (★) covers RN1–RN4 with a diagram on at least half the questions: sorting into nested boxes, Jay's "−4 is not rational", a/b forms, long division with bar notation, Mia's 1/3 = 0.3 on a zoom line, ordering with letters, two numbers between 0.5 and 0.6, sums and differences, products and quotients, a Red Deer temperature day, order of operations with an estimate, and Sam's bank balance. Challenge (★★) adds "is every rational an integer?", deciding terminate or repeat from the denominator, ordering 0.4545…, 0.45, 0.4555… and 5/11, nested "between" fractions, a mixed-operation fraction expression, a four-day stock change, and judging Kim's (−2.4) ÷ 0.08 ≈ −0.3. Exit tickets: one per section, each with a diagram and an "explain how you know" item.

Home practice

Two parent sheets: Kinds of numbers and ordering (Sections 1–2) and Operations with rational numbers (Sections 3–4). Each has an adult tip, one worked example and seven questions, with an answer sheet.

Data and contexts used

  • Jasper low temperatures (−8, −3.5, 1, −0.5 °C) are made up but typical of a Jasper shoulder-season week.
  • The Calgary morning (2.5 °C falling 6.8 °C to −4.3 °C) is made up; drops of that size are common when a chinook breaks.
  • Lethbridge highs (−3.5, 2.1, −6.4 °C) in the notes and (−4.5, 1.5, −3 °C) on the home sheet, Red Deer's day (−7.5 °C, +4.3, −5.6) and the quiz's Grande Prairie (−14.5 °C) and Lethbridge (3.5 °C) are made up but plausible winter values.
  • Stock prices ($0.35 a day; $12.40 with three drops of $0.85 and a rise of $1.10; the Challenge stock at $24.60) are made up.
  • Leo's balance (−$12.50 + $20), Sam's bank balance ($45.20, games at $18.75), the diver at −12.6 m and the recipe (3/4 cup of oil, 21/2 batches) are made up.

Connections

  • Grade 7 Integers and Fractions: the sign rules, zero pairs and the area model are reused without change.
  • Square Roots & Irrational Numbers (topic 2): the nested boxes grow a fourth set, the irrationals, and together they make the real numbers. Terminating and repeating decimals are the test for "rational".
  • Proportional Reasoning (topic 3): a ratio a : b and the fraction a/b are the same idea; k is a rational number.
  • Solving Equations (topic 5): rational coefficients and fraction or decimal solutions rely on the operations here.
  • Inequalities (topic 6): ordering rational numbers on a line, open and closed dots.
