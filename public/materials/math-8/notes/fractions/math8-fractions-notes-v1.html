The rule

Multiply the tops. Multiply the bottoms. Simplify.

a/b × c/d = (a × c)/(b × d)

2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2

Watch out — a common mistake

"Multiplying always makes a number bigger."

Why it is wrong: Multiplying by a number less than 1 means taking only part of it.

What is true: 1/2 × 8 = 4. Half of 8 is less than 8. 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2, smaller than both fractions.

Section 3 · Dividing fractions

Learning target: I can divide fractions using a model, common denominators, and the multiply-by-the-reciprocal rule.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture

How many groups fit?

3 ÷ 1/4 asks: how many quarters fit into 3?

Watch out — a common mistake

"Dividing always makes a number smaller, so 3 ÷ 1/4 must be less than 3."

Why it is wrong: Dividing asks "how many pieces fit?" Small pieces fit many times.

What is true: 4 quarters fit in each whole, so 12 quarters fit in 3. 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12.

Worked Example 6 — Common denominators: 3/4 ÷ 1/8

Make the bottoms the same. 3/4 = /8

Now ask: how many eighths fit in 6 eighths?

The rule: multiply by the reciprocal

Keep the first fraction. Change ÷ to ×. Flip the second fraction.

3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 =

Watch out — a common mistake

"To divide fractions, flip the first one: 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 4/3 × 1/8."

Why it is wrong: That gives 4/24 = 1/6. But the model shows 6 eighths fit in 3/4.

What is true: Keep the first. Flip the one you divide BY. 3/4 × 8/1 = 6.
