Teacher notes

Polynomials

Grade 8 Mathematics · Algebra & Functions · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcome 8A1 · Codes PO1–PO4

Overview

Big idea: a polynomial is built from terms, and a term is a coefficient times a power of the variable: 3x² = 3 · x · x. Reading the power as repeated multiplication is what separates x² from 2x and what makes "like terms" precise: same variable and same exponent, the same shape of algebra tile. A polynomial is a sum of such terms with whole-number exponents, so 2/x and √x are not polynomials. It is named two ways: by the number of terms (monomial, binomial, trinomial) and by its degree, the highest exponent (constant, linear, quadratic, cubic). Adding polynomials is collecting like terms; subtracting is adding the opposite, which flips every sign in the second bracket. Exponents never change when terms are added.

Sequence: powers as repeated multiplication, algebra tiles (x², x, 1), coefficient, variable and exponent, term ↔ product of factors, hidden 1s and conventional form (x not 1x, x not x¹, 5 not 5x⁰) (PO1) → what counts as a polynomial, counting terms, degree of a term and of a polynomial, naming two ways (PO2) → like terms as same-shape tiles, zero pairs, combining, descending and ascending order with each sign moving with its term (PO3) → adding with tiles and in columns, perimeter, the opposite of a polynomial, subtracting by adding the opposite, a two-plot garden checked by substitution (PO4).

Materials: algebra tile sets with x² tiles (or the printable tiles, two colours for positive and negative), sorting cards with expressions for PO2 (3x² + 1, x³ − x, 7, −2x, 1/x, √x + 2, x⁻¹, 4/x²), mini whiteboards, chart paper for a "term anatomy" anchor chart. Optional: an online algebra-tile tool (e.g. the Mathies or Didax virtual tiles).

Depth ceiling: one variable (x), degree up to 3 in examples ("degree n" is named but not used). Add and subtract only: no multiplying polynomials, no distributing a number other than −1, no factoring (all Grade 9). Algebra tiles stop at degree 2; cubic terms are handled symbolically and with the column method. Integer coefficients throughout. Substitution is used only as a check.

Reading level: student pages are written at about F&P N–P. The math words (term, coefficient, variable, exponent, power, constant, polynomial, monomial, binomial, trinomial, degree, linear, quadratic, cubic, conventional form, like terms, descending order, ascending order, opposite, zero pair) are the learning; each has a picture or example beside it.

Notation: the notes write products with a raised dot (3 · x · x) to avoid confusing × with x. Exponents are superscripts (x², x³); x⁰ and x¹ appear only to explain conventional form. In tile pictures, green squares are x², tall tiles x, small squares 1; red or the negative colour means negative. A struck-through pair is a zero pair.

Watch forWhat to do
"x² means 2x"Tiles: x² is a square, 2x is two thin tiles. Substitute x = 5: 25 vs 10.
Coefficient of x or −x² read as 0 or missingWrite the hidden 1: x = 1x¹, −x² = −1x². The table in Worked Example 3.
"The degree is the number of terms"Name it two ways every time: count terms, then look at exponents. x³ + 1: two terms, degree 3.
Calling 2/x or √x a polynomialRewrite 2/x as 2x⁻¹: the exponent must be a counting number or 0.
"3x² + 2x = 5x³"Different tile shapes don't combine. Substitute x = 2: 16 vs 40. Adding never changes an exponent.
Dropping a sign when reordering (5 − 3x + x² → x² + 3x + 5)Circle each term with the sign in front of it before moving it.
"(3x + 2) − (x − 5) = 2x − 3"Flip every tile in the second bracket. Write the bracket again without the brackets before combining.
Combining constants with x terms (3x + 4 = 7x)Sort into columns or bins first: x², x, numbers.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Terms, coefficients and powers · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1–Q2 are Grade 7 collecting like terms, Q3 a power, Q4 integer addition and subtraction (needed in Lesson 4), Q5 substitution (used as a check throughout), Q6 the opposite of −7 and of 2x (the seed of subtraction).
SHOWAlgebra tiles: one x² square, a few x tiles, some unit tiles. "How is the big square related to the tall tile?" Then a cube with edges x: x · x · x = x³.
SAY"A term is a coefficient times a power of x. The exponent counts how many x's are multiplied. The coefficient counts how many of that power."
CUEThe term-anatomy picture (3x²: coefficient, variable, exponent) and 3x² as three x² tiles.
WATCH"x² means 2x." Misconception box: tiles, then x = 5.
CUEWorked Example 1 (4x³ = 4 · x · x · x), Worked Example 2 (−2 · x · x = −2x²).
SAY"Some 1s are hidden. We write x, not 1x; x, not x¹; 5, not 5x⁰. That's conventional form."
CUEWorked Example 3 (coefficient and exponent table: 6x², −x³, x, −8).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Naming polynomials · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: write 5x³ as factors; conventional form of 1x² + 0x; (−2)³; simplify 4x + 2 − x.
DOCard sort in pairs: polynomial or not. Let pairs argue about 1/x and √x before you give the rule.
SAY"A polynomial is a sum of terms. Every exponent is a counting number, or 0 for a constant."
SHOWThe terms sort (monomial, binomial, trinomial), then degree as the highest exponent.
WATCH"The degree is the number of terms." Misconception box with x³ + 1.
CUEWorked Example 1 (4x³ − 2x + 1: cubic trinomial), Worked Example 2 (3x² + 2/x is not a polynomial), Worked Example 3 (1x² + 0x − 5x⁰ → x² − 5, a quadratic binomial).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2.
Lesson 3 — Like terms and order · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: name x³ − x by terms and degree; is 4/x² a polynomial?; −3 + 5; 2 · x · x as a term.
SHOWTiles sorted into x², x and unit groups; a zero pair (x² and −x²) cancelling.
SAY"Like terms have the same variable and the same exponent: the same shape of tile. Add their coefficients. Keep the power."
WATCH"3x² + 2x = 5x³." Misconception box: tiles, then x = 2.
CUEWorked Example 1 (2x² + 3x − x² + 4 − 5x = x² − 2x + 4, with bins and zero pairs).
SAY"Descending order puts the highest degree first. That's the usual way. Each sign travels with its term."
WATCH"5 − 3x + x² is x² + 3x + 5." Misconception box.
CUEWorked Example 2 (4 + 2x³ − 6x + x³ − 1 → 3x³ − 6x + 3, and ascending 3 − 6x + 3x³).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3. Quiz next class (PO1–PO3).

Assessment

Quiz (22 marks)

PO1 (Q1–3), PO2 (Q4–6), PO3 (Q7–9). Given at the start of Lesson 4, before adding and subtracting (PO4). Q1: 4 · x · x · x and −x². Q5 (the tiles 2x² − x − 4): accept the terms in any order; the marks are for trinomial, degree 2 and quadratic. Q6 (Ben: x³ − 5 has degree 2) is the explain item: full marks need "highest exponent, not the number of terms" and the degree 3. Q7: 1 mark for each like term circled (5x², −2x²), minus 1 for each extra. Q8: the final mark is for descending order, x³ − 3x + 6.

Data and contexts used

  • This topic is almost all symbolic. The contexts are made up: the Kaur family's garden plots (x² + 3x and 2x² + x), the rectangles (2x + 1 by x + 3; 3x + 2 by x + 4), the triangle with perimeter 4x² + 3x + 6, and the tile patterns in the Challenge set.
  • No real-world data, so nothing is rounded.

Connections

  • Rational Numbers (topic 1): adding the opposite and integer sign rules drive subtraction.
  • Solving Equations (topic 5): removing brackets and collecting like terms on each side.
  • Linear Functions (topic 7): y = mx + b is a linear (degree 1) polynomial in x.
