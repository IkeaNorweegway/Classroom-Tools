Teacher notes

Solving Equations

Grade 8 Mathematics · Algebra & Functions · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcome 8A2 · Codes SE1–SE4

Overview

Big idea: multiplying or dividing both sides of an equation by the same non-zero number gives an equivalent equation, one with the same solution. That single fact lets students turn an equation with fraction or decimal coefficients into one with integers (multiply every term by the LCD, or by 10 or 100), which they already know how to solve from Grade 7. Brackets add a choice: distribute every term, or undo the factor first. Variables on both sides need the same "do it to both sides" move applied to an x-term. Problems close the loop: name the variable, write the equation, solve, and verify in the story, not just in the equation.

Sequence: doubling a balance with a half-bag, equivalent equations, clearing one and two denominators with the LCD, clearing one and two decimal places, a fraction of x as a bar (SE1) → distributing a negative, fraction or decimal factor, the two ways with brackets, two brackets, fraction and negative answers (SE2) → the Grade 7 balance with x on both sides, decimals and fractions on both sides, subtracting the smaller x-term, brackets with decimals (SE3) → the six-step problem routine: taxi fare, two gym plans, °F to °C, part of a recipe, sharing a cost with a fee, a perimeter (SE4).

Materials: algebra tiles or paper "bags and blocks" for balance models (including a half-bag cut-out), mini whiteboards, calculators (for checking only), grid paper, sticky notes for the error-hunt task, chart paper. Optional: an online balance-scale applet (for example the PhET "Equality Explorer" sim) to show that multiplying both pans keeps the balance.

Depth ceiling: one variable, linear only. Coefficients may be fractions or decimals; answers may be fractions, decimals or negative. Brackets with integer, negative, fraction or decimal factors, at most two brackets in one equation. No inequalities (topic 6) and no literal equations beyond substituting into F = 1.8C + 32. ★ items may have fractions over a binomial, such as x + 1/2 = x − 2/3, but these are stretch only.

Reading level: student pages are written at about Grade 3–4 (F&P N–P). The math words (rational coefficient, equivalent equations, multiple of an equation, LCD, clear the fractions, distributive property, factor, expand, variables on both sides, collect the x-terms, let x =, rate, verify) are the learning; each has a picture beside it.

Notation: the notes write "× 6" above an arrow to show every term being multiplied (the semult diagram). Students should write the multiplier beside each line of working and verify by substituting into the ORIGINAL equation, not the cleared one.

Watch forWhat to do
Multiplying only the fraction term by the LCD (1/2x + 3 = 5 → x + 3 = 5)Draw an arrow from the multiplier to every term, including the right side. Then check x in the original: x = 2 gives 4, not 5.
Using a common multiple that misses one denominator (× 2 for 1/2 and 1/3)Ask "does every fraction become a whole number?" before moving on. 2 × 1/3 is still a fraction.
× 10 for two decimal places (0.25 → 2.5)Count decimal places in every term first. The term with the most places sets the multiplier.
"1/2(x + 8) = 1/2x + 8" or "−2(x − 3) = −2x − 6"Tile groups: each group of (x + 3) has 3 small tiles. For signs, write each product separately: −2 × x, then −2 × (−3).
Dividing by the factor and forgetting the sign (−2(x − 3) = 10 → x − 3 = 5)Say "divide both sides by negative 2" aloud. Check the answer in the original.
Doing it to one side only (0.8x + 3 = 0.3x + 5 → 0.5x + 3 = 0.3x + 5)Back to the balance: take 0.3x off the left pan only and the scale tips. Write the operation under BOTH sides.
Adding the x-term instead of subtracting (1.1x + 3 = 5)Ask "what removes 0.3x?" The opposite of + 0.3x is − 0.3x.
Thinking a fraction or negative answer must be wrongShow that x = −3/4 checks in Worked Example 3 of Section 3. Clean answers are not a sign of correct work; the check is.
"x = 6. Done."Require the answer in words with units, and the verify step in the story. A wrong equation can still give a neat x.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Clearing fractions and decimals · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1–Q2 are Grade 7 equations, Q3 distributing a negative, Q4 an LCD, Q5 × 10 and × 100, Q6 a whole number times a fraction.
SHOWThe balance with a half-bag and 3 blocks against 5 blocks. "What happens if we double everything on both sides?"
SAY"Doubling both sides keeps the balance. The new equation is equivalent: it has the same solution. To clear fractions, multiply EVERY term by the LCD."
CUEWorked Example 1 (1/2x + 3 = 5 → x + 6 = 10, x = 4).
WATCH"Multiply only the fraction term." Misconception box: x = 2 fails the check.
CUEWorked Example 2 (1/2x + 1/3 = 2, LCD 6, x = 10/3), the bar for 1/3x = 4, Worked Example 3 (0.4x + 1.2 = 2, × 10), Worked Example 4 (0.25x − 1.5 = 0.75, × 100).
DO"Is it equivalent?" table as a whole-class vote on whiteboards, then pairs make one equivalent and one non-equivalent equation for a partner.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Brackets · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: solve 1/3x − 1 = 2; expand 3(x − 4); −3 × (−5); LCD of 4 and 6.
SHOWTile groups for 3(x + 2), then the distribution arrows for −2(x − 3) and 1/2(x + 8).
WATCH"1/2(x + 8) = 1/2x + 8." Misconception box with the tile groups.
SAY"With brackets you have two ways. Distribute and solve, or undo the factor first. Pick the one with nicer numbers. Both must give the same answer."
CUEWorked Example 1 (−2(x − 3) = 10 both ways, x = −2), Worked Example 2 (1/3(x + 6) = 4 with the bar), Worked Example 3 (0.5(x − 4) = 3.5), Worked Example 4 (two brackets, x = 12), Worked Example 5 (4(x + 2) = 3, x = −5/4).
DOTwo-ways race (10 min): half the class distributes first, half undoes the factor first, on the same four equations. Compare answers and which way was faster.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2. Quiz next class.
Lesson 3 — Variables on both sides · 60 min
DOQuiz (25 min) first, on SE1–SE2. Then the lesson.
SHOWThe Grade 7 balance 5x + 2 = 3x + 10. "What did we do last year?" Then the six-step flow: clear, expand, x-terms to one side, numbers to the other, divide, check.
CUEWorked Example 1 (1.5x + 2 = 0.5x + 6 both ways, x = 4).
WATCH"Do it to one side only." Misconception box: the balance tips.
CUEWorked Example 2 (1/2x + 1 = 1/3x + 2, × 6, x = 6).
WATCH× 6 on the left side only. Misconception box.
CUEWorked Example 3 (3x − 2 = 7x + 1, x = −3/4: subtract the smaller x-term), Worked Example 4 (0.4(x + 5) = 0.2x + 3).
DOError hunt (10 min): four worked solutions on chart paper, each with one mistake. Groups find and fix the mistake on a sticky note.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3.
Lesson 4 — Equation problems · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: solve 1/2x + 4 = 9; −2(x + 1) = 8; 0.6x + 1 = 0.2x + 3; 5% of $40.
SHOWThe taxi receipt. "What do we know? What don't we know? Call it x."
SAY"Let x = … first. Write each side. Solve. Then put the answer back in the story and answer in words with units."
CUEWorked Example 1 (taxi, 8 km), Worked Example 2 (FitNow and PowerGym, 5 months; the table shows the same answer), Worked Example 3 (50 °F in Lethbridge = 10 °C), Worked Example 4 (3/4 of a bannock recipe), Worked Example 5 (four friends share pizza and a $6 fee), Worked Example 6 (triangle perimeter).
WATCH"x = 6. Done." Misconception box: answer the story, not just the equation.
DOCore worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4.

Assessment

Mid-unit quiz (24 marks)

SE1 (Q1–5), SE2 (Q6–10). Given at the start of Lesson 3, after Sections 1–2 and before variables on both sides (SE3) and problems (SE4). Calculators for checking only. Q1: accept any common multiple (24) for the first mark if the new equation is right. Q3 and Q10 need a written check for the last mark. Q5 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need "every term must be multiplied" and the corrected answer x = 3. Q8: accept x = 5/2 or 2.5; a sign slip at −2x = −5 loses only the last mark.

Worksheets and exits

Core (★) covers SE1–SE4 with a diagram on most questions: 1/4x + 2 = 5, Maya's "only the fraction term" error, 0.6x + 1.5 = 4.5, which equations are equivalent, 3/4x = 9 with a bar, −3(x − 5) = 12, Leo's 1/2(x + 10) error, 0.5(x + 6) = 4.5, a fraction answer with x on both sides, Ravi's one-side error, fractions on both sides, and a taxi fare. Challenge (★★) adds LCD 6 with x on both sides, a negative fraction factor, a decimal bracket on both sides, fractions over binomials, two gyms over a year, the temperature where °F = °C (−40), a rectangle with fraction sides, and 2/3 of a muffin recipe. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it. Exit tickets: one per section, each ending with an "explain how you know" item.

Data and contexts used

  • The taxi fare ($3.50 to start plus $1.75 per km) is made up but close to typical Alberta city rates; the second company at $2 per km is made up.
  • The gym plans (FitNow $40 plus $22.50 a month; PowerGym $30.50 a month; Gym A and Gym B in the Challenge set) are made up. The names are invented.
  • F = 1.8C + 32 is the real conversion. The 50 °F spring day in Lethbridge and the −4 °F Calgary morning are made up but realistic.
  • The phone plans ($20 plus $2.50 per GB and $32 plus $1 per GB) are made up.
  • The bannock and muffin recipe amounts, the pizza cost with a $6 delivery fee, the cabin bill with a $15 cleaning fee, and the triangle and rectangle perimeters are made up.
  • The GST (5%) in retrieval is the real federal rate; Alberta has no provincial sales tax.

Connections

  • Grade 7 Linear Equations: one- and two-step equations and x on both sides with integers; this topic adds rational coefficients.
  • Rational Numbers (topic 1): LCDs, operations with negative fractions and decimals, and checking with fractions.
  • Polynomials (topic 4): like terms and subtracting a bracket (subtract every term) appear inside SE2 and SE3.
  • Inequalities (topic 6): substituting to check a solution is the same skill; solving inequalities by inverse operations waits for Grade 9.
  • Linear Functions (topic 7): "when do two plans cost the same?" is where two lines cross.
