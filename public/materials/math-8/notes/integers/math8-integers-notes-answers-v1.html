Section 1 · Multiplying with models

Learning target: I can show the product of two integers with counters or a number line.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
integera whole number, its opposite, or zero…, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …
zero pairone positive and one negative counter; together they make 0(+1) + (−1) = 0
productthe answer to a multiplicationthe product of 3 and −2 is −6

Yellow counters are positive (+1). Red counters are negative (−1).

Positive × negative: groups

3 × (−2) means 3 groups of −2.

The same thing on a number line: 3 jumps of −2, starting at 0.

Worked Example 1 — A temperature drop

In Edmonton, the temperature drops 4 °C every hour for 3 hours. What is the total change?

3 × (−4) = −12

The temperature changes by −12 °C. It is 12 degrees colder.

Explain it Why do we multiply 3 × (−4) and not 3 × 4?
Answer The change each hour is a drop, so it is negative (−4). Three hours means three groups of −4.

Negative × positive or negative: take away groups

(−3) × 2 means take away 3 groups of +2. Start with zero pairs so there is something to take away.

Worked Example 2 — (−3) × 2

Start with 6 zero pairs (worth 0).

Take away 3 groups of +2 (6 yellow counters).

What is left? 6 red counters. (−3) × 2 = −6

Worked Example 3 — (−3) × (−2)

Start with 6 zero pairs.

Take away 3 groups of −2 (6 red counters).

What is left? 6 yellow counters. (−3) × (−2) = +6

Explain it Why did we start with zero pairs in Worked Examples 2 and 3?
Answer We needed counters to take away, but we did not want to change the value. Zero pairs add counters but are worth 0.

    Answer 2 groups of −5.

    Answer Four jumps of −2 from 0: lands on −8.

Section 2 · Sign rules

Learning target: I can use a pattern to explain the sign rules for multiplying integers.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
signpositive (+) or negative (−)the sign of −6 is negative
patternsomething that repeats in a predictable way6, 4, 2, 0, −2…

Find the pattern

The first number goes down by 1 each time. Watch what happens to the answer.

MultiplicationAnswer
3 × (−2)−6
2 × (−2)−4
1 × (−2)−2
0 × (−2)0
(−1) × (−2)2
(−2) × (−2)4

Each answer goes up by 2. So the pattern keeps going past zero into the positive numbers.

Sign rules for × (and ÷)
SignsAnswer
(+) × (+)positive
(−) × (−)positive
(+) × (−)negative
(−) × (+)negative

Count the negatives. An even number of negatives → positive. An odd number → negative.

Watch out — a common mistake

"Two negatives make a positive, so −3 + (−4) = 7."

Why it is wrong: That rule is for multiplying and dividing. For adding, two negatives make a bigger negative.

What is true: −3 + (−4) = −7 (adding). (−3) × (−4) = +12 (multiplying).

Watch out — a common mistake

"The answer takes the sign of the bigger number, so (−2) × 6 is positive."

Why it is wrong: That idea comes from adding integers. When you multiply, the size of the numbers does not decide the sign.

What is true: Count the negative signs. (−2) × 6 = −12 (one negative). (−2) × (−6) = +12 (two negatives).

Worked Example 4 — Three numbers

(−2) × 3 × (−5)

Count the negatives: 2 (even) → the answer is positive.

Multiply the sizes: 2 × 3 × 5 = 30

Answer: +30

Explain it Why does an even number of negatives give a positive answer?
Answer Every pair of negatives multiplies to a positive, so if all the negatives pair up, nothing negative is left.

Section 3 · Dividing integers

Learning target: I can divide integers and use the sign rules for division.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
quotientthe answer to a division(−12) ÷ 3 = −4, the quotient is −4
inversean operation that undoes another÷ undoes ×
dividend / divisorthe number being divided / the number you divide byin −12 ÷ 3, −12 is the dividend

Division undoes multiplication. If 3 × (−4) = −12, then (−12) ÷ 3 = −4.

Worked Example 5 — Sharing: (−12) ÷ 3

A debt of $12 is shared by 3 friends. How much does each owe?

Split 12 red counters into 3 equal groups.

Each friend owes $4: (−12) ÷ 3 = −4

Worked Example 6 — How many groups: (−12) ÷ (−3)

How many groups of −3 make −12?

4 groups of −3 make −12. So (−12) ÷ (−3) = 4

Explain it Check Worked Example 6 with multiplication. What do you multiply?
Answer 4 × (−3) = −12. ✓ The quotient times the divisor gives the dividend.
Sign rules for ÷

Same as ×. Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative.

(+) ÷ (+) = + · (−) ÷ (−) = + · (+) ÷ (−) = − · (−) ÷ (+) = −

Section 4 · Order of operations and problems

Learning target: I can use the order of operations with integers and solve integer problems.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
order of operationsBrackets, Exponents, Divide and Multiply (left to right), Add and Subtract (left to right)BEDMAS
meanadd the values, then divide by how many there arethe mean of −2, 0, 5 is 1
Worked Example 7 — Use the order

(−3) × 4 + (−10) ÷ 2

Step 1: × and ÷ first. (−3) × 4 = −12 and (−10) ÷ 2 = −5

Step 2: Add. −12 + (−5) = −17

Explain it Why can't we start with 4 + (−10)?
Answer Multiplication and division come before addition, so we must do (−3) × 4 and (−10) ÷ 2 first.
Worked Example 8 — Mean temperature

Lake Louise had these low temperatures over 5 days: −8 °C, −12 °C, −5 °C, 0 °C, −10 °C.

Add: −8 + (−12) + (−5) + 0 + (−10) = −35

Divide by 5: (−35) ÷ 5 = −7

The mean low was −7 °C.

Explain it Why is the mean negative?
Answer Most of the temperatures were below zero, so their total is negative, and a negative divided by a positive is negative.
Your turn
  1. a) 5 + (−2) × 3 = −1
  2. b) (−4 − 2) × (−3) = 18
  3. c) (−24) ÷ (−6) − 7 = −3
  4. d) A Calgary chinook raised the temperature from −18 °C to 6 °C in 4 hours. What was the mean change per hour?
Answer Change = 6 − (−18) = 24. 24 ÷ 4 = +6 °C per hour.
