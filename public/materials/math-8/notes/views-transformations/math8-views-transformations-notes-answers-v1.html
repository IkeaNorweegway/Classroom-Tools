Section 1 · Top, front and side views

Learning target: I can draw the top, front and side views of an object made of cubes.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
viewwhat an object looks like from one directionthe view from the front
top viewlooking straight down from abovelike a map
front viewlooking straight at the frontwhat you see from the "front" arrow
side viewlooking straight at the right sidewe use the right side
isometric drawinga 3-D drawing on dot paper; corners show three facesthe cube drawings in these notes

Each view is flat. It shows only the outline you see from that direction. Count the squares in each view: Top 5, Front 3 columns: 2, 1, 1, Right side 2 columns.

Watch out — a common mistake

"The side view shows all the cubes, even the ones hidden in the back."

Why it is wrong: A view is what you see from one place. Cubes behind other cubes are hidden in that view.

What is true: A view shows the outline only: how tall each column looks from that direction, not how many cubes are behind it.

Worked Example 1 — Draw the views

Top: count the columns that have at least one cube. 3 squares

Front: the tallest stack in each column from left to right. 2, 1

Right side: the tallest stack in each row, front to back. 1, 2

Explain it In the front view, why do we use the tallest stack in each column?
Answer From the front, a tall stack hides the shorter stacks behind it. You only see the tallest one in each column.
Your turn

Draw the top, front and right side views on the grids.

Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. What does a top view show?
    Answer What the object looks like from directly above: which spots have at least one cube.
  2. An object is one stack of 4 cubes. Draw its front view.
    Answer One column, 4 squares tall.
  3. Jo's side view shows 5 squares in a column for a stack that is 3 tall with 2 cubes behind it. What did Jo do wrong?
    Answer She counted hidden cubes. The side view shows the outline only: 3 tall.
  4. Two different objects can have the same front view. Is that true? Give an example.
    Answer True. A single row of 3 cubes and an L made of 4 cubes (one behind) both look like 3 squares from the front.
  5. ★ Stretch How many views do you need to be sure what an object looks like? Why?
    Answer Usually at least three (top, front, side), because one view hides the depth.

Section 2 · Building from views and turning objects

Learning target: I can build an object from its views, and draw the views after a quarter turn.

Words to know

Build from views

Use the top view as a floor plan. Then use the front and side views to decide how tall each stack is.

Worked Example 2 — Build from views

Top view: an L of 3 squares. Front view: columns 2, 1. Side view: columns 1, 2.

Step 1: Put one cube on each square of the top view.

Step 2: The front view says the left column is 2 tall.

Step 3: The side view says the back row is 2 tall. So the back-left stack has 2 cubes.

Explain it Why do you need both the front and the side view to decide which stack is tall?
Answer The front view tells which column is tall, and the side view tells which row. Together they show the exact stack.

Turn the object

Turn this object a quarter turn clockwise (looking down from the top). The old right end swings around to become the new front.

Before:

After a quarter turn clockwise:

The new front view is one column, 2 squares tall. The new side view is 3 columns: 1, 1, 2.

Your turn

An object has front view 3, 1 and side view 3 (one row). Draw its views after a half turn (180°).

Answer After 180° the front view is reversed: 1, 3. The side view is still one column 3 tall. The top view is reversed left to right.
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. Which view is like a floor plan?
    Answer The top view.
  2. A row of cubes: heights 1, 2, 3 from left to right (front view). What is the front view after a half turn?
    Answer 3, 2, 1
  3. Can you build an object from its top view only? Why or why not?
    Answer No. The top view does not show how tall each stack is.
  4. After a quarter turn, which old view becomes the new front?
    Answer One of the side views. For a clockwise turn seen from above, the old right side becomes the new front.
  5. ★ Stretch Find the fewest cubes that fit a top view of 4 squares in a row, a front view of 1, 3, 1, 2, and a side view of one column 3 tall.
    Answer 1 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 7 cubes (one row, no hidden cubes).

Section 3 · Transformations on a grid

Learning target: I can translate, reflect and rotate a shape on a coordinate grid, and give the new coordinates.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
transformationa move of a shapeslide, flip, turn
translation (slide)every point moves the same distance and direction4 left, 2 up
reflection (flip)a mirror image over a linereflect in the x-axis
rotation (turn)a turn about a point90° counterclockwise about (0, 0)
imagethe shape after the move; its points get a ′ (prime)A → A′

Translation

A(1, 1) → A′(−4, 3). Every x goes down by 5. Every y goes up by 2.

B(4, 1) → B′(−1, 3) C(1, 3) → C′(−4, 5)

Reflection in the x-axis

A(1, 1) → A′(1, −1). The x stays the same. The y becomes its opposite.

C(1, 3) → C′(1, −3)

Rotation: 90° counterclockwise about (0, 0)

A(1, 1) → A′(−1, 1) B(4, 1) → B′(−1, 4) C(1, 3) → C′(−3, 1)

Rule: (x, y) → (−y, x)

Coordinate rules

Translate (a right, b up): (x, y) → (x + a, y + b)

Reflect in the x-axis: (x, y) → (x, −y) · Reflect in the y-axis: (x, y) → (−x, y)

Rotate 90° counterclockwise: (x, y) → (−y, x) · Rotate 180°: (x, y) → (−x, −y)

Explain it Check the 90° rule with point B(4, 1). Does the picture agree?
Answer (−y, x) = (−1, 4). In the picture B′ is at (−1, 4). ✓
Your turn

Triangle P(2, 1), Q(5, 1), R(2, 4).

  1. a) Translate 3 down: P′(2, −2) Q′(5, −2) R′(2, 1)
  2. b) Reflect in the y-axis: P′(−2, 1) Q′(−5, 1) R′(−2, 4)
  3. c) Rotate 180° about (0, 0): P′(−2, −1) Q′(−5, −1) R′(−2, −4)
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. A(3, 2) is translated 4 left and 1 up. Where is A′?
    Answer (−1, 3)
  2. B(−2, 5) is reflected in the x-axis. Where is B′?
    Answer (−2, −5)
  3. C(4, 0) is rotated 180° about the origin. Where is C′?
    Answer (−4, 0)
  4. What stays the same in a reflection in the y-axis? What changes?
    Answer The y-coordinate stays the same. The x-coordinate becomes its opposite.
  5. ★ Stretch D(2, 3) is rotated 90° counterclockwise and then 90° counterclockwise again. Where does it end up? What single move does the same thing?
    Answer (2, 3) → (−3, 2) → (−2, −3). Same as a 180° rotation.

Section 4 · Congruence and combined moves

Learning target: I can do two transformations in a row, find the original from the image, and explain why the image is congruent.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
congruentthe same size and the same shape≅
combined transformationtwo or more moves in a rowreflect, then translate
Watch out — a common mistake

"A flipped or turned shape is a different shape, so it is not congruent."

Why it is wrong: Slides, flips and turns never stretch or shrink a shape. Every side and angle stays the same.

What is true: The image of a translation, reflection or rotation is always congruent to the original.

Worked Example 4 — Two moves

Triangle A(1, 1), B(4, 1), C(1, 3). Reflect in the y-axis, then translate 2 down.

Reflect: A(−1, 1), B(−4, 1), C(−1, 3)

Translate 2 down: A′(−1, −1), B′(−4, −1), C′(−1, 1)

Side AB is 3 units long before. A′B′ is 3 units long after. The triangles are congruent.

Explain it How can you check that the two triangles are congruent?
Answer Compare matching sides (and angles). AB = A′B′ = 3, AC = A′C′ = 2, and both have a right angle at A.
Worked Example 5 — Work backwards

After a translation 3 right and 1 up, the image is P′(2, 4). Where was P?

Undo the move: 3 left and 1 down.

P = (2 − 3, 4 − 1) = (−1, 3)

Explain it Why do we do the opposite move to find the original?
Answer The original moved to the image. Going back the other way undoes the move.
Your turn
  1. a) Triangle D(1, 2), E(3, 2), F(3, 5). Translate 4 left, then reflect in the x-axis. Give D′, E′, F′.
Answer After translate: (−3, 2), (−1, 2), (−1, 5). After reflect: D′(−3, −2), E′(−1, −2), F′(−1, −5).
  1. b) An image point is Q′(−5, 2) after a reflection in the y-axis. Where was Q?
Answer (5, 2)
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. What does congruent mean?
    Answer Same size and same shape.
  2. Are the images of translations, reflections and rotations always congruent? Why?
    Answer Yes. They move the shape without changing any lengths or angles.
  3. A(2, −1): rotate 180°, then translate 1 right. Where is A′?
    Answer (−2, 1) then (−1, 1).
  4. After a reflection in the x-axis, B′ is (4, −6). Where was B?
    Answer (4, 6)
  5. ★ Stretch A shape is reflected in the x-axis and then in the y-axis. Which single transformation does the same thing?
    Answer A rotation of 180° about the origin: (x, y) → (x, −y) → (−x, −y).
