Watch out — a common mistake

"a² + b² = c² works for every triangle."

Why it is wrong: Try a triangle with sides 4, 5 and 6 (no right angle). 4² + 5² = 16 + 25 = 41, but 6² = 36. They don't match.

What is true: The theorem only works for right triangles.

Watch out — a common mistake

"The side labelled c is always the hypotenuse" or "the hypotenuse is the side at the bottom."

Why it is wrong: Letters and positions change from picture to picture.

What is true: Find the right angle first. The side across from it is the hypotenuse. It is always the longest side.

Section 3 · Finding a leg

Learning target: I can find a leg when I know the hypotenuse and the other leg.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
leg
subtract

If you know c, subtract to find a missing leg: a² = c² − b².

Watch out — a common mistake

"To find a missing leg, add the squares, like before."

Why it is wrong: Adding gives a number bigger than c². But a leg must be shorter than the hypotenuse.

What is true: Subtract: a² = c² − b². Then check: the leg is shorter than c.

Worked Example 4 — A ladder against a wall

A 5 m ladder leans against a wall. Its foot is 3 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse (across from the right angle).

a² = 5² − 3² = 25 − =

a = √16 = m

Check: 4 m is shorter than the 5 m ladder ✓

Explain it How did you know the ladder is the hypotenuse?
