Section 1 · Strict and non-strict

One answer or many?

An equation has one answer. x + 2 = 5 only works for x = 3.

Look at the sign on Highway 2. You may drive 110 km/h. You may also drive 100, or 95.5.

Let s be your speed. Then s ≤ 110. That is an inequality. It has many solutions.

Strict or non-strict

< and > are strict. The boundary is left out.

≤ and ≥ are non-strict. The line under the symbol means "or equal to".

x ≥ 16 means x > 16 or x = 16.

The small end of the symbol points at the smaller amount.

Worked Example 1 — Words to symbols

Write each one with symbols.

  1. a) You must be at least 122 cm tall. Let h = height. h ≥ 122.
  2. b) Fewer than 30 students fit on the bus. Let n = students. n < 30.
  3. c) The bag holds no more than 23 kg. Let m = mass. m ≤ 23.
  4. d) It is colder than −5 °C. Let t = temperature. t < −5.
Watch out — a common mistake

"x < 3 includes 3. 3 is right there in it."

Why it is wrong: Try it. Is 3 < 3 true? No. 3 is equal to 3, not less. So 3 is not a solution.

What is true: x < 3 leaves 3 out. x ≤ 3 lets it in: 3 ≤ 3 is true.

Watch out — a common mistake

"At least 16 means less than 16."

Why it is wrong: "Least" sounds like "less". But at least 16 means 16 is the smallest you can be. You can be 16 or older.

What is true: at least means ≥. At most means ≤.

Worked Example 2 — Read it both ways

3 < x and x > 3 say the same thing.

Step 1: Read from left to right: "3 is less than x."

Step 2: Turn it around. If 3 is less than x, then x is greater than 3.

Step 3: Write it with x first: x > 3. The symbol still points at the 3.

In the same way, −1 ≥ y is the same as y ≤ −1.

Watch out — a common mistake

"3 < x means x is less than 3."

Why it is wrong: That reads the symbol backwards. Read from left to right: 3 is less than x.

What is true: 3 < x means x > 3. Test 5: is 3 < 5 true? Yes.

Section 2 · Inequalities on a number line

Learning target: I can graph an inequality and write one from a graph.

Words to know

Dot, then direction

Every inequality graph has two parts.

Part 1 is the dot. < and > get an open dot ○. ≤ and ≥ get a closed dot ●.

Part 2 is the ray. Greater numbers are to the right. Less numbers are to the left.

Graphing an inequality
SymbolDotRay
x > aopen ○right
x ≥ aclosed ●right
x < aopen ○left
x ≤ aclosed ●left
Worked Example 1 — Graph x ≤ −1

Step 1: The boundary is −1.

Step 2: ≤ lets −1 in. Draw a closed dot.

Step 3: "Less" is to the left. Draw the ray left.

Step 4: Test −3: is −3 ≤ −1? Yes. −3 is on the ray. ✓

Watch out — a common mistake

"x > 2 points left. The symbol > points left."

Why it is wrong: The symbol's shape is not a direction. x > 2 means numbers bigger than 2, like 3, 5 and 100. They sit to the right.

What is true: Greater is to the right. Test a number on your ray to be sure.

Watch out — a common mistake

"x < 3 gets a closed dot at 3."

Why it is wrong: 3 < 3 is false, so 3 is not a solution. A closed dot would say 3 works.

What is true: Strict < and > get an open dot. Only ≤ and ≥ get a closed dot.

Section 3 · Checking solutions

Learning target: I can check if a number is a solution by putting it in.

Words to know

Put it in and compare

Is x = 4 a solution of 2x + 1 > 7? Put 4 in for x.

The left side is 9. 9 is greater than 7. So 4 is a solution.

Now try x = 2. 2(2) + 1 = 5. Is 5 > 7? No. So 2 is not a solution.

Checking a solution
  1. 1. Substitute the number for the variable.
  2. 2. Work out each side.
  3. 3. Ask: is the statement true? True means it is a solution.

Test the boundary too. It decides open or closed.

Watch out — a common mistake

"The ride needs riders at least 122 cm tall. So h < 122."

Why it is wrong: At least 122 means 122 or taller. A 130 cm rider can ride. A 115 cm rider cannot.

What is true: h ≥ 122. Test 130: 130 ≥ 122 is true. ✓

Watch out — a common mistake

"At most 12 people fit in the van. Draw a ray to the left of 12."

Why it is wrong: A ray would include 11.5 people and −3 people. People come in whole numbers, from 0 up.

What is true: The solutions are the dots 0, 1, 2, … , 12.
