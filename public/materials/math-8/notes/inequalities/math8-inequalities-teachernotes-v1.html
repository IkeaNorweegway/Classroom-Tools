Teacher notes

Inequalities

Grade 8 Mathematics · Algebra & Functions · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcome 8A3 · Codes IQ1–IQ4

Overview

Big idea: an equation usually has one solution; an inequality usually has many. The four symbols split into strict (<, >: the boundary is left out) and non-strict (≤, ≥: the boundary is in). On a number line, the dot says whether the boundary is in (closed) or out (open), and the ray says which way the solutions go (right for greater, left for less). Substitution is the test for everything: put a number in, work out each side, and ask "is it true?" Testing the boundary decides the dot; testing a number on each side decides the direction. Real situations (speed limits, ride heights, budgets, loads) add one more decision: if only whole numbers make sense (people, tickets), the graph is dots, not a ray.

Sequence: one answer or many, the four symbols and their words (at least, at most, no more than, fewer than), strict vs non-strict, reading 3 < x both ways, true-or-false statements (IQ1) → dot then direction, the graphing table, boundaries on either side of the symbol, fraction and decimal boundaries, writing an inequality from a graph (IQ2) → substitute and compare, testing the boundary, negatives, fractions and decimals, checking whether a graph matches (IQ3) → from signs to symbols, minimum and maximum, an elevator load, a ticket budget, any number or whole numbers (IQ4).

Materials: a long floor number line (masking tape, −10 to 10) with paper open and closed dots and string rays, mini whiteboards with number lines printed on them, photos of real signs (Highway 2 at 110 km/h, a playground zone at 30 km/h, a ride height marker, an elevator capacity plate), sticky notes, chart paper. Optional: Desmos (desmos.com/calculator) typing x > 2 or x ≤ −1 to show the shaded region on one axis.

Depth ceiling: write, graph and test only. No solving by inverse operations, and no flipping the sign when multiplying by a negative (both Grade 9). Expressions inside an inequality (2x + 1 > 7, 12n ≤ 40) are checked by substitution and by testing values in a table, never rearranged. One boundary per inequality; compound inequalities ("at least 107 cm and under 137 cm") appear only as ★ stretch items checked one condition at a time.

Reading level: student pages are written at about Grade 3–4 (F&P N–P). The math words (inequality, solution, strict, non-strict, boundary, open dot, closed dot, ray, substitute, test value, limit, minimum, maximum, whole-number solution) are the learning; each has a picture beside it.

Notation: the notes use ○ and ● for open and closed dots and always write the variable first after rewriting (3 < x becomes x > 3). Students should write the test they did, such as "test 5: 3 < 5 ✓", next to every graph.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Strict and non-strict · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1–Q2 ordering negatives and fractions, Q3–Q4 substitution (needed in Lesson 3), Q5 true or false for an equation, Q6 placing decimals on a number line.
SHOWThe Highway 2 sign. "What speeds are legal? Is 110 legal? 95.5? 111?" Agree that s ≤ 110 has many solutions.
SAY"An inequality has many solutions. < and > are strict: the boundary is left out. ≤ and ≥ have a line under them: 'or equal to.' The boundary is in."
CUEThe four-symbols table, Worked Example 1 (ride height h ≥ 122, bus n < 30, bag m ≤ 23, cold t < −5).
WATCH"x < 3 includes 3." Misconception box: 3 < 3 is false.
WATCH"At least means less than." Misconception box with the 16 ✓ 18 ✓ 14 ✗ line.
CUEWorked Example 2 (3 < x is x > 3).
WATCHReading 3 < x backwards. Misconception box.
CUEWorked Example 3 (true or false table, including 1/2 ≤ 0.5).
DOPhrase sort (10 min): pairs sort 16 cards ("no more than", "over", "at least", "fewer than", "minimum", "maximum" …) under the four symbols.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Inequalities on a number line · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: which is greater, −7 or −4; write "y is at least 3" in symbols; 2 × (−3) + 5; is 6 ≥ 6 true?
SHOWThe floor number line. Call out "x > 2": students holding numbers greater than 2 step forward. Then "x ≥ 2": the student holding 2 joins.
SAY"Two parts: the dot, then the direction. Open dot for strict, closed dot for non-strict. Greater goes right, less goes left. Then test one number."
CUEThe graphing table, Worked Example 1 (x ≤ −1, test −3).
WATCH"x > 2 points left." Misconception box.
WATCHA closed dot for x < 3. Misconception box.
CUEWorked Example 2 (4 > x: rewrite first), Worked Example 3 (x ≥ 1.5 and y < −1/2), Worked Example 4 (write the inequality from a graph).
DOWhiteboard rounds (10 min): teacher shows a graph, students write the inequality; then the reverse.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2. Quiz next class.

DOQuiz (25 min) first, on IQ1–IQ2. Then the lesson.

Assessment

Mid-unit quiz (22 marks)

IQ1 (Q1–4, Q9), IQ2 (Q5–8, Q10). Given at the start of Lesson 3, after Sections 1–2 and before checking solutions (IQ3) and real-world inequalities (IQ4). No calculator needed. Q3 b): the reason (−1 is right of −3) is not required for the mark. Q7: one mark for the boundary −2, one for >. Q8: any correct test earns the third mark. Q9 previews IQ4 but is marked as IQ1 (words to symbols). Q10 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need "≤ includes 5, < does not" and "the rays are the same; only the dot changes".

Worksheets and exits

Core (★) covers IQ1–IQ4 with a diagram on most questions: words to symbols, true or false (6 ≥ 6, 0.75 ≤ 3/4), Ben's "x < 5 includes 5", rewriting with the variable first, graphing x > −1 and y ≤ 2.5, writing inequalities from graphs, fixing Ria's left-pointing x > 3, testing 4 and 3 in 3x − 2 > 9, testing three values in 2x + 5 ≥ 1, ride height and playground zone, and a canoe for at most 3 people (dots). Challenge (★★) adds inequalities that include 4 but not 5, sorting by whether −2 is included, a −3/4 boundary written two ways, the smallest whole-number solution of 4x − 3 > 13, checking Jen's graph of 12 − 2x ≤ 4, a negative fraction test, an elevator load with a cart, and concert tickets with an order fee. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it. Exit tickets: one per section, each ending with an "explain how you know" item.

Data and contexts used

  • Highway 2 (Queen Elizabeth II Highway) has a 110 km/h limit; playground zones in Alberta cities are 30 km/h. Both are real; check local signs, since some zone times and limits vary by city.
  • Alberta's learner's licence age is 14 and the class 5 (GDL) road test is 16; the notes use "at least 16" for driving, which matches the probationary licence. Voting age 18 is real.
  • Alberta's general minimum wage is $15 an hour (since 2018); real at the time of writing.
  • The ride heights at Calaway Park (at least 122 cm; at least 107 cm and under 137 cm) are made up but typical of real ride limits; actual limits vary by ride and change by season.
  • The elevator plate (maximum 1000 kg), the 23 kg bag limit, the 4.2 m bridge clearance and the 30-student bus are realistic values but made up for the problems.
  • Movie tickets at $12, bus tickets at $3.50, snacks at $4, concert tickets at $25 with a $10 fee, and all budgets are made up.
  • The Calgary forecast (a low below −8 °C) and the −5 °C and −10 °C temperatures are made up but realistic for an Alberta winter.

Connections

  • Solving Equations (topic 5): substitution to check a solution is the same skill; an equation's solution is the boundary of the matching inequality.
  • Linear Functions (topic 7): a horizontal line y = b and the regions above and below it preview Grade 9 graphing.
