Teacher notes

Similarity & Dilations

Grade 8 Mathematics · Geometry & Measurement · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcomes 8G1.1, 8G1.2 · Codes SD1–SD4

Overview

Big idea: similar shapes have the same shape but may differ in size: matching angles are equal and every pair of matching sides is multiplied by the same number, the scale factor k (copy ÷ original). One k links the whole shape, so it finds missing sides in both directions (× k to the copy, ÷ k back) and decides similarity (one k for every pair, or not similar). Lengths scale by k but areas scale by k², because area has two directions. Maps, blueprints and models are scale drawings. On a grid, a dilation about (0, 0) multiplies both coordinates by k, and rays from the origin pass through each point and its image.

Sequence: zooming a photo vs stretching it, similar means same shape, matching angles by arcs and sides by order, long ÷ short to compare rectangles, all circles and all squares are similar, writing △ABC ~ △RST in matching order (SD1) → k = copy ÷ original, enlargement (k > 1), reduction (0 < k < 1), k = 1, missing sides with × k and ÷ k, one k for every side or not similar (SD2) → counting squares to see k², a bigger garden, Edmonton's river valley trail map, a garage blueprint, a model of the Calgary Tower, a photo enlargement (SD3) → dilations about the origin on a grid: enlarge with k = 2, reduce with k = 1/2, check similarity with side lengths and rays, find k from an image (SD4).

Materials: 1 cm grid paper, rulers, protractors, scissors, a phone or tablet to show pinch-zoom vs stretch, a printed photo and an enlargement of it, a local trail or city map with a scale bar (Edmonton river valley, Elk Island Park), a simple house or garage blueprint, string for rays, coloured pencils. Optional: GeoGebra "Dilate from Point" tool.

Depth ceiling: scale factor as a decimal or simple fraction; area scales by k² (no volume scaling). Dilations about the origin only, with k given or read from the image. Similar triangles are decided by equal angles and proportional sides, not by named similarity postulates. Missing sides use k, not cross-multiplication of proportions (both are fine if a student brings one). Map scales are written 1 cm : n km or 1 : n.

Reading level: student pages are written at about Grade 3–4. The math words (similar, ~, scale copy, corresponding sides and angles, proportional, original, image, scale factor, k, enlargement, reduction, k², scale drawing, scale, blueprint, scale model, dilation, centre of dilation, A′) are the learning; each has a picture beside it.

Notation: k = copy length ÷ original length, always in that order. Images are named with primes: A → A′. Similarity statements list matching vertices in the same order (△ABC ~ △RST means A ↔ R). Ticks mark equal sides and arcs mark equal angles, as in Triangle Theorems.

Watch forWhat to do
"Similar means the same size"Same shape, maybe a different size. Zoom a photo on a phone (similar) and then stretch it in one direction (not similar). "Same size and shape" is congruent, a Grade 7 word.
Subtracting to find k (6 − 2 = 4)Ask "what times 2 makes 6?" Then test the subtraction on the other side: 3 − 1 = 2, not 4, so it can't be one number for the whole shape.
k upside down (original ÷ copy)Say "copy over original" and predict first: is the copy bigger? Then k must be more than 1.
Matching the wrong sides on a turned copyMatch the angles first (by arcs), then write the vertices in matching order. The side between A and B matches the side between the matching letters.
"Bigger means similar": deciding by one pair of sidesCheck every pair: 10 ÷ 5 = 2 but 7 ÷ 3 ≈ 2.33. Two values of k means not similar.
"Double the sides, double the area"Count squares: a 1 by 1 square doubled is 2 by 2 = 4 squares. Then k = 3: 9 squares. Area grows by k × k.
Using the map scale on an area (3 cm × 2 cm = 6, × 2 km = 12 km²)Convert each length first (6 km by 4 km), then multiply: 24 km². Or use k²: 6 × 4 = 24.
Adding k to each coordinate in a dilationA dilation multiplies. Draw the result of adding: the triangle slides but does not grow. Rays from (0, 0) miss the "image".
Multiplying only the x-coordinateBoth coordinates, every point. Check with a ray: (0, 0), A and A′ must be on one straight line.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Similar shapes · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1 previews ratios in lowest terms, Q2 equivalent fractions, Q3 multiplying a decimal, Q4 rectangle area (needed in Lesson 3), Q5 a missing factor (finding k), Q6 plotting a point (Lesson 4).
SHOWA photo zoomed in, then stretched. "Which one still looks right? Why?"
SAY"Similar shapes have the same shape. The size can change. Every length is multiplied by the same number, and the angles stay the same."
WATCH"Similar means the same size." Misconception box: the 3-4-5 and 6-8-10 triangles with the same angles.
CUEWorked Example 1 (rectangles A, B, C: long ÷ short is 1.5, 1.5, 2), Worked Example 2 (two right triangles, every side × 2).
DOSort cut-out shapes: which pairs are always similar (circles, squares, equilateral triangles) and which only sometimes (rectangles, triangles)?
CUEWorked Example 3 (all circles, all squares), Worked Example 4 (a turned copy: match by arcs, △ABC ~ △RST).
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Scale factor · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: 10 ÷ 4; 15 × 0.4; slope through (0, 1) and (2, 5); solve 3n = 12.
SHOWThe 2 cm by 1 cm rectangle and its 6 cm by 3 cm copy. "What one number turns the original into the copy?" Then the number line: reduction, same size, enlargement.
CUEWorked Example 1 (4 by 2 → 10 by 5, k = 2.5).
WATCH"Subtract to find the scale factor." Misconception box.
CUEWorked Example 2 (photo 15 by 10 → small print 6 by 4, k = 0.4, a reduction), Worked Example 3 (missing sides: × 3 to the copy, ÷ 3 back), Worked Example 4 (5 by 3 and 10 by 7: two values of k, not similar).
DOPairs draw a 3 by 2 rectangle on grid paper and copy it with k = 2 and k = 0.5. Partners check by dividing.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2.
Lesson 3 — Area and scale drawings · 60 min
DOWarm-up interleaved: k for 8 cm → 2 cm; 3.5 × 4; area of a 6 m by 4 m rectangle; 1.5².
DOCount the squares: 1 by 1, 2 by 2, 3 by 3 on grid paper, then the 3 by 2 rectangle and its k = 2 copy. Students fill in the table and spot k².
WATCH"Double the sides, double the area." Misconception box: area 6 vs area 24.
CUEWorked Example 1 (garden 3 m by 2 m, k = 2: 24 m² two ways), Worked Example 2 (river valley trail map, 4.5 cm at 1 cm : 2 km = 9 km), Worked Example 3 (garage blueprint, 1 cm : 0.5 m, 24 m²), Worked Example 4 (Calgary Tower model, 191 ÷ 5 = 38.2 cm), Worked Example 5 (photo 10 by 15, k = 2, four times the paper).
DOMeasure a real distance on a local map with its scale bar. Optional: measure the classroom and draw it at 1 cm : 1 m.
DOYour turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3. Quiz at the start of next class.
Lesson 4 — Dilations on a grid · 60 min
DOQuiz (25 min) first. Then the lesson.
SHOW△ABC with A(1, 1), B(3, 1), C(1, 2) and its image for k = 2, with rays from (0, 0). "What happened to each coordinate?"
SAY"A dilation about (0, 0) multiplies both coordinates by k. Each point slides along a ray from the origin."
CUEWorked Example 1 (k = 2: A′(2, 2), B′(6, 2), C′(2, 4)).
WATCH"Add the scale factor to each coordinate." Misconception box: adding slides the triangle.
CUEWorked Example 2 (rectangle PQRS with k = 1/2: the image moves closer to the origin), Worked Example 3 (check similarity: sides × 2, right angles match), Worked Example 4 (find k = 3 from A′ and B′).
DOString rays: tape a string at the origin of a large class grid and stretch it through a vertex. The image vertex must be on the string.
DOCore worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4. The unit synthesis can be homework or the start of the next class.

Assessment

Quiz (24 marks)

SD1 (Q1–3), SD2 (Q4–6), SD3 (Q7–10). Given at the start of Lesson 4, before dilations on a grid (SD4). Calculators allowed. Q1: comparing long ÷ short (or both k values) earns the method mark; a bare "yes" earns nothing. Q2: "two circles", with "same shape, only the size changes"; rectangles and triangles are only sometimes similar. Q3: angles matched by the order of the letters; the side matching BC is QR. Q4: k = 6 ÷ 1.5 = 4, enlargement; 0.25 earns 1 of 2 (upside down). Q5: DE = 10 × 0.5 = 5 cm and BC = 3.5 ÷ 0.5 = 7 cm; check that students divided going back. Q6: two different k values means not similar, so the answer must come from both pairs of sides. Q7 is the "two ways" item: counting or multiplying areas (100 cm² vs 900 cm²) and k² = 9. Q9: convert each side (8 m by 6 m) before finding the area, 48 m². Q10 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need "area grows by k² = 4" or a worked counterexample.

Worksheets and exits

Core (★) covers SD1–SD4 with a diagram on most questions: similar rectangles by long ÷ short, a bigger triangle that is still similar, which pairs are always similar, k with enlargement or reduction, a 12 cm side copied as 3 cm, missing sides with k = 1.5, rectangles that are not similar, the doubled square's area, the Red Deer to Sylvan Lake map distance, a k = 2 dilation, an "add the scale factor" error, and a k = 0.5 dilation. Challenge (★★) adds a garage blueprint and its floor area, a triangle's area with k = 2.5, finding k from a dilated rectangle and comparing areas, whether a photo fits a frame, the Calgary Tower model scale as 1 : n, undoing a dilation, a map area error, and a similar-triangle problem with two unknowns. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it. Exit tickets: one per section, each with a diagram and an "explain how you know" item.

Data and contexts used

  • The Calgary Tower is about 191 m tall (190.8 m to the top of the spire). The 1 cm : 5 m model (38.2 cm) and the 95.5 cm model (1 : 200) are made up.
  • Hawrelak Park and Rundle Park are real Edmonton river valley parks; 9 km apart (4.5 cm at 1 cm : 2 km) is a rounded straight-line estimate, and the map is a sketch.
  • Red Deer to Sylvan Lake: 3.5 cm at 1 cm : 7 km gives 24.5 km, close to the real distance (about 25 km by road).
  • The Elk Island Park trail (8 cm at 1 cm : 1.5 km = 12 km) is made up; the park has trails of about that length.
  • Photo sizes (10 by 15 cm, 4 by 6 inch prints) are standard print sizes; the 15 by 10 to 6 by 4 small print and the 12 by 8 photo in a 30 by 24 frame are made up.
  • The garage (6 m by 4 m from a 1 cm : 0.5 m blueprint; 7 m by 5.5 m in the Challenge), the gardens, the rug, the deck and the beetle drawing are made up.

Connections

  • Proportional Reasoning (topic 3): the scale factor is a constant of proportionality; the draft says k is also called scale factor. Map scales are unit rates.
  • Linear Functions (topic 7): each ray of a dilation is a line through (0, 0); a point and its image have the same y ÷ x.
  • Triangle Theorems (topic 9): similar triangles have equal angles, so the angle sum still holds; a 3-4-5 triangle scaled by k stays a right triangle (6-8-10, 9-12-15).
  • Surface Area (topic 10): surface area of similar solids also scales by k² (mention only; volume scaling is not in Grade 8).
  • Grade 7: congruence and transformations (translations, reflections, rotations) on the grid; a dilation is the first transformation that changes size.
