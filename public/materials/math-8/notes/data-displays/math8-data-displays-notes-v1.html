Watch out — a common mistake

"A circle graph is good for showing how something changes over time."

Why it is wrong: A circle graph shows how ONE whole is split up at ONE time. It has no time axis.

What is true: Use a line graph for change over time. Use a circle graph for parts of a whole.

Section 3 · Misleading graphs

Learning target: I can explain how the way a graph is drawn can make data look different from what it is.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
misleading
broken axis
interval
Watch out — a common mistake

"The taller bar is always a lot bigger."

Why it is wrong: Look where the y-axis starts. If it starts at 45, not 0, a small difference looks huge.

What is true: Read the numbers, not just the heights.

Worked Example 2 — Same data, two pictures

Wins in a season (sample): Team A 52, Team B 48.

In Graph 1, Team A's bar looks about times as tall as Team B's.

In Graph 2, the bars look almost the .

The real difference is only wins. Graph is misleading because its axis starts at 45.

Explain it Why might a team's fan club want to use Graph 1?
Watch out — a common mistake

"If a picture is twice as tall, it shows twice as much."

Why it is wrong: When a picture is made twice as tall AND twice as wide, its area is 4 times as big. It looks like 4 times as much.

What is true: Use pictures of the same size, and repeat them. Or use bars of the same width.

Other tricks to watch for

  • Uneven intervals on an axis (0, 10, 20, 50, 100)
  • Bars of different widths
  • Missing labels or no scale
  • Only showing part of the data (a few months, not the whole year)
