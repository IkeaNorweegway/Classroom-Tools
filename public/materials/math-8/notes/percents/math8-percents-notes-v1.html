To change a percent to a decimal, divide by 100. Move the decimal point places to the left.

More than 100%

100% is one whole grid. So what is 150%?

Watch out — a common mistake

"A percent can't be more than 100%."

Why it is wrong: 100% is one whole. You can have more than one whole. If a town's population grows from 1000 to 1500, it is now 150% of what it was.

What is true: 150% means one whole and a half more. 150% = 1.5.

150% = 150/100 =

Less than 1%

1% is one small square. What is 0.5%?

Watch out — a common mistake

"0.5% is the same as 50%" or "0.5% = 0.5."

Why it is wrong: 0.5% is only half of ONE small square. 50% is half the whole grid. 0.5 is half of the whole too.

What is true: 0.5% = 0.005. Divide by 100: move the decimal two places left.

Watch out — a common mistake

"A town grows 10% one year and 15% the next. That is 25% in all."

Why it is wrong: The 15% is taken from a bigger number, because the town already grew.

What is true: Start with 1000 people. +10%: 1000 + 100 = 1100. +15% of 1100: 1100 + 165 = 1265. That is a 26.5% increase, not 25%.

"A price goes down 20%, then up 20%. It is back where it started."

Why it is wrong: The 20% increase is taken from the smaller, lower price.

What is true: $100 → −20% → $80 → +20% of $80 = $16 → $96. It ends lower than it started.
