Section 1 · Ratios

Learning target: I can write a ratio three ways and tell part-to-part from part-to-whole.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
ratiocompares two or more amounts of the same kind1 can of juice to 3 cans of water, 1:3
part-to-partcompares one part to another partjuice : water = 1 : 3
part-to-wholecompares one part to the totaljuice : all = 1 : 4
termeach number in a ratioin 1:3, the terms are 1 and 3

To make frozen juice, mix 1 can of concentrate with 3 cans of water.

We can write the ratio of concentrate to water three ways:

1 to 3 · 1 : 3 · 1/3

Part-to-part or part-to-whole?

concentrate : water = 1 : 3 (part to part)

concentrate : all the juice = 1 : 4 (part to whole)

As a fraction, the concentrate is 1/4 of the jug. As a percent, that is 25%.

Watch out — a common mistake

"The ratio 1:3 means 1 out of 3 cans is concentrate."

Why it is wrong: 1:3 is part to part. There are 1 + 3 = 4 cans in all.

What is true: 1 out of 4 cans is concentrate. Part-to-whole: 1:4 or 1/4 or 25%.

Worked Example 1 — A three-term ratio

A trail mix has 4 cups of nuts, 2 cups of raisins and 1 cup of chocolate chips.

nuts : raisins : chips = 4 : 2 : 1

Total cups = 7

Raisins are 2/7 of the mix.

Explain it Why do we add all three terms to get the whole?
Answer The whole is everything in the mix, so we add every part: 4 + 2 + 1 = 7.
Your turn

A class has 12 girls and 15 boys.

  1. a) girls : boys = 12 : 15 (or 4 : 5)
  2. b) boys : whole class = 15 : 27
  3. c) What fraction of the class is girls? 12/27 = 4/9
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. Write "3 red to 5 blue" in two other ways.
    Answer 3:5 and 3/5
  2. A bag has 3 red and 5 blue marbles. What is the ratio of red to all marbles?
    Answer 3:8
  3. Kate says the ratio 2:5 (sugar to flour) means 2 out of 5 cups are sugar. Is she right?
    Answer No. There are 2 + 5 = 7 cups in all, so 2 out of 7 are sugar.
  4. A drink is 1 part syrup to 4 parts water. What percent is syrup?
    Answer 1 out of 5 = 20%
  5. ★ Stretch Write a ratio that can be written as a percent, and explain why "60 km per hour" cannot be written as a percent.
    Answer 1 part syrup out of 5 parts = 20%. A ratio compares the same kind of thing, so it can be "out of 100". 60 km per hour compares two different units (km and hours), so there is nothing to be "out of 100".

Section 2 · Equal ratios and proportions

Learning target: I can make equal ratios with a table or a double number line, and use them to solve problems.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
equivalent ratiosratios that show the same comparison1:3 = 2:6 = 3:9
proportiona statement that two ratios are equal1/3 = 2/6
scale factorthe number you multiply both terms by1:3 × 4 → 4:12

To make more juice, keep the same taste. Multiply both terms by the same number.

Concentrate (cans)1234
Water (cans)36912
Watch out — a common mistake

"1:3 and 2:4 are equal. I just added 1 to both."

Why it is wrong: Adding changes the taste. In 1:3, water is 3 times the concentrate. In 2:4, water is only 2 times.

What is true: Multiply both terms by the same number: 1:3 × 2 = 2:6.

Worked Example 2 — Bannock for the whole class

A bannock recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 1 tablespoon of baking powder. You want to use 12 cups of flour.

Scale factor: 12 ÷ 3 = 4

Baking powder: 1 × 4 = 4 tablespoons

So 3 : 1 = 12 : 4

Explain it Why do we multiply the baking powder by the same number as the flour?
Answer To keep the same ratio (the same recipe), both parts must grow by the same factor.
Worked Example 3 — Solve a proportion

2/5 = x/20

5 × 4 = 20, so multiply the top by 4 too: x = 2 × 4 = 8

Check: 8/20 simplifies to 2/5 ✓

Explain it How can you check that two ratios are equal?
Answer Simplify both, or check that the same scale factor turns one into the other.
Your turn
  1. a) 2 : 7 = 6 : 21
  2. b) 5 : 4 = 25 : 20
  3. c) 3/8 = x/40, x = 15
  4. d) Paint is 2 parts blue to 3 parts white. How much white goes with 10 L of blue?
Answer 10 ÷ 2 = 5, so 3 × 5 = 15 L of white.
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. Write two ratios equal to 3:4.
    Answer 6:8, 9:12 (any multiples)
  2. Is 4:6 equal to 6:8? Explain.
    Answer No. 4:6 = 2:3 and 6:8 = 3:4.
  3. Solve: 5/6 = x/18
    Answer x = 15
  4. A juice is 1:3. Ben makes 3:5. Whose juice is stronger? Use a picture or a table.
    Answer Ben's. 1:3 has 1 of 4 parts concentrate (25%). 3:5 has 3 of 8 parts (37.5%).
  5. ★ Stretch Fill in the table for 2 cups of rice to 3 cups of water, up to 10 cups of rice. Then plot it as points.
    Answer (2, 3), (4, 6), (6, 9), (8, 12), (10, 15). The points line up.

Section 3 · Rates and unit rates

Learning target: I can find a unit rate and use it to compare prices or speeds.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
ratecompares two amounts with different units$3 for 4 apples; 240 km in 3 h
unit ratea rate for ONE unit$0.75 per apple; 80 km/h
per"for each"km per hour, dollars per kilogram

A ratio compares the same kind of thing (cans to cans). A rate compares different kinds of things (km to hours, dollars to grams).

Worked Example 4 — Speed

The drive from Red Deer to Calgary is about 150 km. It takes 1.5 h.

Unit rate = 150 km ÷ 1.5 h = 100 km/h

Explain it What does "100 km/h" mean in words?
Answer The car goes 100 km for every 1 hour of driving.
Watch out — a common mistake

"The bigger box is always the better deal."

Why it is wrong: A bigger box costs more too. You need the price for ONE unit to compare.

What is true: Find the unit price of each, then compare.

Worked Example 5 — Best buy

Hockey tape: 3 rolls for $6.00, or 5 rolls for $9.00.

3 rolls: $6.00 ÷ 3 = $2.00 per roll

5 rolls: $9.00 ÷ 5 = $1.80 per roll

The 5-roll pack is the better buy.

Explain it Why do we divide the price by the number of rolls?
Answer That gives the price of one roll, so both packs can be compared fairly.

Fuel use: L/100 km

Cars in Canada show fuel use as litres per 100 km. A truck that uses 12 L/100 km uses 12 L for every 100 km.

Your turn
  1. a) 240 km in 3 h = 80 km/h
  2. b) $4.50 for 3 kg of potatoes = $1.50 per kg
  3. c) A car uses 8 L/100 km. How much fuel for 300 km? 24 L
  4. d) Which is cheaper: 2 L of milk for $4.40, or 4 L for $6.00? Show the unit prices.
Answer 2 L: $2.20 per L. 4 L: $1.50 per L. The 4 L jug is cheaper.
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. What is the difference between a ratio and a rate?
    Answer A ratio compares the same kind of thing. A rate compares different kinds (different units).
  2. Find the unit rate: 360 km in 4 h.
    Answer 90 km/h
  3. Find the unit price: 6 granola bars for $4.20.
    Answer $0.70 per bar
  4. Mo says the 10 kg bag of dog food for $35 is a worse deal than the 4 kg bag for $16, because $35 is more. Is Mo right?
    Answer No. 10 kg: $3.50/kg. 4 kg: $4.00/kg. The big bag is cheaper per kg.
  5. ★ Stretch Why can a ratio like 1:4 be written as 25%, but a rate like 90 km/h cannot be written as a percent?
    Answer A percent is a part out of 100 of the same kind of thing. km and hours are different units, so 90 km/h is not "out of 100" of anything.

Section 4 · Proportion problems

Learning target: I can choose a strategy (table, double number line or unit rate) to solve a ratio or rate problem.

Words to know

WordWhat it means (in your own words)Example or picture
proportional reasoningusing equal ratios or rates to find a missing amountif 2 cost $5, then 6 cost $15
scalethe ratio of a drawing to the real thing1 cm : 50 km
Worked Example 6 — Map scale

On a map of Alberta, 1 cm stands for 50 km. Edmonton and Calgary are 6 cm apart on the map.

Real distance = 6 × 50 = 300 km

Explain it Why does a double number line work for a map scale?
Answer Every 1 cm on the map is always 50 km, so the two lines grow together at the same rate.
Worked Example 7 — Unit rate strategy

A farmer earns $720 for 3 tonnes of wheat. How much for 7 tonnes?

Unit rate: $720 ÷ 3 = $240 per tonne

7 tonnes: 7 × $240 = $1680

Explain it When is the unit rate easier than a scale factor?
Answer When the numbers don't multiply nicely (3 to 7 is not a whole-number scale factor), finding the value of ONE first makes it easy.

What can a/b mean?

The same symbol can mean different things:

3/4 as…Example
a fraction3/4 of a pizza
a ratio3 boys to 4 girls
a rate$3 for 4 apples
a quotient (division)3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
a probability3 chances out of 4
Your turn
  1. a) 4 tickets cost $30. How much do 10 tickets cost?
Answer $7.50 each, so 10 cost $75.
  1. b) A recipe needs 2 eggs for 12 muffins. How many eggs for 30 muffins?
Answer 1 egg makes 6 muffins. 30 ÷ 6 = 5 eggs.
Retrieval check — close your notes
  1. A printer prints 45 pages in 3 minutes. How many pages in 10 minutes?
    Answer 15 pages per minute × 10 = 150 pages
  2. The scale on a map is 1 cm : 20 km. Two towns are 140 km apart. How far apart are they on the map?
    Answer 7 cm
  3. Give an example where 2/3 is a rate, and one where it is a ratio.
    Answer Rate: $2 for 3 pens. Ratio: 2 cats to 3 dogs.
  4. Which strategy would you use for "5 cost $8. How much for 15?" Why?
    Answer Scale factor ×3 (15 ÷ 5 = 3), so $24. The numbers multiply nicely.
  5. ★ Stretch Jasmine runs 5 km in 30 minutes. At the same speed, how long for a 12 km race? Explain your strategy.
    Answer 1 km takes 6 minutes. 12 × 6 = 72 minutes.
