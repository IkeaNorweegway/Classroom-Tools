Teacher notes

Ratios and Rates

Grade 8 Mathematics · Number · 4 lessons + quiz · Outcomes N4, N5 · Codes RR1–RR4

Overview

Big idea: a ratio compares quantities that grow together. Tape diagrams and double number lines show "grow together" before any cross-multiplying.

Sequence: ratios (RR1) → equal ratios and proportions (RR2) → rates and unit rates (RR3) → choosing a strategy (RR4).

Materials: frozen juice concentrate and a pitcher (or a picture), flyers with package prices, an Alberta road map.

Depth ceiling: cross-multiplying is allowed but not taught as the first method. Ratios stay positive.

Watch forWhat to do
Part-to-part read as part-to-wholeAlways ask "How many parts in all?" Draw the tape.
Additive thinking (1:3 → 2:4)Taste test thinking: is 2:4 the same drink? Draw both tapes.
"Bigger box = better deal"Unit price on every comparison.
Ratios vs ratesUnits: same units → ratio; different units → rate. Only ratios become percents.

Lessons

Lesson 1 — Ratios · 60 min
DOPre-knowledge check. Mix (or show) juice: 1 can + 3 cans.
SHOWTape diagram. Write 1 to 3, 1:3, 1/3.
SAY"Part to part compares two parts. Part to whole compares one part to everything."
WATCH"1 out of 3." Ask: "How many cans are in the jug?"
DOThree-term trail mix ratio. Exit ticket Section 1.
Lesson 2 — Equal ratios · 60 min
DOWarm-up: write 3 ratios from the class (glasses to no glasses, etc.).
SHOWDouble number line for juice. "If I use 3 cans of concentrate, how much water?"
SAY"Same taste means multiply both parts by the same number."
WATCHAdding the same number. Show the 2:4 tape next to 1:3.
DOBannock recipe scaling; solve x/20 = 2/5 with a scale factor. Exit ticket Section 2.
Lesson 3 — Rates and unit rates · 60 min
DOWarm-up: equal ratios to 2:5.
SHOWRed Deer to Calgary drive on a double number line.
SAY"A rate compares different units. A unit rate is the amount for ONE."
DOBest-buy flyer activity in pairs: each pair gets two package sizes, finds unit prices, and reports one sentence.
CUE"Why can 1:4 be 25% but 90 km/h can't be a percent?"
DOExit ticket Section 3.
Lesson 4 — Choosing a strategy · 60 min
DOWarm-up: one ratio, one rate, one part-to-whole question (interleaved).
SHOWMap scale and the wheat price example. Ask which strategy each one needs.
DOThe "what can a/b mean" table: students write their own example for each row.
DOCore worksheet, exit ticket Section 4. Quiz next class.

Assessment

Mid-unit quiz (18 marks)

RR1 (Q1–2), RR2 (Q3–4), RR3 (Q5–6), RR4 (Q7). Q2 and Q4 target the part-to-whole and additive misconceptions.

Connections

  • Percents: a percent is a ratio out of 100.
  • Linear Relations: a table of equal ratios graphs as points on a straight line through (0, 0).
  • Grade 9: similarity and scale factors use the same reasoning.
