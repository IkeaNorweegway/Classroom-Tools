Part A — Choose the Answer/8

Circle the best answer. 1 mark each.

1.
Spin a 4-part spinner and roll a die. How many outcomes are there?
A10
B4
C24
D6
Answer C. Each of the 4 parts pairs with all 6 numbers: 4 × 6 = 24.
Common mistakes: A adds 4 + 6. B and D count only one event.
CP1[1]
2.
Which is the sample space for flipping two coins?
AS = {HH, HT, TH, TT}
BS = {HH, HT, TT}
CS = {H, T}
DS = {HH, TT}
Answer A. HT and TH are different outcomes: heads first, or tails first.
Common mistakes: B counts HT and TH as one. C is one coin. D leaves out the mixed outcomes.
CP1[1]
3.
Roll a die two times. What is P(6, then 6)?
A2/6
B1/36
C1/12
D1/6
Answer B. 1/6 × 1/6 = 1/36.
Common mistakes: A adds the fractions. C uses 1/2 for one roll. D is one roll only.
CP2[1]
4.
Which pair of events is independent?
ADraw a card, keep it, then draw another.
BPick two students for a team.
CTake two cookies from a jar and eat the first.
DSpin a spinner, then flip a coin.
Answer D. The spinner cannot change the coin.
Common mistakes: A, B and C take something away, so the second event changes.
CP2[1]
5.
A bag has 5 red and 3 blue counters. You draw a red and keep it out. What is P(red) on the next draw?
A5/8
B4/8
C5/7
D4/7
Answer D. 4 red are left, and 7 counters in all.
Common mistakes: A forgets the draw. B changes only the top. C changes only the bottom.
CP3[1]
6.
Why is drawing twice without replacement dependent?
AThe counters change colour.
BThe first draw changes what is left in the bag.
CYou need two bags.
DThe second draw is always less likely.
Answer B. One counter is kept out, so the second draw has different chances.
Common mistakes: D: the second draw can be more likely. If you take a blue, P(red) goes up.
CP3[1]
7.
Roll two dice. What is P(both numbers are even)?
A1/4
B1/2
C1
D1/36
Answer A. The dice are independent: 3/6 × 3/6 = 9/36 = 1/4.
Common mistakes: B uses one die only. C adds 1/2 + 1/2. D is one cell of the table.
CP4[1]
8.
In a class of 30, 12 students wear glasses. The teacher picks 2 different students. Which finds P(both wear glasses)?
A12/30 × 12/30
B12/30 + 11/29
C12/30 × 11/29
D12/30 × 11/30
Answer C. The same student can't be picked twice, so 11 of 29 are left.
Common mistakes: A treats it as independent. B adds. D changes only the top.

Part B — Show Your Work/21

Show your steps.

9.
Spin a number spinner (1, 2, 3, 4) and a colour spinner (red, yellow, green).
a)Fill in the table.
b)Write the sample space in set notation.
c)Find P(an even number and green).
Answer a) 12 cells: 1R, 1Y, 1G, 2R, … 4G (1 mark).
b) S = {1R, 1Y, 1G, 2R, 2Y, 2G, 3R, 3Y, 3G, 4R, 4Y, 4G} (1 mark).
c) 2G and 4G: 2/12 = 1/6 (1 mark).
CP1[3]
10.
Roll two dice and add.
a)Find P(a sum of 10 or more).
b)Leah says a sum of 7 and a sum of 12 are equally likely, because each is one number. Is she right? Explain.
Answer a) (4,6), (5,5), (6,4), (5,6), (6,5), (6,6): 6 cells (1 mark). 6/36 = 1/6 (1 mark).
b) No. A sum of 7 has 6 cells, but 12 has only 1 cell, (6,6). A sum of 7 is 6 times as likely (1 mark).
CP1[3]
11.
Roll a die and spin this spinner. Find P(yellow and a number less than 3). Write it as a fraction, a decimal and a percent.
Answer P(yellow) = 1/4. P(less than 3) = 2/6 (1 mark).
1/4 × 2/6 = 2/24 = 1/12 (1 mark).
≈ 0.08 ≈ 8.3% (1 mark).
CP2[3]
12.
A basketball player makes 80% of her free throws. Each shot is independent. She takes 3 shots.
a)Why can you multiply?
b)Find P(she makes all 3).
c)Find P(she misses all 3).
Answer a) The shots are independent: one shot does not change the chance on the next (1 mark).
b) 0.8 × 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.512 = 51.2% (1 mark).
c) P(miss) = 0.2. 0.2 × 0.2 × 0.2 = 0.008 = 0.8% (1 mark).
CP2[3]
13.
A box has 4 chocolate and 3 vanilla cookies. Ty takes two without looking and eats them.
a)Fill in the second fractions.
b)Find P(chocolate, chocolate).
c)Find P(one of each).
Answer a) After C: C 3/6, V 3/6. After V: C 4/6, V 2/6 (1 mark).
b) 4/7 × 3/6 = 12/42 = 2/7 (1 mark).
c) CV: 4/7 × 3/6 = 12/42. VC: 3/7 × 4/6 = 12/42. Total 24/42 = 4/7 (1 mark).
CP3[3]
14.
A raffle has 20 tickets. You bought 3 of them. Two prizes are drawn, and a winning ticket is not put back.
a)Find P(you win both prizes).
b)Find P(you win the first prize but not the second).
Answer a) 1st: 3/20. 2nd: 2/19, because one of your tickets is out (1 mark).
3/20 × 2/19 = 6/380 = 3/190 ≈ 1.6% (1 mark).
b) 3/20 × 17/19 = 51/380 ≈ 13.4% (1 mark).
CP3[3]
15.
In a class game, you roll two dice. You win a prize if both dice show a 5 or a 6. 36 students play.
a)Are the two rolls independent or dependent?
b)Find P(win).
c)About how many of the 36 students should win?
Answer a) Independent: one die does not change the other (1 mark).
b) 2/6 × 2/6 = 4/36 = 1/9 (1 mark).
c) 1/9 × 36 = 4 students (1 mark).

Part C — Put It Together/8

16.

A school carnival booth has two games. You win a game if you get gold two times.

GameHow it works
1Spin a spinner with 3 equal parts (gold, red, blue) two times.
2A box holds 5 tickets: 2 gold and 3 white. Draw 2 tickets without putting the first back.
(a)
Write the sample space for Game 1 in set notation. How many outcomes are there?
Answer S = {GG, GR, GB, RG, RR, RB, BG, BR, BB} (1 mark).
3 × 3 = 9 outcomes (1 mark).
CP1[2]
(b)
Find P(win Game 1).
Answer The spins are independent (1 mark).
1/3 × 1/3 = 1/9 (1 mark).
CP2[2]
(c)
Find P(win Game 2).
Answer 1st: 2/5. 2nd: 1/4, because one gold is out (1 mark).
2/5 × 1/4 = 2/20 = 1/10 (1 mark).
CP3[2]
(d)
Which game gives you the better chance? If Game 2 put the first ticket back, would your chance go up or down?
Answer Game 1: 1/9 ≈ 11.1% is more than 1/10 = 10% (1 mark).
Up: 2/5 × 2/5 = 4/25 = 16% (1 mark).

Part D — Explain Your Thinking/3

17.
Owen says, "Putting the counter back or not makes no difference. The bag starts the same either way." A bag has 3 red and 3 blue counters. Find P(red, red) both ways and use your answers to show Owen is wrong.
Answer Model answer: With replacement the draws are independent: 3/6 × 3/6 = 9/36 = 1/4 = 25%. Without replacement they are dependent: after one red is out, 2 of 5 are red, so 3/6 × 2/5 = 6/30 = 1/5 = 20%. The answers are different. The bag starts the same, but keeping a red out changes the second draw: one fewer red and one fewer counter.
