Part A — Choose the Answer

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. 7² =

A) 14B) 72C) 49D) 9
Answer C · Why: 7 × 7 = 49 · Common mistakes: A: 7 × 2. B: wrote 7 and 2 side by side

2. Which number is a perfect square?

A) 81B) 50C) 24D) 90
Answer A · Why: 9 × 9 = 81 · Common mistakes: The others are not a whole number times itself

3. √100 =

A) 50B) 20C) 1000D) 10
Answer D · Why: 10 × 10 = 100 · Common mistakes: A: half of 100

4. A square has an area of 64 cm². How long is one side?

A) 16 cmB) 8 cmC) 32 cmD) 4 cm
Answer B · Why: 8 × 8 = 64 · Common mistakes: A: 64 ÷ 4. C: half of 64

5. √45 is between which two whole numbers?

A) 4 and 5B) 7 and 8C) 22 and 23D) 6 and 7
Answer D · Why: 36 < 45 < 49, so between 6 and 7 · Common mistakes: C: half of 45

6. Which whole number is √70 closest to?

A) 7B) 8C) 9D) 35
Answer B · Why: 64 < 70 < 81, and 70 is closer to 64 · Common mistakes: A: 7 × 7 = 49 is too small. D: half of 70

7. Which number is not a perfect square?

A) 1B) 16C) 60D) 36
Answer C · Why: 7 × 7 = 49 and 8 × 8 = 64, so 60 is not a square

8. A square garden has an area of 121 m². How much fence goes around all 4 sides?

A) 44 mB) 11 mC) 30.25 mD) 484 m
Answer A · Why: Each side is √121 = 11 m. 4 × 11 = 44 m · Common mistakes: B: only one side. D: 4 × 121

Part B — Show Your Work

[3 marks each]   /21

9.

[1](a) Draw a square of tiles that shows 4².

[1](b) Find 4².  

[1](c) Is 4² the same as 4 × 2? Explain.

Answer (a) A 4 by 4 square of tiles. (b) 16 (c) No. 4 × 2 = 8. 4² means 4 × 4 = 16.
  • 1 mark each

10. Here are three ways to arrange 18 tiles.

[2](a) Write each one as a multiplication.

[1](b) Is 18 a perfect square? Explain.

Answer (a) 1 × 18, 2 × 9, 3 × 6 (b) No. None of the rectangles has equal sides, so 18 tiles can't make a square. (4 × 4 = 16 and 5 × 5 = 25.)
  • (a) 2 marks: all three (1 mark for two)
  • (b) 1 mark: no, with a reason

[3]11. Find √144. Use factor pairs or prime factors. Then check your answer by multiplying.

Answer Factor pairs of 144 include 12 × 12 (or 144 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = (2 × 2 × 3) × (2 × 2 × 3)). √144 = 12. Check: 12 × 12 = 144.
  • 1 mark: factor pairs or prime factors
  • 1 mark: 12
  • 1 mark: check by multiplying

[3]12. Ben says:

“√36 = 18, because half of 36 is 18.”

1. Show that Ben is wrong. 2. Give the right answer. 3. Check it.

Answer 18 × 18 = 324, not 36, so 18 is wrong. √36 = 6. Check: 6 × 6 = 36. A square root is not half.
  • 1 mark each part

[3]13. Mark and label √10, √30 and √60 on the number line.

Answer √10 ≈ 3.2 (just past 3). √30 ≈ 5.5 (between 5 and 6). √60 ≈ 7.7 (between 7 and 8, closer to 8).
  • 1 mark each: in the right whole-number gap and labelled

[3]14. Estimate √27 to one decimal place.

1. Write the perfect squares on each side of 27. 2. Pick your estimate. 3. Check by squaring it.

Answer 25 < 27 < 36, so √27 is between 5 and 6, close to 5. Estimate 5.2. Check: 5.2 × 5.2 = 27.04.
  • 1 mark: 25 and 36
  • 1 mark: 5.2 (accept 5.1 or 5.3 with a check)
  • 1 mark: checks by squaring

[3]15. A square poster has an area of 196 cm². Will it fit inside a square frame that is 15 cm wide? Explain.

Answer Each side of the poster is √196 = 14 cm. 14 cm is less than 15 cm, so yes, it fits.
  • 1 mark: √196
  • 1 mark: 14 cm
  • 1 mark: yes, comparing 14 with 15

16. Jade is building a square patio in Calgary. She uses square tiles that are 1 m by 1 m. She has 150 tiles.

[2](a) What is the biggest square patio she can make? How many tiles does it use? Why can't she make a bigger one?

[2](b) Her neighbour has a square patio with an area of 50 m². Estimate the length of one side to one decimal place. Show the perfect squares you used.

[2](c) Jade also builds a square sandbox with an area of 36 m². How long is one side? How much wood does she need to go all the way around it?

[2](d) Jade says: “If I double the area of my patio, each side will be twice as long.” Test her idea with the patio from (a). Is she right?

Answer (a) 12 m by 12 m, using 144 tiles. A 13 by 13 patio needs 169 tiles, and she only has 150.
  • 1 mark: 12 by 12 (144 tiles)
  • 1 mark: explains why 13 by 13 is too big

(b) 49 < 50 < 64, so the side is between 7 and 8, very close to 7. About 7.1 m.

  • 1 mark: 49 and 64
  • 1 mark: 7.1 (accept 7.0 or 7.2)

(c) Side = √36 = 6 m. Around it: 4 × 6 = 24 m of wood.

  • 1 mark each

(d) No. Double 144 is 288. 17 × 17 = 289, so the new side is about 17 m, not 24 m. Doubling the area does not double the side.

  • 1 mark: finds the new side is about 17 m (or shows 24 × 24 = 576)
  • 1 mark: says Jade is wrong

Part D — Explain Your Thinking

[3 marks]   /3

17. Omar says:

“Every whole number has a whole-number square root.”

Is Omar right? Use two examples to explain.

Answer Model answer: Omar is not right. Some numbers have whole-number square roots: √25 = 5. But 20 is not a perfect square. 4 × 4 = 16 and 5 × 5 = 25, so √20 is between 4 and 5. It is not a whole number.
