Part A — Choose the Answer

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. 13/4 written as a mixed number is

A) 3 1/4B) 4 1/3C) 3 3/4D) 1 3/4
Answer A · Why: 13 ÷ 4 = 3 remainder 1 · Common mistakes: C: used the remainder wrong

2. 2 2/3 written as an improper fraction is

A) 4/3B) 6/3C) 8/3D) 7/3
Answer C · Why: 2 × 3 + 2 = 8, so 8/3 · Common mistakes: A: added 2 + 2

3. 1/3 × 3/5 =

A) 5/9B) 4/8C) 3/8D) 1/5
Answer D · Why: 3/15 = 1/5 · Common mistakes: B: added tops and bottoms

4. 5 × 2/3 =

A) 10/15B) 3 1/3C) 7/3D) 2/15
Answer B · Why: 10/3 = 3 1/3 · Common mistakes: A: multiplied the bottom too

5. 2 ÷ 1/4 =

A) 1/2B) 8C) 6D) 2/4
Answer B · Why: 4 quarters in each whole, 2 × 4 = 8 · Common mistakes: A: thinks dividing makes it smaller

6. 3/4 ÷ 3 =

A) 9/4B) 3/7C) 4D) 1/4
Answer D · Why: 3/4 shared into 3 parts is 1/4 each · Common mistakes: A: multiplied

7. Without calculating, 7/8 × 11/12 is closest to

A) 0B) 1/2C) 1D) 2
Answer C · Why: Both fractions are close to 1

8. A 6 m rope is cut into pieces that are 3/4 m long. Which finds the number of pieces?

A) 6 ÷ 3/4B) 6 × 3/4C) 6 + 3/4D) 6 − 3/4
Answer A · Why: How many 3/4 m pieces fit in 6 m: divide · Common mistakes: B: "of" is not in this question

Part B — Show Your Work

[3 marks each]   /21

9. Each bar is cut into thirds.

[1](a) Shade the bars to show 7/3.

[1](b) Write 7/3 as a mixed number.  

[1](c) Is 7/3 closer to 2 or to 3? How do you know?

Answer (a) Two whole bars and one third of the third bar shaded. (b) 2 1/3 (c) Closer to 2: 1/3 is less than half.
  • 1 mark each

[3]10. Use the area model to find 3/4 × 2/3. Shade 3/4 one way and 2/3 the other way. Write the answer in lowest terms.

Answer The overlap is 6 of the 12 parts: 6/12 = 1/2.
  • 1 mark: shades 3/4 and 2/3 correctly
  • 1 mark: 6/12
  • 1 mark: 1/2

[3]11. Find 1 1/2 × 2 1/5. Show your steps.

Answer 3/2 × 11/5 = 33/10 = 3 3/10
  • 1 mark: improper fractions
  • 1 mark: 33/10
  • 1 mark: 3 3/10

[3]12. A student wrote:

3 1/2 × 2 1/4 = 6 1/8, because 3 × 2 = 6 and 1/2 × 1/4 = 1/8.

1. What did the student do wrong? 2. Find the right answer.

Answer The student multiplied the wholes and the fractions separately. Change to improper fractions first: 7/2 × 9/4 = 63/8 = 7 7/8.
  • 1 mark: names the mistake
  • 1 mark: 7/2 × 9/4
  • 1 mark: 7 7/8

[3]13. One serving of rice is 2/3 cup. How many servings are in 4 cups? Show it with a picture or a rule.

Answer 4 ÷ 2/3 = 4 × 3/2 = 6 servings. (Picture: 4 cups cut into thirds gives 12 thirds; 12 thirds make 6 groups of 2 thirds.)
  • 1 mark: division set up (or a picture)
  • 1 mark: multiplies by 3/2 (or counts groups)
  • 1 mark: 6

[3]14. Leo wrote:

5/6 ÷ 5/12 = 6/5 × 5/12 = 1/2

1. What did Leo do wrong? 2. Find the right answer.

Answer Leo flipped the first fraction. Keep the first one and flip the one you divide by: 5/6 × 12/5 = 60/30 = 2.
  • 1 mark: names the mistake
  • 1 mark: 5/6 × 12/5
  • 1 mark: 2

[3]15. Find 1/2 + 3/4 × 2/3. Use the order of operations.

Answer Multiply first: 3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12 = 1/2. Then 1/2 + 1/2 = 1.
  • 1 mark: multiplies first
  • 1 mark: 1/2
  • 1 mark: 1

16. Ms. Lee makes granola bars for a bake sale in Edmonton. One batch uses 2 1/4 cups of oats and 3/4 cup of honey.

[2](a) She makes 1 1/2 batches. How many cups of oats does she need?

[2](b) She has 4 1/2 cups of honey. How many batches can that honey make?

[2](c) She cuts a 5 m roll of ribbon into pieces 1/4 m long to tie the bags. How many pieces does she get? Which operation did you use, and why?

[2](d) Estimate 2 1/4 × 1 1/2 with whole numbers and halves. Is your answer to (a) reasonable?

Answer (a) 9/4 × 3/2 = 27/8 = 3 3/8 cups
  • 1 mark: improper fractions and multiplies
  • 1 mark: 3 3/8

(b) 9/2 ÷ 3/4 = 9/2 × 4/3 = 36/6 = 6 batches

  • 1 mark: divides
  • 1 mark: 6

(c) 5 ÷ 1/4 = 20 pieces. Divide, because the question asks how many 1/4 m pieces fit in 5 m.

  • 1 mark: 20
  • 1 mark: the reason

(d) About 2 × 1 1/2 = 3 (or 2 1/2 × 1 1/2 ≈ 3 3/4). 3 3/8 is close, so yes, it is reasonable.

  • 1 mark: an estimate
  • 1 mark: compares it with (a)

Part D — Explain Your Thinking

[3 marks]   /3

17. Ava says:

“Multiplying always makes a number bigger.”

Test Ava's idea with 6 × 1/2 and 6 × 3/2. When is she right, and when is she wrong?

Answer Model answer: 6 × 1/2 = 3, which is smaller than 6. 6 × 3/2 = 9, which is bigger than 6. Ava is right only when you multiply by a number greater than 1. Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 gives a smaller answer.
