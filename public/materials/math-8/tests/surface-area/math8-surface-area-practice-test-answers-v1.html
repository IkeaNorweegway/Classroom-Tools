Part A — Choose the Answer/8

Circle the best answer. 1 mark each.

1.
Which solid is a polyhedron?
Acylinder
Bcone
Csphere
Dtriangular prism
Answer D. Every face of a triangular prism is a flat polygon.
Common mistakes: A, B and C have a curved surface, so they are not polyhedrons.
SA1[1]
2.
How many faces does a triangular pyramid have?
A3
B4
C5
D6
Answer B. A triangle base and 3 triangles that meet at the top: 4 faces.
Common mistakes: A forgets the base. C counts a square-based pyramid. D counts a cube.
SA1[1]
3.
A cube has edges of 3 cm. What is its surface area?
A54 cm²
B27 cm³
C9 cm²
D36 cm²
Answer A. Each face is 3 × 3 = 9 cm². 6 faces: 6 × 9 = 54 cm².
Common mistakes: B is the volume. C is one face. D counts 4 faces.
SA2[1]
4.
A square-based pyramid has a base edge of 6 cm and a slant height of 4 cm. What is the area of one triangular face?
A24 cm²
B48 cm²
C12 cm²
D10 cm²
Answer C. 1/2 × 6 × 4 = 12 cm².
Common mistakes: A forgets the 1/2. B is 2 triangles with no 1/2. D adds 6 + 4.
SA2[1]
5.
The net of a cylinder has a rectangle. How long is the rectangle?
Athe diameter
Bthe height
Cthe circumference
Dthe radius
Answer C. The rectangle wraps all the way around the circle, so its length is the circumference.
Common mistakes: A and D measure across the circle, not around it. B is the rectangle's width.
SA3[1]
6.
A cylinder has a radius of 5 cm. What is the area of one of its circles?
A31.4 cm²
B78.5 cm²
C15.7 cm²
D314 cm²
Answer B. 3.14 × 5 × 5 = 78.5 cm².
Common mistakes: A is the circumference (3.14 × 10). C is 3.14 × 5. D is 3.14 × 10 × 10.
SA3[1]
7.
How many squares are in the top view of this object?
A5
B6
C3
D4
Answer D. From above you see one square for each stack: 3 in the back row and 1 in the front row.
Common mistakes: A counts every cube. B counts a full 2 by 3 grid. C counts only the back row.
SA4[1]
8.
A factory will cut the shape out of cardboard and fold it into a box. Which 2-D model should it use?
Aa net
Ba top view
Can isometric drawing
Da front view
Answer A. A net shows every face, flat, in one piece that folds up.
Common mistakes: B and D show only one side. C shows what the box looks like, not how to cut it.

Part B — Show Your Work/21

Show your steps.

9.
Look at this pentagonal prism.
a)How many faces does it have?
b)Name the shapes of its faces and how many there are of each.
Answer a) 7 faces (1 mark).
b) 2 pentagons (the bases) (1 mark).
5 rectangles (one on each edge of the pentagon) (1 mark).
SA1[3]
10.
This pattern of 6 squares is not a cube net.
a)Which two squares would land on the same face? Explain.
b)Move one square to make it a cube net.
Answer a) A, B, C and D wrap around to make the four sides. E and F both fold under the row, so they both become the bottom (1 mark).
They overlap, and the top is left open (1 mark).
b) Move E or F to the other side of the row (above any of A, B, C or D). Then one square is the bottom and one is the top (1 mark).
SA1[3]
11.
A cereal box is 20 cm long, 7 cm wide and 30 cm tall. Find its surface area.
Answer Front and back: 2 × (20 × 30) = 1200 cm² (1 mark).
Sides: 2 × (7 × 30) = 420 cm². Top and bottom: 2 × (20 × 7) = 280 cm² (1 mark).
SA = 1200 + 420 + 280 = 1900 cm² (1 mark).
SA2[3]
12.
This triangular prism has right-triangle ends. Find its surface area.
Answer Two triangles: 2 × 1/2 × 4 × 3 = 12 cm² (1 mark).
Three rectangles: 3 × 10 + 4 × 10 + 5 × 10 = 30 + 40 + 50 = 120 cm² (1 mark).
SA = 12 + 120 = 132 cm² (1 mark).
SA2[3]
13.
A farm water tank is a closed cylinder. Its radius is 1.5 m and its height is 2 m. Find its surface area.
Answer Two circles: 2 × 3.14 × 1.5 × 1.5 = 14.13 m² (1 mark).
Rectangle: length 2 × 3.14 × 1.5 = 9.42 m, so 9.42 × 2 = 18.84 m² (1 mark).
SA = 14.13 + 18.84 = 32.97 m² (1 mark).
SA3[3]
14.
A paper label wraps all the way around a can. The can has a diameter of 7 cm and a height of 12 cm.
a)Kai says the label is 7 cm long. Explain his mistake.
b)Find the label's real length.
c)Find the label's area.
Answer a) 7 cm only goes across the can. The label goes around it, so its length is the circumference (1 mark).
b) 3.14 × 7 = 21.98 cm (1 mark).
c) 21.98 × 12 = 263.76 cm² (1 mark).
SA3[3]
15.
Draw the top, front and right side views of this object.
Answer Top: 3 squares in the back row and 2 in the front row, lined up on the left (1 mark).
Front: 3 columns, 2 squares tall on the left, then 1 and 1 (1 mark).
Right side: 2 columns, the back one 2 squares tall and the front one 1 square tall (1 mark).

Part C — Put It Together/8

16.

A store sells a candle in one of two packages. Box A is a cube with edges of 8 cm. Tube B is a closed cylinder with a radius of 4 cm and a height of 8 cm.

PackageShapeSize
ACubeedges 8 cm
BCylinderradius 4 cm, height 8 cm
(a)
Find the surface area of Box A.
Answer One face: 8 × 8 = 64 cm² (1 mark).
6 × 64 = 384 cm² (1 mark).
SA2[2]
(b)
Find the surface area of Tube B. Which package needs less cardboard?
Answer Circles: 2 × 3.14 × 4 × 4 = 100.48 cm². Side: 2 × 3.14 × 4 × 8 = 200.96 cm² (1 mark).
SA = 301.44 cm². Tube B needs less cardboard (1 mark).
SA3[2]
(c)
Is each package a polyhedron? Explain.
Answer Box A: yes. All 6 faces are flat squares (1 mark).
Tube B: no. It has a curved surface (1 mark).
SA1[2]
(d)
The factory needs a model to cut the cardboard. The store's website needs a picture of how the box looks. Which 2-D model fits each job: a net, the three views, or an isometric drawing? Explain.
Answer Factory: a net, because it shows every face flat and folds into the package (1 mark).
Website: an isometric drawing, because it shows the box as a solid, with three sides at once (1 mark).

Part D — Explain Your Thinking/3

17.
Mo says, "The front view is enough to build any cube object." Use Objects 1 and 2 to show that Mo is wrong. Explain what else you need.
Answer Model answer: Both objects have the same front view: two squares side by side. But Object 1 has 2 cubes and Object 2 has 3, because a cube hides behind the left one. The front view cannot show depth. You need the top view and the side view too (or an isometric drawing). Object 1's top view is 2 squares, and Object 2's is 3.
