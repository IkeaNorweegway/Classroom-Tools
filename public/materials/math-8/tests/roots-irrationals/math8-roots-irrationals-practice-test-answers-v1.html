12.
A square table top has an area of 2.25 m². Find the side length and the perimeter. Why do you use only one square root?
Answer s = √2.25 = 1.5 m (1 mark).
Perimeter = 4 × 1.5 = 6 m (1 mark).
−1.5 is also a root of s² = 2.25, but a length can't be negative (1 mark).
SR2[3]
13.
Estimate √20 to one decimal place. Show your benchmarks. Check by squaring.
Answer 16 < 20 < 25, so 4 < √20 < 5 (1 mark).
Estimate about 4.5 (1 mark; 4.4 or 4.5 accepted).
Check: 4.5² = 20.25, a little over 20, so √20 is just under 4.5 (1 mark). (√20 = 4.472…)

14.
A square garden has an area of 75 m². Estimate the side length to one decimal place. Then find how much fencing goes around it.
Answer 64 < 75 < 81, so the side is between 8 and 9 m (1 mark).
About 8.7 m: 8.7² = 75.69 and 8.6² = 73.96 (1 mark; 8.6 or 8.7 accepted).
Fencing: 4 × 8.7 ≈ 34.8 m (1 mark; 34.4–34.8 m accepted, matching their side).

16.

Leah is planning a square community garden in Lethbridge. The garden will have an area of 50 m².

(a)
Estimate the side length, √50, to one decimal place. Show your benchmarks.
Answer 49 < 50 < 64, and 50 is very close to 49 (1 mark).
√50 ≈ 7.1 m (1 mark; 7.0 or 7.1 accepted with a check).

Answer Irrational: 50 is not a perfect square, so √50 never ends or repeats (1 mark).
7.1 is approximate (rounded) (1 mark).

(c)
Fencing is sold in 1 m pieces. How many pieces does Leah need to go all the way around?

Part D — Explain Your Thinking/3

17.
Ty says, "√49 = ±7, because both 7 and −7 squared make 49." Explain the difference between √49 and solving x² = 49.
Answer Model answer: √49 means the principal (positive) root, so √49 = 7. The equation x² = 49 asks for every number that squares to 49, so x = 7 or x = −7, written x = ±7. Ty is right that both square to 49, but the √ sign alone gives only 7.
