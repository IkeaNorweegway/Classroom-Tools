Part A — Choose the Answer

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is

A) the side across from the right angleB) the shortest sideC) the side at the bottomD) either side next to the right angle
Answer A · Why: Across from the right angle, always the longest · Common mistakes: C: the triangle can be turned

2. The squares on the legs of a right triangle have areas of 9 cm² and 16 cm². What is the area of the square on the hypotenuse?

A) 5 cm²B) 144 cm²C) 7 cm²D) 25 cm²
Answer D · Why: 9 + 16 = 25 · Common mistakes: A: took the square root

3. A right triangle has legs of 6 cm and 8 cm. The hypotenuse is

A) 14 cmB) 10 cmC) 48 cmD) 100 cm
Answer B · Why: 36 + 64 = 100, √100 = 10 · Common mistakes: A: added the sides

4. A right triangle has legs of 8 m and 15 m. The hypotenuse is

A) 23 mB) 289 mC) 17 mD) 7 m
Answer C · Why: 64 + 225 = 289, √289 = 17 · Common mistakes: A: added the sides. B: forgot the root

5. A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 25 cm and one leg of 7 cm. The other leg is

A) 32 cmB) 24 cmC) 18 cmD) 26 cm
Answer B · Why: 625 − 49 = 576, √576 = 24 · Common mistakes: A: added the squares

6. To find a missing leg, you

A) add the squares of the other two sidesB) divide the hypotenuse by 2C) multiply the two sidesD) subtract the leg's square from the hypotenuse's square
Answer D · Why: a² = c² − b² · Common mistakes: A: that finds the hypotenuse

7. Which side lengths make a right triangle?

A) 9, 40, 41B) 6, 7, 8C) 4, 5, 7D) 10, 11, 12
Answer A · Why: 81 + 1600 = 1681 = 41² · Common mistakes: B: 36 + 49 = 85, not 64

8. Doubling the Pythagorean triple 3, 4, 5 gives

A) 6, 8, 12B) 5, 6, 7C) 6, 8, 10D) 3, 4, 10
Answer C · Why: Each side × 2 · Common mistakes: A: doubled the wrong side

9. Squares are drawn on the sides of a right triangle.

[1](a) What is the area of the biggest square?  

[1](b) How long is the hypotenuse?  

[1](c) What does the picture show about the three squares?

Answer (a) 36 + 64 = 100 cm² (b) √100 = 10 cm (c) The two smaller squares together have the same area as the biggest square.
  • 1 mark each

[3]10. Find the hypotenuse, c. Round to one decimal place.

Answer c² = 4² + 9² = 16 + 81 = 97, so c = √97 ≈ 9.8 cm
  • 1 mark: 16 + 81
  • 1 mark: 97
  • 1 mark: 9.8 cm

[3]11. A TV screen is 80 cm wide and 45 cm tall. TVs are measured along the diagonal. How long is the diagonal? Round to one decimal place.

Answer d² = 80² + 45² = 6400 + 2025 = 8425, so d = √8425 ≈ 91.8 cm
  • 1 mark: set-up
  • 1 mark: 8425
  • 1 mark: 91.8 cm

[3]12. A 6 m ladder leans against a wall. The foot of the ladder is 2 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach? Round to one decimal place.

Answer h² = 6² − 2² = 36 − 4 = 32, so h = √32 ≈ 5.7 m
  • 1 mark: subtracts (the ladder is the hypotenuse)
  • 1 mark: 32
  • 1 mark: 5.7 m

[3]13. A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 cm and a leg of 5 cm. Ben finds the other leg like this:

5² + 13² = 25 + 169 = 194, and √194 ≈ 13.9 cm

1. How can you tell Ben is wrong without a calculator? 2. Find the right answer.

Answer A leg must be shorter than the hypotenuse, and 13.9 cm is longer than 13 cm. Ben added the squares when he needed to subtract. a² = 169 − 25 = 144, so a = 12 cm.
  • 1 mark: the leg can't be longer than the hypotenuse
  • 1 mark: subtracts
  • 1 mark: 12 cm

[3]14. Is a triangle with sides 12 cm, 16 cm and 20 cm a right triangle? Show your check.

Answer 12² + 16² = 144 + 256 = 400. 20² = 400. They are equal, so yes, it is a right triangle.
  • 1 mark: 400 from the two short sides
  • 1 mark: 20² = 400
  • 1 mark: yes

[3]15. A carpenter checks if a corner is square (90°). She marks 60 cm along one side and 80 cm along the other. The distance between the marks is 98 cm. Is the corner square? Explain.

Answer If the corner were square, the diagonal would be √60² + 80² = √10 000 = 100 cm. It is 98 cm, so the corner is not square (it is a little less than 90°).
  • 1 mark: 3600 + 6400 = 10 000
  • 1 mark: should be 100 cm
  • 1 mark: not square, comparing 98 with 100

Show your work. Round to one decimal place. [8 marks]   /8

16. A baseball diamond in Edmonton is a square. Each side is 27 m. Each corner is a right angle.

[2](a) How far is it from home plate straight to second base?

[2](b) A player at third base throws the ball 33 m. It lands on the line from home plate to first base. How far from home plate does it land?

[2](c) The ground crew measures from home plate to second base and gets 38.0 m. Is the corner at first base a right angle? Explain.

[2](d) A runner goes from home to first to second. How much farther is that than going straight from home to second?

Answer (a) d² = 27² + 27² = 729 + 729 = 1458, so d = √1458 ≈ 38.2 m
  • 1 mark each: set-up, answer

(b) The throw is the hypotenuse. 33² − 27² = 1089 − 729 = 360, so the distance is √360 ≈ 19.0 m

  • 1 mark: subtracts
  • 1 mark: 19.0 m

(c) For a right angle the distance should be about 38.2 m (38² = 1444, not 1458). 38.0 m is a little short, so the corner is not quite a right angle (it is a bit less than 90°). Accept "very close to a right angle" if the comparison is shown.

  • 1 mark: compares 38.0 with 38.2 (or 1444 with 1458)
  • 1 mark: a conclusion

(d) Home → first → second = 27 + 27 = 54 m. 54 − 38.2 = 15.8 m farther.

  • 1 mark: 54 m
  • 1 mark: 15.8 m

17. Zoe says:

“a² + b² = c² works for every triangle.”

Is Zoe right? Test a triangle with sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm, and explain.

Answer Model answer: Zoe is not right. Test 4, 5, 6: 4² + 5² = 16 + 25 = 41, but 6² = 36. 41 is not 36, so the rule doesn't work for this triangle. a² + b² = c² only works for right triangles. (A triangle that passes the test is a right triangle.)
