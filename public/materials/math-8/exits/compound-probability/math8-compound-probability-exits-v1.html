Independent & Dependent Events

Exit Tickets

For the teacher: print and cut. One ticket at the end of each section. Give 3–5 minutes. Read them before the next class and sort into three piles: got it, almost, not yet.

Exit ticket — Section 1 · Sample spaces

Name:Date:
Target: I can show the sample space of one or two events. I can use a list, a table, a tree diagram and set notation. (CP1)

1. Spin this spinner and flip a coin. Write the sample space in set notation.

2. How many outcomes are there when you roll a die and spin a 5-part spinner?

3. Explain how you know a coin and a die do not give 8 outcomes.

Exit ticket — Section 2 · Independent events

Name:Date:
Target: I can tell when two events are independent. I can find P(A and B) by multiplying. (CP2)

1. Flip a coin and roll a die. Find P(heads and an odd number).

2. A bag has 1 red and 4 blue counters. Draw, put it back, draw again. Find P(red, red).

3. You flip 4 tails in a row. Explain how you know P(heads) on the next flip.

Exit ticket — Section 3 · Dependent events

Name:Date:
Target: I can tell when two events are dependent. I can find the probability by modelling the sample space. (CP3)

1. A bag has 3 red and 1 blue counter. Draw two without replacement. Fill in the second fractions.

2. Find P(red, red) for the same bag.

3. Explain how you know the second draw is not 3/4 again.

Exit ticket — Section 4 · Probability problems

Name:Date:
Target: I can decide if events are independent or dependent. I can model and solve problems with two events. (CP4)

1. Five names are in a hat: 2 girls and 3 boys. Two names are drawn for team captains. Find P(two girls).

2. Roll two dice. Find P(sum of 4).

3. Explain how you know whether the captain draw is independent or dependent.
