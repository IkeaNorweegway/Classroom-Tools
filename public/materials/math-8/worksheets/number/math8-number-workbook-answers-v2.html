Number

Strand Workbook — Answer Key

Answer key · Teacher copy
Every card says which topic and notes section must be taught before the question opens. The student copy has no topic labels: tell students which questions are open each day. Misconception tags mark the questions built to catch a common mistake.
1★ Core
Opens after: RN1 — Rational Numbers, Section 1

Jo says −4 is not a rational number. "It is not a fraction."

  1. 1. Sort these numbers into the rings: 7, 0, −4, 3/5, −2.5, 12/3.
  2. 2. Is Jo right? Explain.
Answer 1. Natural: 7 and 12/3 (= 4). Integers: 0 and −4. Rational: 3/5 and −2.5.
2. No. −4 = −4/1. Every integer is also a rational number. The rings sit inside each other.
Misconception target

"Integers aren't rational" / "fractions aren't rational, only integers are." Have the student write each integer as a fraction over 1.

2★ Core
Opens after: SR1 — Square Roots, Section 1

Leo says √36 = 18. "The root is half."

  1. 1. Use the square. Find the side.
  2. 2. Which are perfect squares: 49, 50, 0.25, 4/9?
Answer 1. √36 = 6, because 6 × 6 = 36. Leo is wrong: 18 × 18 = 324.
2. 49 = 7². 0.25 = 0.5². 4/9 = (2/3)². 50 is not a perfect square (7² = 49, 8² = 64).
Misconception target

"√ of a number is half of it." Ask: what number times itself makes 36? Check with the side of the square.

3★ Core
Opens after: PR1 — Proportional Reasoning, Section 1

Kai runs 2 km. Mia runs 2.5 times as far as Kai.

  1. 1. How far does Mia run?
  2. 2. Kai's distance is how many times Mia's?
Answer 1. 2.5 × 2 = 5 km.
2. 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4. Kai runs 0.4 times (or 2/5 of) Mia's distance.
4★ Core
Opens after: RN2 — Rational Numbers, Section 2

Sam says 1/3 = 0.3.

  1. 1. Finish the division. Write 1/3 with a bar.
  2. 2. Is Sam right? Explain.
  3. 3. Ava says there is no number between 0.5 and 0.6. Name two.
Answer 1. The remainder is always 1. The 3s never end: 1/3 = 0.3.
2. No. 0.3 = 3/10. That is a little less than 1/3.
3. Any two, like 0.51 and 0.55. There are endless numbers between: 0.501, 0.5001, …
Misconception target

Two targets. "0.333… is 0.3": point to the remainder that keeps coming back. "There's no number between 0.5 and 0.6": write them as 0.50 and 0.60 and count the hundredths between.

5★ Core
Opens after: SR2 — Square Roots, Section 2

Jo is checking square roots.

  1. 1. Find √25.
  2. 2. Solve x² = 25. Mark the answers on the line.
  3. 3. Jo says √−9 = −3. Square −3 to check.
Answer 1. √25 = 5. The √ sign means the positive (principal) root.
2. x = 5 or x = −5. We write x = ±5. Both are 5 steps from 0.
3. (−3) × (−3) = 9, not −9. No real number squared gives −9. √−9 has no real answer.
Misconception target

Two targets. "√25 = ±5": the radical gives only 5; the equation x² = 25 has two answers. "√−9 = −3": have the student square −3 and look at the sign.

6★ Core
Opens after: PR2 — Proportional Reasoning, Section 2

A sports drink uses 4 scoops of powder for 10 cups of water.

Leo says, "k is always big ÷ small. So k is 2.5 both ways."

  1. 1. Fill in the table. Find k for water = k × powder.
  2. 2. Find k for powder = k × water.
  3. 3. Is Leo right?
Answer 1. 8 scoops: 20 cups. k = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5.
2. k = 4 ÷ 10 = 0.4.
3. No. k depends on the order. Powder to water gives 2.5. Water to powder gives 0.4.
Misconception target

"k is always the bigger number divided by the smaller." Ask: which quantity is multiplied by k to get the other?

7★★ Extended
Opens after: RN3 — Rational Numbers, Section 3

Kai writes −2/3 + 1/2 = −1/5. He added the tops and the bottoms.

  1. 1. Use the chips. Find the right answer.
  2. 2. Find (−1.5) × (−0.4).
  3. 3. Find −3/4 ÷ 1/2.
Answer 1. Common denominator 6: −4/6 + 3/6 = −1/6.
2. 1.5 × 0.4 = 0.6. Two negatives: positive. Answer 0.6.
3. −3/4 × 2/1 = −6/4 = −3/2 (−1.5).
Misconception target

"−1/2 + 1/3 = 0/5" (adding tops and bottoms). Ask: are the chips the same size? Only same-size parts can be counted together.

8★★ Extended
Opens after: SR3 — Square Roots, Section 3

Ana says √60 ≈ 30. Ben says √60 ≈ 7.5, "halfway between 7 and 8."

  1. 1. Is Ana right? Check by squaring.
  2. 2. Use the lines. Is √60 closer to 7 or to 8?
  3. 3. Estimate √60 to one decimal. Check by squaring.
Answer 1. No. 30² = 900. Ana took half.
2. 7² = 49 and 8² = 64. 60 is closer to 64. So √60 is closer to 8.
3. √60 ≈ 7.7. 7.7² = 59.29 and 7.8² = 60.84. 7.7 is closer. Ben's 7.5 is too low (7.5² = 56.25).
Misconception target

"√50 ≈ 25" or "≈ 7.5 halfway." Have the student find where 60 sits between 49 and 64 on the top line, then drop down.

9★★ Extended
Opens after: PR3 — Proportional Reasoning, Section 3

6 L of paint covers 45 m² of wall.

  1. 1. Find k. Then fill in the table.
  2. 2. How much paint covers 120 m²?
Answer 1. k = 45 ÷ 6 = 7.5 m² for each litre. 10 L: 7.5 × 10 = 75 m².
2. a = b ÷ k = 120 ÷ 7.5 = 16 L. Check: 7.5 × 16 = 120 ✓.
10★★ Extended
Opens after: RN4 — Rational Numbers, Section 4

Mia finds −3 + 4 × (−0.5)². She adds −3 + 4 first and gets 0.25.

  1. 1. Fill in the steps.
  2. 2. Estimate (−4.9) × 2.1 + 7.8. Then find it.
Answer 1. Power first: (−0.5)² = 0.25. Then 4 × 0.25 = 1. Then −3 + 1 = −2.
2. Estimate: (−5) × 2 + 8 = −2. Exact: −10.29 + 7.8 = −2.49. Close to −2, so it is reasonable.
11★★ Extended
Opens after: SR4 — Irrational Numbers, Section 4

Tess says π = 3.14 and √2 = 1.41 exactly.

  1. 1. Find 1.41². Is it 2?
  2. 2. Sort into the boxes: √2, 3.14, π, √49, 0.6, −√3.
Answer 1. 1.41² = 1.9881. Not 2. So 1.41 is only close to √2.
2. Natural: √49 = 7. Rational: 3.14 and 0.6 = 2/3. Irrational: √2, π, −√3.
3.14 and 1.41 are approximate. π and √2 are exact; their decimals never end or repeat.
Misconception target

"π = 3.14 exactly" / "1.41 is √2." Squaring 1.41 shows the gap. 3.14 is a rational number, so it cannot be π.

12★★ Extended
Opens after: PR4 — Proportional Reasoning, Section 4

3 painters take 8 h to paint a fence. Leo says 6 painters take 16 h.

  1. 1. Fill in the arrays. Is Leo right?
  2. 2. How long do 4 painters take?
  3. 3. Is this direct or inverse proportion?
Answer 1. No. The job is 24 painter-hours. 6 painters take 24 ÷ 6 = 4 h.
2. 24 ÷ 4 = 6 h.
3. Inverse. More painters, less time. a × b = 24 stays the same.
Misconception target

"More workers, more days." Ask: does the fence get bigger when more people help? The squares in the arrays stay at 24.

Challenge zone

Try these after the others. Each one uses ideas from more than one topic.

13★★ Extended
Opens after: PR2 + RN4 — Proportional Reasoning and Estimating

Gas costs $1.45 for each litre. Sam buys 32.5 L.

  1. 1. Estimate the cost first.
  2. 2. Find the cost. Round to the cent.
  3. 3. Is cost proportional to litres? Give k.
Answer 1. About 1.5 × 30 = $45 (or 1.5 × 32 = $48).
2. 1.45 × 32.5 = 47.125 ≈ $47.13. Close to the estimate.
3. Yes. Cost = 1.45 × litres. k = 1.45 dollars per litre.
14★★ Extended
Opens after: SR3 + SR4 + RN2 — Roots, Irrationals and the Number Line

A square garden has an area of 30 m².

  1. 1. Find the side to one decimal.
  2. 2. About how much fence goes around it?
  3. 3. Order from least to greatest: 5.4, √30, 5.4, 11/2.
Answer 1. 5² = 25 and 6² = 36. 30 is a bit closer to 25. √30 ≈ 5.5 (calculator: 5.477…).
2. 4 × 5.48 ≈ 21.9 m. About 22 m of fence.
3. 5.4 < 5.4 < √30 < 11/2. (5.4, 5.444…, 5.477…, 5.5)
