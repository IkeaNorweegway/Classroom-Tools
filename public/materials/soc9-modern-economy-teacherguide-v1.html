Teacher Guide

Building a Modern Economy

Social Studies 9  ·  Systems  ·  Suggested pacing: 4 classes

Guiding question: How did economic changes transform Canada into a modern economy?

Learning outcome: Students analyze how economic crisis, growth, and global trade shaped Canada's modern economy.

Big idea for students to leave with: Canada's modern economy was not inevitable — it was built through a specific sequence of crisis, deliberate government intervention, and international integration, and each of those choices had trade-offs.

Discussion Prompts

Opener

"Should a government ever spend money it doesn't have?" Poll the class before Section 2, then revisit the same question after — track whether the historical example changes anyone's answer.

Common Misconceptions to Watch For

Misconception
"Deficit spending and debt are the same thing as financial mismanagement."

Distinguish crisis-driven deficit spending (a deliberate, temporary tool used in the Depression and after) from long-term structural debt problems — conflating the two makes it harder for students to evaluate Q6 fairly.

Using the Workbook

This unit connects directly to the urbanization unit (Section 3 here revisits postwar growth and suburbs from an economic angle) — consider explicitly reminding students of that unit before Q3–Q4. Q7 (NAFTA/interdependence) works well as a debate-format activity if time allows, since it has genuinely defensible positions on both sides.
