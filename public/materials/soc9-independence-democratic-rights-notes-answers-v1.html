Guiding Question: How did Canada become a more independent nation and expand democratic rights?

Section 1: Canada's Growing Autonomy

Blank / PromptExpected Answer
Balfour Declaration (1926): Dominions were ___ in status to Britainequal

Canada became a fully ___ nationsovereign / independent
Shift driven by post-___ exhaustionwar (First World War)

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. It declared Britain and the Dominions equal in status, none subordinate to another.
  2. It made that equality legally binding, formally transforming Canada into a sovereign nation.
  3. Post-war exhaustion, diplomatic pressure from the Dominions, and a preference for cooperation over coercion.
  4. Controlling foreign policy meant Canada could independently choose alliances, trade partners, and eventually whether to declare war — all central features of an independent nation.

Section 2: Women's Suffrage and the Persons Case

Blank / PromptExpected Answer
Suffrage: the right to ___ and hold officevote
Famous Five: five prominent ___ womenAlberta
Before the case, law did not clearly recognize women as ___persons
Persons Case affirmed the right to hold ___ officeappointed or elected
Reinforced the principle of ___equality

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. The Famous Five were five prominent Alberta women whose legal challenge became known as the Persons Case.
  2. It established that women were legal "persons" and therefore eligible to hold appointed or elected office.
  3. It matters because rights history is uneven — a legal "win" for some women did not automatically extend to all women, especially Indigenous and racialized women.
  4. Sample: Both show Canada redefining who counts as a full participant in its political system — one nationally (from colony to sovereign country), one about half the population (from non-persons to persons) — both expanding who gets a voice.
