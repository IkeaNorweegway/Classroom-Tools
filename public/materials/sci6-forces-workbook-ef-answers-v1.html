Answer not correct. Because: Newton's Third Law says action and reaction forces are always equal in size. The car and truck exert the same size force on each other. They have different masses, so the effects are different — but the forces are equal.

Core A student says: "Action and reaction forces cancel out. So nothing ever moves." What is wrong with this thinking?
Word Bank  different objects · same object · cannot cancel · balanced forces · Newton's Third Law
Hint: Forces only cancel when they act on the SAME object. Action-reaction pairs act on different objects.

The error is:

Answer The error is: action and reaction forces act on different objects — they cannot cancel each other. Forces only cancel when they act on the same object. Because each force acts on a different object, each object can still accelerate.

Stretch Zone — Challenge Questions
S1
A skateboarder pushes off a wall. She speeds up and moves away. Use Newton's Third Law to explain the full sequence of forces that causes her to move.
Answer Skateboarder pushes wall (action force on wall). Wall pushes back on skateboarder with equal and opposite force (reaction). Reaction force acts on skateboarder, accelerating her away. Two objects, two forces — N3L.
S2
A suspension bridge uses cables to hold up the road deck. The cables are under tension. The towers are under compression. Explain why each force type is correct for each part. Use particle-level thinking.
Answer Cables under tension: forces pull outward → particles pulled apart → tension is correct for cables (which are designed to stretch without breaking). Towers under compression: weight of road deck pushes down → particles pushed together → compression is correct for columns/towers. Particle-level thinking: each structure is engineered for the force it will experience.
S3
Design a material test to find the elastic limit of a rubber band. Name: what you measure, what you change, what you record, and how you know you have passed the elastic limit.
Answer Measure: extension (length) of the rubber band. Change: force applied (add masses). Record: length at each mass. Elastic limit passed: when removing the mass does NOT return the rubber band to its original length (permanent stretch = plastic deformation begins).
S4
In many Indigenous cultures, technology like snowshoes and birchbark canoes was designed to manage forces carefully. Choose one example. Explain which forces the technology had to handle and what properties the material needed.
Answer Example — snowshoes: distribute weight (normal force) over large area → less pressure per cm² → avoid sinking into snow. Material must resist compression and flex without plastic deformation. Example — birchbark canoe: hull under compression from water pressure; paddles use tension in arms and N3L to propel. Accept any well-reasoned example naming specific forces and material properties.
