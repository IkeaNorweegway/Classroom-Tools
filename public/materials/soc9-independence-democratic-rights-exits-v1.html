Canada's Independence and Democratic Rights — Exit Tickets

Social Studies 9 · Alberta Draft Curriculum 2026

One ticket per cluster · 3–4 minutes each · Independent, no notes · Not for marks

Exit Ticket 1 — Steps to independence

LO 1: I can explain how the Balfour Declaration and the Statute of Westminster made Canada more independent.

Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. Name the 1926 document that described Britain's Dominions as equal in status. (1 mark)

2. What did the Statute of Westminster (1931) allow Canada to do that it could not do before? (2 marks)

3. Why did Canada still need the British Parliament to amend its constitution in 1931? (3 marks)

Exit Ticket 2 — The Persons Case

LO 2: I can describe the Persons Case and why the Famous Five mattered.

Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. In 1929, what question did the Privy Council in London answer about women? (1 mark)

2. Who were the Famous Five, and what did they ask the courts to decide? (2 marks)

3. Why was the Persons Case a turning point for women's rights in Canada? (3 marks)

Exit Ticket 3 — Controlling foreign policy

LO 3: I can explain why controlling its own foreign policy mattered to Canada.

Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. What is foreign policy? (1 mark)

2. Why would Canada want to make its own decisions about trade and treaties? (2 marks)

3. How did controlling its own foreign policy change Canada's place in the world? (3 marks)

Exit Ticket 4 — Timeline

LO 4: I can put these events in order on a timeline.

Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. Put in order, earliest first: Statute of Westminster (1931), Balfour Declaration (1926), Persons Case (1929). (2 marks)

2. Which of these events happened first: the Persons Case or the Statute of Westminster? (1 mark)

3. Explain why the order of these events matters for understanding how Canada became more independent. (3 marks)
