Concept 2: How Filtration Works — Layers

I can: explain what job a specific layer in a filter is doing.

A water filter usually works by stacking several materials in layers, and each layer does a different job — some layers catch big particles, some catch smaller ones, some trap things chemically. No single material does the whole job well by itself, which is why real filters (and yours) use more than one.

Concept 3: Testing, Data & Safety — The Most Important Idea in This Unit

I can: state, without being asked, why clear water is not the same as safe water.

Misconception

"If I filter water until it's clear, it's safe to drink."

Why this fails: filtering for clarity only removes particles you can see — dirt, sand, plant bits. It does nothing about things you can't see, like bacteria and other invisible contaminants. That's exactly why real water treatment plants use filtration plus other steps (like disinfection) — filtration alone was never the whole solution.

Correct understanding: Clear water can still be unsafe. Your filter is solving one specific problem — clarity — not "making water safe." We never taste or drink test water in this unit, for any reason, no matter how clear it looks.

Clarity is how clear or cloudy water looks — it's something you can measure and compare (against a printed chart, or by timing how long it takes to see clearly through it). That measurement is your data — a number or observation you record, not just a feeling that it "seems better."
