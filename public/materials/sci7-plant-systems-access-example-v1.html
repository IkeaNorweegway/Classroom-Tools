Grade 7 Science — Plants for Food & Fibre

Plant Systems: Two-Track Access Example

Demonstration activity — not the full unit workbook. Shows one concept (root/shoot systems, LO 7-2-02) built two ways.

Read before choosing a track

Both versions below teach from the same diagram and the same concept: plants have a water pipe (xylem, moves water up from roots to leaves) and a food pipe (phloem, moves sugar down from leaves to the rest of the plant). What changes is the reading/writing load and — in Track B only — the outcome itself.

  • Track A is for a student whose achievement is behind but whose cognitive ability is typical (a skill gap or undiagnosed/diagnosed LD). The outcome stays at grade level (LO 7-2-02, full concept). Only the access route changes.
  • Track B is for a student with a documented intellectual/significant cognitive disability and an IPP specifying modified/alternate outcomes. The outcome itself is simplified. Do not use Track B without an IPP team decision — this shows what a modified version could look like, it does not replace that process.

Both use the same plant picture so a student on either track can sit in the same class, working on the same topic, without a visibly “different” worksheet. See research/significant-learning-gaps-research.md, Part 6.

The shared plant diagram used in both tracks below.
Track A

Grade-level concept, low reading/writing load

Outcome: LO 7-2-02, unchanged. Adjusted: response format, sentence length, oral-answer option. Not adjusted: the concept — including the reasoning question at the end.
1Find the parts

Look at the plant picture. Circle the root. Put a box around the stem. Underline a leaf.

2Match the job

Draw a line from each plant part to its job.

🌱 Roots↔🌤️ Makes food from sunlight
🌿 Stem↔💧 Takes in water and minerals
🍃 Leaves↔🏗️ Holds the plant up, moves stuff between roots and leaves
Hint Roots are underground. What's down there for a plant to take in?
3Label the two pipes

The plant has two pipes inside its stem. Write the correct word on each pipe using the word bank.

Word bank xylem   phloem
4Which way?

Circle the correct answer for each pipe.

Xylem (water pipe): moves water from the roots → leaves / leaves → roots

Phloem (food pipe): moves food from the roots → leaves / leaves → roots

5Finish the sentence

Circle the words that finish the sentence correctly — or say your answer out loud to your teacher or a partner instead of writing.

"The roots take in water and minerals / food that's already made. The leaves make the plant's food using sunlight / soil."

6Think like a scientist★★ Apply

A farmer accidentally cuts a ring all the way around a tree's bark. This cuts the phloem but leaves the xylem underneath undamaged.

Will the leaves still get water? YES / NO

Will the roots still get food from the leaves? YES / NO

Point to the part of the diagram above (Q3) that shows why.

Hint Which pipe did the cut damage — the water pipe or the food pipe?

This question stays at full Apply-level demand — same reasoning a student answering in full paragraphs would need. Only the response format changed.

Track B

Modified outcome, systematic instruction

Sample IPP-aligned outcome — confirm with school team before use.
Outcome: Student will sort pictures of plant parts by their job (takes in water / holds plant up / makes food) with increasing independence. Deliberately narrower than LO 7-2-02 — does not require naming xylem/phloem or explaining direction of flow. Vocabulary exposure to “water pipe” and “food pipe” is exposure only, not assessed.
●Sort the plant parts

Materials: 3 picture cards (root, stem, leaf — same photo style as the class diagram, not younger-grade clip art) and 3 sorting mats.

💧Takes In Water
🏗️Holds It Up
☀️Makes Food
🌱Root
🌿Stem
🍃Leaf
StepWhat the student doesPrompt level (use least first)
1Look at the root pictureIndependent → point to picture → “Look here” + point
2Decide which mat it belongs onIndependent → “Which one takes in water?” → point to correct mat
3Place the card on the matIndependent → gesture toward mat → guide hand
4Repeat for stem, then leafSame sequence — prompts fade as steps repeat

Give immediate feedback after every placement — confirm correct placements right away (“Yes — roots take in water!”), and if incorrect, guide to the correct mat immediately rather than letting the error sit. Track which prompt level was needed each time; the goal across sessions is fewer prompts, not just a correct final placement.

Bonus exposure — not assessed, whole group togetherTeacher points to the shared plant diagram and says: “Scientists have a name for the water pipe — they call it xylem. And a name for the food pipe — phloem.” The student is not asked to recall or write these words — this is the same moment the rest of the class has with the vocabulary, just without an assessment demand attached.

Why both stay on the same page

Per _meta-context.md → Students significantly below grade level: defaulting straight to “make it easier” without first knowing which track applies is the most common design error. Track A shows that “low reading and writing” does not have to mean “lower concept” — the ring-barking question (Q6) is genuine Apply-level science reasoning delivered through circling and pointing. Track B shows that a modified outcome can still keep the student in the same topic, same diagram, same classroom moment as everyone else — age-appropriate materials, not a different unit.
