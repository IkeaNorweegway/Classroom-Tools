Q1Core2 marks

Name two social programs that made Canada a "welfare state" after WWII.

Answer Universal healthcare and old-age pensions.
Q2Core2 marks

Who won the Nobel Peace Prize for his role in resolving the Suez Crisis, and what did his approach create?

Answer Lester B. Pearson; his approach helped create modern peacekeeping.
Q3Core3 marks

Name two government policies that caused long-term harm to Indigenous communities during this period.

Answer Residential schools and the Sixties Scoop.
Q4Core2 marks

What policy contributed to Western alienation, and which two provinces felt it most?

Answer The National Energy Program; Alberta and Saskatchewan.
Q5Core2 marks

What did the Constitution Act (1982) allow Canada to do for the first time?

Answer Gain full control over (patriate) its own constitution without needing British approval.
Q6Core2 marks

Name two non-European regions that became major sources of immigration to Canada after WWII.

Answer Any two: India, China, Vietnam.

Part B — Extended Questions (Paragraph Response)

No word bank. Analyze and evaluate.

Q7Extended5 marks
A company based in Toronto makes a decision that benefits head office. It requires its Alberta branch to change how it operates and share more profit, without asking Alberta staff first.

Is it ever fair for a national policy to affect one region more than others? Use the National Energy Program to support your answer.

Answer Like the company scenario, the National Energy Program favoured the federal government and central Canadian consumers. It reduced revenue and control for Alberta's oil economy, which fed directly into Western alienation. I think it can be fair for a policy to affect one region more, if the affected region has a real voice in shaping it. The core unfairness in the NEP wasn't that Alberta was affected — it was that Alberta felt shut out of the decision.
Q8Extended5 marks
A country is known internationally as a leader in human rights and diplomacy. At the same time, government-run schools inside its own borders are forcibly separating a minority group's children from their families.

Can a country's global reputation, such as Canada's peacekeeping record, be judged separately from harms happening at home, such as residential schools? Or should they always be judged together? Defend your position.

Answer I would argue these cannot be judged separately. Canada built a strong global reputation through NATO, the Korean War, and Pearson's peacekeeping work. At the same time, residential schools and the Sixties Scoop were separating Indigenous children from their families. A country's reputation should reflect its full record, not just its most flattering chapters.
Q9Extended5 marks
A new city bylaw limits what a homeowner can build on their own property, for the good of the whole neighbourhood. One resident says this protects the community. Another says it unfairly restricts their freedom.

Section 1 of the Charter of Rights and Freedoms allows "reasonable limits" on rights. Give one example of a reasonable limit from this unit. Does it strike the right balance between freedom and the good of the community?

Answer One example from this unit is limiting hate speech, such as racism or antisemitism, even though this restricts freedom of expression. I think this strikes a fair balance, because it protects a group from real, direct harm rather than just an inconvenience.
