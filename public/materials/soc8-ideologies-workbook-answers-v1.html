Core & Extended Questions — Q1 to Q12

Q1★ Core
[opens after Lesson 1 — Individualism vs. collectivism] · scenario
Answer

(a) Individualist response: The student who did the work should get the higher or individual grade — effort and contribution should be recognized separately, not shared equally regardless of input.

(b) Collectivist response: The whole group should receive the same grade — the team succeeds or struggles together, and grading should reflect the group's shared outcome, not just one person's part.

Accept any reasonable elaboration that correctly applies the individualism/collectivism distinction.

Q2★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 2 — Historical Roots] · Plato vs. Aristotle, argue which is better
Answer

(a) Plato was most worried about untrained citizens making poor decisions — he wanted the wisest people in charge. Aristotle was most worried about rulers abusing power or making unfair judgments — he wanted decisions bound by shared law.

(b) Any well-reasoned position is acceptable, provided the student states a clear preference and gives at least one specific reason tied to one of the two philosophers' actual arguments (e.g., "Aristotle, because rule of law protects citizens even from a leader who turns out to be unwise or corrupt" or "Plato, because expert decision-making could avoid mistakes made by inexperienced voters"). Mark down answers that state a preference with no reasoning connected to either philosopher's actual argument.

Q3★ Core
[opens after Lesson 3 — Political Spectrum] · place it on the line
Answer

(a) Belongs on the far left — government ownership and control of all major industries is a strongly collectivist position (more government decision-making, less individual/private control).

Q4★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 4 — Four Ideologies] · misconception error analysis
Misconception Target

communism = socialism

Answer

(a) Error: The statement collapses two different ideologies into one and inaccurately claims socialism means "the government owns everything." Socialism means the means of production are owned or regulated by the community — this can coexist with an elected government, private property in other sectors, and existing social classes. Communism is the further, more extreme goal: a classless, stateless society with common ownership of everything — meaning no government left to "own" anything.

(b) Correct version: "Socialism means the community owns or regulates the means of production, often alongside an active government. Communism is a different, more extreme idea: a classless, stateless society with common ownership of everything, and no government at all."

Q5★ Core
[opens after Lesson 5 — Ideology Taken to Extremes] · recognize the pattern
Answer

(a) Matches: one leader/party holding complete power (bans opposition parties); opposition/free press shut down or controlled (takes over media); propaganda controlling belief (state-controlled news); state influence over schools (mandated ideology in schools). All four features from the notes are represented.

(b) Reasonable answers include: whether there is any remaining check on power (courts, free elections), what happens to people who criticize the government (are they punished?), or whether this control extends into everyday/family life. Accept any thoughtful gap the student identifies and justifies.

Q6★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 1 — Individualism vs. collectivism / What Is an Ideology] · misconception error analysis
Misconception Target

ideology = political party

Answer

(a) Error: The statement treats the Liberal Party and liberalism (the ideology) as identical. A political party can change its platform or drift from an ideology's original ideas without the ideology itself changing — the ideology is the underlying idea; the party is one organization that may represent it well or poorly at a given time.

(b) Correct explanation: Liberalism is a set of ideas (progress through reform, active government role for equal opportunity). The Liberal Party is one organization that has organized around versions of that idea. If the party's platform changes, that tells us the party changed — not that the idea of liberalism itself changed. The same ideology can also appear, in different forms, inside other parties or in other countries.

Q7★ Core
[opens after Lesson 2 — Historical Roots] · Athens aristocrats vs. democratic reformers
Answer

(a) Athenian aristocrats wanted political power to stay with the small, wealthy, well-born class that had always held it — they valued tradition and the existing social order.

(b) Democratic reformers like Solon and Cleisthenes wanted to expand political rights to more ordinary citizens over time, reducing the aristocrats' exclusive hold on decision-making.

Q8★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 3 — Political Spectrum] · misconception error analysis
Misconception Target

left/right are fixed universal labels

Answer

(a) Incorrect because left and right are relative to the surrounding political culture, not fixed globally. Example: a policy providing government-funded healthcare for everyone is considered mainstream/centrist in Canada, but the same policy could be labeled "far-left" in a country where private healthcare is the political norm. Same policy, different label, depending on where you are.

(b) What determines the label is comparison to the political norms of that specific place and time — there is no single, fixed, worldwide scale that "left" and "right" are measured against.

Q10★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 5 — Ideology Taken to Extremes] · misconception error analysis
Misconception Target

totalitarianism is only a 1930s–40s German phenomenon

Answer

(a) Incorrect because totalitarianism is a category of government — complete control over political, social, and economic life, crushed opposition, and state propaganda — not a single, closed historical event. Nazi Germany is the historical case study students learn in depth, but the same pattern (one leader/party seizing total control, banning opposition, controlling media) can occur in any era or country if the conditions allow it.

(b) Someone who believes this misconception might fail to recognize the warning signs (banned opposition, controlled media, propaganda, punished dissent) if a similar pattern started developing somewhere else today — treating it as "impossible now" makes people less likely to notice or respond to it.

Answer

Missing cells: Who leads? (Aristotle) — Whoever holds office, but bound by law. Main worry (Plato) — Ordinary/untrained people making uninformed decisions.

Q12★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 1 — Individualism vs. collectivism] · why people are drawn to one or the other
Answer

Strong answers give a specific, plausible reason for each pull — not just a restated definition. For example: a person might be drawn to individualism because they value being judged on their own effort and want the freedom to make their own choices without depending on group decisions. A person might be drawn to collectivism because they value belonging, shared responsibility, and believe some problems (like poverty or infrastructure) are better solved by everyone contributing together than by individuals acting alone. Mark down answers that only restate the definitions from Section 1 without explaining why someone would be pulled toward that view.

Stretch Questions — S1 to S3

Multi-concept synthesis. Use as discussion guides or for extended assessment marking.

S1★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 5 — all sections complete] · full spectrum diagram
Answer

Correct left-to-right order: Communism — Socialism — Liberalism — Conservatism. Communism sits furthest left (most collectivist: classless, stateless, common ownership). Socialism sits left-of-center (community owns/regulates means of production, but state and classes may remain). Liberalism sits right-of-center-left, closer to the middle (uses active government for equal opportunity, but preserves individual rights and private property broadly). Conservatism sits furthest right (minimal state involvement, individual/family/tradition-led decisions). Justification should explain the placement using the ideology's core idea, not just restate the order.

S2★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 5] · Holocaust primary source reflection
Answer

(a) Answers will vary by the specific account shared. Look for a genuine, specific connection between the total political control described in Section 5 (banned opposition, propaganda, state control of information and daily life) and the concrete impact described in the survivor's account — not just a general statement that "the Nazis were bad." Strong answers name a specific detail from the account and connect it to a specific feature of totalitarian control from the notes.

(b) Strong answers explain that survivor accounts make the human cost concrete and specific in a way that statistics alone cannot — they show the Holocaust affected real individuals with names, families, and daily lives, which deepens understanding and empathy beyond the scale of "six million." Accept any thoughtful, respectful answer that goes beyond "because it's sad."

Teacher Note

Preview any survivor account for age-appropriateness before sharing. Recommended sources: Anne Frank House, USC Shoah Foundation, Yad Vashem. Do not substitute an unverified or dramatized source.

S3★★ Extended
[opens after Lesson 5] · full unit synthesis · teach it
Answer

A complete answer includes, in accessible language: (1) an ideology is a set of ideas about how society/government should work; (2) individualism values the person's own choices, collectivism values the group's needs; (3) ideas sit on a spectrum from collectivism (left) to individualism (right), and where the line's "middle" sits can shift by country and era; (4) one reason societies limit government power — for example, to prevent a leader or party from gaining so much control that opposition, free press, and individual rights can be taken away (connects directly to Section 5). Mark on completeness (all four elements present) and clarity for a Grade 4 audience — jargon without explanation should be marked down even if technically accurate.
