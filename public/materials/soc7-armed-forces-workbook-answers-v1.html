Armed Forces and Sovereignty

Grade 7 Social Studies  ·  Practice Workbook — Answer Key  ·  Alberta Draft Curriculum 2026

Teacher Copy — Not for Students

Learning Outcome: Students relate the development of armed forces in Canada to sovereignty.

Guiding Question: How can defence of peace and security play a role in the development of a nation?

Model answers and marking notes for every question. Each card says which notes section must be taught before the question opens. The student copy shows no section labels: announce which questions are open each session.

For opinion and judgement questions, the model answer is one strong example. Accept any position backed by accurate evidence from the unit.

Part A — Core and Extended Questions

Q1★ Core
Opens after Section 1 — Policing the West
AnswerE.g. "We were sent to stop American whisky traders and enforce Canada's laws in the West. Our job is to keep order and show that this land belongs to Canada."
Q2★ Core
Opens after Section 4 — The First World War
Answera) gas attack, Canadians hold the line · b) all four divisions together · c) Newfoundland Regiment · d) deep mud.
Q3★ Core
Opens after Section 2 — Building a military
Answer1855 Militia Act → 1871 British Army leaves → 1904 new Militia Act → 1910 Royal Canadian Navy → 1931 Statute of Westminster.
Q4★ Core
Opens after Section 6 — Sovereignty
AnswerA country's power to run its own affairs. Examples: the NWMP enforcing Canadian law (1873), the navy (1910), signing Versailles (1919), the Statute of Westminster (1931).
Q5★ Core
Opens after Section 3 — The Boer War
AnswerMany English Canadians wanted to support Britain; many French Canadians (Bourassa) said Canada shouldn't fight Britain's wars. Laurier compromised by sending volunteers only.
Q6★ Core
Opens after Section 5 — Reading a memorial
AnswerWho: 31 local people killed in WWI and 18 in WWII. Which wars: both world wars (the WWII names were added later). Who built it: the town council, in 1922, soon after the war. Words: "Lest We Forget" shows the town wanted the sacrifice remembered forever.
Q7★★ Extended
Opens after Sections 1 and 2 — Order and defence
AnswerWith British soldiers gone, Canada had to defend itself and keep order; the NWMP (1873) gave Canada its own force to enforce laws in the West. Both put security in Canadian hands.
Q8★★ Extended
Opens after Section 1 — The NWMP and First Nations
AnswerProtecting: it stopped American whisky traders and violence like the Cypress Hills Massacre. Controlling: it enforced federal rules on reserves and helped the government put down the 1885 resistance. Its job was to make Canada's authority real, whether or not First Nations agreed.
Q9★★ Extended
Opens after Section 4 — At war automatically
AnswerCanada did not control its own foreign affairs; Britain did. Canada could still decide how much to contribute (how many soldiers, how much money).
Q10★★ Extended
Opens after Section 2 — The navy debate
AnswerA Canadian navy was under Canadian control: Canada could defend its own coasts and decide how its ships were used, instead of relying on Britain.
Q11★★ Extended
Opens after Section 3 — Significance of the Boer War
AnswerAgree: only about 7,000 served, but it showed Canada could raise its own soldiers (Paardeberg brought pride) and revealed the English–French divide over the Empire that returned in WWI. Accept disagreement with evidence about Paardeberg.
Q12★★ Extended
Opens after Section 4 — Why Vimy?
AnswerFor the first time all four Canadian divisions attacked together as one Canadian force; they captured a ridge that British and French armies had failed to take; the success and the cost (about 3,600 killed) made it a symbol of Canada acting as a nation.
Q13★★ Extended
Opens after Section 5 — Serving an unequal country
AnswerE.g. No. 2 Construction Battalion: about 600 Black men, turned away from regular units, built roads and railways in France; remembering them honours their service, shows the whole history, and led to Canada's 2022 apology.
Q14★★ Extended
Opens after Section 6 — 1914 and 1939
Answer1914: at war automatically when Britain declared war. 1939: Canada's Parliament decided for itself a week after Britain. In between, Canada's war effort earned it a voice (Versailles, the League), and the Statute of Westminster (1931) gave it control of its foreign affairs.

Part B — Challenge Zone

QS1★★ Extended
Opens after Sections 1, 2 and 6
Answer1870s: the NWMP enforced Canadian law in the West. 1910s: the Royal Canadian Navy; the Canadian Corps at Vimy under a Canadian commander. 1930s: the Statute of Westminster gave control of foreign affairs, earned partly by the war effort.
QS2★★ Extended
Opens after Sections 4 and 5
AnswerAnswers vary; strong answers give accurate who, where, when and what, and connect it to the cost or meaning of the war.
QS3★★ Extended
Opens after All sections
AnswerStrong answers link keeping order (NWMP), self-defence (1871, militia, navy) and wartime contribution (Boer War, Vimy) to growing control over Canada's own affairs, ending with the Statute of Westminster.
