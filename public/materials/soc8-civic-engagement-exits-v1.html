Civic Engagement — Exit Tickets

Grade 8 Social Studies · Alberta Draft Curriculum 2026

Unit Learning Outcome: Students examine civic engagement to understand and influence issues and events. Each ticket below applies this LO to one knowledge cluster.

Exit Ticket 1 — Engaged Citizenship

LO: Students examine civic engagement to understand and influence issues and events (focus: the awareness → decision-making → action framework).

Time: 3–4 minutes &nbsp; Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. Name the three parts of engaged citizenship, in order. (1 mark)

2. Deshawn notices his bus is often late. He looks into why, and decides the schedule needs to change. Which part of the framework is Deshawn's noticing? Which part is his decision? (2 marks)

3. Explain why a person who is only "aware" of an issue is not yet an engaged citizen. Use the framework in your answer. (3 marks)

Exit Ticket 2 — Forms of Civic Engagement

LO: Students examine civic engagement to understand and influence issues and events (focus: forms of civic engagement and age).

Time: 3–4 minutes &nbsp; Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. Name one form of civic engagement that does not require being 18 or older. (1 mark)

2. A 14-year-old wants to help stop noise pollution near their school. Name two forms of civic engagement they could actually use. (2 marks)

3. A student says, "You have to be an adult to really make a difference." Explain why this is incorrect, using at least two specific forms of civic engagement as evidence. (3 marks)

Exit Ticket 3 — Voting and Voter Eligibility

LO: Students examine civic engagement to understand and influence issues and events (focus: voting as a responsibility of democratic citizenship).

Time: 3–4 minutes &nbsp; Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. Name one of the four voter eligibility requirements in Canada. (1 mark)

2. A person is a Canadian citizen and 19 years old, but has proof of residence for a riding they moved out of two years ago. Can they vote in their new riding? Explain. (2 marks)

3. Explain why Canada requires all four voter eligibility requirements to be met at once, rather than accepting "most" of them. Use an example in your answer. (3 marks)

Exit Ticket 4 — Global Issues Affecting Canada

LO: Students examine civic engagement to understand and influence issues and events (focus: how global issues affect Canadians).

Time: 3–4 minutes &nbsp; Marks: /6

Name: _________________________ Date: ____________

1. Name one global issue that can affect life in Canada. (1 mark)

2. A severe drought overseas shrinks a wheat harvest. Explain, in two steps, how this could affect a Canadian grocery store. (2 marks)

3. A student says, "What happens in other countries doesn't really change my life in Canada." Explain why this is incorrect, using one specific example from your notes. (3 marks)

Answer Key

Ticket 1 — Engaged Citizenship

1. Awareness → Decision-making → Action. Mark as correct if all three appear, in order, in the student's own words.

2. Noticing the bus is late = awareness. Deciding the schedule needs to change = decision-making. Award 1 mark for each correct match.

3. Full 3 marks require: naming that awareness is only the first stage; noting that decision-making and action are still required; and giving a reason why noticing alone does not change anything (nothing has actually been done yet). Award 1 mark for identifying awareness as insufficient, 1 mark for naming what's missing, 1 mark for a clear explanation tying it back to the framework.

Ticket 2 — Forms of Civic Engagement

1. Any one of: volunteering, community initiatives, rallies/protests, advocacy, ethical purchasing, political party membership, election volunteering. (Not voting.)

2. Any two of the forms listed above, applied plausibly to noise pollution (e.g., advocacy — writing to the school; a petition/community initiative; a rally). 1 mark each.

3. Full marks require naming two specific forms (not "do stuff") and explicitly stating that neither requires being 18. Award 1 mark per correctly used form (2 marks) + 1 mark for directly refuting the "have to be an adult" claim.

Ticket 3 — Voting and Voter Eligibility

1. Any one of: Canadian citizen; at least 18 years old; valid proof of residence in the riding; valid proof of identity.

2. No — they do not have valid proof of residence for their current (new) riding. Even though they are a citizen and old enough, all four requirements must be met, including residence proof matching the riding they are voting in.

3. Full marks require: stating that all four requirements must be met simultaneously with no exceptions; and using a concrete example (e.g., someone missing only one requirement, such as age or residence, still cannot vote) to show why "most" is not enough.

Ticket 4 — Global Issues Affecting Canada

1. Any one of: economic disruption, poverty, drought and famine, human migration, civil unrest, war.

2. Step 1: the smaller harvest reduces global wheat supply, raising the global price. Step 2: Canadian stores pay more for wheat/flour, so bread and other wheat products cost more for Canadian shoppers. Award 1 mark per correct step.

3. Full marks require a specific example (e.g., the 1979 Vietnamese refugee resettlement, or the wheat/bread price chain) and an explanation of how it created a real, traceable effect on people or prices in Canada — not just a general assertion that global issues "matter."
