Guiding Question: In what ways do citizen rights evolve?

Section 1: Defining Canadian Citizenship

Blank / PromptExpected Answer
Before 1947, people in Canada were ___ subjectsBritish
Act responded to a more ___ populationdiverse
Citizen status: obtained by birth or ___naturalization
Temporary resident: has a study, work, or ___ permitvisitor
Permanent resident: full legal status, some ___restrictions
Refugee: fled danger, seeking ___protection

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. British subjects — there was no separate legal category of Canadian citizen.
  2. Citizen, temporary resident, permanent resident, refugee.

Section 2: Advocacy and Legislative Change

Chronological order: 1. Canadian Bill of Rights (1960) 2. Royal Commission on Bilingualism and Biculturalism (1963) 3. Royal Commission on the Status of Women (1967) 4. Official Languages Act (1969) 5. Canadian Multiculturalism Policy (1971).

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. The Canadian Bill of Rights, 1960.
  2. Sample: The Royal Commission on the Status of Women (1967) responded to unequal treatment of women in law, employment, and politics, and its recommendations helped shape later reforms.

Section 3: The Charter of Rights and Freedoms

Blank / PromptExpected Answer
Section 1 allows ___ limits on rightsreasonable
Changes made through the ___ formulaamending
...or the ___ AccordCharlottetown

Source analysis (sample): Limiting hate speech, such as racism or antisemitism, is a reasonable limit — a democratic society accepts this restriction because it protects groups from real harm, even though it technically limits free expression.

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. Any four: fundamental freedoms, democratic rights, mobility rights, legal rights, equality rights, official language rights, minority language educational rights, multicultural heritage, Indigenous peoples' rights.
  2. It allows reasonable limits on rights and freedoms; example — banning hate speech.
  3. It allows provincial governments to exempt certain laws from specific Charter sections.

Section 4: Rights and Responsibilities

Retrieval Check answers:

  1. Sample: The right to vote (Section 3) connects to the responsibility of voting in elections — the right only has meaning if citizens actually use it.
  2. Sample: Individual rights matter because they protect each person from unfair treatment; collective rights matter because they protect whole communities (such as language or Indigenous rights) that individual rights alone might not cover.
