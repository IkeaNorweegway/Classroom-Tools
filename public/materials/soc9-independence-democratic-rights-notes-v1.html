The Famous Five — five prominent   women — launched a legal challenge, known as the Persons Case, that fundamentally redefined the status of women in Canadian law. Before this case, the law did not clearly recognize women as " " under the British North America Act.

By establishing that women were legal persons, the Persons Case affirmed that women had the right to hold   office in the country — appointed or elected — reinforcing the democratic principle of  .

Common misconception
"The Persons Case gave all Canadian women the right to vote."

It did not — federal voting rights for most women had already been won earlier (1918 federally, with provinces at different times). The Persons Case was specifically about whether women could be appointed to positions like the Senate, and it did not extend equally or immediately to Indigenous women, many racialized women, or others who continued to face legal barriers for decades afterward.
