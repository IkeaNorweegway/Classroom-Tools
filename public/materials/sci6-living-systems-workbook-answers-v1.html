Q13★★ Extendedafter Lesson 3 · respiration misconception: photosynthesis-respiration cancel

Two explanations — one is right.

Explanation A: "Photosynthesis and respiration cancel each other out. Plants absorb as much CO₂ through respiration as they release through photosynthesis, so plants have no net effect on oxygen levels."
Explanation B: "During daylight, a healthy plant photosynthesises far more than it respires. The net result is oxygen out and CO₂ in. Plants are net oxygen producers."

Which explanation is scientifically accurate?

How do you know? Use specific evidence in your answer.

Answer Correct: Explanation B.
Evidence: During daylight, healthy plants photosynthesize far more than they respire — net O₂ is released and net CO₂ is absorbed. The fact that Earth's atmosphere is ~21% oxygen is direct evidence that plants are net oxygen producers over geological time. Explanation A (they cancel) would predict no net oxygen in the atmosphere, which contradicts observed reality.

Answer Partially right: "food" and "starch" aren't identical — glucose is the plant's immediate food, starch is the stored form. But starch IS food in the sense that it stores the plant's energy. The student is wrong to say they're "two different things."

S1★★ Extendedmulti-concept: photosynthesis + sealed system + food web

A scientist seals a healthy potted plant, moist soil, and a small supply of CO₂ inside a completely airtight glass box placed in full sunlight. The plant grows steadily for six months.

(a) The soil mass barely changes over six months. Where is the plant getting the material to grow?

(b) CO₂ was sealed inside at the start. After six months, CO₂ levels inside the box are much lower. What happened to the carbon in the CO₂?

(c) A small animal is then placed in the sealed box. Predict whether the animal can survive long-term. Explain using both photosynthesis and the food web.

Answer (a) Plant gets mass from CO₂ in the air (sealed inside). Carbon from CO₂ is fixed into glucose and then into plant tissue via photosynthesis.
(b) Carbon in CO₂ was absorbed through photosynthesis and incorporated into plant biomass — wood, cellulose, leaves. CO₂ levels dropped because the plant was using it up.
(c) Animal CAN potentially survive if: the plant produces enough O₂ to meet the animal's respiratory needs, AND produces enough edible glucose/starch for the animal to eat. This models a simple closed-loop ecosystem. Long-term viability depends on whether plant growth and O₂ production match animal consumption rate.

S5★★ Extendedsynthesis — all concepts

A student writes:

"An ecosystem is basically just a list of organisms in a place. If you remove one organism, the list gets shorter, but everything else carries on."

Write a response that: identifies what is fundamentally wrong with the "list" idea; uses at least three specific examples from this unit; ends with a one-sentence definition of ecosystem that captures what the student is missing.

My definition:

Answer What is wrong with "list" idea: An ecosystem is not a list — it is a web of interdependencies where every organism's presence affects others through nutrient cycles, food webs, oxygen production, decomposition, and abiotic interactions. Removing one "item" doesn't just shorten the list; it cascades.
Must reference at least 3 examples: Yellowstone wolves (one removal = trophic cascade through the whole ecosystem); photosynthesis (plants build the oxygen atmosphere all animals breathe — the "list" is connected by chemistry); decomposers (without them, nutrient cycle stops and producers collapse); sacred plants (each plays specific ecological roles — not just culturally present but functionally essential).
One-sentence definition: An ecosystem is a community of biotic and abiotic components that are interconnected and interdependent — the removal or change of any one part cascades through the entire system.
