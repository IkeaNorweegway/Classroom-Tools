About this journal: This is your project log for a class-wide build — your apparatus is only one stage in a chain that has to work as a whole, so several steps here involve your neighbors, not just your own group.

How to use it: Work through the sections in order, and write in your own words.

Step 1: Define the Goal

The brief: Your group designs and builds one stage of a class-wide chain reaction. Your apparatus must (a) use at least two distinct simple machines, (b) mount to the shared pegboard using at least one 3D-printed custom bracket designed to the pegboard's real hole spacing, and (c) reliably trigger your downstream neighbor's apparatus across multiple consecutive test runs.

What is your apparatus's job in the chain — what does it receive, and what does it need to produce?

Which two (or more) simple machines do you expect to use, and what job is each one doing?

Step 3: Neighbor Hand-off Negotiation

Before either group finalizes a design, meet with your upstream neighbor (who triggers you) and your downstream neighbor (who you trigger). This is a two-way negotiation, not a one-way critique — both groups have to agree, and both groups have something at stake.

Upstream hand-off (what you will receive): What specific form will the incoming trigger take (a ball arriving at a height/speed, a lever pushed a distance, something else)? Agreed with:   (neighbor group)

Downstream hand-off (what you must produce): What specific form will your outgoing trigger take, and what does your downstream neighbor need it to do (arrive at what height/speed, push how far)? Agreed with:   (neighbor group)

Sketch the agreed hand-off point — jointly, with your neighbor, if possible. Label the exact measurement (height, speed, distance) both groups are building to.

Reflection

Describe one specific hand-off problem that came up this unit and how it was actually resolved — not what should have happened, what did.

Did your apparatus succeed because you and your neighbors understood the physics of the hand-off, or because you kept adjusting until something happened to work? How do you know?

Before this unit, would you have said an apparatus that "works every time by itself" was finished? Has that changed? Why?
