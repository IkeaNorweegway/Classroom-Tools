Q1Core2 marks

What were people in Canada legally considered before the Citizenship Act of 1947?

Answer British subjects.
Q2Core3 marks

Name the four categories of citizenship or residency status covered in this unit.

Answer Citizen, temporary resident, permanent resident, refugee.
Q3Core2 marks

What was the first federal legislation to outline rights and freedoms in Canada, and in what year?

Answer The Canadian Bill of Rights, 1960.
Q4Core3 marks

List three categories of rights protected by the Charter of Rights and Freedoms.

Answer Any three: fundamental freedoms, democratic rights, mobility rights, legal rights, equality rights, official language rights, minority language educational rights, multicultural heritage, Indigenous peoples' rights.
Q5Core2 marks

What does the notwithstanding clause allow?

Answer It allows provincial governments to exempt certain laws from specific sections of the Charter.
Q6Core2 marks

Name two responsibilities that come with Canadian citizenship.

Answer Any two: obeying the law, voting in elections, serving on a jury, helping others in the community, protecting Canadian identity and the environment.

Part B — Extended Questions (Paragraph Response)

No word bank. Analyze and evaluate.

Q7Extended5 marks
A new club at school changes its rule so any student can join, not just students in Grade 9. But new members still can't vote on club decisions until their second year.

Is this club's new rule a full expansion of rights, or a partial one? Use what you learned about Canadian citizenship categories to support your answer.

Answer This is a partial expansion. New members can now join, similar to how immigration categories opened Canadian citizenship to more people over time. But new members still can't vote right away, similar to how a permanent resident has many rights but not full citizenship rights like voting. Real progress can still be incomplete progress.
Q8Extended5 marks
A social media platform removes posts that promote violence against a specific ethnic group. Some users say this protects people. Other users say it violates their freedom of speech.

Is this a reasonable limit on freedom of expression? Use Section 1 of the Charter of Rights and Freedoms to support your answer.

Answer I think this is a reasonable limit. Section 1 of the Charter allows limits on rights, such as banning hate speech, because it protects people from real harm. Removing posts that promote violence against a group protects that group, even though it does restrict what some users can post.
Q9Extended5 marks
A student wants the right to skip class whenever they choose, but does not want to accept the responsibility of making up missed work.

Use this example to explain, in your own words, why rights and responsibilities usually come together. Then give one real example of a Canadian right and its matching responsibility.

Answer Rights and responsibilities come together because a right usually only works if people also accept what it costs. The student wants the right to skip class, but not the responsibility of making up the work — that isn't fair to their classmates or teacher. In Canada, citizens have the right to vote, and the responsibility to actually vote in elections so that the right has real meaning.
