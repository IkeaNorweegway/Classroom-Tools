Building Canada's Economy

Grade 7 Social Studies  ·  Practice Workbook — Answer Key  ·  Alberta Draft Curriculum 2026

Teacher Copy — Not for Students

Learning Outcome: Students examine the Canadian government's role in developing the economy post-Confederation.

Guiding Question: How did the Canadian government help shape the economy after Confederation?

Model answers and marking notes for every question. Each card says which notes section must be taught before the question opens. The student copy shows no section labels: announce which questions are open each session.

For opinion and judgement questions, the model answer is one strong example. Accept any position backed by accurate evidence from the unit.

Part A — Core and Extended Questions

Q1★ Core
Opens after Section 1 — Regional economies
AnswerBC: timber and fish · Prairies: wheat · Ontario and Quebec: factories · Atlantic: fishing and shipbuilding.
Q2★ Core

Answera) $10 · b) $50 · c) The Quebec boots ($45). The Quebec factory and its workers benefit; shoppers pay $45 instead of $40.
Q3★ Core
Opens after Section 5 — GDP
AnswerPrairie Town $12,000; Forest Town $10,500. Prairie Town's GDP is larger. No: GDP measures what is produced in a year, not savings.
Q4★ Core
Opens after Section 2 — The National Policy
AnswerTariffs, the railway, settlement of the West. Example: the railway carried settlers west; settlers grew wheat and bought goods from tariff-protected eastern factories.
Q5★★ Extended
Opens after Sections 1 and 3 — Calgary grows
AnswerThe CPR arrived in 1883, so settlers, goods and businesses could reach Calgary; ranching and farming nearby needed a town to ship from and buy supplies in, so the town grew.
Q6★ Core
Opens after Section 3 — The CPR
AnswerEast: wheat, cattle (also timber, coal). West: tools, machinery, clothing, settlers. Both sides needed the railway to reach buyers.
Q7★ Core
Opens after Section 6 — Banks
AnswerBank: keep savings safe, make loans, help people invest. Bank of Canada: issues banknotes, keeps the money system and prices stable.
Q8★★ Extended
Opens after Section 1 — Why regions differ
AnswerRegions build economies from their own resources: wheat and oil on the Prairies, timber and fish in BC, fishing in the Atlantic. Factories grew in Ontario and Quebec because of markets, water power and tariffs. Copying one industry everywhere ignores each region's strengths.
Q9★★ Extended
Opens after Section 4 — Tariffs or free trade?
AnswerA farmer would likely vote for freer trade: the plough would cost $100, not $120. Ontario factory owners and workers might vote against it, because cheaper U.S. ploughs could take their customers and jobs.
Q10★★ Extended
Opens after Sections 2 and 3 — How the plan fit together
AnswerEach part depended on the others: tariffs protected factories, the railway carried goods and settlers, settlers bought the goods and grew wheat. Without the railway, settlers couldn't reach the West and goods couldn't move, so tariffs and settlement wouldn't connect.
Q11★★ Extended
Opens after Section 2 — Short and long term
AnswerShort term: factory jobs in Central Canada / higher prices for farmers. Long term: a country linked east to west / lasting Western anger. Accept either judgement with evidence on both sides.
Q12★★ Extended
Opens after Section 3 — The railway and the economy
AnswerAgree: wheat needed a cheap way to reach ports and world buyers, and settlers needed a way to reach the land; the CPR provided both. Towns like Calgary and Regina grew along it.
Q13★★ Extended
Opens after Section 5 — Equalization
AnswerUnfair: Albertans pay federal taxes that help fund payments to other provinces while Alberta gets none. Keeps Canada together: every Canadian can get similar schools and hospitals, wherever they live, so poorer regions aren't left behind.
Q14★★ Extended
Opens after Section 6 — GDP and banking
AnswerA good year means more sales, so people and businesses deposit more and need more payments and loans: banks grow. Banks then lend to businesses (e.g. for new equipment or a new well), which produce more, so GDP grows.

Part B — Challenge Zone

QS1★★ Extended
Opens after Sections 2, 4 and 5
AnswerThe National Policy aimed to build Canadian industry and connect the country; it benefited Central Canada's factories most. Equalization aims to give every province similar services; it benefits provinces that raise less money. One built regional differences, the other tries to smooth them.
QS2★★ Extended
Opens after Sections 1, 3 and 6
AnswerA farmer (perhaps with a bank loan for land or tools) grows the wheat; the CPR carries it east to a port; it is shipped and sold to Britain (an export); the farmer deposits the money in a bank and spends it on goods from eastern factories.
QS3★★ Extended
Opens after All sections
AnswerStrong answers use expansion west, the National Policy (tariffs, CPR, settlement), and equalization or the Bank of Canada, and explain at least one trade-off (e.g. tariffs helped factories but raised prices for farmers).
