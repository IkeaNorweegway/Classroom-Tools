1 ★ Core

A town council has enough money to build a new hockey rink OR a new library this year — not both. This is the same choice from your notes.

  1. Name the scarce resource being divided here.
  2. Sentence starter: “The opportunity cost of building the library is ___.” Complete the sentence, assuming the council chooses to build the rink.
  3. Which economic question is the town answering here? Circle one: What  /  How  /  For whom
2 ★ Core

A student says: “My family's taxes basically just pay for the road we drive on. That's really the main thing taxes are for.”

  1. Is this student's claim accurate? Circle one: Yes  /  No
  2. Name three OTHER things tax dollars pay for that this student is missing.
    Word bank healthcare   education   social programs   public safety   infrastructure   roads
  3. In 1–2 sentences, explain why thinking taxes fund "just one thing" is a misunderstanding of government revenue.
3 ★ Core

A student says: “The United States is a pure market economy — the government has zero involvement in the economy at all.”

  1. Is this claim accurate? Circle one: Yes  /  No
  2. Name one example of government involvement in the U.S. economy that proves this student wrong.
    Word bank public schools   Social Security   a public park   a public hospital   a government office
  3. Where would you place the United States on the economic spectrum — closer to the market end, the middle, or the command end? Explain in one sentence.
4 ★★ Extended

A student says: “Our country has $30 billion in debt this year, so that means this year's deficit was $30 billion.”

  1. Explain what is wrong with this statement.
  2. If the country's debt was $22 billion at the start of the year and is $30 billion at the end of the year, what was this year's deficit?
  3. Could a country have a large debt but a small deficit this year? Explain how that is possible.
5 ★ Core

Look at this partly filled table for a country's economy.

MeasureThis yearLast year
GDP growth4%2%
Unemployment rate6%6%
CPI change3%3%
  1. Which measure improved compared to last year?
  2. Which two measures stayed the same?
  3. Based on GDP alone, is it fair to say the economy is "doing better" this year? Why or why not?
6 ★★ Extended

A farmer has one field. She can plant wheat or plant corn, but not both this season. Wheat sells for less per bushel than corn this year, but corn needs more water, and her region is in a drought.

Evaluate: should the farmer plant wheat or corn? Use scarcity and opportunity cost to justify your recommendation, and name one thing she gives up either way.

8 ★★ Extended

A province must choose between spending an extra $500 million on new hospitals or on new highways this year — it cannot fully fund both.

Evaluate this decision: what would you recommend, and what is the opportunity cost of your recommendation? Use reasoning, not just an opinion.

9 ★ Core

A province runs a deficit this year because a major flood destroyed roads and homes, and the government spent extra money on emergency repairs right away.

  1. Was this deficit a sign of poor money management? Circle one: Yes  /  No
  2. Explain your answer in 1–2 sentences.
    Hint Think about why the deficit happened and what the money was used for.
10 ★★ Extended

One year after the Country A / Country B comparison from your notes, new data comes in:

Country ACountry B
GDP growth1%4%
Unemployment rate7%4%
CPI change (inflation)5%2%

Compare this to the original numbers from your notes. What changed for each country? Which country's economy looks stronger now, and which measure changed your mind the most?

11 ★★ Extended

Look at the economic spectrum diagram from your notes, showing Hong Kong, Canada, China, and North Korea placed along the line.

A student claims: “Since Canada and China are both closer to the middle than the extreme ends, they must be almost the same type of economy.”

Evaluate this claim. Is it accurate? Use specific differences between Canada and China (ownership, government role, main goal) to support your answer.

12 ★★ Extended

A school has enough budget for either new sports equipment or new art supplies this year — not both. Using scarcity, the three economic questions, and opportunity cost, write a short recommendation to the school council. Your recommendation must name the opportunity cost of your choice.

Challenge Zone

S1 ★★ Extended

A country's data this year:

MeasureValue
GDP growth0.5%
Unemployment rate9%
CPI change1%
This year's budgetDeficit of $12 billion
Total debt$340 billion (was $328 billion last year)

The Prime Minister says: “Running this deficit was the right call — our economy needed the help.”

Using the economic measures above and what you know about deficits, evaluate whether this deficit seems like a reasonable choice or a risky one. Support your answer with at least two pieces of evidence from the table.

S2 ★★ Extended

Design an economic plan for a new country.

  1. Decide where your country sits on the economic spectrum (market, mixed, or command) and explain why.
  2. Name two things your government will fund with tax revenue, and one thing it will leave mostly to private businesses.
  3. Explain one trade-off your country accepts by choosing this plan instead of a different point on the spectrum.
S3 ★★ Extended

A country has been running a growing deficit for ten years straight, has rising unemployment, and its citizens are asking the government to cut taxes.

Using scarcity, government revenue and spending, and the difference between deficit and debt, write a paragraph explaining the trade-offs the government faces. Your paragraph should mention at least three ideas from this unit.
