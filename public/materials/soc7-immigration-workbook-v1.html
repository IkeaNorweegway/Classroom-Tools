Grade 7 Social Studies — Immigration After Confederation

Practice Workbook

Questions are mixed on purpose. Figuring out what each question is asking is part of the learning.
Learning Outcome: Students examine immigration in post-Confederation Canada.

How this workbook works

Questions from across the unit are mixed together on purpose. Your job is to figure out what each question is asking before you answer it. That thinking is where the learning happens.

Explain how access to land influenced where immigrants chose to settle, and why some groups settled close together.

10★★ Extended

A student writes: "The government gave away free, empty land." Identify two problems with this statement and correct it.

11★★ Extended

Did immigration help build the Canadian state? Use two pieces of evidence about farms, towns or the economy.

12★★ Extended

Explain why the Canadian government created policies that restricted immigration from certain groups. Use one example to support your conclusion about discrimination and government policy.

13★★ Extended

The Komagata Maru passengers said that, as British subjects, they had a right to enter Canada. Evaluate whether the government's response was fair. Use evidence.

14★★ Extended

In 1870 the Manitoba Act protected Catholic schools and French. In 1890 Manitoba took those protections away. Explain why Francophone Catholics saw this as unfair, and why this was called a "constitutional crisis."

Challenge Zone

Try these after the core and extended questions. Each one asks you to pull ideas from more than one part of the unit.

S1★★

"Immigration in post-Confederation Canada created opportunities for settlement and economic growth, while also leading to conflict and discrimination." Support both halves of this statement with one example each, and explain how they were connected.

S2★★

Here are two sample accounts of the 1907 riot, written for this question: a Vancouver newspaper headline, "Asiatic invasion threatens our city," and a Japanese shopkeeper's letter describing broken windows and neighbours defending the street. How would you use both sources to find out what happened? Which parts are facts and which are opinions?

S3★★

Answer the guiding question in a strong paragraph: Did post-Confederation immigration shape the development of the Canadian state? Assess whether government policies in this period were fair, using at least three examples.
