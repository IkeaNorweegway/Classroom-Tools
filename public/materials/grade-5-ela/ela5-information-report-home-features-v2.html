Finding Facts Fast

One skill: using headings, captions and bold words to find information · about 15 minutes

Need a reminder? See the class notes: Section 1: Text features

NameDate

For the adult: Information texts (non-fiction books, websites, park signs, brochures) use text features like headings, bold words, maps and pictures with captions, and "Did you know?" boxes. Your child is learning to use them to find facts quickly. Use any non-fiction book, magazine or website you have at home.

Worked example

Worked example

Question: What is a chinook?

  1. Look at the headings first, not the paragraphs. One heading says "The chinook."
  2. Read only that section. It says "a warm, dry wind that can raise the temperature by 20 degrees or more in a few hours."
  3. Answer in a sentence: A chinook is a warm, dry wind that can warm the air very quickly. It took 20 seconds, not 5 minutes.

Your turn

Find a non-fiction book, magazine article or website about any topic. Title:

1. Write one heading from it.

2. What do you think that section is about, before you read it?

3. Find a bold or highlighted word. Write it and what it means.

4. Find a picture, map or diagram. What does its caption say? If there is no caption, write one.

5. Ask your adult to give you a question about the topic. Use the headings to find the answer fast. How did the text features help?

6. Which text feature helped you most today, and why?
