Telling a Story Short

One skill: writing a short summary of a story in your own words · about 15 minutes

NameDate

For the adult: A summary tells only the most important parts, in your own words. Your child is learning a five-word pattern: Somebody · Wanted · But · So · Then. Use a story you both know (a movie, a book, a family story). Short is the goal: if the summary is long, ask "What could we leave out?"

Worked example

Worked example

Story: The Three Little Pigs

  1. Somebody: three little pigs
  2. Wanted: to build safe houses
  3. But: a wolf blew down the straw and stick houses
  4. So: they all ran to the brick house
  5. Then: the wolf couldn't blow it down, and they were safe.

Summary: Three little pigs built houses, but a wolf blew two of them down, so they hid in the brick house and stayed safe.

Your turn

1. Choose a story you know well. Title:

2. Somebody: · Wanted:

3. But: · So:

4. Then:

5. Put it together into one or two sentences (30 words or fewer).

6. Read your summary to your adult. Did you leave out the small details? What did you cut?
