Lesson 2 · How a script works

Read The Lost Lunch. Use Anchor note 1.

PartExample from Play 1
Cast listPriya, Marcus, Ms. Lam, Owen, Jada, Narrator
SettingThe lunchroom, 11:55 a.m.
A stage direction that shows a feeling(digging through her backpack, faster and faster)
A stage direction that shows an action(pointing dramatically) / (sliding into the seat beside her)
A narrator line"Every school has a mystery."

Write about it. Why doesn't a script use quotation marks? How does the reader know who's talking?

Answer Every line starts with the character's name and a colon, so everything after it is what they say; quotation marks aren't needed.

Lesson 5 · Events and change: The Rematch

Read Play 2. Use Anchor note 3.

BeginningProblemTurning pointEnding
Hare is back, sure he'll winHe's too afraid of losing to keep goingTortoise: "Just keep going"They finish together in a tie

Write about it. How does Hare change? Use a line from the beginning and a line from the end.

Answer At the beginning: "Today, I win!" (proud, bragging). At the end: "Then let's keep going. Together." (humble, working with Tortoise).

Lesson 6 · Verbal language

Use Anchor note 4. Say this line three ways, changing only one word you stress. Write how the meaning changes.

Stressed wordWhat it means then

I didn't take your lunch.I took someone else's
I didn't take your lunch.I took something else of yours

Write about it. Why do playwrights choose words so carefully if actors can change the meaning with their voice?

Lesson 20 · Directions and punctuation

Fix the script punctuation: names in capitals with a colon, no quotation marks, stage directions in brackets, a new line for each speaker.

"Where is the finish line?" asked Hare, looking around. "Just keep going," said Tortoise slowly.

Answer HARE: (looking around) Where is the finish line?

Answer TORTOISE: (slowly) Just keep going.
