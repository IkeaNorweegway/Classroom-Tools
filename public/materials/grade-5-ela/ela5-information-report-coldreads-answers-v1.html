Cold read 1 · Your Skin

Your Skin: The Body's Armour

An information text

1Your skin is your body's largest organ. If you could spread out all the skin of an adult, it would cover about two square metres, about the size of a single bed. It is also one of the busiest organs, working day and night to protect you.

Three layers

2Skin has three layers. The outer layer, the epidermis, is thin and tough. It is always making new cells at the bottom and shedding old, dead cells at the top. In fact, you lose tens of thousands of skin cells every minute, and some of the dust in your home is dead skin. The middle layer, the dermis, holds blood vessels, nerves, hair roots and sweat glands. The bottom layer is made mostly of fat, which keeps you warm and cushions your body.

A shield

3Skin keeps germs out. As long as it is unbroken, most germs cannot get through. That is why a cut needs to be cleaned and covered: the shield has a hole in it. Skin also contains a colouring called melanin, which helps protect you from the sun's harmful rays.

A thermostat

4Skin helps control your temperature. When you get hot, sweat glands release sweat onto your skin. As the sweat dries, it carries heat away, so you cool down. When you get cold, the blood vessels in your skin get narrower to keep warm blood deeper inside your body. That is why your fingers can look pale on a freezing day.

A sense organ

5Skin is full of nerve endings that sense touch, pressure, heat, cold and pain. Your fingertips have some of the most nerve endings in your whole body, which is why you can feel the tiny raised dots of Braille.

Did you know? The skin on your eyelids is the thinnest on your body. The skin on the soles of your feet is the thickest.

1. What is the topic of this text? Name two of its subtopics. (TF11) [2 points]

Answer Topic: skin. Subtopics (any two): three layers; a shield; a thermostat; a sense organ.

2. What is the main idea of the section "A thermostat"? (CO5) [2 points]

Answer Skin helps control body temperature: sweat cools you down and narrowing blood vessels keep heat in.

3. Fill in the cause-and-effect chart. (TF11) [3 points]

CauseEffect
You get hot.Sweat glands release sweat, which carries heat away as it dries
You get cold.Blood vessels in the skin get narrower to keep warm blood inside
The skin is cut.Germs can get in, so it needs to be cleaned and covered

4. What does epidermis mean? What clue in the text helped you? (VO11, VO12) [2 points]

Answer The thin, tough outer layer of skin. The clue is the description right before it: "The outer layer, the epidermis, is thin and tough."

5. Why might your fingers look pale on a freezing day? Use evidence. (CO6, CO9) [2 points]

Answer Because when you are cold, the blood vessels in your skin get narrower to keep warm blood deeper inside, so less blood is near the surface of your fingers (¶4).

6. What text feature does the "Did you know?" box use, and why is it there? (TF5) [1 point]

Answer A sidebar; it adds an extra interesting fact that isn't part of the main sections.

7. Write a 20-word summary of the whole text in your own words. (CO4) [2 points]

Answer e.g., Skin is the body's largest organ; its three layers protect us from germs and sun, control temperature and sense touch.

Cold read 2 · Your Eyes

Your Eyes: How You See

An information text

1You might think your eyes do the seeing. In fact, your eyes and your brain work together. The eyes collect light, and the brain turns that light into the pictures you see.

The parts of the eye

2The coloured part of your eye is the iris. In the middle of the iris is a black hole called the pupil, which lets light in. Behind the pupil is the lens, a clear, bendable part that focuses light, the way a camera lens does. At the back of the eye is the retina, a thin layer packed with millions of cells that sense light.

From light to picture

3First, light bounces off an object and enters the pupil. Next, the lens bends the light so it lands on the retina. Strangely, the picture on the retina is upside down. Then, cells in the retina turn the light into nerve signals. Finally, the optic nerve carries those signals to the brain, which flips the picture the right way up and tells you what you are looking at. All of this happens in a fraction of a second.

Bright and dim

4The iris is a muscle. In bright light, it makes the pupil smaller, so less light gets in. In dim light, it makes the pupil bigger, so more light gets in. You can watch this happen: look in a mirror in a dim room, then switch on a light.

Eyes compared with a camera

5A camera and an eye are alike in many ways. Both let light in through an opening, both use a lens to focus it, and both record a picture at the back. But they are different too. A camera stores the picture; your eye sends it to the brain, which makes sense of it, remembers it and helps you react.

Protecting your eyes

6Eyelids and eyelashes keep dust out, and tears wash the eye clean. Sunglasses protect against the sun's rays, and safety glasses protect against flying objects.

From an online store (made up for practice): "Our Super-Sight Carrots will give you perfect eyesight in just 3 days!"

1. Fill in the summary of the steps in "From light to picture." (CO4, TF11) [3 points]

FirstNextThenFinally
Light enters the pupilThe lens bends the light onto the retinaRetina cells turn light into nerve signalsThe optic nerve carries the signals to the brain

2. What happens to the pupil in bright light, and why? (TF11, CO9) [2 points]

Answer It gets smaller because the iris (a muscle) closes it so less light gets in (¶4).

3. Give one way an eye and a camera are alike and one way they are different. (TF11) [2 points]

Answer Alike: both let light in through an opening / use a lens / record a picture at the back. Different: a camera stores the picture, but the eye sends it to the brain, which makes sense of it.

4. The author says "your eyes and your brain work together" (¶1). Find one piece of evidence. (CO9) [2 points]

Answer The optic nerve carries signals to the brain, which flips the upside-down picture and tells you what you see (¶3).

5. Is the store's claim about Super-Sight Carrots reliable? Explain using one question from our source checklist. (WR21) [2 points]

Answer No. Why was it written? To sell carrots. Can I check it? No other source says carrots give perfect eyesight in 3 days.

6. Which text feature would you add to help a reader understand "The parts of the eye," and what would its caption say? (TF6) [2 points]

Answer A labelled diagram of the eye (iris, pupil, lens, retina, optic nerve) with a caption like "The main parts of the eye."

7. What does lens mean in this text? What clue helped? (VO12) [1 point]

Answer A clear, bendable part of the eye that focuses light; the definition and the camera comparison right after it.
