How to Use the Grade 5 ELA Materials

Every unit has the same set of materials. Here is when to use each one, how, and why it's there.
For the teacher

The short version: a short explicit lesson, a long stretch of reading and writing, writing about reading every day, and needs-based groups. Each unit ends with a piece written for a real audience. (§ = sections of the literacy research file.)

The 60-minute block

5 warm-up (recall, no notes) · 12 mini-lesson (anchor note + mentor text) · 35 work time: notebook and reading, plus two groups and conferences · 8 exit ticket or written response

Groups by need, not only level: decoding (long-word routine), fluency (repeated reading with F&P leveled text), comprehension (grade-level text with heavy support), on track (extension and conferences).

Choose by task, not by child. Listen to a grade-level text with the student: if they understand it heard but not read, it's a decoding or fluency gap.

Don't skip

Modelling: think aloud before students try. The real audience: book the visitors early. Mnemonic practice: don't start modelling until most students can recite POW + the unit strategy. Writing lines are 12 mm apart (15 mm in Edition C); print with "Background graphics" on so the ruled lines in charts appear.
