Information Report — Teacher Guide

Grade 5 ELA · Unit 2 · 30 lessons of 60 minutes (6 weeks) · Anchor topic: human body systems · SRSD strategy: POW + TIDE
For the teacher

At a glance

Students read six information texts about body systems and a model report, learn how information texts are organized (topic and subtopics, main idea, cause and effect, compare and contrast, sequence) and how text features work, then research one body system or organ and write a multi-paragraph report with POW + TIDE. They check sources for reliability, take notes in their own words, and publish with text features. The unit ends with a Body Expo for another class.

ReadingText features, topic and subtopics, main idea and summary, cause and effect, compare and contrast, science vocabulary from context and word parts, evaluating sources
WritingSRSD with POW + TIDE: research questions, notes, TIDE paragraphs, introduction and conclusion, transitions, text features, sources list
Word work-al/-ial, ex-/in-, trans-/sub-, -ous/-ic, -ment/-tion; Tier 2 and Tier 3 words; commas in lists, subject–verb agreement, parentheses
FluencyBody Bulletin newscast (reader's theatre), repeated reading of information passages
Oral languageStructured discussion, Body Expo talk
AssessmentSix exit tickets; cold reads in Lessons 14 and 27; report rubric with three samples

Why it is built this way (research/literacy-reading-writing-research.md): text structure instruction (0.96 for struggling readers, §3), summary writing (0.79 at elementary, §9), SRSD (§8), and one topic for the shared texts so knowledge builds (§2). The anchor topic links to Grade 5 Science; tell the science teacher, and use their vocabulary.

Gather before Lesson 11: library books on the human body at a range of levels (F&P leveled non-fiction works well), and 2–3 bookmarked child-friendly websites (a children's hospital, a science centre). Students need two reliable sources each.

Facts in the texts are standard averages, rounded. If a student finds a different number in a book (e.g., 20,000 vs. 22,000 breaths a day), that is a good lesson: sources round differently, and that is why we cite them.

The daily block

Same shape as every unit: warm-up (5) · mini-lesson (12) · work time with two groups and conferences (35) · exit or write-about-it (8). Groups by need: decoding (long-word routine on science words: res-pi-ra-to-ry, cir-cu-la-to-ry), fluency (repeated reading, leveled non-fiction), comprehension (grade-level text with heavy support; main idea and text structure), on track (extra research, a second sidebar, conferences).

Research is where Edition C students most need access: read sources aloud, or use text-to-speech on websites. Their report may be shorter (two TIDE paragraphs) but keeps the outcome (inform, with facts in their own words).

Week 1 · How information texts work

1Hook: body by numbersCO1, OL6

ShowAsk students to find their pulse (wrist or neck) and count for 15 seconds. "How many times does it beat in a day?" Collect guesses.

DoNotebook L1: brain dump; body-by-numbers true/false (guess only); three questions about the body. Post questions on a "wonder wall".

CheckNote misconceptions to address (muscles push; heart on the left side of the chest only; "we breathe in oxygen and out nothing").

2Text featuresTF5, TF1

ShowAnchor note 1. Preview Text 1 with students: read only the title, headings, bold words, sidebar and glossary. "What do we already know about this text before reading a paragraph?"

DoShared reading of Text 1. Notebook L2: feature hunt, then "write about it".

GroupsDecoding: organ, tissue, circulatory, respiratory, digestive.

CheckDo students use headings to predict? Note who skips captions.

3Topic and subtopicsTF11, CO5

ShowAnchor note 2. Think aloud mapping Text 4 from its headings.

DoNotebook L3: map Text 1; explain "no system works alone" with the soccer example.

CheckStudents who list facts instead of subtopics get a quick re-teach with the headings.

4Main idea and details: the heartCO5, CO9

ShowAnchor note 3. Model the main idea test on "A pump with four rooms".

DoRead Text 2. Notebook L4: main idea and details per section; pulse experiment (one minute of running in place) and explanation.

GroupsFluency: Passage 1 (the heart).

5Summarizing a sectionCO4

ShowModel a 20-word summary of one section; cross out small details aloud.

DoNotebook L5: summaries of two sections; count words.

CheckExit ticket 1.

Week 2 · Structures, words and sources

6Cause and effect: breathingTF11, CO6

ShowAnchor note 4. Signal words. Model the first row of the chart.

DoRead Text 3. Notebook L6: cause-effect chart; explain puffing after a race.

Talk"Hand on chest, breathe in slowly. What moved? Why?" (the diaphragm).

7Compare and contrast: bones and musclesTF11, CO9

ShowAnchor note 5. Demonstrate biceps and triceps with your own arm.

DoRead Text 4. Notebook L7: chart and compare-contrast paragraph; why muscles work in pairs.

GroupsComprehension: build the paragraph together with the frame.

8Science wordsVO4, VO11, VO12

ShowAnchor note 6. Model the three clue types and word parts (ex-, -ous, circ-, trans-).

DoNotebook L8; begin word-work unit words.

CheckCan students explain the clue they used, not just the meaning?

9Can I trust it?WR21, CO17, TF12

ShowAnchor note 7. The gummies example.

DoRead Text 5 (the brain). Notebook L9: compare Source A and Source B; write which to use and why.

TalkPairs: "Name a website or video you trust. Would it pass our four questions?"

10Notes in my own wordsWR23, WR24

ShowAnchor note 8. Model key-word notes and "close the book and say it."

DoRead Text 6. Notebook L10: notes on the path of food; paragraph from notes only.

CheckExit ticket 2. Look for copied phrases in the paragraphs.

Week 3 · Research and the model report

11Pick and narrow a topicWR19, WR22

ShowModel narrowing: "the body" → "the heart" → three questions (What is it? How does it work? How do we keep it healthy?).

DoNotebook L11: choose a topic; three research questions; list two reliable sources (from the gathered books and bookmarked sites).

GroupsConference with students choosing very broad topics.

12Research notesWR23, WR24, WR22

DoNotebook L12: notes for each question, with sources. Students read sources and take notes; you circulate and check for copying.

GroupsEdition C: read sources aloud with them; they record notes as drawings or key words.

CheckEvery note has a source.

13SRSD stages 1–2: the model report and TIDEWR3, WR4, WR20

ShowAnchor note 9 (POW + TIDE). Read Text 7 together; label T, I, D, E in "The heart" paragraph on the board.

DoNotebook L13: map the model report; find TIDE in "The blood"; set a goal. Show the three rubric samples; discuss what makes each level.

CheckExit ticket 3. Begin daily mnemonic practice.

14Cold read 1TF5, TF11, CO4, CO5, CO6, CO9, VO11, VO12

DoCold read 1, Your Skin (35 min). Edition C: read the A text aloud first. Then POW + TIDE memory practice.

CheckScore by code; set Week 4 groups.

15Body Bulletin and memorizeOL3, OL5, TF5

DoPerform Body Bulletin (fluency pack); record and replay if possible. Notebook L15: self-rating; POW + TIDE from memory.

CheckDon't move to modelling until most students can write POW + TIDE from memory.

Week 4 · Model and plan

16SRSD stage 3: model itWR3, WR4, WR20

ShowTurn your own notes on the brain into a TIDE paragraph, thinking aloud (script below). Write it on chart paper.

DoNotebook L16: record what you did and said; write three self-talk statements.

Think-aloud script for Lesson 16 (adapt freely)

"My subtopic is the three parts of the brain. T: what is this paragraph about, in one sentence? 'The brain has three main parts, and each has its own job.' I: which facts matter most? Cerebrum: thinking and memory. Cerebellum: balance. Brain stem: breathing and heartbeat. My notes say 'cerebrum handles thinking, learning, memory, the senses'… that's almost the book's sentence. Let me say it my way: 'The cerebrum, the biggest part, does your thinking and remembering.' D: can I add an example so the reader can picture it? 'The cerebellum is the part that keeps you from tipping over when you ride a bike.' E: how do I wrap it up? 'Together, the three parts keep you thinking, moving and alive.' Do I have T, I, D and E? Yes. Good, that paragraph is done."

17SRSD stage 5: we write togetherWR4, WR20

DoClass writes a TIDE paragraph on the lungs from Text 3 (you scribe; students suggest each part). Notebook L17: copy it.

CheckStudents should name the missing TIDE part before you add it.

18Hooks and conclusionsWR4

ShowAnchor note 10. Read the model report's intro and conclusion; notice the delivery-truck link.

DoNotebook L18: two hooks for their own report; plan the conclusion.

CheckExit ticket 4.

19Organize: TIDE for each paragraphWR3, WR4, WR20

DoNotebook L19: a TIDE plan for each research question. Conferences: one subtopic per paragraph; details that explain.

GroupsEdition B/C students first.

20Transitions and text featuresWR5, TF6

ShowAnchors 10–11. Model joining sentences with transitions; sketch a labelled diagram with a caption.

DoNotebook L20: transition practice; choose text features; sketch the diagram.

CheckExit ticket 5.

Week 5 · Draft and feedback

21Draft: introduction and paragraph 1WR4, WR18

DoDraft with headings, skipping lines. Conferences with 4–5 students.

GroupsEdition C students may dictate (composing outcome: dictation is access).

22Draft: paragraphs 2–3WR4, WR20

ShowTwo-minute mini-lesson: turning a number into a comparison detail (100,000 km of blood vessels → "more than twice around the Earth").

DoContinue drafting.

23Draft: conclusion; self-checkWR4, WR7

DoFinish the draft; count TIDE parts; colour the graph; single-point self-check with the rubric.

SayAttribute progress to the strategy: "You have all twelve TIDE parts because you planned with TIDE."

24Structured peer feedbackWR7, OL6, OL7

ShowModel the routine with a volunteer.

DoNotebook L24 in pairs; author writes one change to make first.

25ReviseWR7, WR5

ShowRevise your brain paragraph: add a comparison detail; cut a fact that doesn't fit; add a transition.

DoNotebook L25: at least two revision moves; before and after.

CheckExit ticket 6.

Week 6 · Publish and share

26EditWR8, CV1, CV2, CV5

DoNotebook L26: one read per target (capitals in headings, commas in lists, subject–verb agreement, parentheses, spelling patterns, science words).

GroupsSubject–verb agreement mini-group (word work Week 3).

27Cold read 2CO4, TF11, CO9, WR21, TF6, VO12

DoCold read 2, Your Eyes (35 min). Edition C: read aloud first. Early finishers: final copy.

CheckCompare with cold read 1 by code.

28Publish and self-assessWR9, WR18, TF6

DoFinal copy as a booklet, poster or typed pages with at least three text features and a sources list. Self-assess (Notebook L28). Score with the rubric; one written feedback point for the next unit.

29Body ExpoOL3, OL5, OL6

DoStudents set up reports around the room; a visiting class (or families) circulates. Each student gives a one-minute talk: hook, three facts, the diagram, a question for the audience. Visitors write one thing they learned.

CheckObservation: projects voice; explains the diagram; answers a question.

30Look back, look aheadWR7, CO2

DoNotebook L30: what helped as a researcher and writer; compare an information report with a tall tale; one question still to answer. Self-check box.

Say"Next unit you'll use facts as evidence to convince someone. That's opinion writing, with a new partner for POW: TREE."
