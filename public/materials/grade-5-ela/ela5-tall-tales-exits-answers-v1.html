Exit Tickets

Tall Tales · One ticket per skill cluster. Answer on your own, from memory.
Edition A
Answer key. Model answers show one strong response; accept others that are supported by the text.
Reading the tickets

Sort tickets into three piles before the next lesson: got it, almost, not yet. Re-teach the "not yet" pile in a group during work time the next day; use the most common error as tomorrow's warm-up. Each item is tagged with its outcome code.

Ticket 1 · Lesson 5 · Tall tales and story parts

Name  

1. Give two features that make Paul Bunyan a tall tale. (TF7)

Answer Any two: a hero with impossible size/strength (rocked in a lumber wagon); exaggeration (words freeze in the air); a real place/job (logging camps); explains nature (hoofprints became lakes).

2. What is the main problem in Paul Bunyan, and how is it solved? (TF3)

Answer The loggers are starving in the Blue Snow winter; Paul builds a giant griddle so they can be fed and get back to work.

Ticket 2 · Lesson 10 · Characters and comparisons

Name  

1. Name one trait of John Henry. Give the evidence and say whether it is something he says, thinks, does, or what others say. (TF10, CO9)

Answer e.g., Determined: he keeps swinging without stopping for water when the machine is ahead (does, ¶6).

2. "The hammer whistled through the air like the wind before a storm." What does this comparison help you picture? (VO14)

Answer How fast and powerful the hammer was; it hints that something huge is about to happen.

Ticket 3 · Lesson 13 · Summary, inference and the story parts

Name  

1. Write a gist sentence (20 words or fewer) for Sally Ann Thunder Ann Whirlwind. (CO4)

Answer e.g., Sally Ann, a fearless frontier woman, rescues Davy Crockett from a tree and scares off a bear with her smile.

2. From memory: write the seven WWW, What = 2, How = 2 questions as single words or short phrases. (WR3)

Answer Who, When, Where, What does the main character want, What happens, How does it end, How do the characters feel.

Ticket 4 · Lesson 18 · Show, don't tell

Name  

1. Rewrite to show, not tell: The town was very cold. (WR15)

Answer e.g., The town was so cold that the church bell froze mid-ring and didn't finish its "dong" until April.

2. Why do authors show instead of tell? (WR11)

Answer It lets readers picture it and figure it out themselves, so the story is more interesting and memorable.

Ticket 5 · Lesson 20 · Dialogue

1. Punctuate correctly. Start a new line where needed. (CV2)

where are you going asked Babe to the mountains said Paul

Answer "Where are you going?" asked Babe.

Answer "To the mountains," said Paul.

2. Change said to a word that shows how Paul spoke, and explain your choice. (WR16)

Answer e.g., "boomed": Paul is a giant with a huge voice.
