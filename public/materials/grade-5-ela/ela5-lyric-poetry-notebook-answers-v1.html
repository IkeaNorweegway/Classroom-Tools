Lesson 2 · Lines, stanzas and speakers

Use Anchor notes 1 and 2.

PoemStanzasLines per stanzaWhat changes from stanza 1 to stanza 2?
Who Has Seen the Wind?24Leaves trembling → trees bowing their heads; "I nor you" → "you nor I"

Write about it. The poem says nobody can see the wind. How do we know it's there? What might the poem be saying about other things we can't see?

Answer We see what it does: leaves tremble and trees bend. The poem might be about other invisible things we know by their effects, like love, kindness or feelings.

Lesson 3 · Hope is a bird

Read Dickinson's poem. Use Anchor note 3.

What birds doWhat the poem says hope does¶/stanza
perch"perches in the soul"1
sing"sings the tune without the words, / And never stops at all"1
keep warm, survive storms"sweetest in the gale"; "kept so many warm"2

Write about it. Why is a small bird a good metaphor for hope? Use two lines from the poem.

Answer Hope is small and easy to miss, but it keeps going even in hard times, like a bird that keeps singing in a storm. "Sweetest in the gale is heard" shows hope is strongest in hard times, and "never… asked a crumb of me" shows it asks for nothing in return.

Lesson 4 · The sound of a river

Read The Song My Paddle Sings aloud with a partner, a stanza each. Use Anchor note 4.

StanzaWhat's happening on the river?How does the sound match?
2The wind dies; the speaker stows the sail and paddlesSlow and sleepy: "Sleep, sleep"
6The rapids roar aheadLoud, crashing words: "Dash, dash… splash"
8They've raced the rapids; calm waterSoft and swaying: "Sway, sway… tinkling tunes"

Find two rhyming pairs and one example of onomatopoeia.

Answer Rhymes (any two): nest/west, too/you, dip/flip/slip, crash/splash, canoe/into. Onomatopoeia: dash, crash, splash, swirl.

Write about it. How does the speaker feel about the river? Use evidence.

Answer Joyful and brave: "August is laughing" (stanza 3), "Be strong, O paddle! be brave, canoe!" and "never a fear my craft will feel" (stanza 7).

Lesson 6 · A child's wondering

Read Where Go the Boats? aloud, then compare it with Who Has Seen the Wind?

Where Go the Boats?Who Has Seen the Wind?
Something moving that you can't followThe river carrying the boats awayThe wind
A question the speaker asks"Where will all come home?""Who has seen the wind?"
The feelingWondering, a little wistful, hopefulCalm, curious

Write about it. Stevenson's speaker imagines "Other little children" finding the boats. What does that tell you about the speaker?

Answer The speaker is imaginative and hopeful; they picture their toys travelling far and making other children happy, as if the river connects them to faraway places.

Lesson 7 · Poems from here

Read Chinook and River Valley, January.

Write about it. Both poems are about an Alberta winter, but they feel different. Compare the feeling of each, with one line from each.

Answer Chinook feels joyful and surprised ("My dad stood in the driveway in a T-shirt / like he'd won something"). River Valley, January feels quiet and hopeful ("still moving, still going somewhere, / the way I keep going").

Find the comparisons. Write one simile from each poem.

Answer Chinook: "the cold sat on us / like a dog that would not move" or "warm as a breath." River Valley: "like a word I haven't said yet."
