1 · Fact or opinion?

Learning target: I can tell a fact from an opinion. (TF12, CO5)

A fact can be  . An opinion tells what someone  . Opinions are not wrong; but a good opinion is supported by  .

Sentence (from the reader)Fact or opinion?How I know
"The class presented a petition with 212 signatures." 
"Ten more minutes outside would make a big difference." 
"Three supervisors watch more than 300 students." 

Opinion signal words: I think · I believe · should · best · worst · most important · strong describing words (amazing, terrible).

Watch out "If a sentence has a number in it, it's a fact."

Why it happens Numbers look official.

Instead "Recess is the best 20 minutes of the day" has a number, but it's an opinion. Ask: could this be checked?

Quick check. Write one fact and one opinion about your school's recess.

9 · Answering the other side, and the letter form

Learning target: I can respond politely to the other side and write in letter form. (WR6, WR5, OL7)

A counterargument is what someone on the other side would say. Answering it makes your piece  , because it shows you've thought about it.

The move: Some people think   . That's a fair point. However,   .

Text 6, counterargument

"Some people worry that we will lose learning time. That is a fair concern. However, if recess is longer, we can solve that by starting five minutes earlier, and focused students learn more in less time."

Transitions for reasons: First · Another reason · Also · Most importantly · For these reasons

Letter form: Date · Dear   , · body paragraphs · Sincerely, · your name

Quick check. Write a counterargument and response for this opinion: Our class should have a class pet.
