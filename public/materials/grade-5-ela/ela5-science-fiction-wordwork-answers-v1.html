How this booklet fits with Words Their Way

Students keep their own WTW sort. This booklet adds the Grade 5 affixes that build the language of science fiction (-ic, -ical, -ial, -ity, -ment, trans-, com-, ex-, per-), Tier 2 words for talking about stories, spelling from memory, sentence combining, and conventions for narrative: dialogue, keeping tense, moving adverbs, and pronouns in third-person writing. Codes: VO4, VO10, VO13, CV2, CV4, CV9, CV11, CV12–14.

Conventions: third-person pronouns (CV11). Circle the pronouns that tell you a story is in the third person.

She opened the door. They looked at me. He laughed. I ran.

Answer She, They, He (third person); me, I (first person).

Week 2

Morphology: -al and -ial

WordBaseMeaning
artificialart (made by skill)made by people, not nature
naturalnaturefrom nature
personalpersonabout one person
globalglobearound the whole world

Sentence combining with when: The panel opened. Snow drifted down.

Answer When the panel opened, snow drifted down.

Conventions: keep the past tense (CV4). Fix the verbs that slip.

Jamal found the phone. He presses the button and the screen glowed.

Answer Jamal found the phone. He pressed the button, and the screen glowed.

Week 3

Morphology: trans- and com- (across and together).

WordMeaning
transmitsend across (a message or signal)
transportcarry across

Extended: The phone transmitted a message across time. Why does trans- fit?

Conventions: move the adverb for effect (CV9).

The panel slid open slowly. Move slowly to the start. What changes?

Answer Slowly, the panel slid open. Starting with slowly builds suspense: the reader waits.

Week 6

Mixed review, from memory

  1. Like a robot: robotic
  2. Made by people, not nature: artificial
  3. trans- means across
  4. real + -ity: reality
  5. Fix the tense: She opens the door and walked in. She opened the door and walked in.
  6. Third or first person? We watched the snow fall. first
  7. Move the adverb: The robot spoke quietly. Quietly, the robot spoke.
