Edition A
Answer key. Model answers show one strong response; accept others that are supported by the text.
How to give and score the cold reads

Cold read 1 in Lesson 14, cold read 2 in Lesson 27, about 35 minutes each. Silent and independent; Edition C students hear the Edition A texts read aloud first, then reread the short versions. Don't pre-teach. Score with the points shown; record by code. The writers and schools are made up.

Cold read 1 · An editorial

Every School Should Have a Garden

An editorial by Ms. Okonkwo, a made-up teacher

1Imagine biting into a carrot you planted yourself in May and pulled out of the ground in September. Every elementary school in Alberta should have a school garden, because gardens teach, feed and bring people together.

2First, a garden is a classroom without walls. Students can measure plant growth in math, observe insects and soil in science, and write about what they see in language arts. When my class planted beans, students who rarely spoke in class were the first ones outside, notebooks open, counting leaves.

3Second, gardens help students eat better. Children who grow vegetables are more willing to taste them. Last fall, my students made a salad from our garden, and several of them tried spinach for the first time. One said, "It tastes better when it's yours."

4Third, a garden brings the whole community together. Parents, grandparents and neighbours can help plant and weed during the summer, when school is closed. Some families have gardening knowledge that goes back generations, and a garden is a way to share it.

5Some people say a garden is too much work, especially in summer. That is true; gardens need care. However, a schedule that gives each family one summer week is not much to ask, and many hands make light work.

6Schools spend money on many things. A few raised beds, some soil and seeds cost less than one class set of textbooks, and they teach lessons no textbook can. Let's dig in.

1. What is the writer's opinion? Write it in one sentence. (WR6, CO4) [1 point]

Answer Every elementary school in Alberta should have a school garden.

2. Complete the chart with the writer's reasons and one piece of evidence for each. (WR6, CO9) [3 points]

ReasonEvidence¶
A garden is a classroom without wallsStudents measure growth, observe insects; quiet students counting leaves2
Gardens help students eat betterStudents tried spinach in the garden salad; "It tastes better when it's yours"3
Gardens bring the community togetherFamilies help in summer; share generations of knowledge4

3. Find one fact and one opinion in paragraph 6. (TF12) [2 points]

Answer Fact: "Schools spend money on many things" (or that raised beds, soil and seeds cost money). Opinion: "they teach lessons no textbook can" / "Let's dig in."

4. Which reason has the weakest evidence? Explain. (WR21, TF12) [2 points]

Answer e.g., Reason 2: the evidence is one class and one quote. Or Reason 3: no example of it actually happening at her school. Either, if explained.

5. What counterargument does the writer answer, and how? (WR6) [2 points]

Answer Gardens are too much work in summer. She answers that a schedule giving each family one week is not much to ask.

6. Whose perspective might be different from the writer's? What might they say? (CO13) [2 points]

Answer e.g., a caretaker who has to mow around the beds; a principal worried about cost; a family away all summer.

7. The writer begins with "Imagine biting into a carrot you planted yourself." Why is this a good opening for an editorial? (WR11) [1 point]

Answer It hooks the reader with a picture that makes them want the garden before the argument even starts.

Cold read 2 · A news report and a letter

Homework Debate Comes to Maple Creek

Text A · News report from a made-up town paper

1Parents and teachers at Maple Creek Elementary are discussing whether Grade 5 students should get homework every night.

2At Monday's school council meeting, the school's principal said teachers are reviewing the homework policy. Right now, Grade 5 students get about 30 minutes of homework four nights a week.

3Some parents want less. "My son has hockey three nights a week," said one parent. "By the time we get home, it's bedtime."

4Others want to keep it. A Grade 5 teacher said short reading practice at home helps students build fluency. "Twenty minutes of reading a night adds up to more than a hundred hours a year," she said.

5The council will survey families before making a decision in May.

Read, Don't Worksheet

Text B · A letter to the editor by Priya, Grade 5 (made up)

1Dear Editor,

2I think Grade 5 homework should be only reading. Worksheets every night take a long time, and many kids just copy answers. Reading is different: you can do it anywhere, even in the car to hockey, and it actually helps you get better. Our teacher says twenty minutes a night adds up to more than a hundred hours a year. That's a lot of practice. Some people say kids need math practice at home. But we already practise math every day at school. Please make Grade 5 homework reading only.

3Sincerely, Priya

1. What is Text A's purpose, and what is Text B's purpose? (TF1) [2 points]

Answer Text A informs (a news report that shows both sides); Text B persuades (argues for reading-only homework).

2. Text A quotes people on two sides. Summarize each side in one sentence. (CO4, CO12) [2 points]

Answer Some parents want less homework because busy evenings leave no time. A teacher wants to keep reading homework because it builds fluency.

3. Priya uses a fact from the news report. What is it, and why does it make her letter stronger? (CO9, WR21) [2 points]

Answer Twenty minutes a night adds up to more than a hundred hours a year. It's a number from a teacher, so it's evidence, not just her opinion.

4. Find one sentence in Text B that is an opinion. (TF12) [1 point]

Answer "I think Grade 5 homework should be only reading." (or "it actually helps you get better")

5. How does Priya answer the other side? Is her answer convincing? Explain. (WR6, CO8) [2 points]

Answer Others say kids need math practice; she answers that they already practise math at school every day. Convincing or not, with a reason (e.g., not fully: some students may need extra practice).

6. Priya says "many kids just copy answers." Is this strong evidence? Why or why not? (WR21) [2 points]

Answer Weak: there's no source or number; it's her impression, and "many" can't be checked.

7. What do you think the council should decide? Give one reason with evidence from either text. (WR6, CO9) [2 points]
