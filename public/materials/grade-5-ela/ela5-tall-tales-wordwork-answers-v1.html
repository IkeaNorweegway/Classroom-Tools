How this booklet fits with Words Their Way

Students keep doing their Words Their Way sort for their own stage. This booklet adds what sorts alone don't give: explicit morphology from the Grade 5 affix list, the unit's Tier 2 words, spelling from memory, sentence combining with the tales, and one conventions target per week. Every week mixes in items from earlier weeks (spaced review). Items marked Extended are optional stretch. Codes: VO4, VO10, VO13–15, CV2, CV4, CV6–7, CV9–10, CV12–14.

Sentence combining. Join into one sentence.

Paul Bunyan was a logger. He was enormous. He lived in the north woods.

Answer Paul Bunyan was an enormous logger who lived in the north woods.

paul and babe hauled logs to the river in minnesota.

Sentence combining with but.

Most people would run for a ladder. Sally Ann laughed.

Answer Most people would run for a ladder, but Sally Ann laughed.

Conventions: keep the past tense (CV4). Tall tales are told in the past. Fix the verbs that slip into the present.

Sally Ann walked into the snow. The bear stands up and roars. She smiled.

Answer Sally Ann walked into the snow. The bear stood up and roared. She smiled.

Review (Week 1). Add -ous or -ious: fury → furious, glory → glorious

Sentence combining with because, so or when. Use each once.

  1. The workers were worried. The machine could take their jobs. (because)
  2. John Henry won the race. The workers cheered. (when)
  3. The loggers were starving. Paul built a giant griddle. (so)

Answer 1. The workers were worried because the machine could take their jobs. 2. When John Henry won the race, the workers cheered. 3. The loggers were starving, so Paul built a giant griddle.

Conventions: joining words (CV10). Circle the joining word in each sentence you wrote.

Week 6

Morphology: -ant / -ent, -ance / -ence

Spelling rule spotted: if the describing word ends in -ant, the idea word ends in -ance; if it ends in -ent, the idea word ends in -ence.

Conventions: subject and object pronouns (CV6, CV7, CV11). Circle the subject pronoun and underline the object pronoun.

  1. She pulled him out of the tree.
  2. They cheered for him when he won.

Answer 1. Subject: She; object: him. 2. Subject: They; object: him.

Mixed review, from memory

  1. Add a suffix to fame: famous
  2. A person who is funny for a job: comedian
  3. Make hero into a describing word: heroic
  4. Make excite into a thing: excitement
  5. What does sub- mean? under
  6. Join with so: The wind was warm. The snow melted. The wind was warm, so the snow melted.
