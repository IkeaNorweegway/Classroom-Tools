Tall Tales Reader

Five tall tales, told in short sentences
Edition C

About these stories

These are tall tales. A tall tale is a story about a hero. The hero is too big to be real. The hero is too strong to be real. That is the fun part!

People told these stories out loud. Each person made the hero a little bigger.

Paul Bunyan and the Blue Snow

Tale 1

1Paul Bunyan was a giant. He was too big for a cradle. His parents rocked him in a big wagon.

2Paul grew up. He cut down trees for his job. He could cut a whole row of trees with one swing.

3One winter, the snow was blue. Nobody had seen blue snow before. It was very, very cold. It was so cold that words froze in the air! The men had to pick up their words and carry them inside.

4Paul heard a sound in the snow. He dug down. He found a baby ox. The ox was blue from the cold. Paul named her Babe. Babe grew very fast.

5The men were hungry. The cook could not make pancakes fast enough. Paul made a giant pan. Boys skated on it with bacon on their boots. That made the pan greasy. Now the pancakes came fast!

6The men ate. Then they worked hard. Babe pulled the logs to the river.

7Spring came. All the frozen words melted at once. The camp was so loud for three days!

giant very, very big

ox a big, strong animal like a cow

froze turned to ice

Sally Ann Thunder Ann Whirlwind

Tale 2

1Sally Ann was very strong. She could laugh the bark off a tree. She wore a hornets' nest for a hat. She used a snake for a belt.

2One day, Sally Ann heard a man yell, "Help!" A man named Davy was stuck in a tree. His head was stuck in the branches.

3Sally Ann did not run for a ladder. She laughed. She made a rope out of snakes. She threw the rope up and pulled. The tree bent. Davy popped out!

4Davy said, "You are the bravest person I ever met." Sally Ann and Davy got married.

5One winter, a big bear came to town. It took the honey. People hid. Sally Ann did not hide. She walked out and smiled her biggest smile. The bear ran away!

6Davy asked, "How did you do it?"

7Sally Ann said, "The bear was hungry. I was hungry and determined. Determined wins."

brave not scared to do hard things

determined you do not give up

John Henry, the Steel-Driving Man

Tale 3

1Long ago, workers made tunnels for trains. They made the tunnels by hand. One man held a steel spike on the rock. Another man hit it with a big hammer. This was called steel driving. It was hard work.

2John Henry was the best steel driver. He was very strong. He sang while he worked.

3One day, a man brought a machine. It was a steam drill. The man said, "This machine can do the work of ten men. You do not need workers now."

4The workers were worried. They needed their jobs. John Henry said, "I will race the machine."

5The race started in the morning. The machine was loud. John Henry swung two hammers. At noon, the machine was ahead. John Henry did not stop. He swung harder.

6At night, they checked. The machine went 9 feet. John Henry went 14 feet. John Henry won!

7But John Henry was too tired. His heart stopped. He died with his hammer in his hand.

8People still sing songs about John Henry. They remember that people matter, even when machines are fast.

tunnel a path dug through a mountain

machine a tool that works by itself

worried scared about what will happen

Big Joe Mufferaw

Tale 4

1Big Joe was a real man. His real name was Joseph Montferrand. He lived about 200 years ago. He worked on the Ottawa River.

2Joe rode on rafts made of logs. He steered the logs down the river. Joe was big and strong. He helped other workers.

3People told stories about Joe. Each story got bigger.

4First, people said Joe could lift a wagon. Then they said he could lift a wagon and the horses. Then they said he could lift the wagon, the horses and the driver!

5One story says Joe jumped up high in a room. He left his boot prints on the ceiling!

6People said Joe could paddle his canoe so fast he beat his own shadow.

7French-speaking workers called him Jos Montferrand. English-speaking workers called him Joe Mufferaw. Both told stories about him.

8The real Joe died long ago. But the stories are still told. A real man became a legend.

raft logs tied together to float

legend an old story many people tell

Chinook Charlie and the Great Thaw

Tale 5

1One winter in Alberta, it was so cold! The cows gave ice cream, not milk. Shadows froze to the sidewalk.

2Only one man was warm. His name was Chinook Charlie. He was taller than a grain elevator. His breath was warm like summer.

3The people came to Charlie. "Help us, Charlie!" said the mayor. "The river is frozen. The cows cannot drink. The bus is stuck in ice!"

4Charlie walked to the mountains. He walked for two days. He climbed the biggest mountain.

5Charlie took a giant breath. His chest got as big as a balloon. Then he blew.

6A warm wind came down the mountain. The snow melted. The river ran again. People danced in T-shirts!

7Now, when a warm wind blows in winter, people in Alberta call it a Chinook.

Chinook a warm wind in Alberta in winter

mountain very big, tall land

melted turned from ice to water
