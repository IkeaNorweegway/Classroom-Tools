One Line, Many Meanings

One skill: using voice and body to change the meaning of a line · about 15 minutes

NameDate

For the adult: Actors change what a line means without changing a single word, using their voice (loud or soft, fast or slow, which word they stress) and body (face, gestures, posture). This is a game: take turns, and guess each other's feelings. It also builds expressive reading.

Worked example

Worked example

Line: "Thanks a lot."

  1. Grateful: warm voice, smile, hand on heart.
  2. Annoyed: flat voice, stress on lot, eye roll.
  3. Surprised: high pitch, eyebrows up, a step back.

Your turn

1. Say "What did you do?" as: curious · angry · impressed. Your adult guesses which. How many did they get?

2. Now your adult says "We're having broccoli." You guess the feeling each time. Write the feelings you guessed.

3. Say "I'm fine" so that it means you're not fine. What did you change?

4. Which word would you stress to show you're jealous? She got a new bike.

5. Stand at one end of a room and say "Dinner's ready!" to your adult at the other end without shouting. What helped your voice carry?
