Write about it. Text 1 says "no system works alone." Use the soccer-ball example to explain what the author means.

Answer Systems depend on each other. When you kick a ball, your brain sends a message through nerves to muscles that pull bones; you breathe faster and your heart beats faster to bring oxygen; and the energy comes from food your digestive system broke down (Text 1 ¶5).

Answer When you run, your muscles use oxygen faster. Because of this, your brain signals you to breathe faster and more deeply, so you puff and pant (Text 3 ¶5).

Lesson 7 · Compare and contrast: bones and muscles

Read Text 4. Use Anchor note 5.

BonesMuscles
JobHold the body up; protect organs; make blood cellsMove the bones; pump blood; move food
What they are likeHard; don't change shapeSoft; contract and relax
How to keep them strongRunning, jumping; calciumExercise / using them

Write about it. Write a compare-and-contrast paragraph about bones and muscles. Use both, but and while.

Answer Bones and muscles are both living tissues that need oxygen and get stronger with use. But bones are hard and give the body its shape, while muscles are soft and contract to move the bones. They work as partners (Text 4 ¶1, ¶6).

Why do muscles work in pairs? Use evidence.

Answer Muscles can only pull, never push, so one muscle pulls the bone one way and its partner pulls it back (Text 4 ¶4: biceps and triceps).

Source A · A children's hospital website (made up for practice)

Sleep helps the brain store what you learned during the day. Children aged 5 to 13 need 9 to 11 hours of sleep a night. Screens in the hour before bed can make it harder to fall asleep. Reviewed by doctors, 2025.

Source B · An online ad (made up for practice)

QuestionSource ASource B

Write about it. Which source would you use in a report? Explain with two reasons.

Answer Source A. It was made by doctors to inform, and its fact (9–11 hours of sleep) matches Text 5. Source B wants to sell gummies, and its "50% smarter" claim can't be checked anywhere.
