How this booklet fits with Words Their Way

Students keep their own WTW sort. This booklet adds explicit morphology from the Grade 5 affix list (here, the affixes that build science words), Tier 2 and Tier 3 words from the reader, spelling from memory, sentence combining with body facts, and one conventions target per week. Earlier weeks and the Tall Tales unit come back as review. Items marked Extended are optional. Codes: VO4, VO10, VO11, VO12, CV1, CV2, CV5, CV10, CV12–14.

Our unit words

Tier 3 (science words) and Tier 2 (words for any subject). Fill in each card the week it comes up. Use your own words.

system
Meansa group of parts that work together
Examplethe respiratory system: nose, windpipe, lungs
Not an exampleone single bone
organ
Meansa body part made of tissues that has a special job
Examplethe heart, the stomach
Not an examplea single cell
oxygen
Meansa gas in air that every cell needs
Examplewhat the lungs take in
Not an examplecarbon dioxide (the waste gas)
nutrient
Meansa tiny part of food the body can use
Examplewhat passes into the blood in the small intestine
Not an examplethe whole sandwich
function
Meansthe job something does
Examplethe heart's function is to pump blood
Not an examplethe colour of the heart
reliable
Meanscan be trusted
Examplea doctor-reviewed hospital website
Not an examplean ad selling gummies
evidence
Meansfacts that show something is true
Examplemy pulse went up after running
Not an example"I just think so"
contract
Meansto tighten and get shorter
Examplethe biceps contracts to bend the arm
Not an examplea muscle relaxing

Conventions: commas in a list (CV2). Add the commas.

The respiratory system includes the nose the windpipe the lungs and the diaphragm.

Answer The respiratory system includes the nose, the windpipe, the lungs and the diaphragm.

Sentence combining with because: You breathe faster when you run. Your muscles need more oxygen.

Answer You breathe faster when you run because your muscles need more oxygen.

The windpipe is also called the trachea. Rewrite with parentheses.

Week 5

Morphology: -ment and -tion (turn a verb into a thing).

VerbThing

Conventions: transition words (WR5, CV10). Join with for example, as a result or also. Use each once.

  1. Bones protect organs. The skull protects the brain.
  2. The diaphragm moves down. Air rushes in.
  3. Muscles move bones. Muscles pump blood.

Answer 1. Bones protect organs; for example, the skull protects the brain. 2. The diaphragm moves down. As a result, air rushes in. 3. Muscles move bones. Heart muscle also pumps blood.
