Lesson 4 · Story map: Paul Bunyan

Use Anchor note 2. Fill in the map for the whole tale.

PartWhat happens¶
Beginning (character, setting)Paul is born huge and grows up to be the greatest logger in the north woods1–2

Write about it. Is the cold the main problem, or is the hunger? Use evidence from the text.

Answer The hunger is the main problem. In paragraph 5 the author writes "the camp had a new problem: the loggers were starving," and the resolution in paragraph 6 is that they are fed and go back to work. The cold is the setting that causes trouble, but Paul doesn't fix the cold; spring does.

Write about it. Which trait is the most important one for Sally Ann? Give two pieces of evidence.

Answer Sally Ann is above all determined. She says "Determined wins every time" (¶8), and when everyone else hides from the grizzly, she walks straight out and faces it (¶6). She doesn't give up or run when things are scary.

Flat or round? Does Sally Ann change during the story? Flat Round. How do you know?

Answer Flat. She is bold and confident at the start (¶1–2) and exactly the same at the end (¶8–9).

Lesson 8 · Reading between the lines

Use Anchor note 6. Answer each question with the frame: I think… In paragraph __, the text says "…". This shows… because…

1. Why do the people in the settlement think Sally Ann will "settle down" after the wedding (¶6)? What does that tell you about what people expected?

Answer I think people expected married women to stay home and act quietly. In paragraph 6 the text says "People in the settlement thought Sally Ann would settle down after the wedding. She did not." This shows they had ideas about how she should behave, and Sally Ann surprised them by staying bold.

2. Why doesn't Sally Ann roar back at the bear?

Answer I think she knew she didn't need to fight. In paragraph 8 she says "That bear was only hungry." This shows she understood the bear and used calm confidence (her smile) instead of force.

Monitor. Was there a sentence you had to reread? Write the ¶ number and what helped you understand it.

Lesson 9 · John Henry: before and during reading

Before reading: context. Your teacher will share some background about railroads in the 1870s. Write two facts that will help you understand the tale.

Answer Railroads were being built fast across the U.S. Tunnels were dug by hand by steel drivers, many of them Black workers who had been enslaved and were now free, earning low pay.

Predict. Read ¶1–4. A salesman brings a steam drill. What do you predict will happen? Why?

Confirm or revise. Read to the end. Was your prediction right? What new information changed or confirmed it? (CO7)

Answer Example: I predicted John Henry would beat the machine, and he did (¶7). But I did not predict that he would die (¶8). The line on the first day, "This hammer is going to be the death of me" (¶2), was a clue I missed.

Lesson 11 · Big Joe Mufferaw: how a legend grows

Read Tale 4. Show how the wagon story grew each time it was told (¶3).

First tellingLater tellingLatest telling
Joe could lift a loaded wagon…with the horses still attached…with the horses and driver, while drinking tea

Real or legend? Sort these. Write R (probably real) or L (legend).

StatementR or L
Joseph Montferrand was born in Montreal in 1802.R
He worked as a raftsman on the Ottawa River.R
He paddled 300 km in an afternoon and beat his own shadow.L
French- and English-speaking raftsmen both told stories about him.R

Write about it. Why do you think the raftsmen kept making Joe's stories bigger? Use what you know about where and when the stories were told. (OL1, CO14)

Answer I think they made the stories bigger to entertain each other on long nights by the fire (¶3) and to show pride in one of their own. Raftsmen had a hard, dangerous job, so a hero who was stronger than anything made them proud. Each teller "tried to beat the last one" (¶3), so it was also a contest.

Lesson 18 · Show, don't tell

Use Anchor note 8. Rewrite each telling sentence so it shows.

1. The giant was hungry.

Answer Example: The giant's stomach growled so loudly that the windows rattled three towns away.

2. The wind was strong.

Answer Example: The wind ripped the barn roof off and delivered it to a farm in Manitoba.

3. Mrs. Okafor was worried.

Answer Example: Mrs. Okafor twisted her mittens and paced back and forth until she had worn a path in the snow.

Exaggeration ladder. Make it bigger in three steps. The top step should show something about the hero.

StepMy hero is strong
1. RealShe can carry two backpacks.
2. BiggerShe can carry a car.
3. Tall taleShe carried the whole town on her back to higher ground when the river flooded.
