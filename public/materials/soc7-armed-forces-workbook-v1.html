Grade 7 Social Studies — Armed Forces and Sovereignty

Practice Workbook

Questions are mixed on purpose. Figuring out what each question is asking is part of the learning.
Learning Outcome: Students relate the development of armed forces in Canada to sovereignty.

How this workbook works

Questions from across the unit are mixed together on purpose. Your job is to figure out what each question is asking before you answer it. That thinking is where the learning happens.

★ Core For everyone. Try these first.
★★ Extended More challenging. Try after Core, or as a challenge.

Show your thinking. A correct answer with no explanation earns part marks on Extended questions.

Before you begin — forethought

Look over the first six questions. Which one do you think will be hardest for you? Write the question number and why.

Hardest question: Q _____      Why:  

1★ Core

It is 1874. You are an NWMP officer arriving at Fort Macleod. Write two sentences in your journal explaining why you were sent west and what your job is.

2★ Core

Match each battle to what happened there.

  1. Second Ypres, 1915
  2. Vimy Ridge, 1917
  3. Beaumont-Hamel, 1916
  4. Passchendaele, 1917

Choices: all four Canadian divisions attack together · Newfoundland Regiment loses most of its men in one morning · first German gas attack on the Western Front; Canadians hold the line · Canadians capture a village in deep mud

3★ Core

Put these steps in order and write the year for each: Royal Canadian Navy created, Militia Act (first), British Army leaves, Statute of Westminster, new Militia Act.

4★ Core

In your own words, what is sovereignty? Give one example of Canada gaining more sovereignty between 1867 and 1931.

5★ Core

Explain why English Canadians and French Canadians disagreed about the Boer War, and how Laurier tried to solve the problem.

6★ Core

A cenotaph in a small Alberta town lists 31 names under "1914–1918" and 18 names under "1939–1945". It was built in 1922 by the town council. At the top it says "Lest We Forget." Use the four memorial questions to describe what this source tells you.

7★★ Extended

How did the NWMP and the British Army leaving in 1871 both push Canada to take responsibility for its own peace and security?

8★★ Extended

The NWMP stopped the whisky trade that was hurting First Nations communities. It also enforced government policies that First Nations had not chosen. Explain how the NWMP could be seen both as protecting First Nations and as controlling them.

Halfway check-in

9★★ Extended

In August 1914 Canada was at war the moment Britain declared war. What does this tell you about Canada's sovereignty in 1914? What could Canada still decide?

10★★ Extended

In 1910 some Canadians said Canada should just give Britain money for its navy. Laurier created a Canadian navy instead. Explain why having its own navy mattered for Canada's sovereignty.

11★★ Extended

"The Boer War mattered more for what it showed about Canada than for what Canada did in South Africa." Do you agree? Use evidence.

12★★ Extended

Many battles were bigger or longer than Vimy Ridge. Explain why Vimy became a symbol for Canada.

13★★ Extended

Black, Indigenous and Sikh Canadians served in the First World War even though they faced discrimination at home. Choose one group. Describe their service, and explain why remembering it matters today.

14★★ Extended

Compare how Canada went to war in 1914 and in 1939. Explain what changed in between and why.

Challenge Zone

Try these after the core and extended questions. Each one asks you to pull ideas from more than one part of the unit.

S1★★

"Development of armed forces advanced Canada's national sovereignty." Support this statement with three pieces of evidence from three different decades.

S2★★

Research one heroic or gallant act by a Canadian or Newfoundland soldier in the First World War (a Victoria Cross recipient, Francis Pegahmagabow, a nurse, or a member of the No. 2 Construction Battalion). Write what they did and what it tells us about the war.

S3★★

Answer the guiding question in a strong paragraph: How can defence of peace and security play a role in the development of a nation? Use at least three examples from Canada between 1873 and 1931.
