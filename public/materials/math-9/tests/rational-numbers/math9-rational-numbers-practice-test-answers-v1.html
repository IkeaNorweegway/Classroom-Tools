Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. Which number is not a rational number?

A) −7B) 0.333…C) 2.75D) √2
Answer D · Why: √2 cannot be written as a fraction of two integers; its decimal never ends or repeats · Common errors: A: thinks negatives or integers are not rational. B: thinks a repeating decimal is not rational. C: thinks decimals are not rational

2. Which statement is true?

A) −5/6 > −7/8B) −3/4 > −1/4C) −2/3 > −1/2D) −1 > −9/10
Answer A · Why: −5/6 = −20/24 and −7/8 = −21/24; −20/24 is closer to zero · Common errors: B, C, D: compares absolute values and ignores the negative sign

3. Which number is equal to −3/5 ?

A) (−3)/(−5)B) 3/(−5)C) −(−3/5)D) 5/(−3)
Answer B · Why: A negative sign in the denominator gives the same value as one in the numerator · Common errors: A, C: two negatives make a positive, 3/5. D: flipped the fraction

4. −3/8 − (−5/8) =

A) −1B) −1/4C) 1/4D) 1
Answer C · Why: −3/8 + 5/8 = 2/8 = 1/4 · Common errors: A: did not change subtracting a negative into adding. B: correct size, wrong sign. D: added the absolute values

5. −4.6 + 1.85 =

A) −2.75B) −6.45C) 2.75D) −3.25
Answer A · Why: 4.6 − 1.85 = 2.75, and the larger absolute value is negative · Common errors: B: added the absolute values. C: correct size, wrong sign. D: regrouping slip in the subtraction

6. (−2/3) × (−9/10) =

A) −3/5B) 20/27C) −11/13D) 3/5
Answer D · Why: Negative × negative is positive; 18/30 = 3/5 · Common errors: A: sign error. B: cross-multiplied (2 × 10 over 3 × 9). C: added numerators and added denominators

7. Which expression has a value greater than 12?

A) 12 × 3/4B) 12 ÷ 4/3C) 12 ÷ 3/4D) 12 × (−4/3)
Answer C · Why: 12 ÷ 3/4 = 12 × 4/3 = 16; dividing by a number between 0 and 1 gives a larger result · Common errors: A, B: both equal 9. D: equals −16, large in size but negative

8. What is the first step in evaluating   1/2 − 3/4 × (2/3)² ?

A) Subtract 1/2 − 3/4B) Square 2/3C) Multiply 3/4 × 2/3D) Rewrite all three fractions with a common denominator
Answer B · Why: Exponents come before multiplication and subtraction · Common errors: A: works left to right. C: multiplies before the exponent. D: common denominators are only needed for the final subtraction

Part B — Understanding (Short Answer)

Show your working. Fraction answers in lowest terms. [3 marks each]   /30

9. Here are five rational numbers:   −0.6,   −2/3,   3/5,   −5/8,   0.58

[2](a) Write them in order from least to greatest.

, , , ,

[1](b) Explain how you decided which of −2/3 and −5/8 is smaller.

Answer (a) −2/3,  −5/8,  −0.6,  0.58,  3/5
  • 1 mark: the three negatives in the correct order
  • 1 mark: complete order correct (0.58 before 3/5 = 0.60)

(b) −2/3 = −16/24 and −5/8 = −15/24, so −2/3 is farther left on the number line. Decimal reasoning is also fine: −0.666… is less than −0.625.

  • 1 mark: a valid comparison (common denominator, decimals, or number line) that reaches −2/3 < −5/8. Award 0 for “2/3 is bigger so −2/3 is bigger.”

10. Evaluate:   −5/6 + 3/4

Answer −5/6 + 3/4 = −10/12 + 9/12 = −1/12
  • 1 mark: LCD of 12 with both fractions rewritten correctly
  • 1 mark: adds numerators with correct signs
  • 1 mark: correct answer

11. Evaluate:   (−8/15) × (5/12)

Answer (−8/15) × (5/12) = −40/180 = −2/9   (cross-cancelling gives (−2/3) × (1/3) directly)
  • 1 mark: negative sign
  • 1 mark: correct product or correct cross-cancelling
  • 1 mark: lowest terms

12. Evaluate:   (−5/9) ÷ (10/3)

Answer (−5/9) ÷ (10/3) = (−5/9) × (3/10) = −15/90 = −1/6
  • 1 mark: rewrites as multiplication by the reciprocal 3/10
  • 1 mark: correct product with correct sign
  • 1 mark: lowest terms

13. A student wrote:

2/3 + 1/4 = 3/7

What did the student do, and why does it not work? Give the correct answer.

Answer The student added the numerators (2 + 1) and added the denominators (3 + 4). Denominators name the size of the pieces; thirds and quarters are different sizes, so they cannot be counted together until both are rewritten in the same size of piece. 2/3 + 1/4 = 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12
  • 1 mark: identifies that numerators and denominators were both added
  • 1 mark: explains why it fails (different-sized pieces need a common denominator, or a check such as 3/7 being less than 2/3)
  • 1 mark: correct answer 11/12

14. Evaluate:   (−1/2 + 1/3) ÷ (−5/6)

Answer Brackets first: −1/2 + 1/3 = −3/6 + 2/6 = −1/6. Then (−1/6) ÷ (−5/6) = (−1/6) × (−6/5) = 1/5
  • 1 mark: bracket evaluated as −1/6
  • 1 mark: multiplies by the reciprocal −6/5
  • 1 mark: correct positive answer in lowest terms

15. Look at this product:   (−3/4) × (−2/9) × (−6/5)

[1](a) Before calculating, state whether the answer is positive or negative. Explain how you know.

[2](b) Calculate the product.

Answer (a) Negative. There are three negative factors, and an odd number of negatives gives a negative product.
  • 1 mark: negative with a reason

(b) 3 × 2 × 6 = 36 and 4 × 9 × 5 = 180, so the product is −36/180 = −1/5

  • 1 mark: correct product or correct cross-cancelling
  • 1 mark: −1/5 in lowest terms

16. Evaluate:   1 2/3 − 2 1/4

Answer 1 2/3 − 2 1/4 = 5/3 − 9/4 = 20/12 − 27/12 = −7/12
  • 1 mark: both mixed numbers converted to improper fractions
  • 1 mark: common denominator of 12
  • 1 mark: correct answer with sign

17. A classmate says: “Dividing always makes a number smaller.”

Calculate   9 ÷ 3/4 , then use your answer to explain why the classmate is wrong.

Answer 9 ÷ 3/4 = 9 × 4/3 = 12. The answer is larger than 9. Dividing by 3/4 asks how many three-quarter pieces fit into 9, and more than 9 of them fit because each piece is smaller than 1. Dividing only makes a number smaller when the divisor is greater than 1.
  • 1 mark: calculates 12
  • 1 mark: states that 12 is greater than 9, so the claim is false
  • 1 mark: explains why (pieces smaller than 1, or dividing by 3/4 is multiplying by 4/3, which is greater than 1)

18. Evaluate:   3/4 − (−1/2)² × 2/3

Answer (−1/2)² = 1/4. Then 1/4 × 2/3 = 1/6. Then 3/4 − 1/6 = 9/12 − 2/12 = 7/12
  • 1 mark: exponent first, giving positive 1/4
  • 1 mark: multiplication before subtraction, giving 1/6
  • 1 mark: correct answer 7/12

Follow-through: across Part B, if one early slip is carried correctly through later steps, deduct only the mark for the slip.

Show all working and write each answer with its units. [8 marks]   /8

19. A chinook is a warm, dry wind that can raise the temperature in southern Alberta very quickly.

At 7:00 a.m. in Pincher Creek the temperature is −9 1/2 °C. A chinook arrives, and the temperature rises 2 3/4 °C each hour for 4 hours.

[2](a) What is the total temperature rise over the 4 hours?

[2](b) What is the temperature at 11:00 a.m.?

[2](c) At 11:00 a.m. the chinook ends. The temperature now falls 1 1/2 °C each hour. How many hours does it take for the temperature to fall back to −9 1/2 °C?

[2](d) To find the temperature 3 hours after the chinook ends, a student writes:

1 1/2 − 1 1/2 × 3 = 0 × 3 = 0 °C

What rule did the student break? Find the correct temperature.

Answer (a) 4 × 2 3/4 = 4 × 11/4 = 11 °C   (RN3)
  • 1 mark: sets up 4 × 2 3/4 (or repeated addition)
  • 1 mark: 11 °C

(b) −9 1/2 + 11 = 1 1/2 °C   (RN2)

  • 1 mark: sets up −9 1/2 + 11
  • 1 mark: 1 1/2 °C (or 1.5 °C)

(c) The temperature has to fall 11 °C. 11 ÷ 1 1/2 = 11 ÷ 3/2 = 11 × 2/3 = 22/3 = 7 1/3 hours (7 h 20 min)   (RN4)

  • 1 mark: sets up 11 ÷ 1 1/2
  • 1 mark: 7 1/3 hours

(d) The student subtracted before multiplying. Multiplication comes first: 1 1/2 × 3 = 4 1/2, then 1 1/2 − 4 1/2 = −3 °C   (RN5)

  • 1 mark: names the order-of-operations error
  • 1 mark: −3 °C

Units: deduct 1 mark once across the whole question if units are missing throughout. Follow-through applies from (a) to (b) and (c).

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. Jordan says:

“There is no rational number between −1/3 and −1/4 , because 3 and 4 are right next to each other.”

Is Jordan correct? Justify your answer using equivalent fractions or a number line. In your answer, state which of −1/3 and −1/4 is greater.

Answer Model answer: Jordan is not correct. −1/3 = −4/12 and −1/4 = −3/12. Those look like neighbours, but writing them as −8/24 and −6/24 shows −7/24 sitting between them. Decimals show the same thing: −0.333… and −0.25 have −0.3 between them. −1/4 is the greater of the two because it is closer to zero. The denominators being consecutive does not matter, because any two different rational numbers have another rational number between them.
