Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. The solution of x4 − 3 = 2 is

A) x = −4B) x = 5/4C) x = 20D) x = 2
Answer C · Why: Add 3: x/4 = 5. Multiply by 4: x = 20 · Common errors: A: subtracted 3 and divided. B: divided by 4 instead of multiplying. D: subtracted 3 from 5

2. The solution of 7 − 2x = 13 is

A) x = −3B) x = 3C) x = 10D) x = −10
Answer A · Why: −2x = 6, so x = −3 · Common errors: B: lost the negative. C, D: added 7 and 13

3. −2(x − 5) =

A) −2x − 10B) −2x + 5C) 2x − 10D) −2x + 10
Answer D · Why: −2 × x = −2x and −2 × (−5) = +10 · Common errors: A: forgot that negative × negative is positive. B: only multiplied the first term. C: sign errors on both

4. To solve 6x − 4 = 2x + 8, a good first step is to

A) divide only the left side by 6B) subtract 2x from both sidesC) add 2x to both sidesD) subtract 8 from the left side only
Answer B · Why: Gets the x-terms on one side: 4x − 4 = 8 · Common errors: A, D: doing something to one side only breaks the balance. C: gives 8x on the left, which still leaves x on both sides

5. To clear the fractions in x3 + 1/4 = 5/6, multiply every term by

A) 3B) 4C) 72D) 12
Answer D · Why: 12 is the lowest common denominator of 3, 4 and 6 · Common errors: A, B: only clear one fraction. C: works, but it isn't the lowest and makes big numbers

6. The solution of −4x > 12 is

A) x > −3B) x < −3C) x > 3D) x < 3
Answer B · Why: Divide by −4 and flip: x < −3 · Common errors: A: forgot to flip. C, D: sign of the answer wrong

7. Which inequality matches this number line?

A) x > 2B) x ≤ 2C) x < 2D) x ≥ 2
Answer C · Why: Open circle: 2 is not included. Shaded left: less than 2 · Common errors: B: a closed circle would mean ≤. A, D: wrong direction

8. “Five more than three times a number is 26.” Which equation matches?

A) 3n + 5 = 26B) 3(n + 5) = 26C) 5n + 3 = 26D) 3n = 26 + 5
Answer A · Why: "Three times a number" is 3n; "five more" adds 5 · Common errors: B: brackets add 5 before multiplying. C: swapped 3 and 5. D: the 5 is on the wrong side

Part B — Understanding (Short Answer)

Show your working. [3 marks each]   /30

[3]9. Solve 5 − x2 = 9, then verify your solution.

Answer 5 − x/2 = 9, so −x/2 = 4, so x = −8. Verify: 5 − (−8)/2 = 5 + 4 = 9 ✓
  • 1 mark: subtracts 5 (−x/2 = 4)
  • 1 mark: x = −8
  • 1 mark: substitutes −8 into the original equation and gets 9

[3]10. Solve 3(2x − 1) = 4x + 9.

Answer 6x − 3 = 4x + 9, so 2x − 3 = 9, so 2x = 12, so x = 6.
  • 1 mark: expands to 6x − 3
  • 1 mark: collects terms (2x = 12)
  • 1 mark: x = 6

[3]11. A student solved 4(x − 3) = 2x + 6 like this:

4x − 3 = 2x + 6  →  2x = 9  →  x = 4.5

Find the mistake, then solve the equation correctly.

Answer The student only multiplied the x by 4: 4(x − 3) is 4x − 12, not 4x − 3. Correct: 4x − 12 = 2x + 6, so 2x = 18, so x = 9.
  • 1 mark: names the error
  • 1 mark: 4x − 12 = 2x + 6
  • 1 mark: x = 9

[3]12. Solve x2 + x5 = 7.

Answer Multiply every term by 10: 5x + 2x = 70, so 7x = 70, so x = 10.
  • 1 mark: multiplies every term by 10 (or another common multiple)
  • 1 mark: 7x = 70
  • 1 mark: x = 10

[3]13. Solve 0.6x − 1.5 = 0.3x + 0.9.

Answer Multiply by 10: 6x − 15 = 3x + 9, so 3x = 24, so x = 8. (Or with decimals: 0.3x = 2.4.)
  • 1 mark: clears the decimals or collects terms
  • 1 mark: 3x = 24 (or 0.3x = 2.4)
  • 1 mark: x = 8

[3]14. Solve 3x − 5 ≤ 7, then graph the solution on the number line.

Answer 3x ≤ 12, so x ≤ 4. Graph: closed circle at 4, shaded to the left.
  • 1 mark: 3x ≤ 12
  • 1 mark: x ≤ 4
  • 1 mark: closed circle at 4, arrow to the left

[3]15. Solve 8 − 2x < 14, then graph the solution on the number line.

Answer −2x < 6. Divide by −2 and flip: x > −3. Graph: open circle at −3, shaded to the right.
  • 1 mark: −2x < 6
  • 1 mark: x > −3 (the flip)
  • 1 mark: open circle at −3, arrow to the right

16.

[1](a) Is x = −2 a solution of 5 − 3x > 10? Show how you know.

[2](b) Solve 5 − 3x > 10. Does your answer agree with part (a)?

Answer (a) 5 − 3(−2) = 5 + 6 = 11, and 11 > 10, so yes.
  • 1 mark

(b) −3x > 5, so x < −5/3 (about −1.67). −2 is less than −5/3, so yes, it agrees.

  • 1 mark: x < −5/3 (with the flip)
  • 1 mark: checks that −2 fits

[3]17. Three consecutive integers add to 72. Define a variable, write an equation, and find the three integers.

Answer Let n = the first integer. n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 72, so 3n + 3 = 72, so n = 23. The integers are 23, 24 and 25.
  • 1 mark: defines n and writes the equation
  • 1 mark: n = 23
  • 1 mark: all three integers

[3]18. A gym in Grande Prairie charges $40 to join plus $12 per visit. Maya has $160 to spend. Write and solve an inequality to find how many visits she can afford, then answer in a sentence.

Answer Let v = the number of visits. 40 + 12v ≤ 160, so 12v ≤ 120, so v ≤ 10. Maya can afford at most 10 visits.
  • 1 mark: the inequality (≤, not =)
  • 1 mark: v ≤ 10
  • 1 mark: a sentence answer

19. A Grade 9 class sells hot chocolate at the Red Deer winter carnival. Supplies cost $90 to start, plus $0.50 for each cup made. They sell each cup for $2.00. Let c be the number of cups.

[2](a) Write an expression for the total cost and an expression for the money taken in (revenue).

Cost:     Revenue:

[2](b) Write and solve an equation to find how many cups they must sell to break even (cost = revenue).

[2](c) The class wants a profit of at least $150. Write and solve an inequality to find how many cups they must sell.

[2](d) They only have supplies for 150 cups. Can they reach their goal? Explain in a sentence.

Answer (a) Cost: 90 + 0.50c. Revenue: 2.00c.
  • 1 mark each

(b) 2c = 90 + 0.5c, so 1.5c = 90, so c = 60 cups to break even. (Multiplying by 10 first gives 20c = 900 + 5c.)

  • 1 mark: the equation
  • 1 mark: 60 cups

(c) Profit = revenue − cost: 2c − (90 + 0.5c) ≥ 150, so 1.5c − 90 ≥ 150, so 1.5c ≥ 240, so c ≥ 160.

  • 1 mark: the inequality
  • 1 mark: c ≥ 160

(d) No. They need to sell at least 160 cups, and they only have supplies for 150. (150 cups give a profit of 1.5 × 150 − 90 = $135.)

  • 1 mark: no
  • 1 mark: compares 160 with 150, or finds the $135 profit

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. Ali says:

“With inequalities, you flip the sign whenever there is a negative number in the problem.”

Is Ali correct? Test the claim by solving x − 4 > −6 and −2x > 6. Explain when the sign flips, and why.

Answer Model answer: Ali is not correct. In x − 4 > −6 there are negative numbers, but I add 4 to both sides and get x > −2. No flip is needed. Check: x = 0 works (0 − 4 = −4, which is greater than −6). In −2x > 6 I divide both sides by −2, so I flip: x < −3. Check: x = −4 gives 8 > 6 ✓, but x = 0 gives 0 > 6 ✗, so the flip was needed. The sign flips only when you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, because that reverses the order of numbers: 2 < 3, but −2 > −3.
