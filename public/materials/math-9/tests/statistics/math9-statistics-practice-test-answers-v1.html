Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. Which of these is bivariate data?

A) hours studied and test mark for 30 studentsB) the heights of 30 studentsC) favourite colours of 30 studentsD) the number of pets in 30 homes
Answer A · Why: Two variables measured on each student · Common errors: B, C, D: one variable each

2. Which graph is best for seeing whether two numerical variables are related?

A) bar graphB) circle graphC) pictographD) scatter plot
Answer D · Why: A scatter plot shows pairs of numerical values · Common errors: A, B, C: show one variable

3. On a scatter plot, as x increases, y tends to decrease. The correlation is

A) positiveB) negativeC) noneD) perfect
Answer B · Why: Downward trend

4. The points on a scatter plot lie close to a straight line. The correlation is

A) zeroB) weakC) strongD) proof of cause
Answer C · Why: Close to a line means strong · Common errors: D: correlation never proves cause

5. A good line of best fit

A) goes through as many points as possibleB) has about the same number of points above and below itC) always goes through the originD) joins the first and last points
Answer B · Why: Balanced points above and below · Common errors: A: the "connect the dots" mix-up. C: only if the data says so

6. A line of best fit has the equation y = 2x + 5. The predicted value of y when x = 10 is

A) 70B) 20C) 15D) 25
Answer D · Why: 2(10) + 5 = 25 · Common errors: B: forgot the + 5. C: added 10 + 5. A: 2 × 35

7. A data set has x-values from 10 to 50. Using the line of best fit to predict y when x = 65 is

A) extrapolationB) interpolationC) correlationD) an outlier
Answer A · Why: 65 is outside 10 to 50

8. A strong correlation between two variables does not prove

A) a relationship in the dataB) a trendC) causationD) that a line of best fit can be drawn
Answer C · Why: Correlation is not causation

Part B — Understanding (Short Answer)

Show your working. [3 marks each]   /30

9. Is a scatter plot a good choice for each data set? Answer yes or no, with a reason. [1 each]

(a) the number of students in each grade at a school

(b) the arm span and height of 20 students

(c) the daily high temperature and the number of jackets sold at a store

Answer (a) No: only one variable (a count for each grade); a bar graph fits. (b) Yes: two numerical measurements for each student. (c) Yes: two numerical variables recorded for each day.
  • 1 mark each (answer with a reason)

10. A study compares hours of screen time per day and hours of sleep for 25 students.

[1](a) Which is the independent variable?  

[1](b) Which axis does it go on?  

[1](c) What does one point on the scatter plot stand for?

Answer (a) Screen time (b) the x-axis (horizontal) (c) one student: their screen time and their sleep.
  • 1 mark each. Accept sleep as independent if the student explains the question is "does sleep affect screen time"

11. The scatter plot shows how far a truck has driven since filling up, and the fuel left in its tank.

[1](a) What is the direction of the correlation?

[1](b) What is the strength?

[1](c) Describe the relationship in a sentence that uses the context.

Answer (a) Negative (b) Strong (points close to a line) (c) The farther the truck has driven since filling up, the less fuel is left in the tank.
  • 1 mark each

[3]12. In Alberta towns, the number of coffee shops and the number of dentists have a strong positive correlation. Does that mean coffee shops cause more dentists? Explain, and name a third variable that could explain the pattern.

Answer No. Correlation doesn't show cause. A third variable, population, drives both: bigger towns have more people, so they have more coffee shops and more dentists.
  • 1 mark: says correlation does not prove causation
  • 1 mark: names a sensible third variable (population, town size)
  • 1 mark: explains how it affects both

[3]13. Two students draw a line of best fit for the same 10 points. Ana's line passes through 4 of the points, with the other 6 all below it. Ben's line passes through 1 point, with 5 points above and 4 below. Whose line is better? Explain using the rules for a line of best fit.

Answer Ben's line. A line of best fit should have about the same number of points above and below it and follow the middle of the data. It doesn't need to pass through points. Ana's line has all 6 other points below it, so it is too high.
  • 1 mark: Ben
  • 1 mark: balance of points above and below
  • 1 mark: passing through points is not the goal (or Ana's line is too high)

[3]14. The scatter plot shows hours of practice and free throws made out of 20. Draw a line of best fit, then find its equation.

Answer A line through the middle of the points, about y = 1.6x + 2.5. Accept a slope from 1.4 to 1.8 and a y-intercept from 1.5 to 3.5.
  • 1 mark: a straight line with about half the points on each side
  • 1 mark: slope from two points on the line (not data points unless on the line)
  • 1 mark: equation in y = mx + b form within the range

15. A coach uses the line of best fit y = 1.5x + 3, where x is hours of practice and y is free throws made out of 20.

[1](a) Predict the free throws made after 5.5 hours of practice.

[1](b) What does the slope, 1.5, mean here?

[1](c) What does the y-intercept, 3, mean here?

Answer (a) 1.5(5.5) + 3 = 11.25, about 11 free throws (b) Each extra hour of practice goes with about 1.5 more free throws made. (c) With no practice, a player makes about 3 free throws.
  • 1 mark each

16. The line of best fit for the number of electric cars registered in a town is E = 40t + 100, where t is the number of years after 2015. The data covers 2015 to 2023.

[1](a) Predict the number for 2020. Is this interpolation or extrapolation?

[1](b) Predict the number for 2040. Is this interpolation or extrapolation?

[1](c) Which prediction is more reliable? Why?

Answer (a) t = 5: 40(5) + 100 = 300, interpolation (b) t = 25: 40(25) + 100 = 1100, extrapolation (c) 2020, because it is inside the data range. 2040 is 17 years past the data, and the trend could change.
  • 1 mark each

17. A student measured a sunflower for 30 days and found the line of best fit h = 9d + 5, where h is the height in cm after d days.

[1](a) Use the line to predict the height after 200 days.

[2](b) Is this prediction reasonable? Explain why this kind of prediction can go wrong.

Answer (a) 9(200) + 5 = 1805 cm (about 18 m)
  • 1 mark

(b) Not reasonable. This is extrapolation far past the 30 days of data. Sunflowers stop growing at some point (and die in the fall), so the straight-line trend doesn't continue.

  • 1 mark: names it as extrapolation beyond the data
  • 1 mark: a reason the trend can't continue

[3]18. A line of best fit passes through (10, 35) and (30, 75). Find its equation.

Answer m = (75 − 35)/(30 − 10) = 40/20 = 2. 35 = 2(10) + b, so b = 15. y = 2x + 15
  • 1 mark: slope 2
  • 1 mark: b = 15
  • 1 mark: equation

Follow-through: across Part B, if one early slip is carried correctly through later steps, take off only the mark for the slip.

19. A Lethbridge outdoor pool recorded the daily high temperature and the number of visitors on 10 days.

Temperature (°C)18202123242627293032
Visitors110135130160170195190220235250

[2](a) Plot the data on the grid. Label both axes.

[2](b) Describe the correlation: direction, strength, and what it means here.

[2](c) Draw a line of best fit and find its equation.

[2](d) Use your equation to predict the number of visitors at 25 °C and at 40 °C. Which prediction is more reliable? Explain.

Answer (a) All 10 points plotted; axes labelled Temperature (°C) and Visitors.
  • 1 mark: at least 9 points correct
  • 1 mark: both axes labelled

(b) Strong positive correlation: on hotter days, more people come to the pool.

  • 1 mark: strong and positive
  • 1 mark: meaning in context

(c) About v = 10t − 70. Accept a slope from 9 to 11 with a matching intercept (predictions within about 10 visitors of the data).

  • 1 mark: a reasonable line
  • 1 mark: equation from two points on the line

(d) 25 °C: about 10(25) − 70 = 180 visitors. 40 °C: about 10(40) − 70 = 330 visitors. The 25 °C prediction is more reliable: it is interpolation, inside the 18 °C to 32 °C data. 40 °C is extrapolation, and the trend may not hold (the pool may be full, or it may be too hot to go out).

  • 1 mark: both predictions from their equation
  • 1 mark: picks 25 °C with the interpolation reason

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. A headline says:

“Students who eat breakfast get higher marks, so breakfast makes you smarter.”

Is the headline's conclusion justified? Explain the difference between correlation and causation, suggest one other variable that could explain the pattern, and describe what kind of study could test the claim.

Answer Model answer: The conclusion is not justified. The data may show a correlation (students who eat breakfast tend to have higher marks), but correlation doesn't prove that one thing causes the other. A third variable could explain both: for example, students with more regular routines or more sleep may be more likely to eat breakfast and also do better in school. To test the claim, you would need a controlled experiment: randomly put similar students into two groups, give one group breakfast and not the other, and compare marks over time, keeping other things the same.
