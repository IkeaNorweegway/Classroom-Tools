Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. Each table has x = 0, 1, 2, 3. Which y-values show a linear relation?

A) 2, 5, 8, 11B) 1, 2, 4, 8C) 0, 1, 4, 9D) 5, 4, 2, −1
Answer A · Why: The y-values go up by 3 every time · Common errors: B: doubles each time. C: square numbers. D: the differences change (−1, −2, −3)

2. “The cost of gas depends on the number of litres you buy.” The independent variable is

A) the costB) the price per litreC) the number of litresD) the gas station
Answer C · Why: You choose how many litres; the cost depends on that · Common errors: A: that is the dependent variable. B: that is the rate. D: not a variable here

3. The slope of the line through (2, 1) and (6, 9) is

A) 4B) 1/2C) 8D) 2
Answer D · Why: Rise 9 − 1 = 8, run 6 − 2 = 4, slope 8/4 = 2 · Common errors: B: run over rise. C: rise only. A: run only

4. A horizontal line has a slope that is

A) positiveB) zeroC) negativeD) undefined
Answer B · Why: No rise, so 0 ÷ run = 0 · Common errors: D: that is a vertical line

5. In y = −4x + 7, the y-intercept is

A) −4B) 7C) 4D) 7/4
Answer B · Why: In y = mx + b, b = 7 · Common errors: A: that is the slope. D: the x-intercept idea mixed in

6. Which equation has a slope of 3 and a y-intercept of −2?

A) y = −2x + 3B) y = −3x − 2C) y = 3x + 2D) y = 3x − 2
Answer D · Why: m = 3 goes in front of x; b = −2 is added on · Common errors: A: swapped m and b. C: wrong sign on b. B: wrong sign on m

7. Which equation shows direct variation?

A) y = x + 5B) y = 5x + 1C) y = 5xD) y = 5
Answer C · Why: y = kx with nothing added; it passes through (0, 0) · Common errors: B, A: have a starting value, so partial. D: a horizontal line

8. The graph of a partial variation

A) crosses the y-axis somewhere other than (0, 0)B) passes through (0, 0)C) is always horizontalD) is always a curve
Answer A · Why: Partial variation has a starting value b ≠ 0 · Common errors: B: that is direct variation. C, D: partial variations are straight, slanted lines

[3]11. Find the slope of the line on the grid. Show the rise and the run.

Answer From (−2, 4) to (4, −5): rise = −5 − 4 = −9, run = 4 − (−2) = 6. Slope = −9/6 = −3/2 (or −1.5).
  • 1 mark: rise −9
  • 1 mark: run 6
  • 1 mark: −3/2, negative because the line goes down from left to right

12. A hiker near Canmore walks down a mountain trail at a steady rate. At 10:00 a.m. she is at an elevation of 2400 m. At 1:00 p.m. she is at 1800 m.

[2](a) Find the slope (rate of change), with units.

[1](b) What does the sign of the slope tell you?

Answer (a) Rise = 1800 − 2400 = −600 m. Run = 3 h. Slope = −200 m/h.
  • 1 mark: −600 ÷ 3
  • 1 mark: −200 m/h with units

(b) It is negative, so her elevation is going down: she drops 200 m every hour.

  • 1 mark

[3]13. Kim found the slope of the line through (1, 3) and (5, 11) like this:

slope = 5 − 111 − 3 = 4/8 = 1/2

What did Kim do wrong? Find the correct slope.

Answer Kim put the run on top. Slope is rise over run: (11 − 3)/(5 − 1) = 8/4 = 2.
  • 1 mark: names the error (run over rise, or x-change on top)
  • 1 mark: correct set-up
  • 1 mark: 2

[3]14. A line passes through (0, −3) and (2, 5). Write its equation in the form y = mx + b.

Answer b = −3 (the line crosses the y-axis at (0, −3)). m = (5 − (−3))/(2 − 0) = 8/2 = 4. y = 4x − 3
  • 1 mark: b = −3
  • 1 mark: m = 4
  • 1 mark: equation

15. For the line y = 2x − 8:

[1](a) State the y-intercept as a point.  

[1](b) Find the x-intercept as a point.

[1](c) Jo says the y-intercept is 4, “because that's where the line hits zero.” Explain Jo's mistake.

Answer (a) (0, −8)   (b) 0 = 2x − 8, so x = 4: (4, 0)
  • 1 mark each

(c) The point where y = 0 is the x-intercept, on the x-axis. The y-intercept is where x = 0, on the y-axis, which is (0, −8).

  • 1 mark

[3]16. A line has a slope of −2 and passes through (3, 1). Find b, then write the equation of the line.

Answer 1 = −2(3) + b, so 1 = −6 + b and b = 7. y = −2x + 7
  • 1 mark: substitutes (3, 1) and m = −2
  • 1 mark: b = 7
  • 1 mark: equation

18. The graph shows the cost of renting a canoe at a lake in Banff.

[1](a) What is the y-intercept, and what does it mean here?

[1](b) What is the slope, and what does it mean here?

[1](c) Is this direct or partial variation? Explain.

Answer (a) $20: the cost before any time is used (a one-time fee). (b) (70 − 20)/5 = $10 per hour: each extra hour costs $10. (c) Partial: the line starts at $20, not at (0, 0).

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. Jordan says:

“A steeper line always has a bigger slope.”

Is Jordan correct? Use at least two equations or graphs to test the claim, and explain what slope tells you about steepness.

Answer Model answer: Jordan is not correct. Compare y = 2x and y = −5x. The line y = −5x is steeper (it drops 5 for every 1 across, while y = 2x only rises 2), but −5 is smaller than 2. Steepness depends on how big the slope is when you ignore its sign. The sign only tells you the direction: positive goes up to the right, negative goes down to the right. Jordan's rule only works when both slopes are positive: y = 5x is steeper than y = 2x, and 5 > 2.
