Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. A chord that passes through the centre of a circle is a

A) radiusB) arcC) tangentD) diameter
Answer D · Why: A diameter is the chord through the centre · Common errors: A: a radius ends at the centre. C: a tangent only touches the circle. B: an arc is part of the circle

2. An angle with its vertex on the circle and its arms along two chords is

A) a central angleB) always a right angleC) an inscribed angleD) a tangent angle
Answer C · Why: Vertex on the circle · Common errors: A: a central angle has its vertex at the centre. B: only on a diameter

3. A circle has a diameter of 26 cm. Its radius is

A) 52 cmB) 13 cmC) 26 cmD) 6.5 cm
Answer B · Why: 26 ÷ 2 = 13 · Common errors: A: doubled. D: halved twice

4. A line from the centre perpendicular to a chord

A) bisects the chordB) is always a radiusC) is a tangentD) is parallel to the chord
Answer A · Why: The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord · Common errors: B: it only reaches the circle if extended

5. In one circle, chords are drawn at different distances from the centre. Which chord is longest?

A) the one 5 cm from the centreB) the one 2 cm from the centreC) the one 7 cm from the centreD) they are all the same length
Answer B · Why: The closer a chord is to the centre, the longer it is · Common errors: C: the "longer chords are farther away" mix-up

6. A central angle is 110°. The inscribed angle on the same arc is

A) 220°B) 110°C) 55°D) 70°
Answer C · Why: Inscribed = central ÷ 2 = 55° · Common errors: A: doubled. B: thinks they are equal. D: 180 − 110

7. An inscribed angle that stands on a diameter is always

A) 90°B) 45°C) 180°D) different for each circle
Answer A · Why: The central angle on a diameter is 180°, and half of 180° is 90° · Common errors: B, C: halved or kept the wrong angle

8. A tangent and the radius to the point of tangency meet at

A) 45°B) 60°C) 180°D) 90°
Answer D · Why: A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency

[3]10. Chord AB is 24 cm long in a circle with a radius of 13 cm. How far is the chord from the centre?

Answer OM ⊥ AB, so M is the midpoint: AM = 12 cm. In right triangle OMA: OM² = 13² − 12² = 169 − 144 = 25, so OM = 5 cm.
  • 1 mark: AM = 12 (the perpendicular bisects the chord)
  • 1 mark: Pythagorean theorem with 13 as the hypotenuse
  • 1 mark: 5 cm

[3]11. A circle has a radius of 10 cm. Chord AB is 6 cm from the centre. How long is the chord?

Answer In right triangle OMB: MB² = 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64, so MB = 8 cm. The perpendicular bisects the chord, so AB = 16 cm.
  • 1 mark: right triangle with 10 cm as the hypotenuse
  • 1 mark: MB = 8 cm
  • 1 mark: doubles to 16 cm

[3]12. Two chords in a circle with a radius of 17 cm are each 30 cm long. How far is each chord from the centre? Name the chord property that lets you answer for both chords at once.

Answer Half of each chord is 15 cm. Distance² = 17² − 15² = 289 − 225 = 64, so each chord is 8 cm from the centre. Equal chords are the same distance from the centre, so one calculation answers for both.
  • 1 mark: uses half the chord (15 cm)
  • 1 mark: 8 cm
  • 1 mark: names the equal-chords property

13. In the diagram, ∠ACB = 38°.

[1](a) Find ∠AOB.  

[1](b) Find ∠ADB.  

[1](c) Name the property you used in (b).

Answer (a) ∠AOB = 2 × 38° = 76° (b) ∠ADB = 38° (c) Inscribed angles on the same arc are equal.
  • 1 mark each

[3]14. AB is a diameter and C is on the circle. ∠CAB = 32°. Find ∠ABC. Explain each step.

Answer AB is a diameter, so ∠ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle). The angles in triangle ABC add to 180°: ∠ABC = 180° − 90° − 32° = 58°.
  • 1 mark: ∠ACB = 90°, with the reason
  • 1 mark: uses the angle sum of a triangle
  • 1 mark: 58°

[3]15. A student wrote:

“The inscribed angle is 50°, so the central angle on the same arc is 25°.”

Explain the mistake and give the correct central angle.

Answer The student halved instead of doubling. The central angle is twice the inscribed angle: 2 × 50° = 100°.
  • 1 mark: names the error (halved, or mixed up which angle is bigger)
  • 1 mark: states the theorem the right way round
  • 1 mark: 100°

[3]16. PT is tangent to the circle at T. The radius is 8 cm and OP = 17 cm. Find the length of PT.

Answer OT ⊥ PT, so triangle OTP has its right angle at T and OP is the hypotenuse. PT² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225, so PT = 15 cm.
  • 1 mark: right angle at T (tangent ⊥ radius)
  • 1 mark: subtracts (17² − 8²)
  • 1 mark: 15 cm

[3]17. PT is tangent to the circle at T, and ∠TOP = 52°. Find ∠OPT. Explain each step.

Answer ∠OTP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius). ∠OPT = 180° − 90° − 52° = 38°.
  • 1 mark: ∠OTP = 90°, with the reason
  • 1 mark: angle sum of 180°
  • 1 mark: 38°

18. PA and PB are tangent to the circle at A and B. PA = 11 cm and the radius is 6 cm.

[1](a) How long is PB?  

[1](b) What is the size of ∠OAP?  

[1](c) Find the perimeter of the shape OAPB.

Answer (a) 11 cm (tangent segments from the same point are equal) (b) 90° (c) 6 + 11 + 11 + 6 = 34 cm
  • 1 mark each

19. A circular skating pond in an Edmonton park has a radius of 25 m.

[2](a) A straight rope barrier, AB, is a chord 48 m long. How far is the rope from the centre of the pond?

[2](b) A second rope, also 48 m long, is put across the pond somewhere else. How far is it from the centre? Explain how you know without calculating again.

[2](c) A bench at P is 65 m from the centre. A path goes from P and just touches the edge of the pond at T. How long is the path PT?

[2](d) Two lifeguard chairs, L and M, are on the edge of the pond. The central angle ∠LOM is 100°. A photographer stands at Q on the edge, on the far side, and fits both chairs exactly in the frame. What angle, ∠LQM, does the camera need to take in?

Answer (a) Half the rope is 24 m. d² = 25² − 24² = 625 − 576 = 49, so the rope is 7 m from the centre.
  • 1 mark: right triangle with half the chord and the radius
  • 1 mark: 7 m

(b) 7 m. Equal chords in the same circle are the same distance from the centre.

  • 1 mark: 7 m
  • 1 mark: names the equal-chords property

(c) OT ⊥ PT, so PT² = 65² − 25² = 4225 − 625 = 3600, so PT = 60 m.

  • 1 mark: right angle at T and OP as the hypotenuse
  • 1 mark: 60 m

(d) ∠LQM is an inscribed angle on the same arc as the central angle ∠LOM, so ∠LQM = 100° ÷ 2 = 50°.

  • 1 mark: uses the inscribed angle theorem
  • 1 mark: 50°

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. Maria says:

“Every angle inscribed in a circle is 90°.”

Is Maria correct? Use the inscribed angle theorem and at least one example to explain when an inscribed angle is 90° and when it is not.

Answer Model answer: Maria is not correct. An inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc. It is 90° only when it stands on a diameter: then the central angle is a straight line, 180°, and half of that is 90°. For any other chord it is not 90°. For example, if the central angle is 80°, the inscribed angle on that arc is 40°. So an inscribed angle is a right angle only when it stands on a diameter (an angle in a semicircle).
