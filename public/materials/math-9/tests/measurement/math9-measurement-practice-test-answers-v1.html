Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. A triangle has a base of 10 cm and a perpendicular height of 6 cm. Its area is

A) 60 cm²B) 16 cm²C) 30 cm²D) 32 cm²
Answer C · Why: (10 × 6) ÷ 2 = 30 · Common errors: A: forgot to halve. B: added. D: perimeter idea

2. The area of a circle with a radius of 5 cm, to the nearest tenth, is

A) 31.4 cm²B) 25.0 cm²C) 15.7 cm²D) 78.5 cm²
Answer D · Why: π × 5² ≈ 78.5 · Common errors: A: 2πr (circumference). C: πr. B: forgot π

3. Which measurement tells you how much cardboard you need to make a closed box?

A) surface areaB) volumeC) perimeterD) capacity
Answer A · Why: Material covers the outside · Common errors: B: volume is the space inside

4. When you unroll the curved surface of a cylinder, it makes a

A) circleB) rectangleC) triangleD) semicircle
Answer B · Why: Its length is the circumference, its width the height

5. The volume of a rectangular prism 4 cm by 5 cm by 6 cm is

A) 60 cm³B) 15 cm³C) 148 cm³D) 120 cm³
Answer D · Why: 4 × 5 × 6 = 120 · Common errors: C: surface area. A: forgot one dimension

6. Which formula gives the volume of a cylinder?

A) 2πrhB) 2πr² + 2πrhC) πr²hD) ⅓πr²h
Answer C · Why: Base area πr² × height · Common errors: A: lateral area. B: surface area. D: a cone

7. A cone and a cylinder have the same radius and the same height. The cone's volume is

A) the same as the cylinder'sB) one-third of the cylinder'sC) half the cylinder'sD) double the cylinder's
Answer B · Why: A cone is one-third of the matching cylinder · Common errors: C: the "half" mix-up

8. Two cubes with 2 cm sides are glued face to face. The surface area of the new shape is

A) 40 cm²B) 48 cm²C) 24 cm²D) 44 cm²
Answer A · Why: 2 × 24 = 48, minus the 2 hidden faces of 4 cm² each · Common errors: B: forgot the hidden faces. D: removed only one face

Part B — Understanding (Short Answer)

Show the formula and your working. [3 marks each]   /30

[3]9. The shape is a rectangle, 10 cm by 6 cm, with a semicircle on one 6 cm side. Find its area.

Answer Rectangle: 10 × 6 = 60 cm². Semicircle: diameter 6, so r = 3: (1/2)π(3)² ≈ 14.1 cm². Total ≈ 74.1 cm².
  • 1 mark: rectangle area
  • 1 mark: semicircle area with r = 3 (not 6)
  • 1 mark: total with units

[3]10. Find the surface area of a closed rectangular prism 10 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm high.

Answer SA = 2(10 × 4) + 2(10 × 3) + 2(4 × 3) = 80 + 60 + 24 = 164 cm²
  • 2 marks: all three pairs of faces (1 mark for two pairs)
  • 1 mark: 164 cm²

[3]11. Find the surface area of a closed cylinder with a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm.

Answer SA = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2π(9) + 2π(3)(10) = 18π + 60π = 78π ≈ 245.0 cm²
  • 1 mark: the two circles (18π)
  • 1 mark: the curved side (60π)
  • 1 mark: 245.0 cm²

[3]12. A triangular prism has a triangular base with a base of 6 cm and a height of 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long. Find its volume.

Answer Base area = (6 × 4) ÷ 2 = 12 cm². V = Bh = 12 × 10 = 120 cm³
  • 1 mark: triangle area 12
  • 1 mark: multiplies by the length 10
  • 1 mark: 120 cm³

[3]13. A water tank is a cylinder with a radius of 1.5 m and a height of 4 m. Find its volume.

Answer V = πr²h = π(1.5)²(4) = 9π ≈ 28.3 m³
  • 1 mark: formula with r = 1.5
  • 1 mark: 9π
  • 1 mark: 28.3 m³

[3]14. A shed is a rectangular prism 6 m wide, 10 m long and 4 m tall, with a triangular prism roof. The roof's triangle has a base of 6 m and a height of 3 m. Find the total volume of the shed.

Answer Main shed: 6 × 10 × 4 = 240 m³. Roof: (6 × 3 ÷ 2) × 10 = 90 m³. Total = 330 m³
  • 1 mark: 240 m³
  • 1 mark: 90 m³
  • 1 mark: total with units

15. A square pyramid has a base 10 cm on each side, a height of 12 cm and a slant height of 13 cm.

[2](a) Find its volume.

[1](b) Find its surface area.

Answer (a) V = (1/3)(10²)(12) = (1/3)(100)(12) = 400 cm³ (b) SA = 10² + 2(10)(13) = 100 + 260 = 360 cm²
  • (a) 1 mark: base area × height; 1 mark: divides by 3, 400 cm³
  • (b) 1 mark

16. A basketball is a sphere with a radius of 12 cm.

[1](a) Find its surface area.

[2](b) Find its volume.

Answer (a) SA = 4π(12)² = 576π ≈ 1809.6 cm² (b) V = (4/3)π(12)³ = 2304π ≈ 7238.2 cm³
  • (a) 1 mark
  • (b) 1 mark: (4/3)π × 1728; 1 mark: 7238.2 cm³

17. A cone has a radius of 5 cm and a height of 12 cm.

[1](a) Find its slant height.

[2](b) Find its surface area.

Answer (a) l = √5² + 12² = √169 = 13 cm (b) SA = π(5)² + π(5)(13) = 25π + 65π = 90π ≈ 282.7 cm²
  • (a) 1 mark
  • (b) 1 mark: base 25π and side 65π; 1 mark: 282.7 cm²

[3]18. A grain bin is a cylinder (radius 3 m, height 10 m) with a cone on top (radius 3 m, height 4 m). Find the total volume.

Answer Cylinder: π(3)²(10) = 90π. Cone: (1/3)π(3)²(4) = 12π. Total = 102π ≈ 320.4 m³
  • 1 mark: cylinder volume
  • 1 mark: cone volume (with the one-third)
  • 1 mark: total with units

Follow-through: across Part B, if one early slip is carried correctly through later steps, take off only the mark for the slip.

Part C — Application (Extended Response)

Show all working. Round to one decimal place and include units. [8 marks]   /8

19. A Lethbridge honey company is choosing a closed container.

Container A: a cylinder with a radius of 4 cm and a height of 10 cm.
Container B: a rectangular prism 7 cm by 7 cm by 10 cm.

[2](a) Find the volume of each container.

[2](b) Find the surface area of each container.

[2](c) Which container holds more honey? Which uses less material? Which would you recommend, and why?

[2](d) A designer suggests a cone with a radius of 4 cm and a height of 10 cm, and says it holds “half as much as Container A.” Find the cone's volume and correct the designer.

Answer (a) A: π(4)²(10) = 160π ≈ 502.7 cm³. B: 7 × 7 × 10 = 490 cm³.
  • 1 mark each

(b) A: 2π(4)² + 2π(4)(10) = 32π + 80π = 112π ≈ 351.9 cm². B: 2(7 × 7) + 4(7 × 10) = 98 + 280 = 378 cm².

  • 1 mark each

(c) Container A holds more (502.7 cm³ vs 490 cm³) and uses less material (351.9 cm² vs 378 cm²), so A is the better choice. (Accept B if the reason is about packing or stacking boxes, as long as the numbers are compared correctly.)

  • 1 mark: compares both volume and surface area correctly
  • 1 mark: a recommendation with a reason

(d) Cone: (1/3)π(4)²(10) = (1/3)(160π) ≈ 167.6 cm³. That is one-third of Container A, not half.

  • 1 mark: 167.6 cm³
  • 1 mark: says it is one-third, not half

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. Owen says:

“If you double the length, the width and the height of a box, you double its volume.”

Is Owen correct? Test the claim with a box of your choice, and explain what really happens to the volume.

Answer Model answer: Owen is not correct. Take a box 2 cm × 3 cm × 4 cm: V = 24 cm³. Double every edge: 4 cm × 6 cm × 8 cm, V = 192 cm³. 192 ÷ 24 = 8, so the volume is 8 times as big, not 2 times. Each of the three dimensions is doubled, so the volume is multiplied by 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. To double the volume, you would double only one of the dimensions.
