Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

Circle the best answer. [1 mark each]   /8

1. Which number is a perfect square?

A) 18B) 32C) 64D) 200
Answer C · Why: 8² = 64 · Common errors: A, B: doubled a number (9 × 2, 16 × 2) instead of squaring it. D: thinks any number ending in 00 is a perfect square (100 and 10 000 are; 200 isn't)

2. √36 =

A) ±6B) 6C) 18D) 1296
Answer B · Why: 6² = 36, and √ means the positive (principal) root · Common errors: A: −6 also squares to 36, but √36 asks for the principal root only. C: halved. D: squared

3. √55 is between which two whole numbers?

A) 5 and 6B) 7 and 8C) 27 and 28D) 54 and 56
Answer B · Why: 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, and 55 is between them · Common errors: A: used the digits 5 and 5. C: halved 55. D: the neighbours of 55, not of its root

4. Which number is irrational?

A) √25B) √0.16C) √9/4D) √12
Answer D · Why: 12 is not a perfect square, so √12 is a decimal that never ends or repeats · Common errors: A: √25 = 5. B: √0.16 = 0.4. C: √9/4 = 3/2

5. √25/64 =

A) 5/8B) 25/8C) 5/64D) 12.532
Answer A · Why: √25 = 5 and √64 = 8, so the root is 5/8 · Common errors: B: rooted only the bottom. C: rooted only the top. D: halved instead of rooting

6. √0.81 =

A) 0.09B) 0.9C) 9D) 0.405
Answer B · Why: 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.81 · Common errors: A: 0.09 × 0.09 = 0.0081. C: ignored the decimal. D: halved

7. In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is

A) the shortest sideB) the side across from the right angleC) either side that touches the right angleD) the side that stands straight up
Answer B · Why: The hypotenuse is across from the right angle, and it is always the longest side · Common errors: A: mixed up longest and shortest. C: those are the legs. D: a triangle can be turned any way

8. Which side lengths make a right triangle?

A) 4, 5, 6B) 6, 8, 10C) 5, 6, 7D) 7, 8, 9
Answer B · Why: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10² · Common errors: A: looks like 3, 4, 5, but 16 + 25 = 41, not 36. C: 25 + 36 = 61, not 49. D: 49 + 64 = 113, not 81

Part B — Understanding (Short Answer)

Show your working. [3 marks each]   /30

9.

[2](a) List every perfect square between 100 and 200.

[1](b) Is 150 a perfect square? Use two of your numbers from (a) to explain.

Answer (a) 121, 144, 169, 196 (11², 12², 13², 14²)
  • 2 marks: all four. 1 mark: three of them

(b) No. 12² = 144 and 13² = 169, so √150 is between 12 and 13. No whole number squares to 150.

  • 1 mark: no, with the two neighbouring squares as the reason

10. Maya says:

“√6.4 = 3.2, because half of 6.4 is 3.2.”

[2](a) Is Maya correct? Show a check that proves your answer.

[1](b) Find √0.64.

Answer (a) No. 3.2 × 3.2 = 10.24, not 6.4. Halving a number is not the same as finding its square root.
  • 1 mark: checks by squaring 3.2
  • 1 mark: says Maya is wrong, with a reason

(b) √0.64 = 0.8, because 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64

  • 1 mark: 0.8

[3]11. Estimate √30 to one decimal place. Show the perfect squares you used, and check your estimate by squaring it.

Answer 5² = 25 and 6² = 36, so √30 is between 5 and 6. Try 5.4² = 29.16 and 5.5² = 30.25. 30 is closer to 30.25, so √30 ≈ 5.5. (A calculator gives 5.477…)
  • 1 mark: benchmarks 25 and 36 (or 5 and 6)
  • 1 mark: estimate of 5.5 (accept 5.4 if the working supports it)
  • 1 mark: checks the estimate by squaring it

[3]12. Mark and label √5, √10 and √20 on the number line.

Answer √5 ≈ 2.2, just past 2.   √10 ≈ 3.2, just past 3.   √20 ≈ 4.5, between 4 and 5, close to 4.5.
  • 1 mark each: placed in the right half-unit and labelled

[3]13. A right triangle has legs of 5 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse, c.

Answer c² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169, so c = √169 = 13 cm
  • 1 mark: a² + b² = c² set up with 5 and 12
  • 1 mark: 169
  • 1 mark: 13 cm

Common error: adding the legs (5 + 12 = 17). The hypotenuse is always shorter than the two legs added together.

[3]14. A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 12 cm and one leg of 6 cm. Find the other leg, b. Give the exact answer and an estimate to one decimal place.

Answer b² = 12² − 6² = 144 − 36 = 108, so b = √108 ≈ 10.4 cm   (10² = 100 and 11² = 121; 10.4² = 108.16)
  • 1 mark: subtracts, because the missing side is a leg
  • 1 mark: √108
  • 1 mark: estimate 10.4 cm (accept 10.3 with working)

Common error: adding gives √180 ≈ 13.4 cm, which is longer than the hypotenuse. A leg can never be longer than the hypotenuse.

[3]15. A student wrote:

√36 + 64 = √36 + √64 = 6 + 8 = 14

What did the student do wrong? Give the correct answer, and show a check that proves 14 is wrong.

Answer The student took the square root of each number separately. A square root doesn't split over addition, so add inside first: √36 + 64 = √100 = 10. Check: 14² = 196, not 100, so 14 can't be right.
  • 1 mark: names the error (rooted each number instead of the sum)
  • 1 mark: correct answer 10
  • 1 mark: a check, such as 14² = 196, not 100

16. Evaluate. [1 each]

(a) √49/100(b) √1.44(c) √4/9 + √0.25
Answer (a) 7/10    (b) 1.2 (1.2 × 1.2 = 1.44)    (c) 2/3 + 1/2 = 4/6 + 3/6 = 7/6 (or 1 1/6)
  • 1 mark each. (c) needs the common denominator

17. A square garden has an area of 196 m².

[1](a) How long is each side?

[2](b) Sam says: “If we make the garden twice as big, 392 m², each side will be twice as long, 28 m.” Is Sam right? Explain.

Answer (a) √196 = 14 m
  • 1 mark: 14 m

(b) No. A square with 28 m sides has an area of 28² = 784 m². That is four times the area, not twice. Doubling the side multiplies the area by 4. The side for 392 m² is √392 ≈ 19.8 m (19² = 361, 20² = 400).

  • 1 mark: shows 28² = 784 (or another check that 28 m is wrong)
  • 1 mark: explains that doubling the side gives four times the area, or finds the side √392 ≈ 19.8 m

18.

[1](a) Write a whole number whose square root is between 7 and 8.  

[1](b) Explain how you know.

[1](c) Is the square root of your number rational or irrational? Explain.

Answer (a) Any whole number from 50 to 63. (b) 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, so any number between 49 and 64 has a square root between 7 and 8. (c) Irrational, because the number is not a perfect square, so its square root is a decimal that never ends or repeats.
  • 1 mark for each part

Follow-through: across Part B, if one early slip is carried correctly through later steps, take off only the mark for the slip.

Part C — Application (Extended Response)

Show all working and write each answer with its units. [8 marks]   /8

19. A school field in Lethbridge is a rectangle 80 m long and 60 m wide. Students cut across it to get from corner A to corner C.

[2](a) How long is Path 1, the diagonal from A to C?

[2](b) How much shorter is Path 1 than walking around the edge from A to B to C?

[2](c) Path 2 goes from A to M, the middle of the far long side. How long is Path 2? Give the exact answer. Is it closer to 70 m or 80 m? Show how you know.

[2](d) The groundskeeper checks that corner A is a right angle. She measures 3 m along one side and 4 m along the other, then measures 5.2 m between the two marks. Is corner A a right angle? Explain using the Pythagorean theorem.

Answer (a) d² = 80² + 60² = 6400 + 3600 = 10 000, so d = 100 m. Shortcut: 80 and 60 are 20 × 4 and 20 × 3, so d = 20 × 5 = 100 m.
  • 1 mark: a² + b² = c² set up with 80 and 60
  • 1 mark: 100 m

(b) A to B to C = 80 + 60 = 140 m. 140 − 100 = 40 m shorter

  • 1 mark: 140 m around the edge
  • 1 mark: 40 m

(c) M is halfway along the 80 m side, so the legs are 40 m and 60 m. AM² = 40² + 60² = 1600 + 3600 = 5200, so AM = √5200 m. 70² = 4900 and 80² = 6400, and 5200 is much closer to 4900, so Path 2 is closer to 70 m (it is about 72 m).

  • 1 mark: uses 40 m (half of 80) and 60 m
  • 1 mark: √5200 and “closer to 70 m”, backed by 70² and 80²

(d) No. If the corner were a right angle, 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5², so the marks would be exactly 5 m apart. But 5.2² = 27.04, not 25. The corner is not a right angle. (The gap is too long, so the corner is a little wider than 90°.)

  • 1 mark: compares 3² + 4² = 25 with 5.2² (or 5 m with 5.2 m)
  • 1 mark: says it is not a right angle

Units: take off 1 mark once if units are missing throughout. Follow-through applies from (a) to (b).

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. A right triangle has legs of 30 cm and 50 cm. Two students try to find the hypotenuse without a calculator.

Jordan says:

“Shrink it by 10. A triangle with legs 3 and 5 has a hypotenuse of √34, so the big triangle's hypotenuse is 10 × √34.”

Riley says:

“30² is 100 times bigger than 3², so the hypotenuse should be 100 × √34.”

Who is correct? Find the hypotenuse the long way to check, and explain why. Then estimate the hypotenuse to the nearest centimetre.

Answer Model answer: Jordan is correct. The long way: c² = 30² + 50² = 900 + 2500 = 3400. 3400 = 100 × 34, so c = √100 × 34 = √100 × √34 = 10 × √34.

Riley is right that each square is 100 times bigger, but the hypotenuse is the square root of that, and √100 = 10. So the hypotenuse is only 10 times bigger. (Riley's answer can't be right: 100 × √34 is more than 500 cm, much longer than the two legs added together.)

Estimate: 5² = 25 and 6² = 36, and 5.8² = 33.64, so √34 ≈ 5.8 and the hypotenuse is about 58 cm (check: 58² = 3364, close to 3400).
