1. In the power (−5)³, the base is

A) 5B) −125C) 3D) −5
Answer D · Why: The brackets make −5 the base · Common errors: A: ignored the brackets. C: that is the exponent. B: that is the value

2. −2⁴ =

A) 16B) −16C) −8D) 8
Answer B · Why: No brackets, so the base is 2: −(2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = −16 · Common errors: A: treated it as (−2)⁴. C, D: multiplied 2 × 4

3. (−9)⁰ =

A) −9B) 0C) 1D) −1
Answer C · Why: Any non-zero base to the power 0 is 1 · Common errors: A: kept the base. B: thinks 0 as an exponent gives 0. D: thinks a negative base makes it −1

4. 6³ × 6⁵ =

A) 6⁸B) 6¹⁵C) 36⁸D) 12⁸
Answer A · Why: Same base, so add the exponents: 3 + 5 = 8 · Common errors: B: multiplied the exponents. C: multiplied the bases too. D: added the bases

5. x¹⁰ ÷ x² =

A) x⁵B) x²⁰C) x¹²D) x⁸
Answer D · Why: Same base, so subtract the exponents: 10 − 2 = 8 · Common errors: A: divided the exponents. C: added. B: multiplied

6. (y³)⁶ =

A) y¹⁸B) y⁹C) 6y³D) y³⁶
Answer A · Why: Power of a power: multiply the exponents, 3 × 6 = 18 · Common errors: B: added. C: multiplied the base by 6. D: squared 6

7. (2m)⁴ =

A) 2m⁴B) 8m⁴C) 16m⁴D) 8m
Answer C · Why: Both factors get the exponent: 2⁴ × m⁴ = 16m⁴ · Common errors: A: forgot the 2. B: 2 × 4 instead of 2⁴. D: multiplied everything by 4

8. 4⁻² =

A) −16B) 1/16C) −8D) 1/8
Answer B · Why: 4⁻² = 1/4² = 1/16 · Common errors: A, C: thinks a negative exponent gives a negative answer. D: 4 × 2 instead of 4²

9.

[2](a) Write (−2)⁵ in expanded form and evaluate it.

[1](b) Without calculating, is (−2)¹¹ positive or negative? Explain.

Answer (a) (−2) × (−2) × (−2) × (−2) × (−2) = −32
  • 1 mark: expanded form with five factors of (−2)
  • 1 mark: −32

(b) Negative. The exponent 11 is odd, so there is an odd number of negative factors. The negatives pair up and one is left over.

  • 1 mark: negative, with the odd-exponent reason

[3]10. Ava says:

“−3² and (−3)² are both 9.”

Evaluate each one, and explain why they are different.

Answer −3² = −(3 × 3) = −9. (−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9. In −3² the base is 3 and the negative sign is applied after squaring. In (−3)² the brackets make −3 the base, so the negative is squared too.
  • 1 mark: −3² = −9
  • 1 mark: (−3)² = 9
  • 1 mark: explains that the brackets decide the base

11. Evaluate. [1 each]

(a) 4⁰(b) −4⁰(c) 2⁰ + 2¹ + 2²
Answer (a) 1   (b) −(4⁰) = −1   (c) 1 + 2 + 4 = 7
  • 1 mark each. (b): the exponent applies to 4 only, so the answer is −1, not 1

14. Jordan wrote (x⁴)³ = x⁷. Priya wrote x⁴ × x³ = x¹².

[2](a) Both made a mistake. Give the correct answer for each.

(x⁴)³ =     x⁴ × x³ =

[1](b) How can you tell whether to add or multiply the exponents?

Answer (a) (x⁴)³ = x¹² and x⁴ × x³ = x⁷. Jordan and Priya swapped the rules.
  • 1 mark each

(b) Look at what connects the powers. Multiplying two powers with the same base: add the exponents. A power raised to a power, (x⁴)³ = x⁴ × x⁴ × x⁴: multiply the exponents.

15. Simplify. [1 each]

(a) (3x²)³(b) (a5)²(c) (−2y⁴)³
Answer (a) 3³x⁶ = 27x⁶   (b) a²/25   (c) (−2)³y¹² = −8y¹²
  • 1 mark each. Common errors: 3x⁶ or 9x⁶ in (a); a²/5 in (b); 8y¹² or −6y¹² in (c)

16.

[2](a) Let x = 2. Show that (x + 3)² is not equal to x² + 9.

[1](b) Explain why you can share an exponent over a product, like (2x)², but not over a sum, like (x + 3)².

Answer (a) (2 + 3)² = 5² = 25, but 2² + 9 = 4 + 9 = 13. 25 is not 13, so they are not equal.
  • 1 mark: (x + 3)² = 25
  • 1 mark: x² + 9 = 13, and says they are different

(b) (2x)² = 2x × 2x = 4x²: everything inside is multiplied, so each factor gets squared. (x + 3)² = (x + 3)(x + 3), and the x and the 3 also multiply each other, which gives an extra middle part (6x). An exponent only shares over multiplication.

  • 1 mark: a reason that refers to multiplying (x + 3) by itself, or to the extra middle term

17. Evaluate. Give each answer as a fraction. [1 each]

(a) 3⁻²(b) (−2)⁻³(c) 2⁻¹ + 2⁻²
Answer (a) 1/3² = 1/9   (b) 1/(−2)³ = −1/8   (c) 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4
  • 1 mark each. (b): the base is negative and the power is odd, so the answer is negative

19. A science class in Red Deer grows bacteria in a dish. The number of bacteria doubles every hour. At 12:00 noon there are 2⁶ bacteria.

[2](a) How many bacteria are there at 3:00 p.m.? Write the answer as a power of 2, then evaluate it.

[2](b) A second dish has 2¹⁰ bacteria at noon. How many times as many bacteria does it have as the first dish? Write the answer as a power of 2, then evaluate it.

[2](c) Six hours before noon, the first dish had 2⁰ bacteria. Kai says that means the dish was empty. Is Kai right? Explain.

[2](d) Using the same pattern, eight hours before noon the first dish would have 2⁻² bacteria. Evaluate 2⁻². Does this number make sense for bacteria? Explain.

Answer (a) Three more hours means three more doublings: 2⁶ × 2³ = 2⁹ = 512 bacteria.
  • 1 mark: 2⁶ × 2³ = 2⁹
  • 1 mark: 512

(b) 2¹⁰ ÷ 2⁶ = 2⁴ = 16 times as many.

  • 1 mark: 2¹⁰ ÷ 2⁶ = 2⁴
  • 1 mark: 16

(c) No. 2⁰ = 1, so there was 1 bacterium in the dish. Counting back from noon halves the number each hour: 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1.

  • 1 mark: 2⁰ = 1
  • 1 mark: explains with the halving pattern or the zero-exponent rule

(d) 2⁻² = 1/2² = 1/4. It doesn't make sense: you can't have a quarter of a bacterium. The doubling pattern only works back to the time when the first bacterium was put in the dish.

  • 1 mark: 1/4
  • 1 mark: says it doesn't make sense, with a reason

Part D — Thinking

One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks]   /4

20. Sam says:

“2¹⁰ is twice as big as 2⁵, because 10 is twice as big as 5.”

Is Sam correct? Use what you know about powers to explain, and show a calculation that supports your answer.

Answer Model answer: Sam is not correct. 2⁵ = 32 and 2¹⁰ = 1024. 1024 ÷ 32 = 32, so 2¹⁰ is 32 times as big as 2⁵, not twice as big. You can also see it with the exponent laws: 2¹⁰ = 2⁵ × 2⁵, so 2¹⁰ is 2⁵ times 2⁵. Another way: 2¹⁰ = (2⁵)². Doubling the exponent squares the value; it doesn't double it. The number that is twice 2⁵ is 2⁶ = 64, because adding 1 to the exponent multiplies by one more 2.
