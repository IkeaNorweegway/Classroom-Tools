Part B — Short Answer

9. (a) (−2) × (−2) × (−2) × (−2) × (−2) = −32

  • 1 mark: expanded form with five factors of (−2)
  • 1 mark: −32

(b) Negative. The exponent 11 is odd, so there is an odd number of negative factors. The negatives pair up and one is left over.

  • 1 mark: negative, with the odd-exponent reason

10. −3² = −(3 × 3) = −9. (−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9. In −3² the base is 3 and the negative sign is applied after squaring. In (−3)² the brackets make −3 the base, so the negative is squared too.

  • 1 mark: −3² = −9
  • 1 mark: (−3)² = 9
  • 1 mark: explains that the brackets decide the base

  • 1 mark each. (b): the exponent applies to 4 only, so the answer is −1, not 1

14. (a) (x⁴)³ = x¹² and x⁴ × x³ = x⁷. Jordan and Priya swapped the rules.

  • 1 mark each

(b) Look at what connects the powers. Multiplying two powers with the same base: add the exponents. A power raised to a power, (x⁴)³ = x⁴ × x⁴ × x⁴: multiply the exponents.

  • 1 mark: a correct way to tell the rules apart (expanded form is fine)

15. (a) 3³x⁶ = 27x⁶   (b) a²/25   (c) (−2)³y¹² = −8y¹²

  • 1 mark each. Common errors: 3x⁶ or 9x⁶ in (a); a²/5 in (b); 8y¹² or −6y¹² in (c)

16. (a) (2 + 3)² = 5² = 25, but 2² + 9 = 4 + 9 = 13. 25 is not 13, so they are not equal.

  • 1 mark: (x + 3)² = 25
  • 1 mark: x² + 9 = 13, and says they are different

(b) (2x)² = 2x × 2x = 4x²: everything inside is multiplied, so each factor gets squared. (x + 3)² = (x + 3)(x + 3), and the x and the 3 also multiply each other, which gives an extra middle part (6x). An exponent only shares over multiplication.

  • 1 mark: a reason that refers to multiplying (x + 3) by itself, or to the extra middle term

17. (a) 1/3² = 1/9   (b) 1/(−2)³ = −1/8   (c) 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4

  • 1 mark each. (b): the base is negative and the power is odd, so the answer is negative

18. 10¹ = 10, 10⁰ = 1, 10⁻¹ = 1/10 (0.1), 10⁻² = 1/100 (0.01). Each time the exponent goes down by 1, the value is divided by 10.

  • 2 marks: all four values (1 mark for two or three)
  • 1 mark: says each step divides by 10

Part D — Thinking

20. Model answer: Sam is not correct. 2⁵ = 32 and 2¹⁰ = 1024. 1024 ÷ 32 = 32, so 2¹⁰ is 32 times as big as 2⁵, not twice as big. You can also see it with the exponent laws: 2¹⁰ = 2⁵ × 2⁵, so 2¹⁰ is 2⁵ times 2⁵. Another way: 2¹⁰ = (2⁵)². Doubling the exponent squares the value; it doesn't double it. The number that is twice 2⁵ is 2⁶ = 64, because adding 1 to the exponent multiplies by one more 2.
