Section 1 — What Is a Rational Number?

Learning target: I can identify rational numbers, represent them in multiple forms, and compare and order them on a number line.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Rational numberAny number written as a/b where a, b are integers and b ≠ 0−3/4, 5, 0.25, 0.333...
NumeratorTop number; tells how many parts you haveIn 3/4, the numerator is 3
DenominatorBottom number; tells the size/type of partsIn 3/4, the denominator is 4
Equivalent fractionsFractions that represent the same value1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6
Opposite (of a rational number)
Absolute valueDistance from zero; always non-negative|−3/4| = 3/4

Guided Notes

A rational number is any number that can be written in the form a/b, where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0.

This includes:

  • All integers: 5 = 5/1, −3 = −3/1
  • All fractions: 3/4, −7/8
  • All terminating decimals: 0.75 = 75 / 100/100
  • All repeating decimals: 0.333... = 1 / 3/3

Every rational number has three equivalent sign forms:

−3/4 is the same as: 3/(−4) and −(3/4)

All three represent the same point on the number line.

Comparing rational numbers:

StatementTrue or False?Reason
−1/2 > −3/4TRUE−1/2 is closer to 0; on the number line, it sits to the right of −3/4
−5/6 < −7/8FALSELCD = 24: −5/6 = −20/24, −7/8 = −21/24; −20/24 > −21/24, so −5/6 > −7/8
2/3 > 3/4FALSELCD = 12: 2/3 = 8/12, 3/4 = 9/12; 8 < 9, so 2/3 < 3/4

Method — Use a common denominator:

Compare −5/6 and −7/8.

Common denominator: 24

−5/6 = −2024    −7/8 = −2124

Compare: −2024 > −2124, so −5/6 > −7/8

Key insight with negatives: For negative fractions, the fraction that is larger in absolute value is actually smaller in value.
(−1/4 is greater than −3/4 even though 1/4 < 3/4)

Misconception

Wrong idea: "−3/4 is bigger than −1/4 because 3/4 is bigger than 1/4."

Why it fails: On the number line, negative numbers to the right are greater. −1/4 sits to the right of −3/4, so −1/4 > −3/4. Think of it as debt: owing $0.25 is better (closer to 0) than owing $0.75.

Correct: −1/4 > −3/4

Place these rational numbers in order from least to greatest:

1/2, −3/4, −1/3, 0, 5/6, −1

−1, −3/4, −1/3, 0, 1/2, 5/6

  1. Is the number −8 a rational number? Explain.
    Answer Yes — it can be written as −8/1 (integer over non-zero integer satisfies the definition)
  2. Write −5/3 in two equivalent sign forms.
    Answer 5/(−3) and −(5/3)

    Answer −2/3. LCD = 12: −2/3 = −8/12, −3/4 = −9/12. Since −8 > −9, −2/3 > −3/4.

Section 2 — Adding and Subtracting Rational Numbers

Learning target: I can add and subtract rational numbers in both fraction and decimal form, including negative values.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Common denominatorA shared denominator for two or more fractions6 is a common denominator for 1/2 and 1/3
Least common denominator (LCD)The smallest shared denominatorLCD of 1/4 and 1/6 is 12
Mixed numberWhole part + fraction part23/4
Improper fractionNumerator ≥ denominator11/4

Guided Notes

Rule: To add or subtract fractions, they must have the same denominator.

Step sequence — adding fractions:

  1. Find the lowest common denominator (LCD)
  2. Rewrite each fraction with the LCD
  3. Add/subtract the numerators
  4. Simplify (reduce) if possible

Worked Example 1 — Positive fractions:

3/4 + 5/6

Step 1: LCD of 4 and 6 = 12

Step 2: 3/4 = 912    5/6 = 1012

Step 3: 912 + 1012 = 1912

Step 4: Simplify: 19/12 = 1 7/12

Explain Step 2: Why do we multiply both the numerator and denominator by the same number when rewriting the fraction?

Answer Step 1 of adding fractions: Find the LCD
Step 3 of adding fractions: Add/subtract the numerators

Worked Example 2 — Negative fractions:

−1/3 + (−3/4)

LCD = Multiplying the top and bottom by the same number is multiplying by 1, so the value does not change; only the size of the pieces changes.

−1/3 = −412    −3/4 = −912

−412 + (−912) = −1312

Simplified: −13/12 = −1 1/12

Pattern: Adding two negative rationals always gives a negative result.

Worked Example 3 — Mixed signs (the harder case):

−5/6 + 1/2

LCD = 6

−5/6 = −5/6    1/2 = 36

−5/6 + 36 = −26

Explain: When the signs differ, do you add or subtract the numerators? How do you determine the sign of the answer?

Misconception

Wrong idea: 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5 (add numerators, add denominators)

Why it fails: Denominators name the kind of pieces (halves, thirds). You cannot add pieces of different sizes by just counting them — just like you cannot add 2 hours + 3 minutes and say "5 hour-minutes." Convert first: 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, so 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Check: 2/5 = 0.40. But 1/2 + 1/3 = 0.5 + 0.333 = 0.833. These are very different numbers.

Subtracting: treat it as adding the opposite.

5/8 − (−3/8)

= 5/8 + 3/8 = 8/8 = 1

Alberta context — Temperature change:
In Fort McMurray, the temperature on Monday was −7.5°C. By Wednesday it had risen 11.25°C. What was Wednesday's temperature?

Temperature = −7.5 + 11.25 = 3.75°C

Section 3 — Multiplying Rational Numbers

Learning target: I can multiply rational numbers in fraction form, determine the correct sign, and simplify the result.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
ProductResult of multiplication(−2/3) × (3/4) = −1/2
Simplify before multiplying (cross-cancel)Divide a numerator and a different fraction's denominator by their GCF before multiplying(4/9) × (3/8): cancel 4↔8 and 3↔9 → (1/3) × (1/2) = 1/6
ReciprocalFlip numerator and denominatorReciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3

Guided Notes

To multiply fractions: Multiply numerator × numerator and multiply denominator × denominator.

You do NOT need a common denominator for multiplication.

(a/b) × (c/d) = ac/bd

Sign rules for multiplication:

Signs of the two factorsSign of the product
Positive × PositivePositive
Negative × NegativePositive
Positive × NegativeNegative
Negative × PositiveNegative

Quick rule: Count the negatives. If there is an odd number of negative factors, the product is negative.

Worked Example 1 — Two fractions:

(−3/5) × (2/7)

Numerators: −3 × 2 = −6

Denominators: 5 × 7 = 35

Sign: negative × positive = negative

Result: −6/35

Explain Step 3: You have one negative factor and one positive factor. What does the sign rule tell you the product will be?

Negative: one negative factor (an odd number) gives a negative product.

Cross-cancellation — simplify BEFORE you multiply:

(8/15) × (5/4)

Step 1: Can any numerator and denominator share a common factor?
8 and 4 share factor 4; 5 and 15 share factor 5.

Reduce: 8/4 = 2/1 and 5/15 = 1/3

Now multiply: (2/3) × (1/1) = 2/3

Explain: Why does cross-cancelling before multiplying give the same answer as multiplying then reducing? Which is easier?

You divide the same common factor out of the top and bottom either way, so the value is the same.Cancelling first is easier: the numbers stay small.

Worked Example 2 — Multiplying a fraction by an integer:

(−3/4) × 8

Rewrite 8 as 8/1

(−3/4) × (8/1) = −24/4 = −6

Misconception

Wrong idea: In (3/8) × (4/9), a student cancels the 3 and 9 "because they look diagonal."

Why it fails: Cross-cancelling works between a numerator and a denominator that share a factor — it is the same as dividing both the top and bottom of the eventual answer by the same number. The 3 in the numerator of 3/8 and the 9 in the denominator of 4/9 are on opposite sides of the multiplication sign, so this is valid. But you cannot cancel a numerator with a numerator, or a denominator with a denominator.

Check: Can you cancel the 8 and the 4? Yes   Why? 8 is a numerator and 4 is a denominator, and they share the factor 4

Alberta context — Pipeline flow rate:
A pipeline carries −5/6 of its designed capacity per hour (negative because flow is below target). Over 3/4 of a day, what total fraction of designed capacity does it deliver?

(−5/6) × (3/4) = −15/24 = −5/8

Retrieval Check — Close Your Notes
  1. Find: (−2/3) × (−3/4)
    Answer Neg × Neg = Pos. 6/12 = 1/2
  2. Find: (5/6) × (−9/10). Use cross-cancellation.
    Answer Cancel 5↔10 (÷5) and 3↔6 (÷3): (1/2) × (−3/2) = −3/4
  3. Find: (−3) × (−2/5)
    Answer Neg × Neg = Pos. 6/5 = 1 1/5
  4. A student evaluates (−2/3) × (−4/5) and gets −8/15. What error did they make?
    Answer Ignored sign rule: neg × neg = positive. Correct answer is +8/15
  5. (Stretch) Find: (−3/4)3. Hint: this means (−3/4) × (−3/4) × (−3/4).
    Answer (−3/4)2 = 9/16 (pos). × (−3/4) = −27/64

Section 4 — Dividing Rational Numbers

Learning target: I can divide rational numbers by multiplying by the reciprocal and explain why this method works.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
ReciprocalFlip the fractionReciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3
QuotientResult of division6 ÷ 2 = 3
DivisorThe number you divide byIn 6 ÷ 3, divisor is 3

Guided Notes

The big idea: Dividing by a number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.

a ÷ (b/c) = a × c/b

The reciprocal of a/b is b/a.

To find a reciprocal: flip the fraction (swap numerator and denominator).

FractionReciprocal
3/44/3
−5/2−2/5
7 (= 7/1)1/7
−1/3−3 (= −3/1)
Misconception

Wrong idea: "If I divide something by a fraction, the answer should be smaller — dividing means making things smaller."

Why it fails: This is only true when dividing by a number greater than 1. When you divide by a fraction less than 1, you are asking "how many of this fraction fit into the whole?" More pieces fit, so the answer is larger.

Example: 6 ÷ (1/2) = 12. There are 12 half-pieces in 6 whole pieces.

Think of it as: 6 ÷ (1/2) = 6 × 2 = 12. Dividing by 1/2 is multiplying by 2.

Test it: Does 6 ÷ (1/3) give something bigger or smaller than 6? Bigger   Calculate it: 6 × 3 = 18

Worked Example 1:

(−3/4) ÷ (2/5)

Step 1: Rewrite as multiplication by the reciprocal:
(−3/4) × (5/2) = −15/8

Explain Step 1: How do you find the reciprocal of 2/5? Why does multiplying by the reciprocal give the same result as dividing?

Flip it: the reciprocal of 2/5 is 5/2.Multiplying by 5/2 undoes multiplying by 2/5, which is exactly what dividing by 2/5 does.

Worked Example 2 — Dividing mixed numbers:

21/2 ÷ (−3/4)

Step 1: Convert to improper fraction: 21/2 = 5/2

Step 2: Rewrite as multiplication: (5/2) × (−4/3) = −20/6

Sign: positive × negative = negative    Result: −10/3 = −3 1/3

Why does the "multiply by reciprocal" rule work?

(2/3) ÷ (4/5) means: "what number times 4/5 equals 2/3?"

Let the answer be n. Then: n × (4/5) = 2/3.

Solving: n = 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = (2/3) × (5/4) = 10/12 = 5/6.

Check: 5/6 × (4/5) = (10/12) × (4/5) = 40/60 = 2/3 ✓

Alberta context — Fair share:
Three families in Edmonton are splitting 2/3 of a shared lot of firewood equally. What fraction does each family receive?

(2/3) ÷ 3 = (2/3) × 1/3 = 2/9

Each family receives 2/9 of the total firewood.

  5. (Stretch) A recipe needs 3/4 cup of oats per batch. You have 51/4 cups. How many complete batches can you make? Write the division and interpret the remainder as a fraction.

Section 5 — Order of Operations with Rational Numbers

Learning target: I can evaluate multi-step expressions with rational numbers using the correct order of operations.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
BEDMASBrackets, Exponents, Division/Multiplication (L→R), Addition/Subtraction (L→R)2 + 3 × 4 = 14, not 20
EvaluateFind the numerical value of an expressionEvaluate 3 + 2 × 4 = 11
Nested bracketsBrackets within brackets; work innermost out(2 + (3 × 4)) = (2 + 12) = 14

Guided Notes

BEDMAS applies to rational numbers without exception.

StepOperationNotes for rational numbers
BBrackets — innermost firstConvert mixed numbers before starting
EExponentsApply sign rules: (−2/3)2 = 4/9
D/MDivision and MultiplicationLeft to right; division = multiply by reciprocal
A/SAddition and SubtractionLeft to right; need LCD for fractions

Worked Example — Full BEDMAS:

(−1/2 + 3/4)2 ÷ (−1/4)

B: −1/2 + 3/4 = −2/4 + 3/4 = 1/4

E: (1/4)2 = 1/16

D: 1/16 ÷ (−1/4) = 1/16 × (−4) = −4/16 = −1/4

Explain the B step: Why do we handle the bracket before squaring? What would go wrong if you applied the exponent first?

The bracket must become one number (1/4) before it is squared.Squaring each part first gives 1/4 + 9/16, which is a different (wrong) value.

Common sequence error:

Evaluate: 1/2 + 3/4 × (−2/3)

Wrong approach: A student adds first: (1/2 + 3/4) × (−2/3)

This gives: (5/4) × (−2/3) = −10/12 = −5/6 (wrong)

Correct approach — multiplication first:

3/4 × (−2/3) = −1/2    Then 1/2 + (−1/2) = 0

Sign traps with exponents:

−(3/4)2 versus (−3/4)2

−(3/4)2 = −(9/16) = −9/16

(−3/4)2 = (−3/4) × (−3/4) = 9/16

These are different. Which answer is negative and why?

−(3/4)² is negative: the exponent applies only to 3/4, and the negative sign is applied after squaring.

Part A — Key ideas

1. What makes a number "rational"? Give the definition and two examples that are NOT obvious (not just 1/2).

Answer Any number expressible as a/b (integers, b ≠ 0). E.g., √4 = 2 = 2/1 is rational; 0.666... = 2/3 is rational.

2. Why do you need a common denominator to add fractions but NOT to multiply them?

Answer Addition: denominators name the size of pieces — you can't add different-sized pieces. Multiplication: you multiply the pieces themselves; the size relationship doesn't need to match.

3. Explain in your own words why dividing by a fraction less than 1 makes the answer bigger than what you started with.

Answer You're asking "how many of this small piece fit?" Since the piece is smaller than 1, more fit — the count exceeds what you started with.

4. State the sign rule for multiplying two negative rational numbers.

Answer Negative × negative = positive (even count of negatives → positive product)

Answer: (−1/4) ÷ (−5/6) = 3/10; then 3/10 × (−2/5) = −6/50 = −3/25
