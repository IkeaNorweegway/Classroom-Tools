Learning target: I can identify and name all major parts of a circle and use precise geometric language when describing circle relationships.

Guided Notes

Label each part on the diagram below:

Fill in the labels: P, Q, O, R, T, and the two unlabelled lines.

Fill in the blanks:

The centre of a circle is the point equidistant from all points on the circle.

The radius (r) goes from the the centre to any point on the circle.

The diameter (d) is the longest chord — it passes through the centre.

d = 2 × r × r        r = d / 2

A chord is a line segment with both endpoints on the circle. (The diameter is a special chord.)

An arc is a portion/part of the circle between two points.

Misconception

A chord is the same as a diameter

Wrong idea: "Any chord that goes through the circle is a diameter."

Why it fails: A diameter is a chord that passes specifically through the centre. Most chords do not pass through the centre and are shorter than the diameter. Every diameter is a chord, but not every chord is a diameter.

In the diagram above, QR passes through the centre O, so QR is a diameter. A chord that does not pass through O would be shorter than the diameter.

Central angle vs. inscribed angle:

A central angle has its vertex at the centre of the circle.

An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle itself (circumference) of the circle, with both sides being chords.

Draw an example of each inside this circle:

The arc intercepted by an angle is the arc that lies inside (between the arms of) the angle.

Tangent:

A tangent is a line that touches the circle at exactly one point (the point of tangency).

At the point of tangency, the tangent is perpendicular (at 90°) to the radius. (We will prove this in Section 4.)

Is a tangent the same as a chord? No Explain: A chord joins two points on the circle; a tangent touches the circle at only one point

Naming arcs:

A minor arc is the shorter arc between two points (less than a semicircle).

A major arc is the longer arc (more than a semicircle).

A semicircle is exactly half of the circle — it is the arc cut by a diameter.

Retrieval Check — Close Your Notes
  1. What is the difference between a chord and a diameter?
    Answer Chord: both endpoints on circle, any length. Diameter: special chord passing through centre; longest chord possible.
  2. What is the difference between a central angle and an inscribed angle?
    Answer Central: vertex at centre. Inscribed: vertex on circle, sides are chords.
  3. In a circle with diameter 14 cm, what is the radius?
    Answer r = 7 cm
  4. A tangent line touches a circle at one point. What property does it have relative to the radius at that point?
    Answer Perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency (90° angle)
  5. (Stretch) Can two different chords in the same circle be equal in length? What would this tell you about their distances from the centre?
    Answer Yes. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre — they are the same distance from the centre.

Section 2 — Chord Properties

Learning target: I can apply the two chord properties — the perpendicular bisector property and the equal chords/equal distance property — to solve problems.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Perpendicular bisectorLine perpendicular to a segment AND cutting it exactly in halfOM ⊥ AB and AM = MB
BisectCut into two equal halvesOM bisects AB
EquidistantSame distance from a reference pointTwo equal chords are equidistant from the centre
Distance from centre to chordLength of the perpendicular segment from centre to chordOM where OM ⊥ AB

Guided Notes

Property 1: The Perpendicular from the Centre Bisects the Chord

If a line from the centre of a circle is perpendicular to a chord, then it bisects that chord (cuts it into two equal halves).

In symbols: If OM ⊥ AB, then AM = MB

The converse is also true: If a line from the centre bisects a chord, then it is perpendicular to the chord.

Worked Example 1:

A chord AB is 16 cm long. The perpendicular from centre O meets AB at M. Find AM.

Since OM ⊥ AB and passes through the centre, it bisects AB.

AM = 16/2 = 8 cm

Now extend — use Pythagorean theorem:

If the radius is 10 cm, find OM.

Right triangle OMA: OA = 10 cm (radius), AM = 8 cm, OM = ?

OA2 = OM2 + AM2

102 = OM2 + 82

OM2 = 10² = 100 − 8² = 64 = 36

OM = 6 cm

Explain: Which side is the hypotenuse in triangle OMA? How do you know?

OA (the radius): it is opposite the right angle at M

Property 2: Equal Chords Are Equidistant from the Centre

In the same circle:

  • If two chords are equal in length, then they are equidistant (the same distance) from the centre.
  • Conversely: if two chords are the same distance from the centre, they are equal.

If AB = CD, then d₁ = d₂

Misconception

A longer chord is farther from the centre

Wrong idea: "The longest chord should be farthest from the centre — bigger chords must extend farther."

Why it fails: It's the opposite. The chord closest to the centre is the longest. The diameter — the longest chord of all — passes through the centre, at distance 0. As a chord moves farther from the centre, it gets shorter.

Think of it: as you move a horizontal chord up toward the top of a circle, it gets shorter and shorter until it disappears at the edge. The closer to the middle, the longer.

Worked Example 2:

Two chords PQ and RS are each 24 cm long in a circle with radius 13 cm.

Since PQ = RS, they are equidistant from the centre.

Let M be the midpoint of PQ. PM = 24/2 = 12 cm

Right triangle OMP: OP = 13 (radius), PM = 12, OM = ?

132 = OM2 + 122

OM = √(13² − 12²) = √25 = 5 cm

Therefore both chords are 5 cm cm from the centre.

Retrieval Check — Close Your Notes
  1. State the perpendicular bisector chord property in your own words.
    Answer If a line from the centre is perpendicular to a chord, it cuts the chord into two equal halves (bisects it at the midpoint).
  2. A chord is 18 cm long. The perpendicular from the centre has length 12 cm. Find the radius. (Draw a diagram first.)
    Answer Half-chord = 9. r2 = 122+92 = 144+81 = 225. r = 15 cm
  3. Two chords in the same circle are both 10 cm long. What can you conclude about their distances from the centre?
    Answer They are equidistant from the centre (same length → same distance by Property 2).
  4. In a circle with radius 5 cm, a chord is 3 cm from the centre. How long is the chord?
    Answer Half-chord = √(25−9) = √16 = 4. Full chord = 8 cm
  5. (Stretch) A chord is 8 cm from the centre of a circle with radius 17 cm. Another chord in the same circle is 15 cm from the centre. Which chord is longer? By how much?
    Answer d=8: half=√(289−64)=√225=15, chord=30 cm. d=15: half=√(289−225)=√64=8, chord=16 cm. Chord at d=8 is longer by 14 cm.

Section 3 — Central Angle and Inscribed Angle Theorem

Learning target: I can apply the inscribed angle theorem to find unknown angles, and explain why inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Inscribed angle theoremInscribed angle = 1/2 × central angle subtending same arcCentral 80° → inscribed 40°
SubtendTo form an angle over (stand opposite to)AOB subtends arc AB
Same arcThe arc between the two points defining both anglesArc AB for both angle AOB and APB

Guided Notes

The Inscribed Angle Theorem:

The inscribed angle is half the central angle that subtends the same arc.

Inscribed angle = (1/2) × Central angle
OR: Central angle = 2 × Inscribed angle

Angle AOB (central) = 2 × APB (measure it)°
Angle APB (inscribed, same arc AB) = ½ × AOB°

Worked Example 1:

A central angle AOB = 80°. Find the inscribed angle APB subtending the same arc.

Inscribed angle = Central angle / 2

APB = 80° / 2 = 40°

Worked Example 2:

An inscribed angle APB = 35°. Find the central angle AOB subtending the same arc.

Central angle = 2 × Inscribed angle

AOB = 2 × 35° = 70°

Explain: An inscribed angle is at the circumference, while a central angle is at the centre. Why does being at the centre make it "see" the arc at twice the angle? (Think about how far each vertex is from the arc.)

The arms of the central angle meet the arc from the centre, so they open across the whole arc; an inscribed angle sits on the far edge of the circle, so for the same arc it opens only half as wide.

Corollary: Inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal.

If angles APB and AQB are both inscribed in the same circle and subtend the same arc AB, then:

APB = AQB = (1/2) × AOB

No matter where P and Q are on the major arc, the inscribed angle is always the same size.

APB = AQB = ½ × AOB

Misconception

All inscribed angles in a circle equal 90°

Wrong idea: "Any angle drawn inside a circle is 90°."

Why it fails: Only inscribed angles that subtend a diameter (semicircle) equal 90°. That is a special case. An inscribed angle subtending an arc of 60° (central angle 60°) gives an inscribed angle of 30°. The angle depends on the arc.

The special case: when AB is a diameter, the central angle AOB = 180°. So the inscribed angle = 180°/2 = 90°. This is why angles in a semicircle are 90°.

Angles in a semicircle:

If AB is a diameter, then any inscribed angle APB = 90°.

This is because the central angle for a diameter is always 180° (a straight line), so the inscribed angle is half: 90°.

Test this: In a circle, chord AB is a diameter. Point P is on the circle. What is angle APB? 90°

Finding unknown angles:

In a circle, central angle AOB = 110°. Find:

a) Inscribed angle APB (same arc) = 55°

b) Inscribed angle AQB where AQB subtends the MAJOR arc AB.
(Hint: the major arc central angle = 360° − 110° = 250°)
AQB = 250°/2 = 125°

Retrieval Check — Close Your Notes
  1. State the inscribed angle theorem in your own words.
    Answer An inscribed angle is half the central angle that subtends the same arc.
  2. A central angle is 144°. What is the inscribed angle subtending the same arc?
    Answer 72°
  3. An inscribed angle is 23°. What is the central angle subtending the same arc?
    Answer 46°
  4. Why do all inscribed angles subtending the same arc equal each other?
    Answer All inscribed angles subtending the same arc equal half the same central angle — they all measure the same value regardless of where on the circle the vertex sits.
  5. (Stretch) Two inscribed angles APB and CPD are in the same circle but subtend different arcs. Angle APB = 40° and arc AB has central angle 80°. Arc CD has central angle 130°. Find angle CPD.
    Answer Major arc CD central = 130°. CPD = 130°/2 = 65°

Section 4 — Tangent-Radius Property

Learning target: I can apply the tangent-radius perpendicularity property and use it with the Pythagorean theorem to solve problems.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
TangentLine touching circle at exactly one pointLine PT where T is on circle
Point of tangencySingle point where tangent meets circleT in diagram
PerpendicularAt 90°Radius OT ⊥ tangent at T
Tangent from external pointSegment from point outside circle to point of tangencySegment PT

Guided Notes

Tangent-Radius Property:

A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.

Angle OTP = 90°

This means: if you know a line is tangent to a circle, you automatically know the angle at the point of tangency is 90°.

Applying the Pythagorean theorem:

Since the radius and tangent are perpendicular, they form a right triangle with any line from the external point to the centre.

Worked Example:

A tangent PT touches a circle with centre O and radius 5 cm. The external point P is 13 cm from the centre. Find the length of the tangent PT.

Right angle is at T (tangent ⊥ radius)

Hypotenuse = PO = 13 cm

PT2 = PO2 − OT2

PT2 = 132 − 52

PT2 = 169 − 25 = 144

PT = 12 cm

Explain: Why is PO the hypotenuse and not PT or OT?

PO is opposite the right angle at T, so it is the longest side

Equal tangents from an external point:

From any external point P, two tangents can be drawn to a circle. The two tangent segments (from P to each point of tangency) are equal in length.

PT₁ = PT₂

Alberta context:
A circular fountain in a Calgary park has radius 3 m. A park bench sits 5 m from the fountain's centre. Two straight paths run from the bench, each touching the fountain at exactly one point. How long is each path from the bench to its point of tangency?

(Hint: sketch the bench as an external point, draw the radius to the point of tangency, then use the tangent-radius property.)

Retrieval Check — Close Your Notes
  1. State the tangent-radius property.
    Answer A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency (90° angle is formed).
  2. A tangent is drawn to a circle of radius 6 cm. The external point is 10 cm from the centre. Find the tangent length.
    Answer PT = √(100−36) = √64 = 8 cm
  3. From an external point P, two tangents are drawn to a circle. One tangent segment is 8 cm. What is the length of the other?
    Answer 8 cm (equal tangents from same external point)
  4. Why does the tangent-radius property create a right triangle? How is this useful for problem solving?
    Answer Perpendicularity (90° at T) is the definition of a right triangle. The centre, the point of tangency, and the external point form a right triangle with OP as hypotenuse.
  5. (Stretch) A circle has centre O and radius 7 cm. A tangent at point T meets an external line at P where OP = 25 cm. Find PT. Then find the angle OPT to the nearest degree (use trigonometry if you know it, or describe what you'd need to find it).
    Answer PT = √(625−49) = √576 = 24 cm. Angle OPT: tan(OPT)=7/24 → arctan(7/24) ≈ 16°
