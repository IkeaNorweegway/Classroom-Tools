Guided Notes

Four ways to see the same idea:

A power is shorthand for repeated multiplication of the same factor.

Verbal Symbolic Expanded (numerical) Value
"3 multiplied by itself 4 times" 3⁴ 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 81
"5 multiplied by itself 2 times" 5² 5 × 5 25
"2 multiplied by itself 6 times" 2⁶ 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 64
"4 multiplied by itself 3 times" 4³ 4 × 4 × 4 64

Notice: the base is the factor being repeated. The exponent is the count of how many times the base appears as a factor.

Integer bases — this is where it gets interesting.

A base can be any integer: positive, negative, or zero (except we will see later that zero as a base causes problems).

Worked Example 1 — Positive base:

Evaluate 2⁵.

2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32

Explain Step 1: Why do we write five 2s, and not 2 fives?

Worked Example 2 — Negative base:

Evaluate (−3)⁴.

(−3)⁴ = (−3) × (−3) × (−3) × (−3)

Step 1: (−3) × (−3) = 9  (negative × negative = positive)

Step 2: 9 × (−3) = −27  (positive × negative = negative)

Step 3: −27 × (−3) = 81  (negative × negative = positive)

(−3)⁴ = 81

Explain Step 2: Without calculating anything, predict whether (−3)⁵ will be positive or negative. What rule explains this?

Misconception

−3⁴ is not the same as (−3)⁴

Wrong idea: "−3⁴ and (−3)⁴ both equal +81 because a negative times a negative is positive."

Why it fails: The brackets decide what the base is.

  • In (−3)⁴, the base is −3. We multiply (−3) by itself 4 times. Result: +81.
  • In −3⁴, the base is 3 only. The exponent applies first (order of operations), then the negative sign is applied. Result: −(3⁴) = −81.

Test it: What is −2³? What is (−2)³? Are they the same or different?

−2³ = −8      (−2)³ = −8

Section 2 · The Zero Exponent

Learning target: I can explain why any nonzero base raised to the power of zero equals 1, using a pattern — not just as a memorized rule.

Guided Notes

Don't memorize a rule — discover it.

The value of a⁰ is not arbitrary. It follows directly from a pattern. Complete this table for base 3:

PowerExpanded formValueHow to get to the next row

What do you get each time you divide by 3? What does 3¹ ÷ 3 equal?

Now repeat with base 5:

PowerValueHow to get the next row
5³125Divide by 5
5²25Divide by 5
5¹5Divide by 5
5⁰1—

5⁰ = 1

The pattern holds for any nonzero base: a⁰ = 1 for any a ≠ 0.

In your own words: Explain why a⁰ = 1 using the pattern above. Don't say "because the rule says so" — explain the logic of the pattern.

Misconception

a⁰ is not zero

Wrong idea: "Anything to the power of zero is zero, because you're multiplying nothing."

Why it fails: An exponent does not mean "multiply by zero" — it tells you how many times to use the base as a factor. The pattern above shows that each time you decrease the exponent by 1, you divide by the base. Starting from a¹ = a and dividing by a gives a ÷ a = 1, not 0.

Verify: What is 10² ÷ 10? What is 10¹ ÷ 10? What does that make 10⁰?

10² ÷ 10 = 10     10¹ ÷ 10 = 1     So 10⁰ = 1

One important exception: What about 0⁰?

The pattern breaks here. If we apply our rule, we would say 0⁰ = 1. But 0¹ ÷ 0 is division by zero, which is undefined. For this course, we will say: 0⁰ is undefined. The rule a⁰ = 1 only applies when a ≠ 0.

Section 3 · Product of Powers

Learning target: I can apply the product of powers rule to simplify expressions, explain why the rule works using expanded form, and recognize when it does and does not apply.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Product of powers ruleWhen multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents: am × an = am+n23 × 24 = 27
Like basesPowers sharing the same base32, 35 have like bases

Guided Notes

Discover the rule first — don't memorize the formula.

Expand each product and count the total factors. The first row is done for you.

ExpressionExpandedTotal factorsSimplified power
2³ × 2⁴(2×2×2) × (2×2×2×2)7 twos2⁷
3² × 3⁵(3×3) × (3×3×3×3×3)7 threes3⁷
5⁴ × 5¹(5×5×5×5) × 55 fives5⁵
a³ × a⁶(a×a×a) × (a×a×a×a×a×a)9 a’sa⁹

What pattern do you see? When you multiply powers with the same base, what happens to the exponents?

Write the rule in words: "When multiplying powers with the same base, keep the base and add the exponents."

Write the rule in symbols: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ

Worked Example — Applying the rule:

Simplify: (−4)³ × (−4)⁵

Step 1: Check the bases. Are they the same? Yes — both are (−4)

Step 2: Apply the product rule: (−4)³ × (−4)⁵ = (−4)(3 + 5) = (−4)(−4)⁸

Step 3: Evaluate if asked: (−4)⁸ = 65 536  (positive or negative? positive because the exponent is even)

Explain Step 2: Why do we add the exponents instead of multiplying them?

Multiple terms:

Simplify: x⁴ × x² × x³

The rule works for more than two factors. Add all the exponents: x(4 + 2 + 3) = x⁹

Misconception

The product rule only applies to the same base

Wrong idea: 2³ × 3⁴ = 6⁷

Why it fails: Expand it: 2³ × 3⁴ = (2×2×2) × (3×3×3×3). You cannot combine these into a single base because the factors are different numbers. 6⁷ would mean 6×6×6×6×6×6×6 — that is a completely different number.

Verify: Evaluate 2³ × 3⁴ (calculator allowed). Now evaluate 6⁷. Are they equal? No: 2³ × 3⁴ = 648, but 6⁷ = 279 936

Connection: The product of powers rule is not a new rule imposed from outside — it is a consequence of what an exponent means. If you forget the rule, you can always go back to expanded form and count. You should not need to always do this, but you should always know why the rule works.

When does this NOT apply?

Put a ✓ if the product rule applies, and a ✗ if it does not. If it does not apply, write why.

Expression✓ or ✗If ✗: Why?
5⁴ × 5³✓—
3² × 4²✗Different bases (3 and 4), so you cannot add exponents
m⁵ × m✓— (m = m¹, so m⁶)
2x³ × 3x²✓ (for x)Multiply the coefficients, add the exponents: 6x⁵
a⁴b² × a³b✓Add exponents for each base: a⁷b³

(Hint on the last two: treat each base separately.)

Discover the rule by cancelling.

ExpressionExpanded formAfter cancellingResult
3⁵ ÷ 3²(3×3×3×3×3) ÷ (3×3)Three 3s remain3³

What pattern do you see? When you divide powers with the same base, what happens to the exponents?

Write the rule in words: When dividing powers with the same base, keep the base and subtract the exponents

Write the rule in symbols: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ  (where a ≠ 0 and m > n)

Worked Example:

Simplify: x⁹ ÷ x⁴

x⁹ ÷ x⁴ = x(9 − 4) = x⁵

Explain it: The cancellation model says we started with 9 factors of x and cancelled 4 of them. How many are left?

Connection to Section 2 — What if the exponents are equal?

Use the quotient rule: a⁵ ÷ a⁵ = a(5 − 5) = a⁰

But we also know that any number divided by itself = 1.

Therefore: a⁰ = 1

This connects Section 2 back to the quotient rule — the zero exponent is not a separate rule, it is a consequence of division.

What about negative exponents? (Preview)

What if the exponent on top is smaller than the exponent on the bottom?

3² ÷ 3⁵ = 3(2−5) = 3⁻³

Expand and cancel to see what this means:

3² ÷ 3⁵ = (3×3) / (3×3×3×3×3) = 1 / (3×3×3) = 1/3³

So 3⁻³ = 1 / 27. A negative exponent means the power moves to the denominator of a fraction.

(We will develop this fully in Section 7.)

Misconception

Product rule vs. Power of a Power rule

Wrong idea: 2³ × 2⁴ = 2¹² because "you multiply exponents when powers are involved."

Why it fails: These are different operations.

  • Multiplying two powers (same base): 2³ × 2⁴ → add the exponents → 2⁷
  • Raising a power to a power: (2³)⁴ → multiply the exponents → 2¹²

The operation that connects the two powers tells you what to do with the exponents.

Section 6 · Power of a Product and Power of a Quotient

Learning target: I can distribute an exponent across a product or quotient and explain why this works, but does not work for sums.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Power of a product ruleExponent distributes to each factor in a product: (ab)n = anbn(2x)3 = 8x3
Power of a quotient ruleExponent distributes to numerator and denominator: (a/b)n = an/bn(x/3)2 = x2/9

Guided Notes

Power of a product — discover by expanding:

ExpressionExpandedRegroupedResult
(2 × 3)²(2×3) × (2×3)(2×2) × (3×3)2² × 3² = 36
(ab)³(ab)(ab)(ab)(a×a×a) × (b×b×b)a³ × b³
(2x)⁴(2x)(2x)(2x)(2x)(2×2×2×2) × (x×x×x×x)16x⁴
(3mn)²(3mn)(3mn)(3×3)(m×m)(n×n)9m²n²

Write the rule: (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ

Power of a quotient — same logic:

(a/b)³ = (a/b) × (a/b) × (a/b) = (a×a×a) / (b×b×b) = a³ / b³

Write the rule: (a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ / bⁿ

Worked Example — Combining rules:

Simplify: (3x²)⁴

Step 1: Distribute the exponent to both factors: 3⁴ × (x²)⁴

Step 2: Evaluate 3⁴ = 81

Step 3: Apply power of a power: (x²)⁴ = x⁸

Result: 81x⁸

Explain Step 1: Why does the exponent apply to the 3 as well as the x²? Many students forget to apply the exponent to the coefficient. Explain why it must be applied to both.

Misconception

The power does NOT distribute over addition

Wrong idea: (a + b)² = a² + b²

Why it fails: Expand it: (a + b)² = (a + b)(a + b) = a² + 2ab + b². The middle term 2ab appears because both factors multiply each other. Distributing an exponent only works when the factors inside are connected by multiplication or division, not addition or subtraction.

Verify with numbers: Let a = 3 and b = 4.

  • (a + b)² = (3 + 4)² = 7² = 49
  • a² + b² = 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25

Are they equal? No — 49 ≠ 25 — This proves the distribution over addition is wrong.

Explain: Why does a negative exponent not make the answer negative?

Misconception

A negative exponent does not mean a negative number

Wrong idea: "2⁻³ = −8 because the negative makes it negative."

Why it fails: The negative exponent is an instruction about position, not sign. It means "move to the denominator and make the exponent positive." 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8, which is a small positive fraction, not a negative number.

Can a power with a negative exponent ever be negative? Yes — but only if the base is negative. (−2)⁻³ = 1/(−2)³ = 1/(−8) = −1/8.

  • Model explanations (any two):
  • Product rule: Expanding shows 3 twos × 4 twos = 7 twos total. Adding exponents counts the combined factors correctly.
  • Zero exponent: Each step down divides by the base. Starting at a1 = a, dividing by a gives a/a = 1. The pattern must give 1, not 0.
  • Negative exponent: Quotient rule applied to a smaller top: a2 ÷ a5 = a−3. Expanding gives 1/a3. So a−n = 1/an — an instruction to invert, not to negate.
  • Power of product vs. sum: Multiplication lets us regroup factors freely; (ab)2 = a2b2 by regrouping. Addition cannot be regrouped the same way — (a+b)2 must be expanded as (a+b)(a+b), producing the cross-term 2ab.
