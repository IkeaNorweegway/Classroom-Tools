Section 1 — Patterns and the Four Representations

Learning target: I can represent a linear pattern using a table of values, an equation, a graph, and a verbal description, and translate between all four forms.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Linear relationA relationship where equal changes in x produce equal changes in y; graphs as a straight liney = 3x + 2
Table of valuesA chart listing specific (x, y) pairsx: 0,1,2 and y: 2,5,8
Independent variableThe variable you choose/control; the input; usually xTime, number of items
Dependent variableThe variable that depends on the input; the output; usually yCost, distance
Equation (of a relation)
Rule of FourFour equivalent ways to represent the same relation: verbal, table, equation, graphAll four show the same relationship

Guided Notes

A linear relation is a relationship between two variables where equal changes in one variable produce equal changes in the other.

The Rule of Four — four ways to represent the same relationship:

FormDescriptionWhat it shows
VerbalA sentence describing the ruleThe meaning — what's happening in context
TablePairs of (x, y) valuesSpecific values and the pattern
EquationAn algebraic rule (e.g., y = 3x + 2)The general rule for any value
GraphPoints/line on coordinate axesThe visual shape and direction

They all describe one rule: start at 10 cm and add 4 cm every year (rate of change 4, starting value 10).

Section 2 — Slope as Rate of Change

Learning target: I can calculate slope using the rise/run formula, interpret slope as a rate of change, and distinguish between positive, negative, zero, and undefined slopes.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
SlopeMeasure of steepness and direction of a line; ratio of rise to runm = 3 means 3 units up per 1 unit right
RiseVertical change; change in y (y₂ − y₁)If y goes from 2 to 8, rise = 6
RunHorizontal change; change in x (x₂ − x₁)If x goes from 1 to 4, run = 3
Rate of changeSlope applied to a real-world context; how much y changes per unit of x100 km/h means 100 km per hour
Positive slopeLine rises from left to rightm = 2
Negative slopeLine falls from left to rightm = −3
Zero slopeHorizontal line; y doesn't changey = 5
Undefined slopeVertical line; x doesn't change (division by zero)x = 3

Guided Notes

Slope measures how steep a line is and in what direction.

slope = rise/run = change in y / change in x

Using two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

Worked Example 1 — From a graph:

A line passes through (1, 2) and (4, 8).

Rise = y₂ − y₁ = 8 − 2 = 6

Run = x₂ − x₁ = 4 − 1 = 3

Slope = 6 / 3 = 2

Explain: The slope of 2 means "for every 1 unit right, the line goes 2 units up." How would you see this on the graph?

From any point on the line, move 1 right and 2 up and you land on the line again.

Worked Example 2 — Negative slope:

A line passes through (0, 6) and (3, 0).

m = 0 − 6/3 − 0 = −6 / 3 = −2

Explain: A negative slope means the line is going down (it falls from left to right). What does −2 mean in a context like "the temperature drops 2°C per hour"?

The temperature falls 2°C for every hour that passes.
Misconception

Wrong idea: "If I use different points on the line, I'll get a different slope."

Why it fails: For a straight line, slope is constant — every pair of points on the same line gives the same rise/run ratio. This is the defining property of a line. If the slope changes depending on which points you pick, the relationship is NOT linear.

Check: A line passes through (0, 1), (2, 5), and (4, 9). Find the slope using (0,1) and (2,5): 4/2 = 2. Now find the slope using (2,5) and (4,9): 4/2 = 2. Same? Yes

The four slope types:

Slope typeWhat the line doesValue
PositiveRises from left to rightm > 0
NegativeFalls from left to rightm < 0
ZeroHorizontal (flat)m = 0
UndefinedVerticalm = undefined

Match to a context:

  • "Temperature stays at 0°C all day" → slope = 0
  • "A wall is perfectly vertical" → slope = undefined
  • "A ski hill drops 3 m per 10 m across" → slope = −3/10 = −0.3

Slope as rate of change:

When slope is applied to a real-world relation, the units matter.

A car travels 300 km in 3 hours.

m = 300 km / 3 h = 100 km/h

The slope tells you: for each hour (run), you travel 100 km (rise).

Alberta context:
A highway in the Rockies descends 120 m over a horizontal distance of 800 m. What is the slope? What does the negative sign mean in this context?

m = −120 / 800 = −0.15    The slope means the road drops 0.15 m for every 1 m across (it goes downhill)

Retrieval Check — Section 2 — Close Your Notes

  1. Calculate the slope of the line through (−2, 3) and (4, −9).

    Answer m = −9−3/4−(−2) = −12/6 = −2

  2. A table of values has x: 0, 1, 2, 3 and y: 4, 7, 10, 13. What is the slope?

    Answer Constant difference = 3. m = 3

  3. A line has zero slope. What does it look like? What context could it describe?

    Answer Horizontal line (y doesn't change). Context: constant temperature, flat road.

  4. A student calculates slope as "rise over run" and gets 3/−2. Another student uses the same two points and gets −3/2. Who is right?

    Answer Both are right — they are equal. Slope sign is in the result, not the numerator alone.

  5. (Stretch) A line passes through (a, 0) and (0, b) where a ≠ 0 and b ≠ 0. Write a formula for the slope in terms of a and b.

    Answer m = b−0/0−a = −b/a

Connection Prompt

Slope is a ratio. You have seen ratios before in other contexts — unit rates, scaling, fractions. How is slope the same kind of thinking as a unit rate? Where is it different?

Both tell how much one quantity changes for each 1 unit of another. A slope can be negative or zero, and it describes a whole line, not just one price.

Section 3 — The Equation y = mx + b

Learning target: I can identify the slope and y-intercept from an equation in slope-intercept form, write an equation given the slope and y-intercept, and interpret both values in context.

Guided Notes

Slope-intercept form: y = mx + b

PartSymbolWhat it isOn the graph
SlopemRate of changeSteepness and direction

Reading the equation directly:

y = 3x − 4

Slope m = 3    y-intercept b = −4

Starting point (0, b) = (0, −4)

For each x that increases by 1, y changes by +3

Worked Example — Write the equation from context:

A Banff hotel charges $90 per night plus a one-time parking fee of $25.

What is the starting value (when nights = 0)? $25

What is the rate of change (cost per night)? $90

Equation: C = 90 n + 25

Graph this equation:

Plot the y-intercept at (0, 25) and use the slope of 90 to find a second point.

Second point: (1, 115)    Draw the line.

Misconception

Wrong idea: "The intercept is where the line hits zero — that's the x-axis."

Why it fails: "Intercept" means "crosses." The y-intercept crosses the y-axis (where x = 0). The x-intercept crosses the x-axis (where y = 0). In y = 3x − 4, the y-intercept is −4 (at the point (0, −4)), and the x-intercept is found by setting y = 0: 0 = 3x − 4, x = 4/3.

Find both intercepts of y = 2x − 6:

y-intercept (set x = 0): y = −6 → point: (0, −6)

x-intercept (set y = 0): x = 3 → point: (3, 0)

Finding b when given slope and a point:

Slope m = 2, passes through (3, 7). Find b.

y = mx + b

7 = 2(3) + b

7 = 6 + b

b = 1

Equation: y = 2x + 1

Explain: Why can we substitute the point's coordinates (3, 7) for (x, y) in this step?

(3, 7) is on the line, so its x and y values must make the equation true.

Retrieval Check — Section 3 — Close Your Notes

  1. Identify the slope and y-intercept of y = −3x + 7.

    Answer m = −3, b = 7

  2. Write the equation of a line with slope 4 and y-intercept −2.

    Answer y = 4x − 2

  3. A line passes through (0, 5) and (2, 11). Write its equation.

    Answer m = 11−5/2−0 = 3. y = 3x + 5

  4. What does the y-intercept represent in the hotel context (cost = 90n + 25)?

    Answer The y-intercept ($25) is the one-time parking fee — the cost before any nights are stayed.

  5. (Stretch) Two lines: y = 3x + 2 and y = 3x − 5. Without graphing, how are these lines similar? How are they different? What does sharing the same slope mean geometrically?

    Answer Same slope → parallel (never intersect). Different y-intercepts → different starting heights. Two lines with the same slope are always parallel.

Section 4 — Direct and Partial Variation

Learning target: I can distinguish between direct and partial variation, recognize each from a table, graph, or equation, and connect them to real-world contexts.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Direct variationy = kx; y is proportional to x; graph through originFuel cost: C = 1.50d
Partial variationy = kx + b where b ≠ 0; graph does NOT pass through originTaxi: C = 2.50 + 1.80d
Constant of variationThe slope k in y = kxk = 1.50 in C = 1.50d
Initial valueThe y-intercept b in partial variation$2.50 base fare

Guided Notes

Direct variation: y varies directly with x.

  • The equation has the form y = kx (no constant added)
  • The graph passes through the origin (0, 0)
  • The ratio y/x is constant for all values

Partial variation: y varies partially with x.

  • The equation has the form y = mx + b where b ≠ 0
  • The graph crosses the y-axis at b (NOT the origin)
  • The ratio y/x is NOT constant — but the differences in y are

Side-by-side comparison:

FeatureDirect variationPartial variation
Equationy = kxy = kx + b, b ≠ 0
Graph passes throughthe origin (0, 0)(0, b), not the origin
Example contextCost = price × quantityCost = monthly fee + price × quantity

How to tell: Look at the y-value when x = 0. If y = 0: direct variation. If y ≠ 0: partial variation.

Alberta context — Two phone plans:

Plan A: $0.15 per text, no monthly fee.
Plan B: $10/month + $0.05 per text.

Plan A equation: C = 0.15t
Plan B equation: C = 0.05t + 10

For 50 texts: Plan A costs $7.50    Plan B costs $12.50
For 200 texts: Plan A costs $30    Plan B costs $20

At what number of texts do the plans cost the same? (Set the two equations equal and solve):

0.10t = 10 → t = 100 texts
Misconception

Wrong idea: "If both x and y increase, the relation is direct variation."

Why it fails: Partial variation also has both variables increasing. Direct variation specifically requires the graph to pass through the origin (0, 0) — meaning when x = 0, y = 0. The phone plan with a monthly fee is NOT direct variation, even though cost rises with usage.

Check: Table A above — when x = 0, y = 0. Does it pass through the origin? Yes, so it is direct variation

Retrieval Check — Section 4 — Close Your Notes

  1. Is y = 4x direct or partial variation? What about y = 4x + 3?

    Answer y=4x: direct. y=4x+3: partial (b=3≠0)

  2. A taxi charges $2.50 base fare plus $1.80 per km. Write the equation. Is this direct or partial variation?

    Answer Equation: C = 1.8d + 2.5. Partial variation (base fare b≠0)

  3. From the table, identify the type and write the equation:

    x0246
    y6101418
    Answer y=6 at x=0 → b=6. Slope=2. y=2x+6. Partial variation.

  4. Why does direct variation always pass through the origin?

    Answer y=kx: when x=0, y=k×0=0 always. The origin is always on the graph by definition.

  5. (Stretch) Two situations both have the same slope (rate of change). One is direct variation and one is partial. Describe what is the same and different about their graphs.

    Answer Same steepness (slope = same). Different y-intercepts → one passes through origin, other doesn't. Parallel lines at different heights.

Answer Verbal (meaning in words), Table (specific values), Equation (general algebraic rule), Graph (visual shape and direction)

Answer m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
