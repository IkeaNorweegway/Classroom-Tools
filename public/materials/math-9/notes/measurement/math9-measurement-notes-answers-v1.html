Section 1 — Review: 2D Area Formulas

Learning target: I can recall and apply area formulas for rectangles, triangles, parallelograms, and circles — these are the building blocks of all 3D surface area calculations.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
AreaThe amount of 2D space inside a shapeA = l × w for a rectangle
PerimeterThe total distance around the outside of a shapeP = 2l + 2w
Composite shapeA shape made of two or more simpler shapesRectangle + semicircle
π (pi)The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter≈ 3.14159…

Guided Notes

All surface area formulas are built from 2D shapes. You must know these.

ShapeFormulaVariables
RectangleA = l × wl = length, w = width
TriangleA = (b × h) / 2b = base, h = perpendicular height
ParallelogramA = b × hh = perpendicular height
CircleA = π × r2r = radius
TrapezoidA = a + b/2 × ha, b = parallel sides

π = 3.14 (to 2 decimal places)

Worked Example — Composite 2D shape:

A shape made of a rectangle (4 cm × 6 cm) with a semicircle on top (diameter 4 cm).

Rectangle area: 4 × 6 = 24 cm2

Semicircle area: (1/2) × π × r2 = (1/2) × π × 22 = 2π ≈ 6.28 cm2

Total area: 24 + 6.28 ≈ 30.28 cm2

Explain: Why do we use (1/2) × πr2 for the semicircle rather than the full formula?

The shape on top is only half a circle, so we take half of the full circle area πr².

Perpendicular height reminder:

The height in area formulas is always the perpendicular (straight, at 90°) distance from the base to the opposite vertex (or side). It is NOT the slant side.

Section 2 — Surface Area of Right Prisms and Cylinders

Learning target: I can find the surface area of right prisms and cylinders by identifying and summing all faces.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Surface areaTotal area of all outer faces of a 3D solidSA of a cube side 2 = 24 cm2
Right prismPrism where the lateral edges are perpendicular to the baseCereal box
CylinderCircular prism — two circular bases + curved lateral surfaceSoup can
Net2D unfolding of a 3D shape showing all facesCross-shaped net of a cube
Lateral surfaceThe non-base face(s) of a prism or cylinderThe curved side of a can
Rectangular prismPrism with rectangular (including square) basesBox

Guided Notes

Surface area = total area of all outer faces.

Strategy: draw the net (unfold the 3D shape into 2D).

Right Prism — general formula:

SA = 2 × (area of base) + height × (perimeter of base)

The lateral surface area = perimeter of the base × height of prism

(Think: unroll the sides into a rectangle with width = height and length = perimeter of base)

Worked Example 1 — Rectangular prism:

Dimensions: l = 5 cm, w = 3 cm, h = 4 cm

Net: three pairs of faces

Two l × w faces: 2 × (5 × 3) = 30 cm2

Two l × h faces: 2 × (5 × 4) = 40 cm2

Two w × h faces: 2 × (3 × 4) = 24 cm2

SA = 30 + 40 + 24 = 94 cm2

Explain: Why does a rectangular prism have 6 faces? Why do they come in pairs?

Every face has an identical face opposite it: top and bottom, front and back, left and right.

Worked Example 2 — Triangular prism:

Equilateral triangle base (side 6 cm, height 5.2 cm), prism height = 10 cm.

Area of one triangular base = 6 × 5.2/2 = 15.6 cm 2 cm2

Two bases: 2 × 15.6 = 31.2 cm2

Perimeter of base = 6 + 6 + 6 = 18 cm

Lateral area = perimeter × height = 18 × 10 = 180 cm2

SA = 31.2 + 180 = 211.2 cm2

Cylinder — surface area:

A cylinder has: two bases (circles) + one curved (lateral) surface (rectangle when unrolled)

SA = 2πr2 + 2πrh

  • 2πr2 = area of the two circular faces
  • 2πrh = lateral area (circumference × height)

Worked Example 3 — Cylinder:

r = 4 cm, h = 9 cm

SA = 2π(4)2 + 2π(4)(9)

= 2π(16) + 2π(36)

= 32 π + 72 π

= 104 π ≈ 326.73 cm2

Explain the lateral area step: Why does 2πrh give the area of the curved surface? What shape does the curved surface become when you unroll it?

It unrolls into a rectangle. Its length is the circumference 2πr and its width is the height h, so its area is 2πr × h.
Misconception

Surface area and volume are the same measurement

Wrong idea: "A bigger box has more surface area AND more volume, so they're basically the same thing."

Why it fails: Surface area measures the total outer area in cm2 (or m2). Volume measures the space inside in cm3 (or m3). A flat sheet of metal can have a huge surface area but almost zero volume. A thick cube can have the same surface area as a flat box but much more volume. They measure fundamentally different things.

Verify: A cube with side 2 cm. SA = 6 × 4 = 24 cm2. Volume = 23 = 8 cm3. Different units, different values.

Practise:

  1. A rectangular prism: 8 cm × 3 cm × 5 cm. Find SA.
  2. A cylinder: r = 2.5 m, h = 6 m. Find SA (leave in terms of π, then evaluate).

Section 3 — Volume of Right Prisms and Cylinders

Learning target: I can calculate the volume of right prisms and cylinders using the base area × height formula.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
VolumeAmount of 3D space inside a solidV=lwh for a rectangular prism
Cubic unitsVolume units (cm3, m3)1 cm3 = 1 mL
Base area (B)Area of the base shape of a prism or cylinderFor cylinder: B=πr2

Guided Notes

Volume of any right prism or cylinder:

V = B × h

where B = area of the base and h = height of the solid

Worked Example 1 — Rectangular prism:

l = 6 m, w = 4 m, h = 3 m

B = l × w = 6 × 4 = 24 m2

V = B × h = 24 × 3 = 72 m3

Worked Example 2 — Cylinder:

r = 5 cm, h = 12 cm

B = πr2 = π(5)2 = 25 π cm2

V = B × h = 25 π × 12 = 300 π ≈ 942.48 cm3

Explain: Both a cylinder and a rectangular prism use V = Bh. What makes a cylinder different is the shape of B. What is B for each?

Rectangular prism: B = l × w

Cylinder: B = πr 2

Triangular prism:

Base is a right triangle with legs 6 cm and 8 cm. Prism height = 10 cm.

B = base × height/2 = 6 × 8/2 = 24 cm2

V = 24 × 10 = 240 cm3

Comparing volume and surface area units:

MeasurementUnitsExample
Surface areacm2 (square)Amount of wrapping paper
Volumecm3 (cubic)Amount of liquid it holds

Alberta context — Oil tank:
A cylindrical oil storage tank near Fort McMurray has radius 8 m and height 15 m.

Volume of oil it can hold: V = π × 8² = 64 × 15 = 960 π ≈ 3015.93 m3

Note: 1 m3 = 1000 L, so the tank holds approximately 3 016 000 (about 3 million) L.

Retrieval Check — Close Your Notes
  1. State the general formula for the volume of any prism.
    Answer V = B × h (base area × height)
  2. A cylinder has r = 3 m and h = 10 m. Find the volume. Leave in terms of π and then evaluate.
    Answer V=π(9)(10)=90π ≈ 282.74 m3
  3. A right prism has a trapezoidal base with parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, height of trapezoid 4 cm, and prism height 8 cm. Find the volume.
    Answer B=6+10/2×4=32 cm2. V=32×8=256 cm3
  4. A container holds 500 cm3 of water. If its base area is 25 cm2, how tall is the container?
    Answer h=500/25=20 cm
  5. (Stretch) Two cylinders: Cylinder A has r = 4 cm, h = 6 cm. Cylinder B has r = 6 cm, h = 4 cm. Which has greater volume? Which has greater surface area? Show working and explain.
    Answer VA=96π; VB=144π → B has greater volume. SA_A=80π; SA_B=120π → B also has greater SA.

Section 4 — Surface Area and Volume of Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres

Learning target: I can apply the formulas for pyramids, cones, and spheres, and explain why pyramid/cone volume is one-third of the corresponding prism/cylinder.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Pyramid3D solid with polygon base and triangular faces meeting at apexEgyptian pyramid
ApexThe top point of a pyramid or coneTip of a cone
Slant heightDistance from apex to midpoint of base edge along a facel in cone formula
ConePyramid with circular baseIce cream cone
SphereAll points equidistant from a centre in 3DBall

Guided Notes

Pyramid — Surface Area:

SA = area of base + area of four triangular faces

For a square-based pyramid (base side s, slant height l):
SA = s2 + 4 × (1/2 × s × l) = s2 + 2sl

Pyramid — Volume:

V = (1/3) × B × h

where B = base area, h = perpendicular height (NOT slant height)

Key insight: Volume of pyramid = 1/3 × volume of matching prism

A square pyramid with the same base and height as a square prism fits exactly 3 times inside the prism. This is not a coincidence — it is a geometric fact.

Similarly, a cone fits 3 times inside a cylinder with the same base and height.

Worked Example 1 — Square-based pyramid:

Base 6 cm × 6 cm, perpendicular height 4 cm, slant height 5 cm.

Base area = 6 × 6 = 36 cm 2 cm2

SA = 36 + 2(6)(5) = 36 + 60 = 36+60 = 96 cm 2 cm2

V = (1/3)(36)(4) = 48 cm 3 cm3

Explain: The slant height (5 cm) is used for surface area but NOT for volume. Why?

Volume needs the straight-up (perpendicular) height of the solid. The slant height runs along a face, so it is only used for the areas of the triangular faces.

Cone — Surface Area and Volume:

SA = πr2 + πrl

  • πr2 = area of the circular base
  • πrl = lateral surface area (l = slant height)

V = (1/3)πr2h

  • h = perpendicular height, NOT slant height

Finding slant height from perpendicular height:

l2 = r2 + h2 (Pythagorean theorem — l is the hypotenuse)

Worked Example 2 — Cone:

r = 3 cm, h = 4 cm.

Slant height: l = √(r2 + h2) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 cm cm

SA = π(3)2 + π(3)(5)
= 9π + 15 π = 24 π ≈ 24π ≈ 75.40 cm 2 cm2

V = (1/3)π(3)2(4) = (1/3)π(9)(4)
= 12 π ≈ 37.70 cm3

Sphere:

SA = 4πr2

V = (4/3)πr3

Worked Example 3 — Sphere:

r = 6 cm.

SA = 4π(6)2 = 4π(36) = 144 π ≈ 452.39 cm2

V = (4/3)π(6)3 = (4/3)π(216) = 288 π ≈ 904.78 cm3

Misconception

A cone has half the volume of a cylinder

Wrong idea: "The cone fits inside the cylinder — it must take up half the space."

Why it fails: A cone takes up exactly one-third of the matching cylinder's volume. You can demonstrate this by filling a cone and pouring into the cylinder: you need exactly 3 cone-fulls to fill the cylinder.

The factor (1/3) in the formula is not an estimate — it is precise.

Calculate: Cylinder with r = 3, h = 6. Volume = π(9)(6) = 54π. Cone with same r and h: V = (1/3)π(9)(6) = 18 π. Ratio: The cone is 1/3 of the cylinder (18π : 54π = 1 : 3)

Section 5 — Composite Objects

Learning target: I can find the surface area and volume of composite 3D objects by adding and subtracting the appropriate parts.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition in your own wordsOne example
Composite object3D solid built from two or more basic shapesCylinder + rectangular prism
Hidden faceA face inside the composite, not on the outer surfaceThe circle where sphere meets cylinder
Overlapping surfaceThe shared area at the joint of two shapesThe touching circle between two joined shapes

Guided Notes

Composite objects are built by combining or removing basic 3D shapes.

The key rule: When two shapes are joined, the faces at the joint are hidden — they are inside the object and not part of the outer surface.

Strategy for surface area:

  1. Identify each component shape
  2. Calculate the full SA of each
  3. Subtract the hidden faces (the joined faces, twice — once from each shape)

Strategy for volume:

  1. Calculate the volume of each component
  2. Add the volumes (or subtract if one is removed)

Worked Example:

A cylinder (r = 4 cm, h = 10 cm) sits on top of a rectangular prism (20 cm × 20 cm × 5 cm).

Volume:

V = Volume of prism + Volume of cylinder
= (20)(20)(5) + π(4)2(10)
= 2000 + 160 π
≈ 2502.65 cm3

Surface area:

The circular base of the cylinder sits on top of the rectangular prism — that circle is hidden on BOTH shapes.

Hidden area = area of one circle = π(4)2 = 16 π cm2

SA = SA of prism − circle on top + SA of cylinder − circle on bottom

SA of prism = 2(20×20) + 4(20×5) = 800 + 400 = 1200 cm2
  Subtract top circle: 1200 cm2 − 16 π ≈ 1149.73 cm2

SA of cylinder = 2π(4)2 + 2π(4)(10) = 32 π + 80 π = 112 π cm2
  Subtract bottom circle: 112 π − 16 π = 96 π cm2

Total SA ≈ 1149.73 + 301.59 (96π) ≈ 1451.33 cm2

Explain: Why do we subtract the hidden circle from BOTH the prism's surface area and the cylinder's surface area?

The circle is covered on top of the prism and is also the cylinder’s bottom face. Neither copy is on the outside, so each is removed once.

Common mistake — forgetting hidden faces:

A student calculates the SA of two boxes glued together by adding both full surface areas. What does this overcount?

The two glued faces (one from each box), which are hidden inside.

How much should be subtracted? 2 × the area of the glued face

Alberta context — Grain elevator:
An Alberta grain elevator is modelled as a rectangular prism (base 8 m × 8 m × 20 m) with a square pyramid on top (base 8 m × 8 m, slant height 5 m).

Volume:

V of prism = 8 × 8 × 20 = 1280 m3

V of pyramid = (1/3)(64)(h). First find perpendicular height: h = √(l2 − (s/2)2) = √(25 − 16) = 3 m

V of pyramid = (1/3)(64)(3) = 64 m3

Total V = 1280 + 64 = 1344 m3

Surface area:

The base of the pyramid and the top of the prism are hidden. Identify which faces to include and exclude, then calculate.

SA = Prism without its top: 64 + 4(8 × 20) = 704; pyramid without its base: 4 × (8 × 5 ÷ 2) = 80; total 784 m²
