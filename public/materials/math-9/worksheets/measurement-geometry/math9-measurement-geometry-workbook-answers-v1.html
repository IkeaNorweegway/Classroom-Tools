Q8★★ ExtendedError analysis

Surface area vs. volume

A student is packing a cylindrical tin (r = 4 cm, h = 10 cm) and says:

Student claim

""The amount of metal needed to make the tin and the amount it can hold are both about 251 square/cubic centimetres, so they're basically the same thing.""

(a) Calculate the surface area. Is it approximately 251 cm2?

SA = 2πr2 + 2πrh = 2π( ) + 2π( )( )

SA =   π +   π =   π ≈   cm2

(b) Calculate the volume. Is it approximately 251 cm3?

V = πr2h = π( )( ) =   π ≈   cm3

(c) What is the student wrong about? What is actually different between the two measurements?

Answer

Q8 ★★ — SA vs V error analysis

Cylinder r=4, h=10.

(a) SA:
SA = 2π(16)+2π(4)(10) = 32π+80π = 112π ≈ 351.9 cm2
Not approximately 251 — closer to 352.

(b) V:
V = π(16)(10) = 160π ≈ 502.7 cm3
Not approximately 251 — closer to 503.

(c) Error:
The student conflated two fundamentally different measurements. SA measures total outer area (cm2) — amount of metal needed. V measures interior capacity (cm3) — amount it holds. They have different units, different meanings, and different values. Even if the numbers appeared similar, they couldn't "be the same thing" — one is 2D, the other is 3D.
