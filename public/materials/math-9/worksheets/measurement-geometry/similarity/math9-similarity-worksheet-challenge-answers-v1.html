(b) A student uses the shadow method at 10 AM and measures the building shadow. Then comes back at 2 PM to measure the measuring stick's shadow. They set up the proportion anyway.

Why might their answer be wrong?

(c) A student writes the similarity statement △ABC ~ △FDE and sets up the proportion AB/FD = BC/DE.

Is this proportion correct given the similarity statement? Check what the statement says about correspondence.

Answer
(a) △ABC sides 3,4,5 and △DEF sides 6,8,9:
Third ratio: 5/9 ≈ 0.556 | Is it equal to 1/2 = 0.5? No
Error: Checked only two ratios. For SSS similarity, ALL three ratios must be equal.
Not similar — the third pair of sides (5 and 9) is not in proportion 1:2.
(b) Shadows measured at different times:
The sun's elevation angle changes significantly from 10 AM to 2 PM. The shadows at those times correspond to different sun angles, so the two triangles formed are NOT similar to each other. The proportion only works when both shadows are measured simultaneously (same sun angle → AA criterion applies).
(c) △ABC~△FDE — proportion AB/FD=BC/DE:
Statement △ABC~△FDE: A↔F, B↔D, C↔E.
Corresponding sides: AB↔FD and BC↔DE.
The proportion AB/FD=BC/DE is correct for this similarity statement. The student's proportion matches the correspondence.

2. Similar triangles in a diagram.

In the diagram, line DE is parallel to BC. AD = 5, AB = 15, DE = 4.

(a) Prove △ADE ~ △ABC using AA. State which angles are equal and why.

(b) Find the scale factor (from △ADE to △ABC).

(c) Find BC.

(d) Find DB and EC.

Answer

DE ∥ BC, AD=5, AB=15, DE=4

(a) Prove △ADE ~ △ABC:
Angle A is shared by both triangles (common angle).
Since DE∥BC: angle ADE = angle ABC (corresponding angles with parallel lines and transversal AB). Similarly, angle AED = angle ACB.
AA criterion: two pairs of equal angles.
(b) Scale factor from △ADE to △ABC:
AD/AB = 5/15 = 1/3
(c) BC:
DE/BC = 1/3 → 4/BC = 1/3 → BC = 12
(d) DB and EC:
DB = AB − AD = 15 − 5 = 10
EC: AE/AC = 1/3, so AE = AC/3 and EC = 2AC/3. Without AC given, EC cannot be calculated numerically. If BC=12, similar proportions apply but AC is not given.

6. Indirect measurement — two methods. A tree is growing in a park. You cannot measure its height directly.

Method A (shadow): The tree casts a 9 m shadow. A 1.8 m pole casts a 1.2 m shadow at the same time.

Method B (mirror): A mirror is placed 4.5 m from the tree's base. You stand 1.8 m from the mirror and your eye height is 1.7 m.

(a) Use Method A to find the tree height.

(b) Use Method B to find the tree height.

(c) Do both methods give the same answer? Should they? Explain what could cause small differences in practice.

Answer
(a) Method A (shadow):
Setup: h/9 = 1.8/1.2 = 3/2
Tree height = 13.5 m
(b) Method B (mirror):
Setup: h/4.5 = 1.7/1.8
Tree height ≈ 4.25 m
(c) Same answer?
⚠️ The two methods give different answers (13.5 m vs 4.25 m) — the worksheet measurements are hypothetical and were generated independently for each method, not for the same tree. In a real scenario, both methods should give the same answer (within measurement error). Small practical differences arise from: positioning the mirror not quite flat, eye height estimation, slight slopes in the ground, or parallax errors. The large difference here indicates the problem uses two separate, unconnected sets of measurements.

(a) Find the corresponding dimensions in △XYZ.

XY = cm    Corresponding height = cm

(b) Area of △PQR = (1/2)()() = cm²

Area of △XYZ = (1/2)()() = cm²

(c) Ratio of areas = / = = scale factor² = ² ✓

(d) Explain why areas scale by k² when lengths scale by k.

Answer
(a) Corresponding dimensions in △XYZ:
XY = 4 × 3 = 12 cm | Corresponding height = 3 × 3 = 9 cm
(b) Areas:
Area △PQR = (1/2)(4)(3) = 6 cm²
Area △XYZ = (1/2)(12)(9) = 54 cm²
(c) Area ratio:
54/6 = 9 = 3² = scale factor² ✓
(d) Why areas scale by k²:
Area = 1/2 × base × height. Both the base and the height scale by k (each is a linear measurement). So area = 1/2 × (k×base) × (k×height) = k² × (1/2 × base × height) = k² × original area. Linear dimensions → ×k. Area (2D) → ×k².

8. Open-ended. A model of the Banff Springs Hotel is built at scale 1 : 200.

(a) The real building is approximately 60 m tall. How tall is the model in cm?

(b) One room in the model is 1.2 cm × 0.8 cm. What are the real dimensions?

(c) The model uses a certain amount of material for its exterior walls. By what factor would you need to multiply the model's surface area to get the real building's surface area?

(d) If the real building has 10 000 windows, how many windows does the model have?

Reflection: Similar and congruent are often confused. Write a one-paragraph explanation that a younger student could understand — using a real-world example to show the difference.

Answer
(a) Model height:
60 m = 6000 cm. Model = 6000/200 = 30 cm
(b) Real room dimensions:
1.2 cm × 200 = 240 cm = 2.4 m
0.8 cm × 200 = 160 cm = 1.6 m
(c) Surface area scale factor:
Lengths scale by 200; areas scale by 200² = 40,000
To get real building SA: multiply model SA by 40,000
(d) Number of windows:
Windows are countable items, not lengths or areas — they do not scale by 200 or 200². The model has the same number of windows as the real building: 10,000. (Each window in the model represents one real window.)
