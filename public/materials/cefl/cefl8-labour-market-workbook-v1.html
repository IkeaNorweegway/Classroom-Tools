Moment 1The labour market keeps moving

The situation

In 2014, oil prices were high and Alberta had lots of jobs. Then oil prices fell. In November 2014, Alberta's unemployment rate was 4.4%. By November 2016, it was 9.0%. Thousands of people who had steady jobs had to find new work.

Worked example

Why did so many Albertans lose work between 2014 and 2016?

  1. Find the force. Oil prices dropped. That is an economic cycle (a bust after a boom).
  2. Trace the effect. Oil companies earned less, so they hired fewer people and cut projects.
  3. Follow the ripple. Fewer oil jobs meant less spending in towns, so stores, restaurants and trades lost work too.
  4. The lesson: the labour market is dynamic. It changes, and jobs that seem safe can change with it.

1. Which force is it? Write economic cycle, technology, or social change.
(a) More seniors means more need for home-care workers.
(b) Self-checkout machines mean stores need fewer cashiers.
(c) A recession means fewer houses are built.

2. The unemployment rate went from 4.4% to 9.0%. About how many times bigger is 9.0% than 4.4%? What does that tell you?

3. Explain why being able to adapt might be a better career plan than counting on one job forever.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Name one industry that does well when oil prices are high, and one that might grow when they are low.

E2. Name a social change happening now. What new jobs might it create?

Moment 2Technology: jobs lost, jobs gained

The situation

Farms now use GPS-guided tractors and drones. Fewer people drive tractors all day, but farms need people who can fix, program and fly the machines. Technology takes away some tasks and creates new ones.

Worked example

Is new technology good or bad for workers?

  1. Negative impact: some tasks disappear, so some workers lose jobs or hours.
  2. Positive impact: new tasks appear, often needing new skills, and work can get safer or faster.
  3. Who does well? Workers who learn the new skills can move into the growing tasks.
  4. So: technology is both. What matters is whether workers can adapt.

1. Pick a job you know (cashier, bank teller, truck driver, librarian). Name one task technology could take over, and one new task it could create.

2. List one positive and one negative impact of technology on the labour market.

3. A student says: "Robots will take all the jobs, so there's no point planning a career." Do you agree? Use the farm example.

Extra practice · optional

E1. What could a cashier do now to be ready if self-checkouts take over?

E2. Name a job that technology has made safer.

Moment 3Career resilience and human capital

The situation

Two oil workers lost their jobs in 2016. Dev had done one job for 15 years and never took training. Rosa had taken safety and project-management courses, kept in touch with people she'd worked with, and learned new software. Rosa found new work in 2 months. Dev took over a year.

Worked example

Why did Rosa find work so much faster than Dev?

  1. Job security vs. career security. Dev had job security: one role for a long time. When it ended, he had nothing to fall back on. Rosa had career security: the ability to find new work quickly.
  2. Human capital. Rosa kept adding to hers: new courses (education), new software (skills).
  3. Networking. People she'd worked with knew her and told her about openings.
  4. So: Rosa built career resilience on purpose, before she needed it.

1. Write job security or career security.
(a) "I've worked at the same plant for 20 years."
(b) "If I lost this job, I could find another in a few weeks."

2. Name two things a Grade 8 student can do now to build human capital.

3. Explain how networking helped Rosa. Who is in your network already?

Extra practice · optional

E1. Is it possible to have job security and career security at the same time? Explain.

E2. Rank these from most to least useful for career resilience, and give a reason for your top pick: a new certificate · a big network · good communication skills

Moment 4Goals with milestones

The situation

Amira wants to be a software developer. She writes one big goal and some smaller steps (milestones) to get there. She also adds a backup plan, in case her goal changes.

Worked example

Why does Amira's plan have milestones and a backup?

  1. Milestones break a long-term goal into short-term steps she can check off.
  2. A clear goal helps her choose courses and experiences now.
  3. A backup keeps her flexible. If her plans change, many of the same milestones still help.
  4. A goal that is too rigid (only one job, no backup) can add stress if the labour market changes.

1. Write short-term or long-term: (a) finish a first-aid course this spring (b) become a licensed electrician

2. Write one long-term career goal and two short-term milestones for it.

Watch out

3. A student says: "The safest plan is to pick one job and stay in it forever." Use what you learned in this workbook to explain what is risky about this idea.
