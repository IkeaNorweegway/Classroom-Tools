Keeping My Options Open

Four short teaching moments for the My High School Map project · Outcome 8-PS · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

Before you start

What high school courses do you think you'll take? Do you think the courses you pick in Grade 10 can change what you can do after Grade 12?

Moment 1The ticket in: admission requirements

The situation

Noor wants to study engineering at university. Her cousin Sam wants to be an electrician. Both pathways have admission requirements: courses and grades you need before you can start.

Worked example

Noor has a 76% average. The program's minimum is 75%. Is she in?

  1. Minimum means the lowest average they will look at.
  2. Engineering is competitive: more people apply than there are seats. The school takes the highest averages first.
  3. If most students admitted last year had averages above 85% (sample), 76% might not be enough.
  4. So: meeting the minimum does not guarantee a seat. Noor should plan for a higher average and a backup program.

1. Noor takes Math 30-2 instead of Math 30-1. Can she apply to engineering? Why or why not?

2. Why might a program get more applications than it has seats?

3. A student says: "If I get the minimum mark, I'm guaranteed to get in." Explain what is wrong with this idea.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Look up one real program on a school's website (NAIT, SAIT, a college, or a university). List its admission requirements.

E2. Why would an employer want an apprentice who took Math and Science, even if it's not required?

Moment 2Planning high school

The situation

To graduate with an Alberta High School Diploma, you need 100 credits and some required courses. Most courses are worth 3 or 5 credits. The courses you pick also decide which programs you can apply to later.

Worked example

Sam isn't sure yet between electrician and engineering technologist. How can he plan to keep both open?

  1. Find what both need. Both use math and science.
  2. Choose the stronger sequence when you can. Taking Math 20-1 (instead of 20-2) keeps Math 30-1 open, which more programs accept.
  3. Add a useful elective. A CTS electrical or construction course helps him find out if he likes the trade.
  4. Early planning means he won't discover in Grade 12 that he's missing a course.

1. How many credits do you need for an Alberta High School Diploma?

2. Why might taking the "-1" course (like Math 20-1 or ELA 30-1) keep more options open than the "-2" course?

3. Pick a career you're curious about. Name two high school courses that could help, and say why.

Extra practice · optional

E1. A 5-credit course and a 3-credit course: how many 5-credit courses make 100 credits?

E2. Who at a high school could help you plan your courses?

Moment 3Paying for it

The situation

Tuition is different for every program and school. One full year of 10 courses at the University of Alberta in 2026–27 costs about $7,294 in tuition. But there are ways to bring the cost down, and some money never has to be paid back.

Worked example

Noor earns the Alexander Rutherford Scholarship for her high school marks. How much does it lower her first-year tuition?

  1. The Rutherford Scholarship is an Alberta scholarship for Grade 10–12 marks, worth up to $2,500.
  2. First-year tuition at the University of Alberta (2026–27): 10 × $729.36 = $7,293.60.
  3. If Noor gets the full $2,500: $7,293.60 − $2,500 = $4,793.60 left to pay.
  4. Scholarships reward high school marks, so Grade 10–12 matters for money too.

Connects to Math: subtracting and comparing money amounts

1. Write scholarship, bursary, grant, or loan.
(a) Money for a student who can't afford school, from a local group.
(b) $1,000 for the student with the top marks in Biology 30.
(c) Money you must pay back after you finish school.

2. Tuition at one college program is $4,800 for a year. A student gets a $1,200 bursary and a $500 scholarship. How much tuition is left?

3. Why is it smart to start looking for scholarships before Grade 12?

Extra practice · optional

E1. Find one scholarship or bursary from your community, a club, or a school. Who can apply?

E2. Which would you rather have: a $2,000 loan or a $1,000 grant? Explain.

Moment 4Networking: who you know helps you grow

The situation

Sam wanted to know what an electrician's day is like. His soccer coach's brother is an electrician. Sam asked his coach to introduce them, and they talked for 15 minutes. Now Sam knows which courses help, and he has someone to ask when he's ready to look for an apprenticeship.

Worked example

How did networking help Sam?

  1. Networking means building relationships to share information, ideas and support.
  2. Sam started with someone he knew (his coach), who connected him to someone in the career.
  3. He asked good questions: How did you get started? What courses helped?
  4. He thanked them and kept in touch. That relationship may help him find an apprenticeship later.

1. Name three people in your network who could connect you to someone in a career.

2. Write two questions you would ask someone who works in a career you're curious about.

3. Is networking only about getting a job? What else can it help with?

Extra practice · optional

E1. Write how you would start the conversation: what would you say first?

E2. Why is it important to say thank you after someone helps you?
