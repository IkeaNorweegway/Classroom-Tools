Moment 1Four kinds of pathways

The situation

Post-secondary means education or training after high school. In Alberta there are many ways to get ready for a career. They take different amounts of time, and you learn in different ways. All of them can lead to a good career.

Worked example

Becoming an electrician: which pathway, and how does it work?

  1. Electrician is a trade, so the main pathway is an apprenticeship.
  2. Apprentices work for an employer and get paid while they learn.
  3. Each year, they also go to a college or polytechnic for a few weeks of classes.
  4. In Alberta, an electrician apprenticeship takes about 4 years. Then they write an exam to become a certified journeyperson.

1. Match each career to its usual pathway: university, college, or apprenticeship.
(a) Registered nurse
(b) Electrician
(c) Vet tech

Answer

(a) university (b) apprenticeship (c) college

2. Some careers have more than one pathway. To build software, you can take a 4-year university degree or a 2-year polytechnic diploma. Name one way these two pathways are different.

Answer

Any one real difference: length (4 years vs. 2 years); type of learning (more theory and research vs. more hands-on and applied); cost (a degree usually costs more in total); the credential (degree vs. diploma); starting work sooner with a diploma.

3. A student says: "University is the only real choice after high school. Everything else is second best." Do you agree? Give a reason, and name one thing an apprenticeship offers that university does not.

Answer

Look for disagreement with a reason: all pathways can lead to good careers, and different careers need different pathways. Apprenticeship offers at least one of: getting paid while you learn, less debt, mostly hands-on learning on a real job site, a trade certificate that is in demand.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Can someone change pathways? Describe how a person could start at a college and later move to a university, or start working and later go back to school.

E2. Name a post-secondary school in or near your community. What kind of school is it?

Answer

E1. For example: a student finishes a 2-year college diploma, then transfers credits into a university degree; or works for a few years, then goes back to school as an adult.

E2. Any real school with its type (university, college, polytechnic, or a trade school).

Moment 2The true cost

The situation

Liam is choosing between a university degree and an electrician apprenticeship. His aunt says, "Don't just look at tuition. Look at the true cost." What does she mean?

Worked example

What is the true cost of one year at the University of Alberta (2026–27) if Liam lives at home?

  1. Tuition: a full year is about 10 courses. Each costs $729.36. 10 × $729.36 = $7,293.60.
  2. Lost earnings: school runs about 8 months. Full-time work at $15 an hour is about $2,400 a month (40 hours × 4 weeks × $15). 8 × $2,400 = $19,200.
  3. True cost: $7,293.60 + $19,200 = $26,493.60 for one year, before books and travel.

Most of the true cost is hidden: the lost earnings are bigger than the tuition.

Connects to Math: multiplying and adding money amounts

1. A degree usually takes 4 years. What is the tuition for 4 years at $7,293.60 a year?

Answer

4 × $7,293.60 = $29,174.40

2. At Portage College, an electrician apprentice pays tuition of $1,044 for each of periods 1, 2 and 3, and $1,524 for period 4. What is the total tuition for all 4 periods?

Answer

3 × $1,044 = $3,132. Then $3,132 + $1,524 = $4,656.

3. Apprentices are paid while they learn. Explain why that makes the true cost of an apprenticeship much lower than its tuition alone suggests.

Answer

Apprentices earn wages while they train, so they lose little or no income. Most of a university student's true cost is lost earnings; an apprentice avoids most of that, and can pay tuition from their wages.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Liam moves away for university and pays about $1,000 a month for rent and food (sample amount) for 8 months. What is the true cost of one year now?

E2. How much more is 4 years of university tuition than the total apprenticeship tuition from Question 2?

Answer

E1. $7,293.60 + (8 × $1,000 = $8,000) + $19,200 = $34,493.60

E2. $29,174.40 − $4,656 = $24,518.40 more.

Moment 3Shortages and surpluses

The situation

The labour market is all the people looking for work and all the employers looking for workers. Alberta's government forecasts which jobs will need more workers. Its 2023–2033 forecast says Alberta may be short more than 4,000 registered nurses and more than 4,000 electricians.

Worked example

Why might the nursing shortage matter to a Grade 7 student who likes helping people?

  1. A shortage means there are more nursing jobs than nurses.
  2. When Alberta needs more nurses, graduates usually find jobs more easily.
  3. So nursing could be a pathway with a good chance of work after school.
  4. But forecasts can change. The student should still choose something that fits their interests.

1. Write shortage or surplus.
(a) 300 people apply for 5 jobs at a new store.
(b) A hospital can't find enough nurses to fill its shifts.

Answer

(a) surplus (b) shortage

2. Why is it useful to check the labour market before choosing a pathway? Give one reason.

Answer

Any one: a shortage can make it easier to find a job after you finish; you don't want to spend years training for a job that is hard to get; it helps you plan a backup.

3. The forecast is for 2023–2033. You will finish school around 2031–2036. Why should you check again when you are closer to choosing?

Answer

Forecasts can change. By the time you finish school, the economy, technology, and the number of people training for that job may be different.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Name one thing that could turn a shortage into a surplus over time.

E2. Look up one career on alis.alberta.ca. What does it say about the job outlook?

Answer

E1. Any one: many people start training for that job; the economy slows and employers hire less; new technology does some of the work; workers move in from other places.

E2. Personal; look for a real outlook statement from alis.alberta.ca.

Moment 4Making the choice

The situation

Priya loves animals. She could become a veterinarian (many years of university, and very hard to get in) or a vet tech (a 2-year college diploma). Both work with animals every day. How should she decide?

Worked example

Priya chooses the vet tech pathway. What is she giving up, and why might that be OK?

  1. Trade-off: she gives up the higher pay and the chance to do surgery as a vet.
  2. What she gains: she starts working with animals years sooner, with much less cost and debt.
  3. Why it could be OK: her main goal is working with animals every day, and both pathways do that. She could also go back to school later.

1. Most programs choose students using their high school courses and grades. Why does that mean choices in Grades 10–12 matter?

Answer

Programs check high school courses and grades, so students need to take the right courses (prerequisites) and do well in them. Planning early keeps more pathways open.

2. Name two things a person might give up (sacrifice) by choosing a longer pathway.

Answer

Any two: time; income you could have earned (lost earnings); money for tuition (possible debt); flexibility (being tied to one place or schedule).

3. Pick one career you are curious about. Which pathway would you choose? Name one trade-off and explain why it would be OK for you.

Answer

Look for a named pathway, a real trade-off (time, money, income, flexibility, distance), and a reason it is acceptable connected to the student's goals.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Would you rather start earning sooner or study longer for a higher wage? Explain.

E2. Add one more row to Priya's table that matters to you (for example: distance from home). Fill it in.

Answer

E1. Either choice with a reason.

E2. A sensible criterion with both columns filled in.

Data used

  • University of Alberta 2026–27 domestic undergraduate tuition: $729.36 per 3-credit course (registrar, fee index × $121.56). A year of 10 courses ≈ $7,293.60, before other fees.
  • Portage College electrician apprenticeship tuition: $1,044 (periods 1–3), $1,524 (period 4). Other schools differ.
  • Government of Alberta, job market forecast 2023–2033: occupations that may face shortages of more than 4,000 workers include registered nurses and registered psychiatric nurses, and electricians (except industrial and power system).
  • Vet tech average wage: ALIS 2023 Alberta Wage and Salary Survey ($25.57/hour).
  • Lost earnings use the 2026 Alberta minimum wage ($15/hour). The $1,000/month living cost is a sample.
  • People in this workbook are made up.
