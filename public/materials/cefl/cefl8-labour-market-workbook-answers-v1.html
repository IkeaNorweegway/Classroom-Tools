Ready for Change

Answers and what to look for · Outcome 8-LM · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

Teacher note

Listen for: "staying in one job is the safest plan" (job security vs. career security); "robots will take every job"; "technology is only bad for workers." The misconception target is Moment 4, Question 3.

Moment 1The labour market keeps moving

1. (a) social change (b) technology (c) economic cycle

2. 9.0 ÷ 4.4 ≈ 2 times (about double). Roughly twice as many people in the labour force were looking for work and couldn't find it.

3. The labour market changes (booms and busts, new technology, social change), so any one job can disappear. A worker who can adapt (learn new skills, move to a growing area) can keep working when one job ends.

Answer

E1. For example: oil and gas, pipelines and related trades do well with high prices; when prices are low, a province may try to grow technology, tourism or agriculture. Accept reasoned answers.

E2. For example: an aging population creates jobs in home care, physiotherapy and pharmacy; climate change creates jobs in renewable energy and retrofitting buildings.

Moment 2Technology: jobs lost, jobs gained

1. Any reasonable pair. For example: bank teller, where machines handle deposits, but new tasks include helping customers with online banking and financial advice.

2. Positive: new jobs and tasks, safer or faster work, new industries. Negative: some tasks or jobs disappear, and workers need retraining.

3. Look for disagreement with the farm example: technology removed some tasks (driving tractors) but created others (fixing machines, flying drones, reading data). Planning matters more, so you can build skills for the growing tasks.

Answer

E1. For example: learn customer-service and tech-support skills, train to fix and set up the machines, take courses toward a new role.

E2. For example: robots doing dangerous mining or welding tasks; drones inspecting tall towers instead of people climbing them.

Moment 3Career resilience and human capital

1. (a) job security (b) career security

2. Any two: take courses that interest them; join clubs or teams (skills, experience); volunteer; learn a new tool or language; get a first-aid or babysitting certificate; read and learn about careers.

3. People Rosa had worked with knew her skills and told her about openings. A Grade 8 network might include family, neighbours, coaches, teachers, family friends, club leaders.

Answer

E1. Yes: someone can have a stable job (job security) and the skills and network to find another one quickly (career security). The best position has both.

E2. Any ranking with a reason. For example: communication skills first, because every job needs them and they help you network and interview.

Moment 4Goals with milestones

1. (a) short-term (b) long-term

2. A long-term career goal with two specific short-term steps (a course, a club, a certificate, a volunteer role).

Watch out

3. The labour market changes, so one job can disappear (Alberta 2014–2016). A worker with only job security and a rigid goal has nothing to fall back on. Career security (skills, learning, network) and a flexible goal with a backup are safer.

Answer

E1. A backup career that shares skills, with the milestones that still help named.

E2. No, the plan doesn't fail. Milestones can be moved; a plan is meant to be updated.
