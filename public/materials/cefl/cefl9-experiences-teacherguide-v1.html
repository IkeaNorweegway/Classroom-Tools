Career Education & Financial Literacy 9 · Experiences and Employment · Teacher guide

Pitch It

7 sessions × 60 minutes · project-based · Outcome 9-EX · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

At a glance

Outcome9-EX: Students relate experiences and employment opportunities to career pathways.
UnderstandingsExperiences can spark interest in career pathways. Employment opportunities can contribute to career pathways.
Driving questionHow can an experience I create move me toward my career?
Student materialsMini-workbook `cefl9-experiences-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl9-experiences-project-v1`
Teams4–5. One venture per team; one cover letter and digital-identity check per student.
AudienceA pitch panel (staff, community members, local business owners), Session 7
AssessmentSingle-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test.

Teacher note

Before Session 1:

  • Book the panel for Session 7; local business owners or a school administrator make it feel real.
  • Hook examples: find three real youth ventures (local news stories, the school store, a student club) or describe composite ones.
  • Ask your school what work experience, RAP or dual-credit options exist for Grade 10, so Moment 1 points to real next steps.
  • If ventures will run for real, check school policy on selling, handling money and permission forms.

Why it is built this way

  • Driving question first. Real youth ventures in the hook show that 15-year-olds can create experiences, not just wait for them.
  • Experience as a career tool. Volunteering, work-integrated learning and small ventures are linked to better adult work outcomes (OECD career readiness indicators).
  • Two products. The team venture practises entrepreneurship; each student's cover letter and digital-identity check covers attaining employment.
  • A panel with real questions. It tests whether the plan is realistic.

Watch out

"What I post online is private." Moment 4. Posts can be public by default, shared or screenshotted; employers do search names.

Teacher note

Digital identity privacy. Students check their own digital identity privately; don't ask them to show their accounts. The checklist is self-reported.

Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

  1. Pitch slideshow (6 slides): problem → idea → customers → numbers and break-even → risks and roles → career connections.
  2. Prototype + live pitch: a sample product, flyer or mock-up on the table while the team pitches.
  3. One-page business plan: headings for each part, a small break-even table, and a roles chart.

When a team gets stuck

What you seeWhat to do
Idea is too big ("open a restaurant")Shrink it: a one-day bake sale or a weekly service at school.
Volunteer project with no numbersUse costs and how they'll be covered instead of break-even.
Cover letter is genericAsk: why this role in your venture? What evidence do you have?
Unsafe ventureChecker reviews safety and school rules; redirect early.

Access supports (UDL)

  • Break-even card (fixed cost ÷ profit per item); word cards in the brief.
  • Product choice; a prototype suits hands-on learners.
  • Students who can't run a real venture still complete the full plan and pitch.

Background for the teacher

  • RAP (Registered Apprenticeship Program): high school students can start an apprenticeship and earn high school credits; ask your school or check alberta.ca.
  • Bilingualism: about 18% of Canadians could conduct a conversation in both English and French (2021 Census).
  • Employment services: Alberta has employment services for newcomers, Francophones and under-represented groups (for example, through alis.alberta.ca and community agencies).
