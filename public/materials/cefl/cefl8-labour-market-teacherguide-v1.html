Career Education & Financial Literacy 8 · Careers and the Labour Market · Teacher guide

Future-Proof Me

7 sessions × 60 minutes · project-based · Outcome 8-LM · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

At a glance

Outcome8-LM: Students analyze the dynamic nature of the labour market and develop an understanding of the knowledge, skills, and goals required to build career resilience.
UnderstandingsThe labour market is dynamic, making adaptability a more reliable career strategy than job stability alone. Career resilience is built deliberately. Human capital shapes employability.
Driving questionHow can I build a career that can survive change?
Student materialsMini-workbook `cefl8-labour-market-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl8-labour-market-project-v1`
Teams4–5. Each student makes their own plan; teams research and give feedback.
AudienceA Grade 7 class, Session 7 ("advice from Grade 8s")
AssessmentSingle-point rubric on the report-card scale (4 Excellent · 3 Proficient · 2 Meets · 1 Has yet to meet), scored on Steps 1–4 and the product. No unit test.

Teacher note

Before Session 1: book the Grade 7 class for Session 7. Check alis.alberta.ca (OCCinfo) on student devices. Hook answers (accept reasoned debate): new: social media manager, drone pilot, app developer; shrinking: video rental clerk (almost gone), travel agent, bank teller; still strong: electrician, nurse.

Why it is built this way

  • Driving question first. The hook sorting shows that jobs really do appear and disappear; the moments explain why.
  • Real Alberta example. The 2014–2016 oil downturn (unemployment 4.4% → 9.0%) makes "the labour market is dynamic" concrete. Some students' families lived it, so handle it with care.
  • Personal plan, team support. Each plan is personal; teams catch weak reasoning.
  • Advice for Grade 7s is a real audience with a reason to explain clearly.

Watch out

"The safest plan is one job forever." Moment 4, Question 3 targets this. Use Dev and Rosa: Dev had job security; Rosa had career security. Return to it in the Session 5 warm-up.

Watch out

"Robots will take all the jobs." Moment 2, Question 3. Technology shifts tasks; it rarely removes a whole field at once. The farm shows both sides.

Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

  1. Infographic. Top third: "What could change my career" with three icons. Middle: "My resilience" as a skills bar chart. Bottom: a milestone timeline with a backup branch.
  2. Letter to my future self. A one-page letter from age 13 to age 25 that explains the plan, the backup, and what to do if the labour market changes.
  3. Short video. A 90-second "news report from 2040" about the career, followed by the student explaining how they prepared.

When a student gets stuck

What you seeWhat to do
Can't choose a careerUse the Grade 7 Career Pathway Map career, or pick one from the hook list.
"Technology won't affect my career"Ask: what tools do people in it use? What would a robot or app do?
Milestones are vague ("work hard")Ask: what course, club, certificate or job, and when?
Family job loss comes upAcknowledge it; keep the focus on what workers can do, not blame.

Access supports (UDL)

  • Read the scenarios and OCCinfo pages aloud; word cards are at the end of the brief.
  • Any product on the menu, including the letter (writing) or video (spoken).
  • The network table uses roles ("aunt", "coach"), not names, so no student has to share family details.

Background for the teacher

  • Alberta unemployment: 4.4% in November 2014, peaking at 9.0% in November 2016 (Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey, monthly). Annual averages are lower (about 7% for 2016).
  • Course names in Amira's plan (Math 10C, Math 30-1, CTS) are Alberta high school courses.
  • OECD career readiness research: teenagers who explore and plan their careers tend to have better adult outcomes (an association, not proof of cause).
