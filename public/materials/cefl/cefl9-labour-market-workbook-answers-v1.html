Career Education & Financial Literacy 9 · Careers and the Labour Market · Mini-workbook · Answer key

Show What You Can Do

Answers and what to look for · Outcome 9-LM · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

Teacher note

Listen for: "I have no experience, so I can't apply" (Moment 4, the main misconception target); "a résumé lists every job you've had" rather than evidence matched to one posting; "automation only affects factory work."

Moment 1Automation and globalization

1. (a) automation (b) globalization (c) automation

2. For example: plumber, nurse, electrician, early childhood educator, hairstylist. These need hands-on work in a specific place, complex judgment, or caring human contact.

3. A named industry and role with a realistic change. For example, retail: self-checkout reduces cashier roles but creates jobs maintaining machines and running online orders.

Answer

E1. It can sell to customers in other countries, find suppliers, and hire talent from anywhere.

E2. Any two: creativity, empathy and caring, complex communication, hands-on problem solving in changing places, leadership, ethical judgment.

Moment 2Where is the labour market moving?

1. Any three: alis.alberta.ca (ALIS/OCCinfo), jobbank.gc.ca, Statistics Canada, alberta.ca job market forecasts, industry reports, professional associations.

2. One year can be a short-term dip or bump (a slow season, a big project ending). A trend over several years, plus a forecast, shows the real direction.

3. Where the jobs are going: growing or declining trends, forecasts, and whether automation or globalization might change the job. Also their own interests and the training required.

Answer

E1. An industry, a classification, and evidence (a number, a trend over years, or an outlook statement) with its source.

E2. For example: a recession, government funding cuts, falling prices for the industry's product, new technology, or a slowdown in population growth.

Moment 3Your résumé and personal brand

1. For example: "Sorted and packed food hampers for 40 families each week at the Red Deer Food Bank."

2. Look for who the student is (a strength or two) and what they offer an employer, in one or two sentences.

3. Each posting asks for different skills. Tailoring puts the most relevant experience and the posting's own words first, so the employer quickly sees the match.

Answer

E1. Any three real experiences (volunteering, babysitting, clubs, teams, school roles, certificates).

E2. It shows leadership, responsibility, working with children, and reliability: the same skills a day-camp leader needs.

Moment 4Interviews and self-advocacy

1. Look for all four parts in order, with a clear personal action ("I…").

2. Requirements: 15+, available weekends. Asset: customer service experience.

3. Speaking up clearly and politely about your skills, value and needs. For example: explaining a scheduling conflict and suggesting other days, or pointing out a skill the interviewer didn't ask about.

Watch out

Experience doesn't have to be paid. Leo's coaching, library volunteering and first-aid certificate are all experience that shows the skills employers want. Many entry-level postings expect little or no work history.

Answer

E1. For example: "What does a typical shift look like?" "What do your best employees do well?" "When will you make a decision?"

E2. Any two: research the employer, reread the posting, practise STAR answers, plan your outfit and route, prepare questions to ask.
