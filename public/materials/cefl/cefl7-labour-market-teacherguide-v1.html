Career Education & Financial Literacy 7 · Careers and the Labour Market · Teacher guide

Career Pathway Map

7 sessions × 60 minutes · project-based · Outcome 7-LM · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

At a glance

Outcome7-LM: Students explore the foundations of careers.
UnderstandingCareers are dynamic and are shaped by a variety of factors.
Driving questionWhat path could take me from here to a career I'd want?
Student materialsMini-workbook `cefl7-labour-market-workbook-v1` (4 moments) · project brief `cefl7-labour-market-project-v1`
Teams4–5 students. Each student makes their own map; the team researches together and gives feedback.
AudienceA Career Café in Session 7: families, another class, or school staff visit and ask questions
AssessmentSingle-point rubric ("How you will be marked") on the report-card scale (4 Excellent · 3 Proficient · 2 Meets · 1 Has yet to meet), scored on Steps 1–4 and the finished map. No unit test.

Teacher note

Do this before Session 1

  • Book the Career Café for Session 7. Invite families (a short note home), another class, or staff. Ask a few adults from different careers to drop in if possible.
  • Line up conversations for Step 4. Most students can ask a family member or neighbour. For students who can't, arrange 2–3 guests (in person or video) or staff with past careers. No student should be left without someone to talk to.
  • Check CAREERinsite (alis.alberta.ca/careerinsite) and OCCinfo (alis.alberta.ca/occinfo) work on student devices. Print 15–20 occupation profiles as a backup.
  • Hook answers: babysitter → daycare director; dishwasher → chef; farm helper → vet clinic manager.

Why it is built this way

  • Driving question first. The hook (matching first jobs to careers) makes students wonder how paths really work; the moments answer that.
  • Contact with real work. Teenagers who talk with people in jobs and explore careers do better as adults (OECD career readiness indicators). Step 4's conversation is the core of the project, not an extra.
  • Personal product, team support. Career choice is personal, so every student makes their own map, but structured team roles keep peer help on task and catch wrong facts.
  • Critique and revise. Session 6 feedback has to lead to at least two changes.

Session plans

Session 1 — Hook and Moment 1

TimeWhat happens
0–10Hook. Teams match first jobs to careers in the brief, then explain their thinking. Reveal the answers. Ask: "What surprised you?"
10–15Form teams, assign Session 1 jobs. Read the driving question together.
15–20Need-to-know list. Keep a class chart up all unit. Expect: "How do people choose?", "What if I don't know what I want?", "How much do jobs pay?"
20–35Moment 1. Walk through Mia's path. Students do Questions 1–3; early finishers do Extra practice.
35–55Step 1: Me, now. Each student fills in their page. Teammates help each other think of past chores, clubs and jobs.
55–60Self-check. Tick answered questions on the need-to-know chart.

Look for

Every student has something in every box. Students who say "I haven't done anything" usually have: chores, caring for siblings, clubs, gaming communities, helping at a family business. Prompt them.

Session 2 — Moment 2 and skills

TimeWhat happens
0–5Warm-up: (1) Job or career: "five years as a nurse, then nursing teacher"? (2) Name one step in Mia's path. (3) What is a transferable skill?
5–20Moment 2. The skills diagram and the camp counsellor example. Questions 1–3.
20–50Step 2: My skills. Students fill in their skills, then ask a teammate what skill they see in them.
50–60Check-in: the Checker confirms each teammate has evidence ("where I showed it") for each skill.

Watch out

"Skills only count in the job where you learned them." Use the lifeguard ad: every skill in it is used by nurses, teachers and electricians too.

Session 3 — Moment 3 and picking a career

TimeWhat happens
0–5Warm-up: (1) Name two transferable skills. (2) Where did you use one this week? (3) Job or career: babysitting?
5–20Moment 3. Jayden's timeline; check a career against interests and values. Questions 1–3.
20–50Step 3: Pick a career. Students explore CAREERinsite and choose three careers, then one.
50–60Teacher check-in with each team.

Look for

The reason a career fits names a real interest or value from Step 1, not only "it pays a lot" or "it's cool." Students who can't choose can pick one to explore for now; say clearly that this is not a lifetime decision.

Session 4 — Moment 4, research, and the conversation

TimeWhat happens
0–5Warm-up: (1) What is one of your values for work? (2) Why can it be OK to change careers? (3) $38.41 − $25.57 = ?
5–20Moment 4. The wage chart and the electrician vs. vet tech example. Questions 1–3 and the "watch out" item.
20–45Step 4: Research. Students fill in the research table from OCCinfo. The Checker checks facts against the website.
45–55Plan the conversation. Students write their own question and decide who to ask. The conversation itself can happen at home, with a guest, or in class before Session 5.
55–60Check-in on who each student will talk to. Follow up with anyone who has no one.

Watch out

"The highest-paying career is always the best." Moment 4's "watch out" item targets this. Come back to it in the Session 5 warm-up: "Name a reason someone might choose a lower-paying career."

Session 5 — Workshop and making the map

TimeWhat happens
0–5Warm-up: (1) Name a reason someone might choose a lower-paying career. (2) What education does your career need? (3) What did your person say about their first job?
5–15Workshop: making a map easy to follow. Show three different sample maps (below). Ask what makes each clear.
15–55Step 5. Students plan their steps, choose a product, sketch, and start their final map.
55–60Self-check against the Step 6 checklist.

Session 6 — Feedback

TimeWhat happens
0–5Model the feedback frames with a sample map. Show one weak comment and one strong one.
5–25Teams trade maps; each student gets at least two pieces of feedback using the frames.
25–55Make at least 2 changes; fill in "Feedback I got / Change I made." Finish the map.
55–60Practise the 1-minute talk with a partner.

Session 7 — Career Café

TimeWhat happens
0–5Set up maps around the room.
5–40Career Café. Visitors move between maps, ask questions, and leave sticky notes. Half the class presents while the other half visits, then they switch.
40–60Look back page, authenticity check, and My Plan page.

Sample maps (show all three in Session 5)

  1. Road poster. A winding road with signs for each step, a car at "me, now," and a finish flag at "registered nurse." Skills to build are billboards beside the road.
  2. Comic, "My future self." Six panels: a 13-year-old meets their 25-year-old self, who explains the steps they took to become an electrician.
  3. Board game path. Squares for each step, with "skill cards" to collect along the way, and a "detour" square showing that plans can change.

When a student gets stuck

What you seeWhat to do
"I don't know what I want to be."That's normal. Pick one career to explore for this project. Look at Step 1 interests and suggest two careers that match.
Picks a very rare career (pro athlete, famous streamer)Keep it, and add a "Plan B" career that uses the same skills (coach, physiotherapist, video editor).
No one to talk toArrange a guest, a staff member, or a recorded interview from CAREERinsite.
Map has only 2 stepsAsk: "What happens between grade 12 and this job? What training? What first job?"
Copies facts without understandingAsk them to explain one fact in their own words to a teammate.

Access supports (UDL)

  • Reading: read the scenarios aloud; OCCinfo pages can be read aloud with the browser's read-aloud tool.
  • Writing: the map can be drawn, recorded, or built (board game). Sentence starters for the Career Café talk are in the brief.
  • Choice: students choose their career, product, and person to talk to.
  • Sensitivity: some students' families may not work or may have hard job situations. Never require students to share family jobs or pay. "A trusted adult" can be anyone safe.

Background for the teacher

  • Wages in the workbook are the 2023 Alberta Wage and Salary Survey averages from ALIS (all experience levels). Starting wages are lower; top wages are higher.
  • OECD career readiness research (2021): teenagers who explore careers (conversations, workplace visits, career research), experience work (part-time jobs, volunteering), and think about their futures tend to have lower unemployment and higher pay as adults. These are links, not proof of cause.
