Keeping My Options Open

Answers and what to look for · Outcome 8-PS · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

Teacher note

Listen for: "meeting the minimum guarantees a seat"; "course choices in Grade 10 don't matter yet"; "scholarships are only for top students"; mixing up grants and loans. The misconception target is Moment 1, Question 3.

Moment 1The ticket in

1. No (for this typical engineering program). It requires Math 30-1 (and Math 31). Math 30-2 doesn't meet the requirement, so she would need to take Math 30-1 first.

2. For example: the program is popular, it leads to well-paid or in-demand jobs, or it has limited space (labs, instructors, placements).

3. The minimum is only the lowest mark the school will consider. Competitive programs fill seats with the highest applicants, so a student at the minimum may not get in. Plan for a higher average and a backup.

Answer

E1. A real program with its listed requirements.

E2. Electricians use math (calculations, measurements, electrical formulas) and science (how electricity works) every day, so the apprentice will learn faster and work more safely.

Moment 2Planning high school

1. 100 credits.

2. Many programs require the -1 course (for example, Math 30-1 or ELA 30-1). Taking the -1 sequence lets you apply to programs that accept either; the -2 sequence can close some doors unless you upgrade later.

3. Two relevant courses with reasons. For example, nurse: Biology 30 (body systems) and Chemistry 30 (often required for admission).

Answer

E1. 100 ÷ 5 = 20 five-credit courses.

E2. A school counsellor, teachers, a career advisor, or older students.

Moment 3Paying for it

1. (a) bursary (b) scholarship (c) loan

2. $4,800 − $1,200 − $500 = $3,100.

3. Many scholarships use Grade 10–12 marks (like Rutherford), have early deadlines, or reward activities over several years. Starting early means you don't miss them and can build the record they look for.

Answer

E1. A real local award with who can apply.

E2. Most students should choose the $1,000 grant: it never has to be paid back. A $2,000 loan must be repaid, often with interest. Accept a reasoned answer about needing more money now, if the student names the cost of repaying.

Moment 4Networking

1. Three roles (for example: aunt, coach, family friend, teacher, neighbour).

2. Two open questions. For example: How did you get into this work? What do you wish you'd known in Grade 8? What courses helped most?

3. No. Networking also helps you learn about careers and programs, find volunteer and job-shadow opportunities, get advice and references, and find mentors.

Answer

E1. For example: "Hi, my coach said you're an electrician. I'm in Grade 8 and thinking about trades. Could I ask you a few questions about your work?"

E2. It shows respect for their time, and people are more likely to help again or share opportunities later.
