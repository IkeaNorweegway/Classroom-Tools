Moment 1Kinds of experiences

The situation

Noah is 13. This year he:

  • plays trumpet in the school band
  • did a project on wind power for the science fair
  • shovels snow for three neighbours, who pay him $10 a driveway
  • helps sort food at the food bank one Saturday a month

Worked example

What do Noah's experiences tell him about work he might like?

  1. Sort them. Science fair = curricular. Band = extracurricular. Snow shovelling = work-related (entrepreneurship). Food bank = work-related (volunteering).
  2. Look for patterns. He liked the science fair and running his own snow business. Both let him plan things his own way.
  3. Connect to work. Noah might enjoy work where he can plan and make decisions, maybe even running his own business someday.

1. Sort each experience: curricular, extracurricular, or work-related.
(a) Being on the school volleyball team
(b) Selling bracelets you make at a craft fair
(c) Building a bridge model in science class

2. Name one experience you have had. What did it tell you about the kind of work you might like?

3. A student says: "Only paid jobs count as work experience." Use Noah's experiences to explain why this is not true.

Moment 2Where and how people work

The situation

Jobs are very different. Some people work outside all day. Some work from home on a computer. Some work alone, and some always work on a team. Jobs can also be full-time or part-time, steady or seasonal.

Worked example

Describe the lifeguard job using the words for types of work.

  1. Employee or self-employed? The town pays the lifeguard, so they are an employee.
  2. Full-time or part-time? About 20 hours a week, so part-time.
  3. Regular or casual? Shifts change each week, so casual.
  4. Permanent or temporary? Summer only, so temporary (seasonal).

Moment 3Work-life balance

The situation

Noah's mom asks him to look at his week. "You have school, band, snow, and the food bank. Do you still have time to sleep, do homework, and have fun?" This is work-life balance: fitting work and the rest of your life together in a healthy way.

Worked example

How much time does Noah have left for everything else?

  1. A week has 7 × 24 = 168 hours.
  2. Sleep: 9 hours × 7 nights = 63. School: 6.5 hours × 5 days = 32.5. Band: 3. Snow and food bank: about 4.
  3. Add them: 63 + 32.5 + 3 + 4 = 102.5 hours.
  4. Left over: 168 − 102.5 = 65.5 hours for meals, homework, family, friends, and fun. His week looks balanced.

Connects to Health: sleep and well-being

1. In Alberta, workers aged 13 and 14 need a parent's OK. They can work up to 2 hours on a school day and up to 8 hours on a non-school day, and not between 9 pm and 6 am. A 13-year-old is asked to work 6 pm to 10 pm on a Friday (a school day). Is that allowed? Give two reasons.

2. Make a quick estimate of your own week. How many hours for sleep, school, and activities? How many are left?

3. Noah is offered a Saturday job for 8 hours every week. What might he have to give up? Would you take it if you were Noah? Why?

Extra practice · optional

Moment 4Belonging and safety at work

The situation

Ava is 15 and just started at a café. On day two, her supervisor says, "We're busy. Can you use the meat slicer? It's easy." Ava has never been trained on it. What should she do?

Worked example

What should Ava do about the meat slicer?

  1. Is it dangerous? A meat slicer can cause serious cuts, and she has no training. Yes, it is dangerous for her.
  2. Which right? The right to refuse dangerous work. She also has the right to know: to be trained first.
  3. What to say: "I haven't been trained on the slicer. I'm not comfortable using it until someone shows me how."
  4. If she's pressured: tell a manager or a trusted adult. In Alberta, workers can't be punished for refusing dangerous work.

1. Which right is this? Write know, participate, or refuse.
(a) Sam joins the store's safety meeting and suggests a non-slip mat.
(b) On the first day, Lee is shown where the fire exits are.
(c) Jo says no to climbing a broken ladder.

2. Name two things a workplace can do so that new workers feel they belong.

3. Kai is a new volunteer. Other volunteers leave him out and joke about his accent. Is this a place where everyone belongs? What could someone do?
