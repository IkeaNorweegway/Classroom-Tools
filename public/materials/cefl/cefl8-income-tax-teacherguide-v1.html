Job Offer Showdown

7 sessions × 60 minutes · project-based · Outcome 8-IT · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

At a glance

Outcome8-IT: Students analyze income taxation systems and total compensation to evaluate employment options and understand the role taxes play in supporting society.
UnderstandingsTotal compensation and tax implications help when evaluating a job offer. Taxes are how individuals collectively fund public services.
Driving questionWhich job offer is really worth more?
Student materialsMini-workbook `cefl8-income-tax-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl8-income-tax-project-v1`
Teams4–5; one recommendation per team; individual tax calculations first
AudienceA panel (staff or family volunteers) playing Sam, Session 7
GameJob Offer Showdown (CEFL Town), on the unit page, optional: one chapter after each of Sessions 1–4. See "The Job Offer Showdown game" below.
AssessmentSingle-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test.

Why it is built this way

  • Driving question first. "Offer A pays $3,000 more" sounds obvious, so students want to find out why it might not be.
  • Brackets in a real decision. Students calculate tax because they need it for the recommendation, not as a drill.
  • Values, not one right answer. The panel tests reasoning, which matches the outcome's "evaluate" verb.

Watch out

"A raise into a higher bracket can make you take home less." Moment 1, Question 3, and the panel question in Step 7. Only the income above the line is taxed at the higher rate.

The Job Offer Showdown game (CEFL Town)

A companion game on the unit page, one mission in CEFL Town, the top-down town every CEFL unit shares. Each student makes a character once, or keeps the one from Grade 7. Sam, the bank teller, is finishing college and has the brief's two job offers; Ms. Okafor at the Job Centre helps the student advise Sam.

  • When: sessions are full, so set each chapter as homework, early-finisher work, or a device day before the next session. Chapters play in order. Chapter length has not been timed with students yet.
  • Saving: the game saves in that browser, and the character carries over to every CEFL Town mission. To move to another computer, use Menu → Take your town home (a save code or a file).
  • Controls: arrow keys or tap to walk, Enter or tap to talk. Tapping the gold goal line at the top walks the student there, which suits students who find moving around hard.
  • The side story: TaxZapper taxes all income at the top bracket, forgets the basic personal credit, and compares offers by salary only. Students prove each mistake at the Tax Forum and fix the app; Mo takes his raise, and the app's new slice view is named after the student. The story never changes the goal.
  • The goal: Sam takes the student's advice: at least 3 of 4 of Sam's questions answered right on the first try. The Bracket Blitz game counts right answers; it never changes the goal.
  • Gentle and Real (Menu): Gentle gives a hint after a wrong try and shows the answer after 3 tries; Real gives no hints and shows it after 2. Gentle also warns once before a student signs off on a wrong line.
  • Between lessons (optional): after a chapter, students can choose "Not yet: help around town." Townspeople then ask for help with their own version of what the chapter taught, for a little play money in a wallet that never touches the mission, and one Town Mail letter arrives each day.
  • Report (Menu → Report for my teacher): first-try answers by chapter, what the student wrote and chose, game results, how far they got in the side story, and a Between lessons table. It prints. First-try answers make a quick look-for before each step's checkpoint.
  • Simplifications to tell students if asked: income tax only (no CPP, EI or other credits); Alberta's top brackets are beyond this unit; benefit values are samples. Brackets and credits are 2026.

Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

  1. Side-by-side poster. Two columns, each with a "pay stack" (salary, tax, after-tax, benefits) and a row of icons for the non-money factors.
  2. Letter of advice. A one-page letter to Sam with a small table of numbers and three paragraphs: money, life, and recommendation.
  3. "Sam's choice" video. Two short scenes of Sam a year into each job, then the team's numbers and recommendation.

Access supports (UDL)

  • Calculators; a tax "recipe card" (the four worked-example steps) can stay on the desk.
  • Product choice; the letter suits strong writers, the video suits speakers.
  • Real postings are optional; Sam's offers keep everyone on equal footing.

Background for the teacher

  • 2026 federal: 14% to $58,523; 20.5% to $117,045; 26% to $181,440; 29% to $258,482; 33% above; basic personal amount $16,452 (credit at 14%).
  • 2026 Alberta: 8% to $61,200; 10% to $154,259; 12%, 13%, 14%, 15% above; basic personal amount $22,769 (credit at 8%).
  • The method ignores CPP and EI (and their credits) to keep the bracket idea clear; real tax is a little different.
