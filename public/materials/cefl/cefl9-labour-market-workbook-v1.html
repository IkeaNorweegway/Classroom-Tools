Moment 1Automation and globalization

The situation

At some Alberta oil sands mines, huge haul trucks now drive themselves. Fewer people drive trucks, but more people monitor, program and repair them. Meanwhile, a company in Calgary can hire a designer in another country over the internet, and an Alberta business can sell its products worldwide.

Worked example

How might automation and globalization change a bank teller's job?

  1. Automation: ATMs and online banking handle deposits and transfers, so fewer tellers are needed.
  2. Globalization: some customer service is done by call centres in other countries.
  3. New roles: banks need people who give financial advice, help customers with technology, and protect against fraud.
  4. So: workers who build those new skills move into the growing roles. Both forces disrupt and create.

1. Write automation or globalization.
(a) A store uses self-checkout machines.
(b) An Alberta company hires a programmer who lives in another country.
(c) A factory uses robots to weld car parts.

2. Name a job that is hard to automate or send to another country. Why?

3. Pick one industry you're interested in. How could automation or globalization reshape one role in it?

Extra practice · optional

E1. How can globalization help an Alberta business grow?

E2. Which skills are hardest for machines to copy? Name two.

Moment 2Where is the labour market moving?

The situation

Smart job seekers don't just ask "Where are the jobs now?" They ask "Where are the jobs going?" Industries can be growing, stable, or declining. Government data helps you tell which.

Worked example

Is health care in Alberta growing, stable, or declining?

  1. Find the data: health care jobs grew about 15% from 2022 to 2025, compared with 9% across Canada.
  2. Check the time: that's three years, so it's a trend, not a one-month blip.
  3. Look for reasons: a growing and aging population needs more health care.
  4. Classify: health care is growing. Construction dropped 3.1% in one year, but one year isn't enough to call it declining; check more years and the forecast.

1. Name three places to find labour market information.

2. Why is one year of data not enough to say an industry is declining?

3. A friend says: "I'll pick a career based on which jobs have the most openings right now." What should they also check? Why?

Extra practice · optional

E1. Look up one industry on alis.alberta.ca or jobbank.gc.ca. Is it growing, stable, or declining? What's your evidence?

E2. Name something that could make a growing industry slow down.

Moment 3Your résumé and personal brand

The situation

Leo is 15 and applying to be a junior day-camp leader. He has never had a "real" job, but he has coached his little brother's soccer team, volunteered at the library, and earned his first-aid certificate. That's human capital: education, skills and experience. A résumé shows it on one page.

Worked example

How should Leo tailor his résumé to the day-camp posting?

  1. Read the posting. It asks for: "works well with children, first aid an asset, energetic, reliable."
  2. Match the words. Leo puts First Aid and working with kids near the top, and uses "reliable" and "upbeat" in his brand statement.
  3. Show, don't just tell. Instead of "good with kids," he writes "Led warm-ups and drills for 12 players each week."
  4. Keep it to one page, with clear headings and no spelling mistakes.

1. Rewrite this weak résumé line with an action verb and a specific detail: "I did stuff at the food bank."

2. Write a one- or two-sentence personal brand statement for yourself.

3. Why should a résumé be changed for each job you apply to?

Extra practice · optional

E1. List three experiences you could put on a résumé right now, paid or unpaid.

E2. Why would an employer care that Leo coached soccer, even though the job is at a day camp?

Moment 4Interviews and self-advocacy

The situation

Leo got an interview. The manager asks: "Tell me about a time you solved a problem." Leo freezes, then remembers a way to organize his answer: STAR.

Worked example

Leo's STAR answer to "Tell me about a time you solved a problem":

  1. Situation: "At soccer practice, two kids kept arguing over who got to be goalie."
  2. Task: "As assistant coach, I needed to keep practice fun and fair."
  3. Action: "I made a rotation chart so everyone got a turn, and explained it to the team."
  4. Result: "The arguing stopped, and more kids tried new positions."

Self-advocacy: at the end, Leo says, "I have a hockey game on Thursday evenings. Could I be scheduled on other days?" He speaks up clearly and politely about his needs.

1. Use STAR to answer: "Tell me about a time you worked on a team." Write one sentence for each letter.

2. Read this posting line: "Must be 15+, available weekends, customer service experience an asset." Which are requirements and which are assets (nice to have)?

3. What does self-advocacy mean in a job interview? Give an example of doing it well.

Watch out

A student says: "I have no experience, so there's no point applying." Use Leo's résumé to explain why this isn't true.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Write one question you could ask the interviewer at the end.

E2. Name two things to do before an interview to prepare.
