Career Education & Financial Literacy 9 · Careers and the Labour Market · Teacher guide

Get Hired

7 sessions × 60 minutes · project-based · Outcome 9-LM · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

At a glance

Outcome9-LM: Students recognize that success in career goals requires proactive measures.
UnderstandingsIndividuals who manage and communicate their human capital are better positioned to navigate labour market disruptions. Career success depends on identifying where the labour market is moving.
Driving questionHow do I show an employer what I can do?
Student materialsMini-workbook `cefl9-labour-market-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl9-labour-market-project-v1`
Teams4–5. Each student applies for their own posting; teams coach.
AudienceVolunteer interviewers (staff, family, local employers), Session 7
GameGet Hired (CEFL Town), on the unit page, optional: one chapter after each of Sessions 1–4. See "The Get Hired game" below.
AssessmentSingle-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test.

Teacher note

Before Session 1:

  • Recruit interviewers for Session 7: aim for one per 4–5 students. Give them a one-page feedback card (strengths, one tip, would you interview this person?).
  • Two sample résumés for the hook: one cluttered and generic, one clear and tailored (like Leo's). Make them up.
  • Postings: print 10–15 youth-friendly postings (Job Bank, local businesses, municipal summer jobs) for students who can't search.
  • Résumé template: a simple one-page template in your school's word processor.

Why it is built this way

  • Driving question first. "50 applications, 10 minutes" makes the stakes of a clear résumé obvious.
  • Real postings and real interviewers. Talking with people in the world of work is one of the strongest career-readiness experiences (OECD).
  • Labour market data first. Students classify the industry before writing the résumé, so they apply with an eye on where work is going.
  • Coaching teams. Practice rounds with a timekeeper make the interview less frightening.

Watch out

"I have no experience, so there's no point applying." Moment 4. Leo's résumé is built from coaching, volunteering and a certificate. Help every student find at least three experiences.

The Get Hired game (CEFL Town)

A companion game on the unit page, one mission in CEFL Town, the top-down town every CEFL unit shares. Each student makes a character once, or keeps an earlier one. Ms. Okafor at the Job Centre helps them apply for a posting at the town's Job Fair.

  • When: sessions are full, so set each chapter as homework, early-finisher work, or a device day before the next session. Chapters play in order. Chapter length has not been timed with students yet.
  • Saving: the game saves in that browser, and the character carries over to every CEFL Town mission. To move to another computer, use Menu → Take your town home (a save code or a file).
  • Controls: arrow keys or tap to walk, Enter or tap to talk. Tapping the gold goal line at the top walks the student there, which suits students who find moving around hard.
  • The side story: Jas's résumé came from the ResumeRocket kiosk: generic, no evidence, not tailored, like everyone else's. At the Job Fair the student runs a résumé clinic; Jas gets hired, and the clinic table is named after the student. The story never changes the goal.
  • The goal: the employer offers the job at the mock interview: at least 3 of 4 questions answered right on the first try. The Résumé Rush game counts weak lines caught; it never changes the goal.
  • Gentle and Real (Menu): Gentle gives a hint after a wrong try and shows the answer after 3 tries; Real gives no hints and shows it after 2. In Real the interview still needs 3 strong answers.
  • Between lessons (optional): after a chapter, students can choose "Not yet: help around town." Townspeople then ask for help with their own version of what the chapter taught, for a little play money in a wallet that never touches the mission, and one Town Mail letter arrives each day.
  • Report (Menu → Report for my teacher): first-try answers by chapter, what the student wrote and chose, game results, how far they got in the side story, and a Between lessons table. It prints. First-try answers make a quick look-for before each step's checkpoint.
  • Simplifications to tell students if asked: the four postings are made up; the industry data are dated (Job Bank, May 2026) and should be re-checked.

Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

  1. Clean one-column résumé (like Leo's): brand statement, skills, experience with action verbs, education.
  2. Skills-first résumé for students with little experience: skills grouped with evidence under each.
  3. Interview clip. A 60-second recorded STAR answer (teacher-made or from a willing past student) to model pace and eye contact.

When a student gets stuck

What you seeWhat to do
"I've done nothing"Ask about chores, sibling care, teams, clubs, gaming communities, school roles, certificates.
Résumé copies the posting word for wordAsk for evidence: where did you show that?
Freezes in practiceShorten to just the Action step, then build out.
Very anxious about the interviewOffer a smaller panel, a known adult, or a recorded answer first.

Access supports (UDL)

  • Résumé template; a STAR card; word cards in the brief.
  • Practice rounds with peers before adults.
  • Privacy: students use a school or made-up email and only a city, not a full address.

Background for the teacher

  • Alberta health care employment rose about 15% from 2022 to 2025 (Canada: 9%); construction fell 3.1% and wholesale/retail rose 1.5% from May 2025 to May 2026 (Job Bank Alberta snapshot, May 2026). Update with the latest snapshot each year.
  • Alberta youth employment rules: ages 15–17 can do most jobs with some limits; check alberta.ca before students choose postings.
