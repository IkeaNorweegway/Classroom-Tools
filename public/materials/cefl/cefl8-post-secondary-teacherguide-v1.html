Teacher note

Before Session 1: book the counsellor (or 3–4 high school students) for Session 7. Get your feeder high school's course guide (print or link); students need it for Step 2. Check that school admission pages (NAIT, SAIT, colleges, universities, apprenticeship entrance requirements on tradesecrets.alberta.ca) load on student devices.

Why it is built this way

  • Driving question first. Jordan's missing courses (the hook) make course planning feel urgent and real.
  • Two programs, not one. Planning for a first choice and a different backup teaches students to keep options open, the core of the outcome.
  • Real requirements and real money. Students look up actual admission pages and awards.
  • Networking is done, not described. Step 4 is a real conversation.

Watch out

"If I meet the minimum, I'm in." Moment 1, Question 3. Contrast minimum vs. typical admitted averages for a competitive program. Return to it in the Session 2 warm-up.

Watch out

"Grade 10 choices don't matter yet." Jordan's story and the -1/-2 sequences answer this. Be careful not to imply -2 courses are "worse": they're the right fit for many pathways, and upgrading is possible.

Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

  1. Course map poster. Three columns (Grade 10, 11, 12) of course "tiles", colour-coded by which program each one meets, with a cost box at the bottom.
  2. Flowchart. "If I get into Program A → … If not → Program B → …", with the shared courses at the top.
  3. Planner page. A one-page school-planner layout with milestones (deadlines, scholarship dates, conversation notes).

Access supports (UDL)

  • Read admission pages aloud; the word cards cover the key terms.
  • Choice of product (poster, flowchart, video, planner).
  • Some students plan to work right after Grade 12 or are unsure; a workplace-training program or apprenticeship counts as a full choice.

Background for the teacher

  • Alberta High School Diploma: 100 credits, including ELA 30-1 or 30-2, Social 30-1 or 30-2, a 20-level math, a 20-level science, PE 10, CALM, 10 credits of CTS/fine arts/second languages/PE 20–30/K&E, and 10 more 30-level credits. Check Alberta Education's current requirements.
  • Alexander Rutherford Scholarship: up to $2,500 for Grade 10–12 marks (applications through Alberta Student Aid).
  • U of A 2026–27 tuition: $729.36 per 3-credit course.
