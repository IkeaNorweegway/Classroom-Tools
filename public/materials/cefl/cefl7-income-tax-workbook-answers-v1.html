Teacher note

Common errors to listen for: using 5.95% of the full gross for CPP (forgetting the $135); taking CPP off for workers under 18; reading the wrong row of the tax table; subtracting only one deduction from gross; treating "net" as "gross". The misconception target is Moment 2, Question 3.

Moment 1Four ways to get paid

The situation

Four people work in the same Alberta town. They all earn money, but they get paid in different ways.

  • Jordan works at a hardware store for $20.00 an hour.
  • Priya manages an office. She earns the same amount every pay.
  • Sam sells furniture. Sam gets a part of every sale.
  • Leah is a server. She earns a wage, and customers also give her tips.

Worked example

Jordan earns $20.00 an hour and works 40 hours a week. Jordan is paid every two weeks. What is Jordan's pay before anything is taken off?

  1. Find the hours in one pay period. Two weeks × 40 hours = 80 hours.
  2. Multiply the hours by the wage. 80 × $20.00 = $1,600.00.
  3. This is Jordan's gross pay: what Jordan earned before anything comes off.

Connects to Math: multiplying decimals and finding a percent of a number

2. Sam earns 5% commission on furniture sales. In two weeks, Sam sold $18,000 of furniture.
(a) How much commission did Sam earn? Show your work.

(b) Next month, the store is very quiet. What could happen to Sam's pay? Why?

Answer

(a) 5% of $18,000 → 0.05 × 18,000 = $900.
(b) Sam's pay would go down. Commission depends on sales, so fewer sales means less pay. Commission pay is less predictable than a wage or salary.

3. You are looking for your first job. Which pay type would you want: wage, salary, commission, or tips? Give one reason it is good and one downside.

Answer

Any pay type, with a reason and a downside that fit it. For example:

  • Wage: you get paid for every hour you work; but fewer hours means less pay.
  • Salary: the same pay every time, easy to plan; but working extra hours often does not add pay.
  • Commission: you can earn a lot if you sell a lot; but a slow month means low pay.
  • Tips: good service can bring in extra money; but tips change from day to day and are not guaranteed.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Priya earns a salary of $54,600 a year. She is paid every two weeks, which is 26 pays a year. How much is each pay?

E2. In Alberta, most workers get overtime pay of 1.5 times their wage for hours over 8 in a day or over 44 in a week. Jordan works 8 hours a day for 6 days (48 hours) one week at $20.00 an hour. What is Jordan's pay for that week?

E3. Maya is 16. Students under 18 in Alberta can be paid $13.00 an hour (2026). Maya works 30 hours a week during summer break. What does Maya earn in one week?

Answer

E1. $54,600 ÷ 26 = $2,100 per pay.

E2. No day is over 8 hours, so overtime comes from the week. Regular: 44 × $20.00 = $880.00. Overtime: 4 hours × ($20.00 × 1.5 = $30.00) = $120.00. Total: $1,000.00.

E3. 30 × $13.00 = $390.00. (The $13 student rate applies during school breaks.)

Moment 2Gross pay and net pay

The situation

Jordan just got a first pay stub from the hardware store. Jordan worked 80 hours and expected $1,600. But only $1,293.40 went into Jordan's bank account. Where did the rest go?

Worked example

Check Jordan's pay stub. Jordan is 24 and earned $1,600 gross.

  1. EI: 1.63% of $1,600 → 0.0163 × 1,600 = $26.08.
  2. CPP: Jordan is over 18. $1,600 − $135 = $1,465. Then 0.0595 × 1,465 = $87.17 (rounded to the nearest cent).
  3. Income tax: find $1,600 in the table → $193.35.
  4. Total deductions: $26.08 + $87.17 + $193.35 = $306.60.
  5. Net pay: $1,600.00 − $306.60 = $1,293.40. This matches the pay stub.

2. Ana is 24 and works at a garden centre. Her gross pay this period is $1,200. Find her EI, CPP, income tax, and net pay. Show each step.

Answer

Ana, 24, gross $1,200.

  1. EI: 0.0163 × 1,200 = $19.56
  2. CPP: 1,200 − 135 = 1,065; 0.0595 × 1,065 = $63.37
  3. Income tax (table): $105.35
  4. Total deductions: 19.56 + 63.37 + 105.35 = $188.28
  5. Net pay: 1,200.00 − 188.28 = $1,011.72

3. Ty says: "I worked 40 hours at $13 an hour. So $520 will land in my bank account."
Is Ty right? Use the words gross pay and net pay in your answer.

Answer

Ty is not quite right. $520 is Ty's gross pay. EI ($8.48) comes off first, so Ty's net pay, the amount that lands in the bank, is $511.52. Ty pays no CPP (under 18) and no income tax (his yearly pay is below the tax-free amount), so the gap is small, but it is still there.

Moment 3Where do the deductions go?

The situation

Jordan's $306.60 in deductions did not disappear. Each one goes somewhere, and two of them come back to Jordan later.

Maya is 16 and earns $312 every two weeks. Her pay stub shows $0 income tax. Why?

Worked example

Why does Maya pay no income tax?

  1. Find Maya's pay for a whole year. She is paid 26 times a year. 26 × $312 = $8,112.
  2. Compare it to the tax-free amounts. Canada does not tax the first $16,452. Alberta does not tax the first $22,769 (2026).
  3. $8,112 is below both lines. So Maya pays $0 income tax.
  4. Maya is under 18, so no CPP comes off either. Only EI comes off her pay.

Canada's income tax is progressive: people who earn more pay a bigger share of their pay in tax. The tax-free amount is one reason.

1. Match each situation to the deduction that helps. Write income tax, CPP, or EI.
(a) Ravi's store closed, and he lost his job.
(b) Grace is 68 and retired. She gets a cheque every month.
(c) Theo broke his arm. A hospital put on a cast.

Answer

(a) EI (b) CPP (c) income tax

2. Kai is 19. Kai earns $600 every two weeks, all year (26 pays).
(a) What is Kai's yearly pay?
(b) Does Kai pay federal income tax? Alberta income tax? Explain using the tax-free amounts.
(c) Which deductions do come off Kai's pay?

Answer

(a) 26 × $600 = $15,600.
(b) No federal income tax: $15,600 is below $16,452. No Alberta income tax: $15,600 is below $22,769. (The table also shows $0 for $600.)
(c) CPP and EI. Kai is 19, so CPP comes off; EI comes off at any age.

3. Lin earns $15,000 a year. Omar earns $45,000 a year. Who pays a bigger share of their pay in income tax? Why do you think Canada sets it up this way?

Answer

Omar pays a bigger share. Lin's $15,000 is below the tax-free amounts, so Lin pays $0 income tax. Omar pays tax on the part of his pay above each line (about $5,800, or roughly 13% of his pay). Reasons Canada does this (any one, explained): people with low incomes need all their money for basics like food and rent; people who earn more can afford to pay a bigger share; it is seen as fairer. Accept a reasoned disagreement.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Some jobs take off other deductions too. Sort these into must come off (the law says so) or depends on the job: union dues · income tax · health and dental insurance · EI · retirement savings plan · CPP

E2. Noor earns $900 every two weeks. Find Noor's yearly pay. Is it above the federal line? The Alberta line? Look at the table: does that explain why Noor's income tax is small?

Answer

E1. Must come off: income tax, CPP, EI. Depends on the job: union dues, health and dental insurance, retirement savings plan.

E2. 26 × $900 = $23,400. Above the federal line ($16,452) by $6,948 and above the Alberta line ($22,769) by only $631. So Noor pays some federal tax and only a little Alberta tax, which is why the table shows a small amount ($39.35).

Moment 4What do taxes pay for?

The situation

In 2026, the Government of Alberta planned to spend about $84 billion. About $34.4 billion was for health and about $10.8 billion was for education. That money comes from income tax and other sources.

Worked example

Jordan pays about $1,506 a year in Alberta income tax. If it were split the same way Alberta spends, how much would go to health?

  1. Health gets about 41% of Alberta's spending (34.4 ÷ 84 ≈ 0.41).
  2. 41% of $1,506 → 0.41 × 1,506 = $617.46.
  3. So about $617 of Jordan's Alberta income tax would go to health.

This is a model. Tax money goes into one big pot, but this shows the size of each part.

Connects to Social Studies: how governments collect and spend money

1. Use Jordan's $1,506. About how much would go to education (13%)?

Answer

0.13 × 1,506 = $195.78 → about $196.

2. Name three services you or your family used this month that taxes help pay for. For each, say who pays for it: the Government of Canada or the Government of Alberta.

ServiceCanada or Alberta?
Answer

Any three real services with a sensible government. For example: school or school bus (Alberta), hospital, doctor visit, or ambulance (Alberta), highway (Alberta), Canada Child Benefit (Canada), armed forces or passport office (Canada). Accept "both" with a reason. City services such as a library, local roads, or the fire hall are mostly paid for by the town or city; accept them with that explanation.

3. Some people say less tax should be taken off workers' pay. Others say more should be taken off to pay for better services. What do you think?
Write a claim, one reason, and one trade-off (what would be lost).

Answer

Look for a clear claim, a reason connected to pay or services, and a real trade-off. For example: "Less tax should come off pay, because workers could keep more of what they earn for rent and food. The trade-off is that the government would have less money for hospitals and schools, so wait times might get longer."

Data used

All numbers are for 2026. They change every year.

  • Alberta minimum wage $15.00/hour; $13.00/hour for students under 18 (up to 28 hours in a school week, or during school breaks).
  • Federal income tax: 14% on the first $58,523 of taxable income; tax-free (basic personal) amount $16,452.
  • Alberta income tax: 8% on the first $61,200; tax-free (basic personal) amount $22,769.
  • CPP: 5.95% of pay above $3,500 a year (about $135 every two weeks); starts the month after a worker turns 18.
  • EI: 1.63% of pay, any age.
  • Alberta Budget 2026–27: about $84 billion total; health $34.4 billion; education $10.8 billion.
  • People and businesses in this workbook are made up; the rates are real.
