Where Does My Paycheque Go?

Full worked solutions · Outcome 7-IT · Alberta draft curriculum (2026)

Teacher note

Common errors to listen for: using 5.95% of the full gross for CPP (forgetting the $135); taking CPP off for workers under 18; reading the wrong row of the tax table; subtracting only one deduction from gross; treating "net" as "gross". The misconception target is Moment 2, Question 3.

Moment 1Four ways to get paid

1. Hourly pay: 24 × $15.00 = $360.00. Total with tips: $360.00 + $186 = $546.00.

2. (a) 5% of $18,000 → 0.05 × 18,000 = $900.
(b) Sam's pay would go down. Commission depends on sales, so fewer sales means less pay. Commission pay is less predictable than a wage or salary.

3. Any pay type, with a reason and a downside that fit it. For example:

  • Wage: you get paid for every hour you work; but fewer hours means less pay.
  • Salary: the same pay every time, easy to plan; but working extra hours often does not add pay.
  • Commission: you can earn a lot if you sell a lot; but a slow month means low pay.
  • Tips: good service can bring in extra money; but tips change from day to day and are not guaranteed.

Answer

E1. $54,600 ÷ 26 = $2,100 per pay.

E2. No day is over 8 hours, so overtime comes from the week. Regular: 44 × $20.00 = $880.00. Overtime: 4 hours × ($20.00 × 1.5 = $30.00) = $120.00. Total: $1,000.00.

E3. 30 × $13.00 = $390.00. (The $13 student rate applies during school breaks.)

Moment 3Where do the deductions go?

1. (a) EI (b) CPP (c) income tax

2. (a) 26 × $600 = $15,600.
(b) No federal income tax: $15,600 is below $16,452. No Alberta income tax: $15,600 is below $22,769. (The table also shows $0 for $600.)
(c) CPP and EI. Kai is 19, so CPP comes off; EI comes off at any age.

3. Omar pays a bigger share. Lin's $15,000 is below the tax-free amounts, so Lin pays $0 income tax. Omar pays tax on the part of his pay above each line (about $5,800, or roughly 13% of his pay). Reasons Canada does this (any one, explained): people with low incomes need all their money for basics like food and rent; people who earn more can afford to pay a bigger share; it is seen as fairer. Accept a reasoned disagreement.

Answer

E1. Must come off: income tax, CPP, EI. Depends on the job: union dues, health and dental insurance, retirement savings plan.

E2. 26 × $900 = $23,400. Above the federal line ($16,452) by $6,948 and above the Alberta line ($22,769) by only $631. So Noor pays some federal tax and only a little Alberta tax, which is why the table shows a small amount ($39.35).

Moment 4What do taxes pay for?

1. 0.13 × 1,506 = $195.78 → about $196.

2. Any three real services with a sensible government. For example: school or school bus (Alberta), hospital, doctor visit, or ambulance (Alberta), highway (Alberta), Canada Child Benefit (Canada), armed forces or passport office (Canada). Accept "both" with a reason. City services such as a library, local roads, or the fire hall are mostly paid for by the town or city; accept them with that explanation.

3. Look for a clear claim, a reason connected to pay or services, and a real trade-off. For example: "Less tax should come off pay, because workers could keep more of what they earn for rent and food. The trade-off is that the government would have less money for hospitals and schools, so wait times might get longer."
