Moment 1A job or a career?

The situation

Mia is 34. She runs a daycare in Red Deer. When she was 13, she babysat for her neighbours. People asked her, "Did you always want to run a daycare?" She laughed. "No. It just grew, one step at a time."

Worked example

Is "babysitter" a job or a career?

  1. A job is one role or position. You do certain tasks, often for pay.
  2. A career is the whole path of learning, work, and experiences over many years.
  3. Babysitting is one role. So it is a job. It is also a step in Mia's career, because it helped her learn she liked working with kids.

1. Write job or career beside each one.
(a) Working at a grocery store checkout this summer.
(b) Thirty years of working with animals: farm helper, vet tech, then clinic manager.
(c) Coaching a kids' soccer team.

2. Look at Mia's path. Pick one step. How might it have helped her get to the next step?

3. A student says: "You pick your career once, at the end of high school, and then you're done." Use Mia's path to explain why this is not quite right.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Draw a career path with 4 steps for someone who ends up as a chef. Label each step job or learning.

E2. Ask an adult at home about their career path. Write two of the steps.

Moment 2Skills that travel

The situation

A camp counsellor and a nurse have very different jobs. But both need to stay calm, talk clearly, and work with a team. These are transferable skills: skills you can carry from one job to another.

Worked example

Mia worked as a camp counsellor. What transferable skills did she build, and where else could she use them?

  1. Think about the tasks. She planned activities, kept kids safe, and solved fights.
  2. Name the skills. Planning = time management. Solving fights = problem solving and communication.
  3. Find other jobs that need them. A nurse, a teacher, and a store manager all use these skills.

1. Read this job ad. Circle the transferable skills.
"Pool lifeguard wanted. Must stay calm, talk clearly with swimmers and parents, show up on time, and work well with other guards."
Then list the skills you circled.

2. Name one thing you do now (a sport, a club, a chore, a hobby). Which transferable skill does it build? How?

3. Pick one transferable skill you want to get better at. Write two things you could do this month to practise it.

Extra practice · optional

E1. Name two very different careers that both need problem solving. Give one example of a problem each might solve.

E2. Which transferable skill do you think matters most in every job? Give a reason.

Moment 3Interests change, and that's OK

The situation

When Jayden was 8, he wanted to be a hockey player. At 13, he loves building things in shop class and fixing bikes. His values are changing too: he now cares a lot about working outside and helping his town.

Worked example

How can Jayden check whether a career fits him right now?

  1. List what he is interested in: building, fixing, being outside.
  2. List what he values: working outside, helping his town.
  3. Check a career against both lists. Electrician: building and fixing ✓, often outside ✓, helps the town ✓. A good fit for now.
  4. Remember it may change. Checking again next year is part of a good plan.

3. Jayden's friend says: "If you change your mind about a career, you wasted your time." Do you agree? Use the idea of transferable skills in your answer.

Moment 4What shapes a career choice?

The situation

Careers need different amounts of school or training. They also pay differently. Here are five real Alberta careers.

Worked example

Jayden is comparing electrician and vet tech. What else should he think about besides pay?

  1. Pay: electrician $38.41 an hour; vet tech $25.57. Difference: 38.41 − 25.57 = $12.84 an hour.
  2. Training: electrician is a 4-year apprenticeship, paid while you learn. Vet tech is a 2-year college diploma.
  3. Interests and values: Jayden likes building and being outside, which fits electrician better.
  4. Access: Is training close to home? Can he afford it? (Both are offered in Alberta.)

A good career decision weighs all of these, not just pay.

Connects to Math: subtracting and comparing decimals

1. How much more per hour does a registered nurse earn than an early childhood educator? Show your work.

2. Name three things besides pay that could change someone's career choice.

3. Pick two careers from the chart. Which would you choose right now? Use pay, training, and your interests in your answer.

Watch out

A student says: "The career that pays the most is always the best choice." What is missing from this idea?
