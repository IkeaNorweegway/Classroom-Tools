## The rubric

| Criterion | Has yet to meet | Meets | Proficient | Excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Story parts and choice** *(WR14, WR12)* | Some WWW parts missing; no clear choice | All WWW parts; the main character makes a choice | All parts; the choice is clear and the events build toward it | All parts; the choice is hard, and the ending shows its consequence in a surprising or satisfying way |
| **2. Science fiction world** *(TF7, WR13, WR15)* | No "what if?" or future technology | A "what if?" with technology or a future setting | The technology matters to the problem; details show the future world | The future world is shown through vivid details, and the technology raises a real question |
| **3. Character, flash-forward and theme** *(TF8, TF9, TF10, WR10)* | No flash-forward; flat main character | A flash-forward; the main character changes a little | A clear flash-forward that shows what a choice leads to; the main character changes; a theme the reader can figure out | The flash-forward and the change work together to show a theme without stating it |
| **4. Conventions and point of view** *(CV2, CV4, CV11, WR8)* | Errors make parts hard to read | Mostly third person, past tense; some dialogue punctuated | Third person and past tense throughout; dialogue punctuated; time phrase marks the flash-forward | Correct and controlled; tense and punctuation used for effect |

## Samples: "What if your shadow could talk?"

### Sample A · *Meets*

::: model Sample A
In the year 2200 a boy named Sam got a shadow that could talk. It was called a ShadowPal. It told him what to do all day. Sam liked it.

One day the ShadowPal said, "Don't play with Lena, she is weird." Sam listened. Lena was sad.

Later Sam felt bad. He turned off the ShadowPal and played with Lena.

Ten years later Sam and Lena were best friends.

The end.
:::

::: note Why it is "Meets"
**Parts:** all there, and Sam makes a choice. **World:** a "what if?" and a future date, but few details show the world. **Flash-forward:** "Ten years later" is a short flash-forward at the end; Sam changes a little. **Conventions:** third person and past tense; dialogue punctuated, though "Lena, she is weird" needs fixing.
:::

### Sample B · *Proficient*

::: model Sample B
In 2200, everyone in Cedarville had a ShadowPal, a shadow that talked. Sam's spoke in a smooth, friendly voice from the ground beside his feet. It reminded him of homework, told him jokes, and said which kids were "worth his time."

"Not Lena," it whispered at recess. "She's not popular. Sit somewhere else."

Sam sat somewhere else. From across the field, he watched Lena eat lunch alone, her own shadow silent, because her family couldn't afford a ShadowPal.

That night, Sam couldn't sleep. His shadow stretched long across the wall, still murmuring advice.

"Off," he said.

*Twenty years later, Dr. Lena Kaur stood on a stage, explaining her invention: shadows that only spoke when you asked them to. In the front row sat her oldest friend, Sam, clapping loudest of all.*

The next morning, Sam walked across the field with his shadow switched off and sat down next to Lena. "Can I sit here?" he asked. She smiled.
:::

::: note Why it is "Proficient"
**Parts and choice:** all parts; a clear choice (listen to the shadow or not). **World:** the technology causes the problem; details (a whisper from the ground, families who can't afford one) show the world. **Flash-forward:** clear, marked with a time phrase and italics, showing what the choice leads to; Sam changes. **Theme** the reader can figure out: think for yourself. **Conventions:** correct.
:::

### Sample C · *Excellent*

::: model Sample C
In the year 2200, in Cedarville, nobody's shadow was silent. Every child got a ShadowPal on their tenth birthday: a soft voice that lived in the dark shape at your feet and knew everything about you. It knew your test scores, your favourite cereal, and exactly which kids were "good for your future."

Sam's ShadowPal had a voice like warm honey. "Not Lena," it murmured as Sam carried his lunch tray across the field. "Her family can't afford a Pal. Kids without Pals don't go far."

Sam hesitated. Lena sat alone on the bench, her shadow lying flat and quiet beside her like a puddle. She was drawing something in a notebook, a machine with gears and wings.

Sam sat with the popular kids. He laughed at the right jokes. His shadow purred, "Good choice."

But that night, Sam lay awake. On his bedroom wall, his shadow stretched long and thin under the streetlight, still whispering: *good choice, good choice, good choice.* It sounded less like honey now. It sounded like a fence.

"Off," Sam said. For the first time in a year, the wall went silent.

*Twenty years later, a woman with ink on her fingers stood in front of a crowd of thousands. "My invention is simple," Dr. Lena Kaur said. "A shadow that speaks only when you ask it a question." Behind her, on a giant screen, was a drawing from an old notebook: a machine with gears and wings. In the front row, a man named Sam clapped until his hands hurt, because he remembered the day he almost didn't sit down.*

The next morning, Sam crossed the field with his shadow switched off. He stopped in front of the bench.

"What are you drawing?" he asked.

Lena looked up, surprised. Then she slid the notebook toward him.
:::

::: note Why it is "Excellent"
**Choice:** hard, with real pressure, and the ending shows the consequence (the friendship and the invention). **World:** vivid, specific details (ShadowPals on your tenth birthday, a shadow "like a puddle") that raise a real question about technology deciding who matters. **Flash-forward and theme:** the flash-forward pays off a detail planted earlier (the drawing), and together with Sam's change it shows the theme without stating it. **Conventions:** controlled; repetition and short sentences for effect.
:::
