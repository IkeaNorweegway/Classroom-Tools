## 1 · What makes science fiction?

**Learning target:** I can tell what makes a story science fiction. *(TF7, TF2)*

::: wordbank
a hero's impossible size or strength
a robot could do your homework
choices
Every student gets a robot
Hex, a homework robot
in the future or a changed world
in the past, in a real place
Maya has to choose whether to let Hex do her work
science and technology
scientific
technology
:::

**Science fiction** is a kind of modern fantasy that describes [[technology]], future situations, or real or imagined [[scientific]] events. It starts with a "**What if…?**" question.

| Feature | *The Homework Helper* (Story 1) |
| --- | --- |
| A "what if?" question | What if [[a robot could do your homework]]? |
| Technology or science that doesn't exist yet | [[Hex, a homework robot]] |
| A future or changed world | [[Every student gets a robot]] |
| It shows how the technology changes people's [[choices]] | [[Maya has to choose whether to let Hex do her work]] |

| | Tall tale | Science fiction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The amazing part comes from… | [[a hero's impossible size or strength]] | [[science and technology]] |
| Usually set… | [[in the past, in a real place]] | [[in the future or a changed world]] |

::: myth
WRONG: "Science fiction is just stories with spaceships and aliens."
WHY: Movies show a lot of space battles.
RIGHT: Science fiction is about how science and technology change **people's lives and choices**. A homework robot is science fiction too.
:::

**Quick check.** Write a "what if?" question that could start a science fiction story.

::: frame
What if ___ ?
:::

(lines 2)

=> Example: What if you could download a new skill, like swimming, in one second?

## 2 · Third-person point of view

**Learning target:** I can tell who is telling a story and write in the third person. *(TF3, WR14)*

::: wordbank
do and say
he, she, they, character names
I, me, my, we
think and feel
:::

| Point of view | Who tells it | Words that show it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **First person** | A character in the story | [[I, me, my, we]] | "I found an old phone." |
| **Third person** | A narrator outside the story | [[he, she, they, character names]] | "Jamal found an old phone." |

In **third person**, the narrator can tell us what characters [[do and say]], and sometimes what they [[think and feel]]. In *The Homework Helper*, the narrator tells us Maya felt "a small, cold feeling she couldn't name."

**Quick check.** Rewrite in the third person: *I pressed the button, and the screen glowed blue. I couldn't believe it.*

::: frame
___ pressed the button, and the screen glowed blue. ___ couldn't believe it.
:::

(lines 2)

=> Jamal pressed the button, and the screen glowed blue. He couldn't believe it.

## 3 · Theme is a sentence

**Learning target:** I can state a theme as a sentence and support it with evidence. *(TF9, CO8, CO9)*

::: wordbank
learn or change
message
sentence
Shortcuts can take away the learning that makes you proud.
Technology can help, but it can't replace time with people who love you.
:::

The **theme** is the underlying [[message]] of a story: what it says about life. A **topic** is one word. A **theme** is a [[sentence]].

| Story | Topic (one word) | Theme (a sentence) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Homework Helper | learning | [[Shortcuts can take away the learning that makes you proud.]] |
| Kiko's Grandmother | family | [[Technology can help, but it can't replace time with people who love you.]] |

**How to find a theme:** What does the main character [[learn or change]]? What would the author want you to remember?

::: myth
WRONG: "The theme is *friendship*." (or *technology*, *family*)
WHY: Those words name what the story is about.
RIGHT: That's a topic. A theme says something about it: *Real friends tell you the truth even when it's hard.*
:::

**Quick check.** Write a theme sentence for *Under the Dome*.

::: frame
The story shows that ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> Example: Being safe and comfortable isn't everything; sometimes we need the things that make us feel alive.

## 4 · Setting and context

**Learning target:** I can explain how the setting and context shape what characters think and do. *(CO15, CO18, CO14)*

::: wordbank
Born before the dome; remembers snow days
Has always lived safely under the dome; outside storms are dangerous
He stays home: "Why would anyone want to be cold?"
live
She goes to the Commons and laughs in the snow
why
:::

The **setting** is when and where a story happens. In science fiction, the setting often changes how people [[live]].

**Context** is the situation around a character: the world, their family, the rules. It helps explain [[why]] characters act as they do.

| Character | Context | How it shapes their actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theo's father (Story 2) | [[Has always lived safely under the dome; outside storms are dangerous]] | [[He stays home: "Why would anyone want to be cold?"]] |
| Mémère (Story 2) | [[Born before the dome; remembers snow days]] | [[She goes to the Commons and laughs in the snow]] |

**Quick check.** Why do you think Theo is curious about snow when most people stay home? Use the context.

::: frame
Theo is curious because ___ . In paragraph ___ , ___ .
:::

(lines 3)

=> Because his great-grandmother told him snow days were the best days of her life and showed him photos of children laughing in snow (¶2, ¶4), so he wants to feel it himself.

## 5 · Round and flat characters

**Learning target:** I can tell round and flat characters apart and explain how a character changes. *(TF10, CO11)*

::: wordbank
change
Flat
It does the same thing, the same way, the whole story
Round
She goes from "now we don't have to worry" to "we should all visit more"
She goes from letting Hex do her work to choosing to learn it herself
stays the same
think or do something different
think or want one thing
:::

A **round** character is interesting and layered, and may [[change]] during the story. A **flat** character [[stays the same]].

| Character | Round or flat? | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maya (Story 1) | [[Round]] | [[She goes from letting Hex do her work to choosing to learn it herself]] |
| Hex (Story 1) | [[Flat]] | [[It does the same thing, the same way, the whole story]] |
| Kiko's mother (Story 3) | [[Round]] | [[She goes from "now we don't have to worry" to "we should all visit more"]] |

**How a character changes:** at the start they [[think or want one thing]]; something happens; at the end they [[think or do something different]].

**Quick check.** Is the CareBot round or flat? Explain.

::: frame
The CareBot is ___ because ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> Flat: it does its job the same way from start to finish and doesn't learn or change (it doesn't understand why anyone would follow frogs).

## 6 · Flash-forward

**Learning target:** I can recognize a flash-forward and explain why an author uses one. *(TF8)*

::: wordbank
future
interrupts
leads to
tense or style
:::

A **flash-forward** [[interrupts]] the story to take the reader forward in time to events in the [[future]]. Then the story usually comes back to the present.

**Signals of a flash-forward:** a time phrase (*Sixty years later…*, *One day, long after…*), a change in [[tense or style]] (italics, a new paragraph), older versions of characters.

::: model Story 4, the flash-forward
*"Sixty years later, the old man sat by a clear blue river in a village in Ghana and watched children fill their buckets."*
:::

**Why use one?** To show the reader what a choice [[leads to]], to build suspense or meaning, or to make the present moment feel important.

**Quick check.** In Story 4, where does the flash-forward start and end? What does it show?

::: frame
It starts when ___ and ends when ___ . It shows that ___ .
:::

(lines 3)

=> It starts at "Sixty years later" and ends before "Back in 2027." It shows that Jamal's science fair choice led to clean water for people around the world.

## 7 · Planning: POW + WWW + a flash-forward

**Learning target:** I can plan a science fiction story with all the parts and a flash-forward. *(WR3, WR14, WR12)*

::: strategy POW
**P** **Pick** my idea (a "what if?")
**O** **Organize** my notes (WWW, What = 2, How = 2 + flash-forward)
**W** **Write** and say more
:::

::: strategy WWW, What = 2, How = 2
**W** **Who** is the main character? Who else?
**W** **When** does it take place? (In the future!)
**W** **Where** does it take place?
**What** **What** does the main character want to do?
**What** **What** happens then?
**How** **How** does it end?
**How** **How** do the characters feel?
:::

**Add for science fiction:** the **technology** (what it does, and its problem) · the **choice** the main character makes · the **flash-forward** (where it goes, what it shows) · the **theme** (one sentence).

**Quick check.** From memory, write what POW stands for and the seven WWW questions as single words.

::: frame
P ___ · O ___ · W ___ / Who, When, ___ , What, ___ , How, ___
:::

(lines 3)

=> Pick my idea, Organize my notes, Write and say more. Who, When, Where, What does the character want, What happens, How does it end, How do they feel.

## 8 · Showing a future world

**Learning target:** I can show a future world through details, and keep my verb tenses clear. *(WR13, WR15, CV4)*

::: wordbank
past
when
:::

Don't tell the reader "It was the future." **Show** it with details:

| Show the future through… | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Technology** in everyday life | "The weather system announced the day's forecast in a calm voice." |
| **A date or how old something is** | "In the year 2091…" |
| **What people do differently** | "He had never been cold." |
| **Sensory details** | "The panel slid open with a hiss." |

**Tense:** my story is told in the [[past]] tense (*Jamal **found** the phone*). A flash-forward jumps ahead, but stays in a clear tense too; a time phrase tells the reader [[when]] we are.

**Quick check.** Turn this telling sentence into showing: *Kiko's school was very futuristic.*

::: frame
At Kiko's school, ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> Example: At Kiko's school, the doors slid open when they recognized your face, and the lunch menu floated in the air above each table.
