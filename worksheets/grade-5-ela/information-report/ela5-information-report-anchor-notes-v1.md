## 1 · Text features

**Learning target:** I can use text features to find and understand important information. *(TF5, TF1)*

An **information text** teaches [[facts]] about a topic. Its purpose is to [[inform]], not to entertain. Text features help the reader [[find what is important]].

| Text feature | What it does | Example in the reader |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heading** | Tells what a [[section]] is about | "Two loops" (Text 2) |
| **Bold word** | Marks an [[important word]] to learn | **alveoli** (Text 3) |
| **Diagram** (flow chart) | Shows how something [[works or moves]], step by step | "The path of food" (Text 6) |
| **Caption** | Explains what a [[picture or diagram]] shows | "How blood travels (simplified)" |
| **Sidebar** ("Did you know?") | Adds an extra, interesting [[fact]] | The stirrup bone (Text 4) |
| **Glossary** | Gives the [[meaning]] of key words | Text 1 glossary |

::: myth
WRONG: "Text features are decoration. I can skip them."
WHY: They look separate from the paragraphs.
RIGHT: Text features often hold the most important ideas. Read the headings first to preview, and read every caption.
:::

**Quick check.** You want to know how long the small intestine is. Which text feature would help you find it fastest, and why?

%% frame: I would look at the ___ because it ___ .

(lines 2)

=> The heading "Step 4: the small intestine" in Text 6, because headings tell what each section is about, so I can jump straight to it.

## 2 · Topic and subtopics

**Learning target:** I can find the topic and subtopics of an information text. *(TF11, CO5)*

The **topic** is what the whole text is about. **Subtopics** are the smaller parts of the topic. Each subtopic usually gets its own [[heading]] or [[paragraph]].

| | Text 4: *Bones and Muscles* |
| --- | --- |
| **Topic** | [[Bones and muscles / the skeletal and muscular systems]] |
| **Subtopic 1** | [[Bones: the frame]] |
| **Subtopic 2** | [[Muscles: the movers]] |
| **Subtopic 3** | [[How they are alike and different]] |
| **Subtopic 4** | [[Keeping them strong]] |

::: example Watch me
I read the title first: *Bones and Muscles*. That's the topic. Then I read only the headings, in order. Each heading is a subtopic. Last, I check: does every paragraph fit under one of the headings? Yes, so my map is complete.
:::

**Quick check.** What are the topic and two subtopics of Text 2?

%% frame: The topic is ___ . Two subtopics are ___ and ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Topic: the heart / the circulatory system. Subtopics (any two): a pump with four rooms; two loops; blood vessels; keeping it healthy.

## 3 · Main idea and summary

**Learning target:** I can find the main idea of a section and summarize it with key details. *(CO4, CO5)*

The **main idea** is the most important point a section makes. **Key details** are the facts that [[support / prove]] it. A summary of an information text gives the main idea and the [[most important details]], in my own words.

| Section | Main idea | Two key details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text 3, "Why the air sacs matter" | [[Oxygen and carbon dioxide swap between the air sacs and the blood]] | [[The walls are very thin; oxygen goes into the blood and carbon dioxide comes out]] |

**Main idea test:** if I say my main idea, could every detail in the section fit under it? If not, my main idea is [[too small]].

::: myth
WRONG: "The main idea is the first sentence."
WHY: Many paragraphs start with it, so we expect it there.
RIGHT: Sometimes the main idea is in the middle or at the end, or it isn't written at all. Ask: what is this whole section mostly about?
:::

**Quick check.** Write the main idea of Text 5's section "The message highway" in one sentence.

%% frame: This section is mostly about how ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Nerves carry messages between the body and the brain through the spinal cord, and some messages come back fast as reflexes.

## 4 · Cause and effect

**Learning target:** I can find causes and effects in an information text. *(TF11, CO5)*

A **cause** is why something happens. An **effect** is [[what happens]].

**Signal words:** because · so · as a result · therefore · since · that is why · when ___, ___

| Cause | Signal word | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The diaphragm tightens and moves down | so | [[air rushes into the lungs]] |
| Your muscles use oxygen faster when you run | because of this | [[you breathe faster and more deeply]] |
| [[The walls of the air sacs and capillaries are very thin]] | because | oxygen can pass into the blood |

::: myth
WRONG: "If two things happen one after the other, the first one caused the second."
WHY: Order in time feels like cause.
RIGHT: Look for a reason in the text (a signal word or an explanation). Things can happen in order without one causing the other.
:::

**Quick check.** From Text 3: why is the air in your lungs cleaner than the air outside?

%% frame: Because ___ , the air ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Because hairs and sticky mucus in the nose trap dust, the air is cleaner by the time it reaches the lungs.

## 5 · Compare and contrast

**Learning target:** I can explain how two things are alike and different. *(TF11, CO9)*

**Compare** = how things are [[alike]]. **Contrast** = how they are [[different]].

**Signal words:** both · also · similarly · but · however · unlike · while · on the other hand

| | Bones | Muscles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alike | [[living tissue; need food and oxygen; get stronger with use]] | [[living tissue; need food and oxygen; get stronger with use]] |
| Different | [[hard; don't change shape; hold you up and protect]] | [[soft; contract and relax; move the bones]] |

**Write it as a sentence:** *Bones and muscles are **both** living tissues, **but** bones are hard while muscles can contract.*

**Quick check.** Compare arteries and veins (Text 2) in one sentence using **both** and **but**.

%% frame: Arteries and veins are both ___ , but arteries ___ while veins ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Arteries and veins are both blood vessels, but arteries carry blood away from the heart while veins carry it back.

## 6 · Science words: clues and parts

**Learning target:** I can work out the meaning of a new science word from context and word parts. *(VO4, VO11, VO12)*

**Context clues** are hints in the sentence around a new word:

| Clue type | Example | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition** right after it | "tiny air sacs called **alveoli**" | [[tiny air sacs in the lungs]] |
| **Parentheses** | "your windpipe (also called the **trachea**)" | [[the windpipe]] |
| **Example** | "smooth muscle lines your stomach and blood vessels" | [[muscle inside organs]] |

**Word parts:**

| Part | Means | Word | So the word means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ex- | out | exhale | [[breathe out]] |
| -ous | full of | nervous | [[full of nerves; to do with nerves]] |
| circ- | circle, around | circulatory | [[moving around in a circle]] |
| trans- | across | transport | [[carry across from one place to another]] |

**Quick check.** "The **diaphragm**, a dome-shaped muscle under your lungs, does the work." What is a diaphragm? What clue told you?

%% frame: A diaphragm is ___ . The clue is ___ .

(lines 2)

=> A dome-shaped muscle under the lungs that makes you breathe. The definition right after the word, between commas.

## 7 · Can I trust this source?

**Learning target:** I can decide whether a source is reliable. *(WR21, CO17, TF12)*

Ask four questions about every source:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** wrote it? Are they an expert? | A doctor or science group knows more than an [[advertiser]] |
| **Why** was it written? To inform, or to [[sell / persuade]]? | Sellers may only tell you the good parts |
| **When** was it written? | Science learns new things, so [[newer]] is often better |
| **Can I check it** in another good source? | If two good sources agree, the fact is more [[reliable]] |

::: example Watch me
A website says, "Brain-Boost Gummies make kids 50% smarter!" **Who:** the company that sells the gummies. **Why:** to sell them. **Can I check it?** I can't find the 50% anywhere else. This source is **not reliable** for my report.
:::

::: myth
WRONG: "If it's on the internet (or in a video), it's true."
WHY: Anything looks official on a screen.
RIGHT: Anyone can post anything. Check who made it, why, and whether a second good source agrees.
:::

**Quick check.** Which is more reliable for a report on bones: a children's hospital website or a video by a company selling vitamins? Explain using one of the four questions.

%% frame: The ___ is more reliable because ___ .

(lines 2)

=> The children's hospital website: it's written by experts to inform, while the company wants to sell vitamins.

## 8 · Notes in my own words

**Learning target:** I can take notes in my own words and give credit to my sources. *(WR23, WR24, WR22)*

**Notes are not sentences.** Write [[key words]] and short phrases, then close the book and say it in your own words.

::: example Watch me
**Text:** "The stomach is a stretchy muscle bag. It churns food and mixes it with strong acid and juices that break it down further."
**My notes:** stomach = stretchy muscle bag · churns food · acid + juices → break down
**In my own words:** The stomach squeezes food and mixes it with acid to break it down.
:::

**Copying** a writer's sentences and calling them yours is called **plagiarism**. Instead, I write facts in [[my own words]] and list my [[sources]] at the end.

**How to list a source:** Title · author or group (if given) · type of source. *Example: Body Systems Reader, Text 6 · our class.*

**Quick check.** Turn this sentence into notes (key words only): "Bones are alive. They have blood vessels and nerves, and they can repair themselves when they break."

%% frame: bones = ___ · have ___ · can ___

(lines 2)

=> bones alive · blood vessels + nerves · repair when broken

## 9 · Planning paragraphs: POW + TIDE

**Learning target:** I can use POW + TIDE to plan a paragraph for each subtopic. *(WR3, WR4, WR20)*

::: strategy POW: what good writers do
**P** **Pick** my idea (topic and subtopics)
**O** **Organize** my notes (TIDE for each paragraph)
**W** **Write** and say more
:::

::: strategy TIDE: the parts of an information paragraph
**T** **Topic** sentence: tells the subtopic
**I** **Important** evidence: two or three facts
**D** **Detail**: an example, a number or a comparison that explains
**E** **Ending** sentence: wraps up the paragraph
:::

| TIDE | Model report, "The heart" paragraph (Text 7) |
| --- | --- |
| T | [[The heart is the engine of the circulatory system.]] |
| I | [[It is a muscle the size of your fist; it beats about 100,000 times a day; it has four chambers and valves]] |
| D | [[For example, the right side sends blood to the lungs and the left side to the rest of the body]] |
| E | [[Without this pump, blood would not move at all.]] |

**Self-talk:** "What's my subtopic?" · "Do I have T, I, D and E?" · "That fact is copied, so let me say it my way." · "Good, that paragraph is done."

**Quick check.** From memory, write what each letter of TIDE stands for.

%% frame: T = ___ · I = ___ · D = ___ · E = ___

(lines 2)

=> Topic sentence, Important evidence, Detail, Ending sentence.

## 10 · Beginnings, endings and transitions

**Learning target:** I can write an introduction and conclusion and connect my paragraphs. *(WR4, WR5)*

**Introduction:** a **hook** + the topic. Hooks: a question ("Did you know your heart never takes a day off?"), a surprising fact, or a picture in words ("Imagine a delivery truck…").

**Conclusion:** sum up the [[main idea]] + leave the reader with a [[thought]] (a tip, a question, or a link back to the hook).

**Transitions** connect ideas:

| To add | To give an example | To show cause | To finish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| also, in addition, another | for example, such as | because, so, as a result | finally, in the end, overall |

::: model Text 7, conclusion
"The heart, the blood and the blood vessels work as a team to keep every cell alive. The next time you feel your pulse, remember: that beat is the delivery truck making its rounds."
:::

How does the conclusion link back to the introduction? [[It returns to the delivery-truck idea from the hook.]]

**Quick check.** Write a hook for a report on the brain.

%% frame: Did you know that your brain ___ ?

(lines 2)

=> Example: Right now, while you read this, your brain is also keeping your heart beating and remembering your best friend's name.

## 11 · Text features in my report

**Learning target:** I can add text features that help my reader. *(TF6, WR9)*

Choose at least **three** for your published report:

| Feature | How to make it |
| --- | --- |
| **Title and headings** | One heading per subtopic, short and clear |
| **Labelled diagram** | Draw it, then label the parts with lines; add a **caption** that says what it shows |
| **Bold words + glossary** | Bold 3–5 key words; define them at the end in your own words |
| **Sidebar** | A "Did you know?" box with one surprising fact |
| **Sources list** | Every source you used, at the end |

A text feature must [[help the reader]], not just decorate the page.

**Quick check.** What caption would you write under a diagram of the lungs?

%% frame: This diagram shows ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Example: The path of air from the nose to the air sacs in the lungs.
