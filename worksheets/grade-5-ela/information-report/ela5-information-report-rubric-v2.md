## The rubric

| Criterion | Has yet to meet | Meets | Proficient | Excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Organization** *(WR4, WR5)* | Facts are listed without clear paragraphs, or there is no introduction or conclusion | Introduction, body paragraphs and a conclusion; each paragraph is mostly about one subtopic | Hook + topic in the introduction; each paragraph is one subtopic with all four TIDE parts; transitions connect ideas | A strong hook; every paragraph uses TIDE with details that make facts vivid; the conclusion links back to the hook |
| **2. Facts and research** *(WR18, WR20, WR22–24)* | Few facts, or facts copied word for word; no sources | Accurate facts mostly in my own words; at least one source listed | Accurate facts in my own words from two or more reliable sources, all listed | Facts from several reliable sources, put together in my own words; numbers and comparisons explain them clearly |
| **3. Text features** *(TF6, WR9)* | No text features, or features that don't help | Title, headings and one other feature | Three or more features that help the reader, including a labelled map or diagram with a caption | Features chosen for the reader: a clear map or diagram and caption, bold words with a glossary, a sidebar fact |
| **4. Conventions and words** *(CV1, CV2, CV5, WR8, VO11)* | Errors make parts hard to read | Mostly correct capitals, commas and spelling; some topic words used | Capitals in titles, headings and place names; commas in lists; subjects and verbs agree; topic words spelled correctly | Correct and controlled; topic words used precisely and explained for the reader |

## Sample reports (all about the Grassland)

### Sample A · *Meets*

::: model Sample A
**The Grassland**

The Grassland is a region in Alberta. It is in the south. It is very flat.

The Grassland is dry. It does not rain much. There are not many trees. There is lots of grass.

Animals live in the Grassland. Pronghorns live there and burrowing owls. There are badlands with dinosaur bones.

The Grassland is a cool region and you should visit it.

Source: Regions of Alberta Reader
:::

::: note Why it is "Meets"
**Organization:** there is an introduction, body paragraphs and a conclusion, but the introduction has no hook and paragraph 3 mixes two subtopics (animals and the badlands). **Facts:** accurate and in the writer's own words; one source. **Text features:** a title only. **Conventions:** mostly correct, but the topic words aren't explained ("There are badlands": what are they?).
:::

### Sample B · *Proficient*

::: model Sample B
**The Grassland: Alberta's Big Sky Country**

Have you ever stood somewhere so flat that you could see a storm coming from an hour away? Welcome to the Grassland, the prairie region in the southeast corner of Alberta.

**A dry land**

The Grassland is the driest region in Alberta. First, wet air from the Pacific drops its rain on the Rocky Mountains. Then the air is dry by the time it reaches the prairie, so the Grassland is in a **rain shadow**. Some summers it hardly rains at all. That is why so few trees grow there.

**Tough grasses**

Prairie grasses are built for dry times. Rough fescue, Alberta's official grass, has roots that spread wide and deep to find water. For example, after a fire burns the grass, the roots survive and new grass grows back. The grass is tougher than it looks.

**Prairie animals**

Many animals live on the open prairie. The pronghorn can outrun almost any predator, and burrowing owls nest in old ground squirrel holes. Also, ground squirrels (gophers) dig tunnels to hide from hawks. Each animal has a way to survive in a place with nowhere to hide.

The Grassland may look empty, but it is full of life. Next time you drive through it, look closely!

*Map: Alberta, with the Grassland coloured in.* **Glossary:** rain shadow: a dry area on the far side of mountains.
**Sources:** Regions of Alberta Reader, Texts 2 and 3; a provincial parks website.
:::

::: note Why it is "Proficient"
**Organization:** a question hook; one subtopic per paragraph with a heading; each paragraph has a topic sentence, facts, a detail ("for example, after a fire…") and an ending; transitions (first, then, also). **Facts:** accurate, in the writer's own words, two sources. **Text features:** headings, bold word, glossary, labelled map. **Conventions:** parentheses for the second name; capitals on place names; correct.
:::

### Sample C · *Excellent*

::: model Sample C
**The Grassland: Alberta's Big Sky Country**

On the prairie of southeastern Alberta, the sky is so big and the land so flat that you can watch a thunderstorm roll in for an hour before the first drop falls. This is the Grassland, one of Alberta's six natural regions. It looks empty at first, but it is packed with clever plants, fast animals and a long human history.

**A land in the rain shadow**

The Grassland is the driest and hottest part of Alberta, and the Rocky Mountains are the reason. Wet air from the Pacific Ocean rises over the mountains, cools, and drops most of its water as snow on the western slopes. By the time the air sinks down onto the prairie, it is dry. As a result, the Grassland sits in a **rain shadow**, and some summers bring almost no rain. In winter, that same sinking air can arrive as a warm **chinook** that melts the snow in an afternoon.

**Roots that go deep**

Trees can't find enough water here, but grasses can. Rough fescue, Alberta's official grass, keeps much of its body underground, with roots that reach far deeper than the grass is tall. Think of the prairie as an upside-down forest: most of the "trees" are below your feet. Because of those roots, the grass survives drought, fire and grazing, and grows back again and again.

**Built for open spaces**

On land with nowhere to hide, animals survive by being fast or by going underground. The pronghorn, the fastest land animal in North America, can run about as fast as a car on the highway. Burrowing owls move into old ground squirrel holes, and ground squirrels (gophers) dig tunnels to escape hawks and the heat. Long ago, millions of plains bison grazed here, and the Niitsitapi (Blackfoot) peoples depended on them for food, clothing and shelter.

So the next time someone says the prairie is empty, tell them to look under the grass and into the burrows. Under that big sky is one of the busiest regions in Alberta.

*Map: Alberta's six regions, with the Grassland coloured and labelled. Caption: "The Grassland covers the southeast corner, about one seventh of Alberta."* · *Sidebar: "Did you know? At Áísínai'pi (Writing-on-Stone), carvings on the sandstone hold Blackfoot stories that are still important today."* · **Glossary:** rain shadow, chinook, burrow.
**Sources:** Regions of Alberta Reader, Texts 2 and 3; a provincial parks website; a library book about Alberta's natural regions.
:::

::: note Why it is "Excellent"
**Organization:** a picture-in-words hook that sets up the whole report; each paragraph has TIDE with vivid details (an upside-down forest, as fast as a car); transitions show cause ("as a result", "because of"); the conclusion links back to the "empty" idea from the hook. **Facts:** accurate, explained, from three sources, in the writer's voice. **Text features:** headings, bold words, glossary, a labelled map with a caption that explains, and a sidebar. **Conventions:** correct; topic words used precisely and explained.
:::
