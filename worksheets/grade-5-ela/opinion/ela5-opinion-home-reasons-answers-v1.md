::: tip
**For the adult:** Answers depend on the debate chosen. Look for these signs of a strong answer.
:::

## What strong answers look like

**1.** A clear opinion in one sentence ("We should get a dog," not "Dogs are nice").

**2–3.** Each reason has evidence that could be checked or is a real example ("Our neighbour's dog gets walked twice a day, so we'd go outside more").

**4.** The other side is stated fairly, then answered with a reason, not a put-down.

**5.** The adult's answer gives your child feedback: the reason with evidence usually convinces most.

**6.** The ending repeats the opinion and asks for action ("Can we visit the shelter this weekend?").
