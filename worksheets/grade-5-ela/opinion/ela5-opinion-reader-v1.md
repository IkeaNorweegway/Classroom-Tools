## How to use this reader

Every text here is about the same question, but each one does a different job. A **news article** reports what happened, with facts and quotes from different sides. An **editorial** (or opinion piece) argues for one side. A **photo essay** uses pictures and captions to make a point. Your job is to read like a judge: what is fact, what is opinion, and which evidence is strongest?

| Text | Form | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Students Ask: Is Recess Long Enough? | News article | To inform |
| 2. Give Us Ten More Minutes | Editorial | To persuade (for longer recess) |

| 6. A Letter to Our Principal | Model opinion letter | Our mentor text for writing |

**Note:** Prairie View School, its students, staff and survey are **made up** for this reader. The facts about activity guidelines and doctors' advice are real, and their sources are named.

::: passage Students Ask: Is Recess Long Enough? | Text 1 · News article · The Prairie View Post (a made-up school paper)

### Grade 5 class asks council for longer recess

Students in Ms. Abara's Grade 5 class at Prairie View School asked the school council on Tuesday to make lunch recess ten minutes longer. The class presented a petition with 212 signatures and a survey of 180 students.

"Twenty minutes isn't enough time to eat, get outside and actually play," said Jonah, one of the students who presented. "By the time we pick teams, the bell rings."

Right now, Prairie View students have a 15-minute morning recess and a 20-minute lunch recess outside, after 20 minutes to eat.

### What experts say

Canada's national activity guidelines for children and youth, called the Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines, say children aged 5 to 17 should get an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous physical activity every day. According to Statistics Canada, only about 2 in 5 Canadian children and youth were meeting that goal in a 2019 report.

The American Academy of Pediatrics, a group of children's doctors, has called recess "a crucial and necessary component of a child's development" and says it should not be taken away as a punishment.

### Not everyone agrees

Principal Delorme said she supports outdoor play but has concerns. "If recess gets longer, something else gets shorter," she said. "We would need to find the time in the day, and we need enough adults outside to keep everyone safe."

A parent on the council, Mr. Nguyen, suggested a different idea: "Maybe the answer isn't more time. Maybe it's better equipment and more games, so every minute counts."

The council will vote on the request at its next meeting on March 3.
:::

::: note Words to know
**petition** a written request signed by many people · **council** a group that makes decisions · **moderate to vigorous** activity that makes you breathe harder, from a fast walk to a run
:::

::: passage Give Us Ten More Minutes | Text 2 · Editorial · by Amira, Grade 6 (a made-up writer)

Every school day at 12:40, the bell rings, and a hundred conversations, games and races stop in the middle. I think Prairie View should add ten minutes to lunch recess, for three important reasons.

First, kids need more activity. Canada's 24-Hour Movement Guidelines say children should get at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day, and Statistics Canada reports that most kids don't get there. For many students, recess is the only time all day they run. Ten more minutes outside is ten more minutes toward that goal.

Second, we learn better after we move. I notice it in my own class: after a short recess, everyone is fidgety; after a long gym class, we're calm and ready. When children's doctors call recess "crucial," they are talking about learning, not just fun.

Third, recess is where we practise getting along. On the playground, we make up rules, solve arguments and include new kids. Those are skills too, and right now we barely have time to choose teams.

Some people say longer recess means less time for learning. But ten minutes is only about three percent of our school day, and if we come back focused, we'll learn more in the time we have.

Ten minutes is a small change that would make a big difference. Let's give it a try for one term and see.
:::

::: passage Better, Not Longer | Text 3 · Editorial · by Mr. Nguyen, parent (a made-up writer)

Everyone agrees that kids need to move. I'm a parent, and I want my children running, climbing and laughing every day. But I don't think a longer recess is the best answer. I think we should make the recess we already have **better**.

First, time is tight. The school day is set, and the buses leave at the same time every afternoon. Adding ten minutes to recess means taking ten minutes from reading, math or science. That time matters too.

Second, a longer recess needs more adults outside. Right now, three supervisors watch more than 300 students. Stretching their shift would be hard, and safety comes first.

Third, a lot of recess time is wasted. Students spend minutes looking for a ball, waiting for the one soccer net, or standing around with nothing to do. If we bought more equipment, painted games on the blacktop, and had older students lead games, every minute would be active.

Some students say twenty minutes isn't enough time to play. I understand. But a better recess could give them more real playing time than a longer one with nothing to do.

Let's spend our energy, and the parent council's money, on making recess better, not just longer.
:::

::: passage Twenty Minutes Outside | Text 4 · Photo essay (the photos are described in words)

> **Photo 1:** A line of students at the door, coats half zipped, staring at the clock. *Caption: 12:20. Lunch is over, and recess begins as soon as everyone is dressed: about four minutes, if your zipper cooperates.*

> **Photo 2:** Two students arguing over a single red ball. *Caption: One ball, two games, forty players. Who gets it decides who plays.*

> **Photo 3:** A wide shot of the field: one group playing soccer, many students standing along the fence. *Caption: Not everyone is moving. Some kids say they "don't know what to do."*

> **Photo 4:** A grade 6 student teaching younger kids a game of four square on painted lines. *Caption: When there's something to do, everyone joins in.*

> **Photo 5:** Students running back toward the door as the bell rings, one still holding a skipping rope. *Caption: 12:40. The game ends mid-turn.*
:::

::: note Think about it
Photo essays persuade with **what they choose to show**. The photographer picked these five moments. Which photos support longer recess? Which support better recess? What might a different photographer have shown?
:::

::: passage What Our School Thinks | Text 5 · Survey results (made up for this reader)

The Grade 5 class surveyed 180 students in Grades 3 to 6. Each student answered two questions.
:::

| Question 1: Is lunch recess long enough? | Students |
| --- | --- |
| Yes, it's long enough | 36 |
| No, it should be longer | 126 |
| Not sure | 18 |
| **Total** | **180** |

| Question 2: What would make recess better? (students could pick one) | Students |
| --- | --- |
| More time | 81 |
| More equipment | 45 |
| Organized games | 30 |
| More places to sit and talk | 24 |
| **Total** | **180** |

::: note Read the data carefully
126 of 180 students (70%) say recess should be longer. But on Question 2, only 81 students (45%) chose **more time** as the thing that would make recess better; 99 picked something else. A survey can support **both** editorials, depending on which question you look at. Who answered matters too: the survey only asked students, not teachers, supervisors or parents.
:::

::: passage A Letter to Our Principal | Text 6 · A model opinion letter (our mentor text)

Dear Principal Delorme,

I believe Prairie View should make lunch recess fifteen minutes longer, even if that means starting school five minutes earlier and shortening our silent reading time a little.

My first reason is that students need more exercise. Canada's 24-Hour Movement Guidelines say kids need at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity every day, but Statistics Canada reports that only about 2 in 5 kids get it. For a lot of us, recess is our main time to run. A longer recess would help more students reach the goal.

Another reason is that we would come back ready to learn. Our class tried a 30-minute recess on Track and Field Day, and our teacher said it was our calmest afternoon of the year. When we use our energy outside, we can focus inside.

My most important reason is that more time means more students get to play. Right now, by the time we get dressed and pick teams, the game is almost over. In our survey, 126 out of 180 students said lunch recess isn't long enough.

Some people worry that we will lose learning time. That is a fair concern. However, if recess is longer, we can solve that by starting five minutes earlier, and focused students learn more in less time.

For these reasons, I hope you will try a longer recess for one term. Thank you for reading my letter.

Sincerely,
Leo, Grade 5
:::

::: note What makes it a model
The letter uses **TREE**: a **T**opic sentence that states the opinion clearly; **R**easons (three, with the strongest last); **E**xplanations with evidence for each reason (a fact with its source, an example, survey data); and an **E**nding that restates the opinion and asks for action. It also answers the other side (a **counterargument**) politely, and uses the letter form: greeting, closing and signature.
:::
