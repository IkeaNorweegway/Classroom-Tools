## The rubric

| Criterion | Has yet to meet | Meets | Proficient | Excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Opinion and organization** *(WR6, WR4, WR5)* | The opinion is unclear or changes; reasons are mixed together | A clear opinion at the start; reasons in separate parts; an ending | Clear opinion with a hook; TREE in order; transitions between reasons; the strongest reason last | A hook that sets up the argument; TREE throughout; every part pushes toward the ending's call to action |
| **2. Reasons and evidence** *(WR6, CO9, WR21)* | Reasons with little or no evidence, or evidence that doesn't fit | Two or three reasons, each with some support | Three reasons, each explained with relevant evidence (a fact with a source, a number, an example) | Strong, reliable evidence for every reason, explained so the reader sees why it matters |
| **3. The other side and fairness** *(WR6, CO13, WR11)* | The other side isn't mentioned, or it's mocked | The other side is mentioned | The other side is stated fairly and answered with a reason | The strongest counterargument is answered convincingly and respectfully; no unfair tricks |
| **4. Conventions and form** *(CV2, CV8, CV10, WR8)* | Errors make parts hard to read; no form | Mostly correct; letter or editorial form | Correct letter form with commas; commas after transitions; pronouns match | Correct and controlled; precise, strong word choices |

## Samples: "Should our class have a class pet?"

### Sample A · *Meets*

::: model Sample A
Dear Ms. Abara,

I think we should have a class pet. Pets are fun and everyone would like it.

A pet would teach us responsibility. We would have to feed it.

Also, pets make people happy.

Please get us a pet.

Sincerely, Maya
:::

::: note Why it is "Meets"
**Opinion:** clear. **Reasons:** two reasons, with a little support ("we would have to feed it") but no real evidence. **Other side:** not mentioned. **Form:** letter form, mostly correct (commas missing after *Dear Ms. Abara* and *Sincerely* in some drafts).
:::

### Sample B · *Proficient*

::: model Sample B
Dear Ms. Abara,

Have you ever noticed how the whole class goes quiet when someone brings in a pet for show and tell? I believe our class should get a guinea pig.

First, a class pet would teach us responsibility. We could make a schedule so two students each week feed it, give it water and clean the cage. That's a real job, not just a worksheet.

Another reason is that pets help people feel calm. When the reading buddies' class had a fish tank, their teacher said students who were upset liked to sit and watch the fish.

Most importantly, a pet would help us learn science. We could measure how much it eats and grows and graph it in math.

Some people think a pet is too much work over weekends and holidays. That's a fair point. However, families could sign up to take it home, the way some classes do now.

For these reasons, I hope you will let us try a guinea pig for the rest of the year.

Sincerely,
Maya
:::

::: note Why it is "Proficient"
**Opinion and organization:** a question hook, a clear opinion, TREE in order with transitions (first, another reason, most importantly), strongest reason last. **Evidence:** an example for each reason. **Other side:** stated fairly and answered. **Form:** correct letter form and commas.
:::

### Sample C · *Excellent*

::: model Sample C
Dear Ms. Abara,

Every Monday, someone asks, "Can we get a class pet?" and every Monday the answer is "We'll see." I'd like to give you the evidence to say yes: our class should adopt a guinea pig.

First, a guinea pig would give us real responsibility. With a sign-up chart, two students a week would feed it, refill its water and clean its cage. Unlike a worksheet, a guinea pig can't wait until tomorrow, so we'd learn to keep our promises.

Another reason is that animals can help students feel calmer. Mr. Fehr's class has a fish tank, and he says students who are upset often sit beside it for a few minutes and come back ready to work. A guinea pig, which can be held, might help even more.

Most importantly, a pet would turn our classroom into a science lab. We could weigh our guinea pig every Friday, graph its growth in math, and research what it needs to stay healthy. That's three subjects from one small animal.

Some people worry that a pet is too much work on weekends and holidays, or that someone might be allergic. Those are fair concerns. However, families could sign up to take the guinea pig home, and we could check for allergies before it arrives. Many classes in our district already do this successfully.

A guinea pig would teach us to be responsible, help us feel calm and make science real. Instead of "We'll see," could the answer this Monday be "Let's try"?

Sincerely,
Maya, Grade 5
:::

::: note Why it is "Excellent"
**Opinion and organization:** a hook that's specific to the reader, a clear opinion, TREE throughout, and an ending that echoes the hook ("We'll see" → "Let's try"). **Evidence:** a specific, checkable example (Mr. Fehr's tank) and a plan for each reason, explained. **Other side:** two concerns, both answered respectfully with a solution. **Form:** correct; precise words (*adopt, weigh, successfully*).
:::
