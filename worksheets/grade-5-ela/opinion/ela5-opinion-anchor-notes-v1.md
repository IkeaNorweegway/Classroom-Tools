## 1 · Fact or opinion?

**Learning target:** I can tell a fact from an opinion. *(TF12, CO5)*

A **fact** can be [[checked or proven]]. An **opinion** tells what someone [[thinks, feels or believes]]. Opinions are not wrong; but a good opinion is supported by [[reasons and evidence]].

| Sentence (from the reader) | Fact or opinion? | How I know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The class presented a petition with 212 signatures." | [[Fact]] | [[It can be counted and checked]] |
| "Ten more minutes outside would make a big difference." | [[Opinion]] | [[It's what the writer believes]] |
| "Three supervisors watch more than 300 students." | [[Fact]] | [[It can be checked]] |

**Opinion signal words:** I think · I believe · should · best · worst · most important · *strong describing words* (amazing, terrible).

::: myth
WRONG: "If a sentence has a number in it, it's a fact."
WHY: Numbers look official.
RIGHT: "Recess is the best 20 minutes of the day" has a number, but it's an opinion. Ask: could this be checked?
:::

**Quick check.** Write one fact and one opinion about your school's recess.

%% frame: Fact: Our recess is ___ . Opinion: I think recess ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Example: Fact: Our lunch recess is 20 minutes long. Opinion: Lunch recess is the best part of the day.

## 2 · News article or editorial?

**Learning target:** I can tell the difference between a news article and an editorial. *(TF1, TF3)*

| | News article (Text 1) | Editorial (Texts 2 and 3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | To [[inform]] | To [[persuade]] |
| Point of view | Gives [[more than one side]] | Argues for [[one side]] |
| Voice | Third person; the reporter doesn't say "I think" | Often first person: "[[I think]]…" |
| Built with | **Headline**, **lead** (who, what, when, where, why), quotes, facts | Opinion, reasons, evidence, ending that calls for action |

::: example Watch me
Text 1's **lead** (first sentence): "Students in Ms. Abara's Grade 5 class at Prairie View School asked the school council on Tuesday to make lunch recess ten minutes longer." **Who:** the Grade 5 class. **What:** asked for longer recess. **When:** Tuesday. **Where:** Prairie View School. The *why* comes in the quote that follows.
:::

**Quick check.** Text 1 quotes the principal and a parent who don't agree with the students. Why would a news article do that?

%% frame: A news article includes ___ because its job is to ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Because a news article's job is to inform fairly, so it shows more than one side.

## 3 · The parts of an opinion piece

**Learning target:** I can find the opinion, reasons and evidence in an opinion piece. *(WR6, CO9, CO4)*

| Part | What it is | Text 2 (Amira) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opinion** (claim) | What the writer wants you to believe or do | [[Prairie View should add ten minutes to lunch recess]] |
| **Reason** | Why the writer thinks so | [[Kids need more activity]] |
| **Evidence** | Facts, examples or expert words that prove the reason | [[The 60-minute guideline; Statistics Canada: most kids don't get there]] |
| **Ending** | Sums up and asks for [[action]] | [[Let's try it for one term]] |

**A reason is not evidence.** A reason says *why*; evidence *proves* it. "Kids need activity" is a reason. "Statistics Canada reports only about 2 in 5 kids get 60 minutes a day" is [[evidence]].

**Quick check.** Write Amira's second reason and the evidence she gives for it.

%% frame: Her second reason is ___ . Her evidence is ___ .

(lines 3)

=> Reason: we learn better after we move. Evidence: her own class is calm after a long gym class (an example) and children's doctors call recess "crucial."

## 5 · Judging evidence

**Learning target:** I can decide whether evidence is strong. *(WR21, TF12, CO9)*

Strong evidence is:

| Test | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Relevant** | Does it actually support *this* reason? | Activity guidelines → supports "kids need to move" ✓ |
| **Reliable** | Who says so? Can I check it? | Statistics Canada ✓ · "my cousin says" ✗ |
| **Enough** | Is one example enough, or do I need more? | One calm afternoon is a [[small]] amount of evidence |

::: myth
WRONG: "The more evidence the better, any kind."
WHY: More feels stronger.
RIGHT: One reliable, relevant fact beats three weak ones. "Everyone knows recess is too short" isn't evidence at all.
:::

**Quick check.** Rank these from strongest (1) to weakest (3) as evidence that kids need longer recess.

| Evidence | Rank |
| --- | --- |
| "Recess is fun." | [[3]] |
| Statistics Canada: about 2 in 5 kids meet the activity goal. | [[1]] |
| "My friend gets bored in class after a short recess." | [[2]] |

%% frame: The strongest is ___ because it ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Statistics Canada is strongest: reliable and relevant. The friend's experience is one example. "Recess is fun" is an opinion, not evidence.

## 6 · Persuasive language: fair and unfair

**Learning target:** I can notice how writers use words to persuade, and judge whether it's fair. *(WR11, VO9, CO8)*

| Technique | Example | Fair? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strong, precise words** | "the game ends mid-turn" | Fair: it's true and vivid |
| **A question to the reader** | "Wouldn't you want ten more minutes?" | Fair, if it makes you think |
| **Numbers** | "126 of 180 students" | Fair, if they're accurate and [[not cherry-picked]] |
| **Showing the other side** | "Some people say… But…" | Fair and [[convincing]] |
| **Exaggeration** | "Recess is basically a prison yard!" | [[Unfair: it's not true and makes readers distrust you]] |
| **Name-calling** | "Only boring adults want short recess." | [[Unfair: it attacks people instead of ideas]] |

**Quick check.** Rewrite the name-calling example so it persuades fairly.

%% frame: Some adults worry that ___ , but ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Some adults worry about losing learning time, but a short break can help students focus when they come back.

## 7 · Using numbers as evidence

**Learning target:** I can read survey data and use it fairly as evidence. *(CO8, CO9, WR6)*

| Question 1 (Text 5) | Students |
| --- | --- |
| Recess should be longer | 126 of 180 |

126 out of 180 is [[70]] percent, which is about [[7]] in 10 students.

But Question 2 shows only [[81]] students chose *more time* as the best way to improve recess. The other [[99]] chose equipment, games or places to sit.

**Using data fairly:**
- Say **who** was asked (180 students in Grades 3–6, not adults).
- Don't hide numbers that [[disagree]] with you.
- A number is stronger with an explanation: what does it **mean**?

**Quick check.** Which editorial could use Question 2 as evidence? Explain.

%% frame: Text ___ could use it because ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Text 3 (Better, Not Longer): more than half the students (99 of 180) picked something other than more time to make recess better.

## 8 · Planning my opinion: POW + TREE

**Learning target:** I can use POW + TREE to plan an opinion piece. *(WR3, WR6, WR4)*

::: strategy POW: what good writers do
**P** **Pick** my idea (decide my opinion)
**O** **Organize** my notes (TREE)
**W** **Write** and say more
:::

::: strategy TREE: the parts of an opinion piece
**T** **Topic** sentence: tell what I believe
**R** **Reasons**: three or more. Why do I believe it? Will my reader believe it?
**E** **Explain** reasons: say more about each reason with evidence
**E** **Ending**: wrap it up right, and ask for action
:::

| TREE | Text 6 (Leo's letter) |
| --- | --- |
| T | [[Prairie View should make lunch recess fifteen minutes longer]] |
| R | [[Exercise · ready to learn · more students get to play]] |
| E | [[60-minute guideline and Statistics Canada · Track and Field Day example · survey: 126 of 180]] |
| E | [[Try a longer recess for one term; thank you]] |

**Self-talk:** "What do I believe?" · "Will my reader believe this reason?" · "Where's my evidence?" · "Good, that reason is explained."

**Quick check.** From memory, write what each letter in TREE stands for.

%% frame: T = ___ · R = ___ · E = ___ · E = ___

(lines 2)

=> Topic sentence, Reasons, Explain reasons, Ending.

## 9 · Answering the other side, and the letter form

**Learning target:** I can respond politely to the other side and write in letter form. *(WR6, WR5, OL7)*

A **counterargument** is what someone on the other side would say. Answering it makes your piece [[stronger]], because it shows you've thought about it.

**The move:** Some people think ___ . That's a fair point. However, ___ .

::: model Text 6, counterargument
"Some people worry that we will lose learning time. That is a fair concern. However, if recess is longer, we can solve that by starting five minutes earlier, and focused students learn more in less time."
:::

**Transitions for reasons:** First · Another reason · Also · Most importantly · For these reasons

**Letter form:** Date · **Dear** ___ **,** · body paragraphs · **Sincerely,** · your name

**Quick check.** Write a counterargument and response for this opinion: *Our class should have a class pet.*

%% frame: Some people think ___ . That's a fair point. However, ___ .

(lines 3)

=> Example: Some people think a pet is too much work. That's a fair point. However, we could make a care schedule so every student takes a turn.
