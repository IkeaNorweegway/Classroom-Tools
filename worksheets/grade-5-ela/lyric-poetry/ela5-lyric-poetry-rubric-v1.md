## The rubric

| Criterion | Has yet to meet | Meets | Proficient | Excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Images and senses** *(WR13, TF20)* | General words; hard to picture the place | Some details of the place; mostly sight | Precise details from two or more senses; I can picture the place | Vivid, precise details from several senses that make the place unforgettable |
| **2. Comparisons and feeling** *(VO15, TF15, TF17)* | No clear feeling; no comparison | A feeling is named; one comparison | A feeling shown through details, with a comparison that fits it | A feeling built through the whole poem; fresh comparisons that deepen it |
| **3. Shape and sound** *(TF16, TF17)* | Written like a paragraph | Lines and stanzas | Stanzas that each do a job and move somewhere; some sound (repetition, rhythm, rhyme) | Line breaks and stanzas used on purpose; sound matches the meaning |
| **4. Recitation** *(TF14, OL3, OL4)* | Read from the page; hard to hear | Recited with some prompts; heard | Recited from memory with expression and clear phrasing | Recited from memory; voice, pace and pauses bring out the meaning; connects with the audience |

## Samples: a backyard rink

### Sample A · *Meets*

::: poem Sample A | about a backyard rink
My backyard rink is fun.
I skate on it every day.
The ice is white and shiny
like a big plate.

I play hockey with my dad.
It is cold outside.
I love my rink.
:::

::: note Why it is "Meets"
**Images:** some details, mostly sight. **Feeling:** named ("I love my rink," "fun") but not shown. **Comparison:** one simile. **Shape:** lines and stanzas, but they read like sentences in a paragraph.
:::

### Sample B · *Proficient*

::: poem Sample B | about a backyard rink
After supper, Dad floods the rink
and the steam rises off it
like the yard is breathing.

In the morning it's glass.
My skates scrape and hiss,
my stick clicks the puck,
my breath puffs ahead of me.

Out here, at minus fifteen,
it's just me and the boards and the stars,
and nothing feels too hard.
:::

::: note Why it is "Proficient"
**Images:** precise details from sight, sound and touch (scrape, hiss, clicks, puffs, minus fifteen). **Feeling:** shown (calm, confident), with a comparison that fits ("like the yard is breathing"). **Shape:** three stanzas that move from night to morning to a feeling; some sound (scrape/hiss/clicks).
:::

### Sample C · *Excellent*

::: poem Sample C | about a backyard rink
Every night in January
my dad stands in the dark yard with the hose,
painting the rink one thin coat at a time,
the water hissing as it turns to glass.

By morning the ice is a held breath,
so smooth it holds the moon, still there,
pale as a thumbprint in the blue.

I lace up. The first stride
is a zipper pulled across the cold:
*scrrritch.* The puck goes *tock* off the boards.

Some days are heavy. Some days the world
pushes. But out here, gliding,
I am a stone skipped across a lake,
and nothing can catch me.
:::

::: note Why it is "Excellent"
**Images:** vivid, precise details from several senses, rooted in a real place and season. **Comparisons:** fresh and connected (the rink as glass, a held breath, a zipper; the skater as a skipped stone), and they build the feeling. **Feeling:** grows through the poem, from the work of making the rink to freedom, and the last stanza reveals why the place matters. **Shape and sound:** each stanza moves forward in time; onomatopoeia (*scrrritch, tock*) and line breaks ("the world / pushes") are used on purpose.
:::
