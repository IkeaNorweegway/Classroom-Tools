## 1 · What is a lyric poem?

**Learning target:** I can explain what a lyric poem is and how it makes a reader feel. *(TF13, TF2)*

A **lyric poem** expresses the speaker's personal [[feelings]] or emotions. It is usually [[short]], and it uses **sound**, **rhythm** and **pictures in words** to share that feeling. The **speaker** is the voice in the poem; it isn't always the poet.

| | A story | A lyric poem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Written in… | paragraphs | [[lines and stanzas]] |
| Mostly about… | what happens (plot) | [[a feeling or a moment]] |
| Best read… | silently or aloud | [[aloud, more than once]] |

**Listening for feeling:** after you hear a poem, ask: *What did I picture? What did I feel? Which words made me feel it?*

**Quick check.** Listen to *Who Has Seen the Wind?* twice. What feeling or mood does it give you? Which words?

%% frame: It feels ___ because of the words " ___ ."

(lines 2)

=> e.g., Calm and curious, because of "the leaves hang trembling" and "the trees bow down their heads."

## 2 · Lines and stanzas

**Learning target:** I can explain how stanzas organize a poem. *(TF16, TF3)*

A **line** is one row of a poem. A **stanza** is a group of lines that relate to a [[similar idea]], like a paragraph in a poem.

| Poem | How many stanzas? | Lines per stanza | What each stanza does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who Has Seen the Wind? | [[2]] | [[4]] | [[The same question, two different signs of the wind]] |
| Coulee in October | [[3]] | [[4]] | [[The land → the view → a memory]] |

**Line breaks matter.** Poets choose where a line ends. A line break makes the reader [[pause]], and puts weight on the last word.

**Quick check.** In *Coulee in October*, why do you think the poet put the memory of Grandpa in the last stanza?

%% frame: The memory is last because ___ .

(lines 2)

=> So the poem ends on why the place matters; the feeling builds from what the speaker sees to what they remember.

## 3 · Pictures in words: comparisons

**Learning target:** I can explain what a comparison in a poem means and how it makes me feel. *(VO13, VO14, TF15)*

| Type | What it is | Example | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simile** | compares using *like* or *as* | "like a cupped hand holds water" | [[The coulee holds the light gently, carefully]] |
| **Metaphor** | says one thing *is* another | "Hope is the thing with feathers" | [[Hope is like a small bird that lives inside you and keeps singing]] |
| **Personification** *(extension)* | gives a thing human actions | "August is laughing across the sky" | [[The month feels happy and bright]] |

**An extended metaphor** lasts through a whole poem. In Dickinson's poem, hope is a [[bird]] in every stanza: it perches, sings, keeps people warm, and never asks for a crumb.

**Quick check.** "My breath hangs in front of me / like a word I haven't said yet" (*River Valley, January*). What two things are compared, and how does it make you feel?

%% frame: The poet compares ___ to ___ . It makes me feel ___ .

(lines 3)

=> Breath in the cold air and an unspoken word: both hang there, waiting. It feels quiet and thoughtful, maybe a little lonely.

## 4 · Sound in poems

**Learning target:** I can explain how sound and rhythm make a poem feel a certain way. *(TF13, TF15, TF16)*

| Sound tool | What it is | Example (Johnson) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rhythm** | the beat of the words | "**Dip**, **dip**, / While the **wa**ters **flip**" |
| **Rhyme** | words that end with the same sound | flip / slip; crash / [[splash]] |
| **Repetition** | repeating a word or line | "Swirl, swirl!" · "Who has seen the wind?" |
| **Onomatopoeia** | words that sound like what they mean | "[[dash, crash, splash]]" |

In *The Song My Paddle Sings*, the poem starts slow and sleepy ("Sleep, sleep"), then the short, quick lines speed up as the river reaches the [[rapids]], then slow down again ("Sway, sway"). The sound **matches** the journey.

**Quick check.** Read stanza 6 of Johnson's poem aloud. How does the sound match what's happening?

%% frame: The words ___ sound like ___ , which matches ___ .

(lines 3)

=> The short, crashing words ("Dash, dash, / With a mighty crash, / They seethe, and boil, and bound, and splash") sound loud and fast, like the roaring rapids.

## 5 · Poems of place

**Learning target:** I can explain how a poem connects a place to a person's feelings and experiences. *(TF20, CO16)*

Many lyric poems are about a **place**: a river, a prairie, a coulee, a backyard. The poet uses details of the land to show what the place [[means to them]].

| Poem | The place | What it means to the speaker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Song My Paddle Sings | [[A river with rapids, hills and a fir tree]] | [[Joy, courage and freedom on the water]] |
| River Valley, January | [[The frozen North Saskatchewan in Edmonton]] | [[Hope: the river keeps moving under the ice, and so does the speaker]] |
| Coulee in October | [[A prairie coulee at sunset]] | [[A memory of Grandpa; "the land remembers everyone"]] |

**Quick check.** Think of a place that matters to you. Write three details you could put in a poem about it.

%% frame: My place is ___ . I see ___ , I hear ___ , and I feel ___ .

(lines 3)

## 6 · Memorizing a poem

**Learning target:** I can learn a poem by heart and recite it with expression. *(TF14, OL3, OL4)*

**How to memorize:**
1. **Understand it first.** Know what every line means and feels like.
2. **Picture each line.** Make a mental movie.
3. **Chunk it.** Learn one [[stanza]] at a time.
4. **Build up.** Say stanza 1; then 1 and 2; then 1, 2 and 3.
5. **Hide and say.** Cover the poem, say it, check, repeat.
6. **Sleep on it.** Practise a little every day; your brain [[stores]] it at night.

**Reciting:** stand tall, breathe, project your voice, look at your audience, and let the meaning set your pace. Slow down for the most important line.

**Quick check.** Write the first two lines of a poem you're learning, from memory.

(lines 2)

## 7 · Planning and writing a lyric poem

**Learning target:** I can plan and write a lyric poem with images, comparisons and stanzas. *(TF17, WR13, VO15)*

::: strategy Our planning routine
**P** **Place** or moment: where and when?
**S** **Senses**: what do I see, hear, smell, feel?
**F** **Feeling**: what is the feeling underneath?
**C** **Compare**: one simile or metaphor that fits the feeling
**S** **Shape**: stanzas that move somewhere (closer, later, deeper)
:::

| Step | *Coulee in October* |
| --- | --- |
| Place | [[A coulee at sunset in October]] |
| Senses | [[toast-coloured grass, silver creek, dust-and-cold wind, a hawk circling]] |
| Feeling | [[Quiet remembering; missing and understanding Grandpa]] |
| Compare | [["like a cupped hand holds water"; "the creek is a silver thread"]] |
| Shape | [[3 stanzas: land → view → memory]] |

**Revising a poem:** cut extra words (*very, really, so*); trade a general word for a precise one (*bird* → *hawk*); end lines on strong words; read it aloud to hear it.

**Quick check.** Replace each general word with a precise one: *The bird sat on the tree in the nice light.*

%% frame: The ___ sat on the ___ in the ___ light.

(lines 2)

=> e.g., The magpie perched on the poplar in the honey-coloured light.
