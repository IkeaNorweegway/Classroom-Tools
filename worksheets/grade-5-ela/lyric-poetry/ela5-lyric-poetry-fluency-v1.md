::: teacher How to use this pack
Poems are the best fluency texts there are: short, worth rereading, and built for expression. This unit has **no words-per-minute passages**; the focus is **phrasing and expression** (prosody). Use **choral reading** (whole class, daily warm-up), **poems for voices** (groups), and **echo reading** for students who need it (you read a line, they repeat). Students practise their recitation poem 5 minutes a day from Lesson 8.
:::

## Choral reading: *The Song My Paddle Sings*

The class reads Johnson's poem together, in parts. **All** = everyone. **A** and **B** = two halves of the class. **Solo** = one reader.

::: script The Song My Paddle Sings (for voices)
SOLO 1: West wind, blow from your prairie nest,
SOLO 2: Blow from the mountains, blow from the west.
ALL: The sail is idle, the sailor too;
ALL: O! wind of the west, we wait for you.
A: Blow, blow!
B: Blow, blow!
SOLO 1: I have wooed you so,
SOLO 2: But never a favour you bestow.
(Stanza 2: softly, slowing down)
ALL: I stow the sail, unship the mast:
A: Sleep, sleep,
B: By your mountain steep,
ALL: For soft is the song my paddle sings.
(Stanzas 4–6: speeding up and getting louder)
A: Dip, dip,
B: While the waters flip
A: Swirl, swirl!
B: How the ripples curl
ALL: (loud) Dash, dash, / With a mighty crash, / They seethe, and boil, and bound, and splash.
(Stanza 7: strong and brave)
ALL: Be strong, O paddle! be brave, canoe!
(Stanza 9: quiet, swaying)
A: Swings, swings,
B: Its emerald wings,
ALL: (softly) Swelling the song that my paddle sings.
:::

::: note How to read it
This arrangement uses lines from the full poem (in your reader). **Volume** follows the river: soft in the calm, loud at the rapids, soft again at the end. **Pace** follows it too. Watch the conductor.
:::

## A poem for two voices: *Who Has Seen the Wind?*

Partner **A** reads the left column; partner **B** reads the right; read **both** lines together.

| A | B |
| --- | --- |
| Who has seen the wind? | |
| | Neither I nor you: |
| But when the leaves hang trembling | But when the leaves hang trembling |
| The wind is passing thro'. | |
| | Who has seen the wind? |
| Neither you nor I: | |
| But when the trees bow down their heads | But when the trees bow down their heads |
| The wind is passing by. | The wind is passing by. |

**Try it three ways:** whispery, like the wind · wondering, like a question · calm, like a lullaby. Which fits the poem best? Why?

## How did it sound? (recitation scale)

| | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Memory** | Read from the page | Needed many prompts | One or two prompts | No prompts |
| **Expression** | Flat | Some feeling | Feeling matches most lines | Feeling matches the poem; voice changes on purpose |
| **Phrasing** | Stopped at every line end | Some smooth phrases | Pauses where the meaning needs | Pauses and stresses make the meaning clear |
