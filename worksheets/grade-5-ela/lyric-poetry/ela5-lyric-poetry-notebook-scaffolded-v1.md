**Write about it.** The poem says nobody can see the wind. How *do* we know it's there? What might the poem be saying about other things we can't see?

## Lesson 3 · Hope is a bird

Read Dickinson's poem. Use **Anchor note 3**.

| What birds do | What the poem says hope does | ¶/stanza |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✎ perch | "perches in the soul" | 1 |
| sing | [["sings the tune without the words, / And never stops at all"]] | [[1]] |
| keep warm, survive storms | [["sweetest in the gale"; "kept so many warm"]] | [[2]] |
| need food | [["never… It asked a crumb of me"]] | [[3]] |

**Write about it.** Why is a small bird a good metaphor for hope? Use two lines from the poem.

::: frame
A bird is a good metaphor because ___ . The poem says " ___ ," which shows ___ .
:::

(lines 5)

=> Hope is small and easy to miss, but it keeps going even in hard times, like a bird that keeps singing in a storm. "Sweetest in the gale is heard" shows hope is strongest in hard times, and "never… asked a crumb of me" shows it asks for nothing in return.

## Lesson 4 · The sound of a river

Read *The Song My Paddle Sings* aloud with a partner, a stanza each. Use **Anchor note 4**.

| Stanza | What's happening on the river? | How does the sound match? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✎ 2 | The wind dies; the speaker stows the sail and paddles | Slow and sleepy: "Sleep, sleep" |
| 6 | [[The rapids roar ahead]] | [[Loud, crashing words: "Dash, dash… splash"]] |
| 8 | [[They've raced the rapids; calm water]] | [[Soft and swaying: "Sway, sway… tinkling tunes"]] |

**Find** two rhyming pairs and one example of onomatopoeia.

(lines 2)

=> Rhymes (any two): nest/west, too/you, dip/flip/slip, crash/splash, canoe/into. Onomatopoeia: dash, crash, splash, swirl.

**Write about it.** How does the speaker feel about the river? Use evidence.

(lines 4)

=> Joyful and brave: "August is laughing" (stanza 3), "Be strong, O paddle! be brave, canoe!" and "never a fear my craft will feel" (stanza 7).

## Lesson 6 · A child's wondering

Read *Where Go the Boats?* aloud, then compare it with *Who Has Seen the Wind?*

| | Where Go the Boats? | Who Has Seen the Wind? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✎ Something moving that you can't follow | The river carrying the boats away | The wind |
| A question the speaker asks | [["Where will all come home?"]] | [["Who has seen the wind?"]] |
| The feeling | [[Wondering, a little wistful, hopeful]] | [[Calm, curious]] |

**Write about it.** Stevenson's speaker imagines "Other little children" finding the boats. What does that tell you about the speaker?

(lines 3)

=> The speaker is imaginative and hopeful; they picture their toys travelling far and making other children happy, as if the river connects them to faraway places.

## Lesson 7 · Poems from here

Read *Chinook* and *River Valley, January*.

**Write about it.** Both poems are about an Alberta winter, but they feel different. Compare the feeling of each, with one line from each.

::: frame
*Chinook* feels ___ , like when it says " ___ ." *River Valley, January* feels ___ , like when it says " ___ ."
:::

(lines 5)

=> *Chinook* feels joyful and surprised ("My dad stood in the driveway in a T-shirt / like he'd won something"). *River Valley, January* feels quiet and hopeful ("still moving, still going somewhere, / the way I keep going").

**Find the comparisons.** Write one simile from each poem.

(lines 2)

=> *Chinook*: "the cold sat on us / like a dog that would not move" or "warm as a breath." *River Valley*: "like a word I haven't said yet."
