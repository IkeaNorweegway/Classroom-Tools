::: teacher How to use Edition C
Poems aren't retold: these are the shorter poems from Edition A, plus the first stanzas of the longer ones, with word supports. Read each poem aloud first, then read it together (echo reading: you read a line, the student repeats), then the student reads alone. Repeated reading of a short poem is excellent fluency practice. Use the full Edition A poems for listening and discussion.
:::

## About poems

A **poem** is written in **lines**. A group of lines is a **stanza**. Poems use sound and pictures to show a **feeling**.

Read each poem out loud. Read it two times.

::: poem Who Has Seen the Wind? | Christina Rossetti (1872)
Who has seen the wind?
Neither I nor you:
But when the leaves hang trembling
The wind is passing thro'.

Who has seen the wind?
Neither you nor I:
But when the trees bow down their heads
The wind is passing by.
:::

::: support
**neither** not this one and not that one

**thro'** through

**trembling** shaking a little
:::

::: poem The Song My Paddle Sings (stanzas 4 and 5) | E. Pauline Johnson, Tekahionwake (1895)
The river rolls in its rocky bed;
My paddle is plying its way ahead;
Dip, dip,
While the waters flip
In foam as over their breast we slip.

And oh, the river runs swifter now;
The eddies circle about my bow.
Swirl, swirl!
How the ripples curl
In many a dangerous pool awhirl!
:::

::: support
**paddle** what you use to move a canoe

**swirl** spin around

**dip** go down into the water
:::

::: poem Where Go the Boats? (stanza 1) | Robert Louis Stevenson (1885)
Dark brown is the river,
  Golden is the sand.
It flows along for ever,
  With trees on either hand.
:::

::: poem Prairie Morning (stanza 2) | a new poem
The meadowlark goes off like a kettle,
one bright whistle, then two.
The wheat is talking in whispers
the way it always knew.
:::

::: support
**meadowlark** a prairie bird that sings

**golden** shiny yellow
:::

::: poem Coulee in October (stanza 1) | a new poem · our model
The coulee holds the last of the light
like a cupped hand holds water.
The grass has turned the colour of toast,
and the wind smells like dust and cold.
:::

::: support
**coulee** a deep, dry valley in the prairie

**like** shows a comparison: the coulee is like a hand
:::
