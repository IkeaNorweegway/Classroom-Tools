## Cold read 1

::: poem Thunderstorm, July | a new poem
All afternoon the sky was a held breath,
yellow and heavy over the canola.
The dog went under the porch.
The swallows flew low and fast, stitching the air.

Then the first drop, fat as a coin,
then a thousand, drumming the tin roof
like a marching band that lost its way,
and the thunder rolled its barrels down the sky.

Afterward, the whole field steamed.
The air smelled green and new.
My little brother ran out barefoot
and the world was washed, and so were you.
:::

**1.** How many stanzas does the poem have? What is each stanza about? *(TF16)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 3)

=> Three: (1) waiting before the storm; (2) the storm; (3) after the storm.

**2.** "the sky was a held breath" (stanza 1). Is this a simile or a metaphor? What does it mean? *(VO13, VO14)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> A metaphor: the sky felt tense and still, like everything was waiting.

**3.** Find a simile in stanza 2. What does it help you picture or hear? *(VO13, TF15)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> "like a marching band that lost its way": loud, messy, uneven drumming of rain on the roof.

**4.** How does the feeling change from stanza 1 to stanza 3? Use words from each. *(TF13, CO8)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 3)

=> From tense and heavy ("held breath," "the dog went under the porch") to fresh and joyful ("smelled green and new," "ran out barefoot").

**5.** Find two senses the poet uses besides sight, with an example of each. *(WR13, TF15)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> Hearing: "drumming the tin roof," "thunder rolled." Smell: "smelled green and new." Touch: "barefoot."

**6.** Which details tell you the poem is set on the prairie? *(TF20)* **[1 point]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 1)

=> The canola field, the porch and tin roof of a farm, the wide sky.

## Cold read 2

::: poem What the Lake Knows | a new poem
In the morning the lake is a mirror
and the mountain looks at itself,
checking its snow, its stones,
the one pine that leans.

A loon calls, long and low,
the saddest happy sound I know,
and the mirror shivers,
and the mountain breaks into a hundred mountains.

I skip a stone: one, two, three, four.
The rings go out and out and out,
the way a thing you say
keeps going after you've gone quiet.
:::

**1.** "the lake is a mirror" (stanza 1). What does this metaphor help you picture? *(VO14)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> The lake is so calm and smooth that the mountain is reflected perfectly in it.

**2.** "the mountain breaks into a hundred mountains" (stanza 2). What is really happening? *(CO6, VO14)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> The loon's call (or the breeze) makes ripples on the water, so the reflection breaks into many wobbly pieces.

**3.** "the saddest happy sound I know" seems to say two opposite things. What do you think the speaker means? *(VO14, TF13)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 3)

=> A loon's call is beautiful (happy) but long and lonely-sounding (sad); it makes the speaker feel both at once.

**4.** Find an example of repetition. Why might the poet repeat those words? *(TF15)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> "out and out and out": it makes the rings seem to keep spreading, on and on.

**5.** The last stanza compares the rings on the water to something else. What, and what does it mean? *(VO13, CO8)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 3)

=> To words we say: like ripples, words keep spreading and affecting people even after we stop talking.

**6.** Compare this poem with *Coulee in October*. How do both speakers feel about their place? *(TF20, CO12)* **[2 points]**

::: frame
I think ___ . In stanza ___ , the poem says "___ ." This shows ___ .
:::

(lines 3)

=> Both are quiet and thoughtful, finding a bigger meaning in nature (the land remembers; words keep going).
