## How to use this reader

A **script** is a story written to be **performed**. Instead of paragraphs, it has a **cast list**, a **setting**, **scenes**, **character names** before each line, and **stage directions** in brackets that tell actors what to do and how to say things. There are no quotation marks: the words after a character's name are what they say.

## Play 1 · The Lost Lunch

**Cast:** Priya (worried about her lunch) · Marcus (her best friend, a detective at heart) · Ms. Lam (the lunch supervisor) · Owen (a Grade 2 student) · Jada (always hungry) · Narrator

**Setting:** The school lunchroom, 11:55 a.m. A table, a lunch cart, a lost-and-found box.

::: script The Lost Lunch
NARRATOR: Every school has a mystery. At Westwood Elementary, it began at 11:55 on a Tuesday.
PRIYA: (digging through her backpack, faster and faster) It's gone. It's GONE.
MARCUS: (sliding into the seat beside her) What's gone?
PRIYA: My lunch! My grandma's samosas. She made them last night. They're in a green container with a dinosaur sticker.
MARCUS: (pulling an imaginary notebook from his pocket) Don't panic. Every mystery has clues. When did you last see it?
PRIYA: (thinking) This morning. In my cubby. Next to Jada's.
MARCUS: (narrowing his eyes) Jada's cubby. Interesting.
JADA: (walking past with a full tray, chewing) Hey, guys. What's up?
MARCUS: (slowly) Jada. What are you eating?
JADA: (holding up a sandwich) Peanut butter and banana. Why?
MARCUS: (leaning in) No… samosas?
JADA: (offended) I would NEVER take someone's samosas. (pause) Unless they offered.
PRIYA: (sighing) It's not her, Marcus.
MS. LAM: (walking over with the lost-and-found box) Everything okay here?
PRIYA: I lost my lunch. A green container. Dinosaur sticker.
MS. LAM: (smiling) Hmm. Owen? Can you come here, please?
OWEN: (shuffling over, holding a green container behind his back) …Yes?
MARCUS: (pointing dramatically) The dinosaur sticker!
OWEN: (holding it out, eyes wide) I thought it was mine! I have a dinosaur sticker too! (showing his own, identical container) See?
NARRATOR: There were two green containers. Two dinosaur stickers. And one very confused Grade 2.
PRIYA: (opening the container, laughing) Owen, did you eat any?
OWEN: (whispering) Just one. They were really, really good.
PRIYA: (handing him another) Then you'd better have one more. My grandma would want you to.
MARCUS: (closing his imaginary notebook) Case closed.
JADA: (sliding closer) So… is anyone else offering?
NARRATOR: And that is how Westwood Elementary solved the Mystery of the Lost Lunch, and how Owen became Grandma's biggest fan.
:::

## Play 2 · The Tortoise and the Hare: The Rematch

*In the old fable by Aesop, a fast hare races a slow tortoise, stops for a nap, and loses. This play imagines what happens next.*

**Cast:** Narrator 1 · Narrator 2 · Hare (proud, fast-talking) · Tortoise (calm, slow-talking) · Fox (the race announcer) · Owl (the referee) · Squirrel and Rabbit (fans)

**Setting:** A forest racetrack. A starting line, a finish line, and an oak tree in the middle.

::: script The Tortoise and the Hare: The Rematch
NARRATOR 1: One year after the most embarrassing day of his life, Hare was back.
HARE: (stretching, bouncing on his toes) One year! One year of training! One year of NO NAPS! Today, I win!
NARRATOR 2: Tortoise, meanwhile, was eating a dandelion.
TORTOISE: (slowly, chewing) Good… morning… Hare.
HARE: (rolling his eyes) Do you even know there's a rematch today?
TORTOISE: (nodding, very slowly) I… do.
FOX: (into a pinecone microphone) Welcome, forest friends, to the Rematch of the Century! In this corner, the fastest feet in the forest: HARE!
SQUIRREL AND RABBIT: (cheering) HARE! HARE! HARE!
FOX: And in this corner, last year's surprise champion: TORTOISE!
SQUIRREL AND RABBIT: (politely clapping) Yay.
OWL: (stepping between them, serious) Rules. Stay on the path. No shortcuts. No naps. On your marks. Get set. (pause) GO!
SOUND: (a whistle)
NARRATOR 1: Hare shot off like a firework. In ten seconds, he was halfway there.
HARE: (calling back over his shoulder) See you NEXT YEAR, Tortoise!
NARRATOR 2: But at the oak tree in the middle of the track, Hare stopped.
HARE: (staring, worried) Wait. Last year, this is where I fell asleep. (shaking his head hard) No naps, no naps, no naps…
NARRATOR 1: He was so busy NOT napping that he started to worry. What if he tripped? What if he was too fast and missed the finish line? What if everyone laughed again?
HARE: (sitting down, head in his paws) What if I lose AGAIN?
NARRATOR 2: Twenty minutes later, Tortoise arrived at the oak tree.
TORTOISE: (slowly) Hare. Why… are you… sitting?
HARE: (quietly) Because if I don't finish, I can't lose.
TORTOISE: (lowering himself down beside him) Hare. Last year… I didn't win… because I was fast. I won… because I kept going.
HARE: (looking up) That's it? Just keep going?
TORTOISE: (nodding) Just… keep… going.
NARRATOR 1: And so, for the first time in forest history, Hare did something no one expected.
HARE: (standing, offering a paw) Then let's keep going. Together.
NARRATOR 2: They crossed the finish line side by side. Hare walking very, very slowly.
FOX: (into the microphone, confused) It's… a tie?
OWL: (smiling) It's a tie.
SQUIRREL AND RABBIT: (cheering) TORTOISE AND HARE! TORTOISE AND HARE!
TORTOISE: (to the audience, slowly) Slow… and steady…
HARE: (to the audience, quickly) …and sometimes you need a friend to remind you!
:::

## Play 3 · Under the Dome: A Play in Three Scenes

*Adapted from "Under the Dome," Story 2 in our Science Fiction Reader. Notice what changed when the story became a script.*

**Cast:** Theo (age 11) · Mémère (his great-grandmother, 103) · Dad · The Weather Voice (heard, not seen) · Kids in the Commons (2–4) · Narrator

**Setting:** New Banff, 2091, a town under a glass dome.

::: script Scene 1 · Theo's apartment, morning
WEATHER VOICE: (calm, flat, from everywhere at once) Good morning, New Banff. Today: twenty-two degrees and sunny.
DAD: (not looking up from his tablet) Of course it is.
THEO: (holding an old photograph, to Mémère) What were these kids throwing?
MÉMÈRE: (taking the photo, smiling) Snowballs. That was me, in the red hat.
THEO: What does snow feel like?
MÉMÈRE: (closing her eyes) Cold. And wet, and soft, and sharp. It squeaks when you walk on it at thirty below. (opening her eyes) The best days of my life were snow days.
DAD: (looking up) And the worst storms in history came right after. That's why we built the dome, Theo.
WEATHER VOICE: Special announcement. Today, for one hour, as an experiment, a panel in the Commons will open to outside weather. The outside forecast: snow.
THEO: (jumping up) Snow! Dad, can we go?
DAD: (shaking his head) Why would anyone want to be cold? We built the dome so we'd never have to be.
:::

::: script Scene 2 · The Commons, one o'clock
NARRATOR: Most people stayed home. But a few curious kids, a few old people, and one boy pushing his great-grandmother's wheelchair gathered in the Commons.
SOUND: (a long mechanical hiss)
KIDS: (looking up, whispering) It's opening…
NARRATOR: At first, nothing happened. Then, a single white flake drifted down.
THEO: (holding out his sleeve, barely breathing) Mémère… look. It's like a tiny star.
SOUND: (wind; a gust)
KIDS: (shrieking, spinning) It's snowing! It's SNOWING!
THEO: (laughing, shivering, mouth open to the sky) My fingers hurt! My nose is running! (shouting) I've never felt so alive!
MÉMÈRE: (laughing, snow in her hair) That's it, Theo. That's a snow day.
:::

::: script Scene 3 · Theo's apartment, that night
WEATHER VOICE: (calm) Returning to twenty-two degrees.
DAD: (handing Theo a towel, a little smile) You're soaked.
THEO: (sitting at the table with a pen and paper) Dad, how do you write a letter to the Weather Council?
DAD: (pausing) What would you say?
THEO: (writing, saying it aloud) The dome keeps us safe. But I think we need snow days too. Here is why…
NARRATOR: Dad read over his son's shoulder for a long time. Then he pulled up a chair.
DAD: (quietly) Start with Mémère's red hat.
:::

::: note Story to script: what changed?
**Narration became dialogue or stage directions.** In the story, the narrator tells us Theo's father thought outside weather was dangerous; in the script, Dad *says* it. **Feelings became actions.** "He had never felt so alive" became a line Theo shouts, plus directions: *(laughing, shivering, mouth open to the sky)*. **The story was cut into scenes** by place and time. **Something new was added:** in the script, Dad changes a little at the end; that's a choice the playwright made to give an actor something to show. A Narrator covers what can't be shown.
:::
