## Cold read 1 · The Last Puzzle Piece

**Cast:** Nia (age 10) · Grandpa Joe · Leo (age 6, Nia's brother)

**Setting:** A living room on a snowy Sunday. A table with a 1,000-piece puzzle, almost finished.

::: script The Last Puzzle Piece
NIA: (leaning over the table, triumphant) Nine hundred ninety-nine. One piece left, Grandpa!
GRANDPA JOE: (putting on his glasses, slowly) Three weeks. Three weeks of sky. Every piece blue.
NIA: (searching the table, then under it, faster and faster) Where is it? Where IS it?
GRANDPA JOE: (calmly sipping tea) It'll turn up. Puzzles are like that.
NIA: (flopping onto the couch) This is the worst day of my life.
LEO: (entering with a teddy bear, humming)
NIA: (sitting up, eyes narrowing) Leo. What's in the bear's mouth?
LEO: (hiding the bear behind his back) …Nothing.
GRANDPA JOE: (chuckling) Leo, my boy. Is the bear hungry?
LEO: (whispering) He wanted a snack. It looked like a blueberry.
NIA: (holding out her hand, trying not to laugh) Can I have the blueberry, please?
LEO: (handing it over, then quickly) Can I put it in?
NIA: (pausing, then smiling) Okay. Together.
GRANDPA JOE: (standing, as they press the piece in) One thousand.
NIA AND LEO: (cheering) One thousand!
GRANDPA JOE: (to the audience, winking) Puzzles are like that.
:::

**1.** What is the setting, and where in the script do you find it? *(TF18)* **[1 point]**

(lines 2)

=> A living room on a snowy Sunday; it's at the top, under the cast list.

**2.** Fill in the chart. *(TF19)* **[3 points]**

| Problem | Turning point | Ending |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[The last puzzle piece is missing]] | [[Leo's bear has it in its mouth ("a blueberry")]] | [[Nia and Leo put it in together; the puzzle is finished]] |

**3.** Describe Nia using one line and one stage direction as evidence. *(TF19, WR16)* **[2 points]**

(lines 3)

=> e.g., She's impatient and dramatic: "This is the worst day of my life," and she searches "faster and faster." (Also kind: she lets Leo put it in.)

**4.** How is Grandpa Joe different from Nia? *(CO12)* **[2 points]**

(lines 2)

=> He is calm and patient (sipping tea, "It'll turn up"), while Nia is frantic.

**5.** How should the actor say "Where IS it?" Name one paraverbal choice and one non-verbal choice. *(OL3)* **[2 points]**

%% frame: Paraverbal: I would say it ___ . Non-verbal: I would ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Paraverbal: louder, stressing "IS," frustrated tone. Non-verbal: searching under the table, throwing up her hands.

**6.** Grandpa says "Puzzles are like that" twice. What do you think he means the second time? *(CO6, TF9)* **[2 points]**

(lines 3)

=> That things often work out if you're patient, and sometimes the missing piece turns up in a surprising way, like life.

## Cold read 2 · From story to stage

**Read this story paragraph.**

::: passage Morning at the Bus Stop | A short story

The bus was late again. Amir stamped his boots to stay warm and checked the time for the fifth time. "If it doesn't come in two minutes," he told his friend Ruby, "I'm walking." Ruby didn't answer. She was staring at something in the snowbank: a tiny grey kitten, shivering. "Amir," she whispered, "look." Amir forgot all about the bus. He knelt down slowly and unzipped his coat.
:::

**1.** Write a script for this paragraph. Include a setting, character names, dialogue and at least **two** stage directions. *(WR2, WR12, CV2, CV3)* **[4 points]**

(write 10)

=> Example:
=> Setting: A bus stop on a freezing winter morning. A snowbank.
=> AMIR: (stamping his boots, checking his watch) If it doesn't come in two minutes, I'm walking.
=> RUBY: (not listening, staring at the snowbank, then whispering) Amir… look.
=> AMIR: (kneeling slowly, unzipping his coat) Hey, little one. It's okay.
=> (Scoring: setting 1, names and format 1, dialogue that keeps the meaning 1, two useful stage directions 1.)

**2.** The story says "Amir forgot all about the bus." How did you show that on stage without a narrator? *(TF18, WR15)* **[2 points]**

(lines 3)

=> e.g., A stage direction: he stops checking his watch and kneels by the kitten; or he says something about the kitten instead of the bus.

**3.** How would Ruby say "Amir, look"? Explain your choice. *(OL3, WR16)* **[2 points]**

%% frame: Ruby would say it ___ because ___ .

(lines 2)

=> Whispering, softly, so she doesn't scare the kitten.

**4.** What might happen in the next scene? Write two lines of dialogue for it. *(WR12, CO7)* **[2 points]**
