## 1 · How a script works

**Learning target:** I can name the parts of a script and read one in a role. *(TF18, TF3)*

::: wordbank
character
do
place and time
says
shown
speaking
where and when
:::

| Part | What it does | Example (Play 1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cast list** | Names every [[character]] | Priya, Marcus, Ms. Lam, Owen, Jada, Narrator |
| **Setting** | Tells [[where and when]] | The school lunchroom, 11:55 a.m. |
| **Scene** | One part of the play in one [[place and time]] | Play 3 has three scenes |
| **Character name** | Shows who is [[speaking]] | MARCUS: |
| **Dialogue** | The words the actor [[says]] (no quotation marks!) | Don't panic. Every mystery has clues. |
| **Stage directions** | Tell the actor what to [[do]] or how to say it, in brackets | (pulling an imaginary notebook from his pocket) |
| **Narrator** | Tells the audience what can't be [[shown]] | "Every school has a mystery." |

**Reading a script aloud:** read only your character's words, not the name and not the stage directions. Follow along so you know your **cue** (the line before yours).

::: myth
WRONG: "Read the stage directions out loud."
WHY: They're on the page, so they seem part of the lines.
RIGHT: Stage directions are for the actor to **do**, not say. Act them out.
:::

**Quick check.** Write one line from Play 1 that has a stage direction. What should the actor do?

::: frame
The line is ___ . The actor should ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> e.g., PRIYA: (digging through her backpack, faster and faster) It's gone. The actor digs faster and faster, getting more upset.

## 2 · Characters on stage

**Learning target:** I can describe a character from what they say and do in a script. *(TF19, TF10, WR16)*

::: wordbank
a dramatic, pretend detective
actions
calm, patient, wise
dialogue
worried, afraid of failing
:::

In a script, there's no narrator inside a character's head, so we learn about characters from their [[dialogue]] and their [[actions]] (stage directions).

| Character | A line or action | So the character is… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marcus (Play 1) | (narrowing his eyes) Jada's cubby. Interesting. | [[a dramatic, pretend detective]] |
| Hare (Play 2) | What if I lose AGAIN? | [[worried, afraid of failing]] |
| Tortoise (Play 2) | I won… because I kept going. | [[calm, patient, wise]] |

**How dialogue shows character:** *how much* they talk, *how* they talk (fast, slow, ALL CAPS), and *what* they care about.

**Quick check.** How does the way Tortoise talks (with "…") match his character?

::: frame
Tortoise talks ___ , which shows he is ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> He talks slowly with pauses, which shows he's calm and patient, just like how he races.

## 3 · Events in scenes

**Learning target:** I can map the main events of a play and explain how the characters change. *(TF19, TF3)*

::: wordbank
At the oak tree, Hare is too afraid of losing to keep going
Hare goes from bragging to working with Tortoise
Hare is back for a rematch, sure he'll win
They finish together in a tie
Tortoise catches up and tells him the secret: keep going
:::

| Part | Play 2: The Rematch |
| --- | --- |
| Beginning | [[Hare is back for a rematch, sure he'll win]] |
| Problem | [[At the oak tree, Hare is too afraid of losing to keep going]] |
| Events | [[Tortoise catches up and tells him the secret: keep going]] |
| Resolution | [[They finish together in a tie]] |
| How a character changes | [[Hare goes from bragging to working with Tortoise]] |

**Quick check.** In one sentence, what is the theme of the Rematch?

::: frame
The play shows that ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> e.g., Fear of failing can stop you more than any opponent, and a friend can help you keep going.

## 4 · Three kinds of language on stage

**Learning target:** I can use verbal, non-verbal and paraverbal language to bring a character to life. *(OL3, OL4)*

::: wordbank
body
stress, pitch, pace and face
words
:::

| Kind | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Verbal** | The [[words]] you say | Precise word choice; the playwright's lines |
| **Non-verbal** | Your [[body]] | Facial expression, gesture, posture, movement, eye contact |
| **Paraverbal** | **How** you say the words | **Stress**, **pace**, **pitch** (high/low), **volume**, **tone**, **articulation** (clear sounds) |

**Projection:** to be heard at the back of the room, use **breath** (from your belly), **body** (stand tall, face the audience) and **energy**, not shouting.

**Try it:** say "What are you eating?" four ways: suspicious · friendly · surprised · bored. What changed? [[stress, pitch, pace and face]]

**Quick check.** Hare says "What if I lose AGAIN?" Describe how you'd say it using one non-verbal and one paraverbal choice.

::: frame
I would ___ (body) and say it ___ (voice) because ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> e.g., Sit down with my head in my hands (non-verbal) and say it quietly and slowly, stressing "AGAIN" (paraverbal), because he's scared and embarrassed.

## 5 · From story to script

**Learning target:** I can turn part of a story into a script. *(WR2, WR12, TF18)*

::: wordbank
A line plus actions that show it
A line the character says, or a narrator line
A new scene with a new setting
A stage direction
NAME: then the words, no quotation marks
:::

| In a story… | In a script it becomes… |
| --- | --- |
| Narration about what someone thinks | [[A line the character says, or a narrator line]] |
| A description of an action | [[A stage direction]] |
| Quotation marks around dialogue | [[NAME: then the words, no quotation marks]] |
| A change of place or time | [[A new scene with a new setting]] |
| A feeling ("he felt alive") | [[A line plus actions that show it]] |

::: example Watch me (Chinook Charlie, ¶3)
**Story:** *The townspeople came knocking at Charlie's cabin. Their teeth were chattering so hard that they sounded like a hundred typewriters. "Charlie," said Mrs. Okafor, the mayor, "the river is frozen right down to the bottom."*

**Script:**
SOUND: (knocking; chattering teeth)
MRS. OKAFOR: (shivering, hugging herself) Ch-Charlie! The river is frozen right down to the bottom!
:::

**Quick check.** Turn this into one line of script: *Sally Ann laughed so hard the branches shook. "Hold still," she called.*

::: frame
SALLY ANN: ( ___ ) ___
:::

(lines 2)

=> SALLY ANN: (laughing so hard the branches shake) Hold still!

## 6 · Planning a script: POW + the script planner

**Learning target:** I can plan a short play with my group. *(WR3, WR12, OL5)*

::: strategy POW
**P** **Pick** our story (a tale, a sci-fi story, or our own idea)
**O** **Organize** our notes (the script planner)
**W** **Write** and say more (scene by scene)
:::

::: strategy The script planner (WWW for the stage)
**Who** **Cast**: every character, with one word for how they act
**Where** **Setting**: one for each scene (2–3 scenes)
**What** **Problem**: what goes wrong or what does someone want?
**What** **Events**: what happens in each scene?
**How** **Ending**: how is it resolved? How do characters feel?
**+** **Show it**: one line or action per character that shows who they are
:::

**Group roles:** writer (keeps the script), director (watches the whole stage), stage manager (props, sound, cues), and everyone acts.

**Quick check.** From memory: what goes in a cast list, and what goes in stage directions?

::: frame
A cast list has ___ . Stage directions tell ___ .
:::

(lines 2)

=> A cast list names every character (sometimes with a description). Stage directions tell actors what to do and how to say lines.

## 7 · Writing dialogue and directions

**Learning target:** I can write lines that show character and punctuate a script correctly. *(WR16, CV2, CV3)*

::: wordbank
colon
line
quotation marks
:::

**Script punctuation:**
1. Character name in **CAPITALS**, then a [[colon]]: `OWEN:`
2. No [[quotation marks]] around the words.
3. Stage directions in **brackets (parentheses)**, often in *italics*: (whispering)
4. A new character = a new [[line]].
5. Punctuation shows delivery: **CAPITALS** for stress, **…** for a pause, **!** for energy, **—** for being cut off.

**Lines that show, not tell:**
- *Tells:* JADA: I am always hungry.
- *Shows:* JADA: (sliding closer) So… is anyone else offering?

**Quick check.** Rewrite so it *shows* that Owen is nervous: *OWEN: I am nervous.*

::: frame
OWEN: ( ___ ) ___
:::

(lines 2)

=> e.g., OWEN: (shuffling over, holding the container behind his back) …Yes?
