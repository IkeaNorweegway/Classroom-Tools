::: card Ticket 1 · Lesson 5 · Script parts and characters
Name ____________________

**1.** Name three parts of a script and what each does. *(TF18)*

(lines 3)

=> Any three: cast list (characters), setting (where/when), scene (one place and time), character name (who speaks), dialogue (what they say), stage directions (what to do / how to say it), narrator (what can't be shown).

**2.** How does Hare change in *The Rematch*? *(TF19)*

(lines 2)

=> From proud and sure he'll win to scared, then to working together with Tortoise.
:::

::: card Ticket 2 · Lesson 10 · Verbal, non-verbal, paraverbal
Name ____________________

**1.** Give one example of each: verbal, non-verbal, paraverbal. *(OL3)*

(lines 3)

=> Verbal: choosing the word "whispered." Non-verbal: shrugging. Paraverbal: speaking slowly and softly.

**2.** Name two things that help you project your voice. *(OL4)*

(lines 2)

=> Breath from the belly; standing tall facing the audience; energy.
:::

::: card Ticket 3 · Lesson 13 · Story to script
Name ____________________

**1.** Turn into a script line: *"Look out!" shouted Owen, pointing at the door.* *(WR2, CV2)*

(lines 1)

=> OWEN: (shouting, pointing at the door) Look out!

**2.** What happens to narration (like "Theo felt alive") when a story becomes a script? *(TF18)*

(lines 2)

=> It becomes a line the character says or actions in stage directions (or a narrator line).
:::

::: card Ticket 4 · Lesson 18 · Lines that show
Name ____________________

**1.** Rewrite to show, not tell: *JADA: I am so hungry.* *(WR16)*

(lines 2)

=> e.g., JADA: (staring at Priya's container) Are you… going to finish those?

**2.** Why do stage directions matter for showing character? *(WR16)*

(lines 2)

=> They tell actors how to move and speak, so the audience sees feelings without the character saying them.
:::

::: card Ticket 5 · Lesson 20 · Script punctuation
Name ____________________

**1.** Fix: *"I'm here," said Tortoise slowly. "Finally!" said Hare.* *(CV2, CV3)*

(lines 3)

=> TORTOISE: (slowly) I'm here. / HARE: Finally!

**2.** What does "…" tell an actor? What does "—" tell an actor? *(CV3)*

(lines 2)

=> "…" means pause or trail off; "—" means the line is cut off or interrupted.
:::
