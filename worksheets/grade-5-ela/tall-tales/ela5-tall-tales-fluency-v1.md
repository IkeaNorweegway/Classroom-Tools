::: teacher How to use this pack
**Reader's theatre** (whole class, Weeks 2–3, performed in Lesson 15): Monday the teacher reads the script aloud; Tuesday–Thursday groups rehearse 10–15 minutes with feedback on phrasing and expression; perform. Give struggling readers parts with enough lines to practise (Narrator 2, Sourdough Sam).
**Repeated reading** (fluency group, 3–5 times a week, about 10 minutes): model the passage; the student reads it 3–4 times with an adult or trained partner correcting errors right away; time one read for words correct per minute (WCPM); graph it; ask one question about meaning. Use the four passages below, then F&P leveled passages at about 95% accuracy.
**Partner reading** (PALS-style, whole class, 3 days a week): pair students by splitting the ranked class list in half and matching the top of each half. Stronger reader reads first; partner rereads; listener corrects errors; then "paragraph shrinking".
Fluency is accuracy, pace **and** expression. Score expression with the scale on the last page, not only speed (research §5).
:::

## Reader's theatre: *Paul Bunyan and the Blue Snow*

**Parts:** Narrator 1 · Narrator 2 · Paul Bunyan · Sourdough Sam (the cook) · Logger 1 · Logger 2 · Babe the Blue Ox (sound effects and one line!) · Skater

::: script Paul Bunyan and the Blue Snow
NARRATOR 1: Gather round, folks, and we'll tell you about the coldest winter that ever was.
NARRATOR 2: It was the Winter of the Blue Snow, and it happened in Paul Bunyan's logging camp.
PAUL: (booming) GOOD MORNING, LOGGERS!
NARRATOR 1: When Paul said good morning, the trees in the next valley fell over.
LOGGER 1: Morning, Paul! Say, what's that falling from the sky?
LOGGER 2: It's snow. But it's… blue!
NARRATOR 2: Blue flakes as big as dinner plates came floating down. And then came the cold.
LOGGER 1: (shivering) It's so cold, my words are freezing in the air! Look, there goes "Good morning," right onto the ground.
LOGGER 2: Pick it up and carry it inside, or nobody will ever hear it!
NARRATOR 1: Out in a snowdrift, Paul heard something small and sad.
BABE: (tiny voice) Mooo…
PAUL: Well, look at you, little one. Blue as the sky and shivering like a leaf. Come inside my coat.
NARRATOR 2: Paul named her Babe. Watch her for an hour, and you could see her grow.
BABE: (bigger voice) MOOOO!
NARRATOR 1: But the camp had a new problem.
LOGGER 1: Paul, we're starving!
LOGGER 2: Hungry loggers can't chop trees!
SAM: I'm flipping pancakes as fast as I can, boss, but these fellows eat a hundred stacks each!
PAUL: Then we need a bigger griddle, Sam. The biggest griddle in the world.
NARRATOR 2: Paul built a griddle so wide you couldn't see across it on a foggy day.
SAM: But how will I grease a griddle that big?
SKATER: Leave it to us! Strap some bacon to our boots!
NARRATOR 1: Fifty skaters zipped back and forth across the hot iron.
SKATER: (skating) Whee! Sizzle, sizzle, slide!
SAM: Pancakes! Pancakes by the wagonload! Babe, haul them out!
BABE: (proudly) MOOOOOO!
LOGGER 1: (mouth full) Best… pancakes… ever!
LOGGER 2: Now let's get back to work!
NARRATOR 2: That winter the camp cut more wood than any camp in history.
NARRATOR 1: And when spring came, every frozen word thawed out at once.
ALL: (all talking at once) GOOD MORNING! PASS THE SYRUP! WATCH OUT FOR THAT TREE! MOOOO!
NARRATOR 2: For three days nobody could hear a thing.
PAUL: (whispering, for once) And that, folks, is the Winter of the Blue Snow.
:::

**Rehearsal checklist:** ☐ I highlighted my lines ☐ I practised them three times ☐ I know which word in each line to stress ☐ I pause at commas and periods ☐ My voice matches the character

## Repeated reading passages

Read the passage to your partner. The partner follows along and marks any word you miss. Use the numbers to count the words you read in one minute. If you finish before the minute is up, start again at the top and keep counting. Then read it again, and again. Try to sound like a storyteller, not a race car.

## How did it sound? (expression scale)

Your partner or teacher circles one number in each row.

| | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Expression and volume** | Flat, quiet, like reading a list | Some expression, but not always matching | Mostly matches the meaning | Sounds like a storyteller; volume changes on purpose |
| **Phrasing** | Word by word | Two- or three-word chunks | Mostly good phrases; pauses at punctuation | Smooth phrases; stress on the important words |
| **Smoothness** | Many stops and sound-outs | Some long pauses | A few rough spots, fixes them quickly | Smooth; fixes mistakes without stopping the flow |
| **Pace** | Slow and laboured | Uneven | Mostly like talking | Like talking; slows down for effect |
