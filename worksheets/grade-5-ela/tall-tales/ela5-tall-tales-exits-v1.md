::: card Ticket 1 · Lesson 5 · Tall tales and story parts
Name ____________________

**1.** Give two features that make *Paul Bunyan* a tall tale. *(TF7)*

(lines 2)

=> Any two: a hero with impossible size/strength (rocked in a lumber wagon); exaggeration (words freeze in the air); a real place/job (logging camps); explains nature (hoofprints became lakes).

**2.** What is the main problem in *Paul Bunyan*, and how is it solved? *(TF3)*

(lines 2)

=> The loggers are starving in the Blue Snow winter; Paul builds a giant griddle so they can be fed and get back to work.
:::

::: card Ticket 2 · Lesson 10 · Characters and comparisons
Name ____________________

**1.** Name one trait of John Henry. Give the evidence and say whether it is something he *says*, *thinks*, *does*, or what *others* say. *(TF10, CO9)*

(lines 3)

=> e.g., Determined: he keeps swinging without stopping for water when the machine is ahead (does, ¶6).

**2.** "The hammer whistled through the air like the wind before a storm." What does this comparison help you picture? *(VO14)*

(lines 2)

=> How fast and powerful the hammer was; it hints that something huge is about to happen.
:::

::: card Ticket 3 · Lesson 13 · Summary, inference and the story parts
Name ____________________

**1.** Write a gist sentence (20 words or fewer) for *Sally Ann Thunder Ann Whirlwind*. *(CO4)*

(lines 2)

=> e.g., Sally Ann, a fearless frontier woman, rescues Davy Crockett from a tree and scares off a bear with her smile.

**2.** From memory: write the seven WWW, What = 2, How = 2 questions as single words or short phrases. *(WR3)*

(lines 3)

=> Who, When, Where, What does the main character want, What happens, How does it end, How do the characters feel.

::: card Ticket 4 · Lesson 18 · Show, don't tell
Name ____________________

**1.** Rewrite to *show*, not tell: *The town was very cold.* *(WR15)*

(lines 2)

=> e.g., The town was so cold that the church bell froze mid-ring and didn't finish its "dong" until April.

**2.** Why do authors show instead of tell? *(WR11)*

(lines 2)

=> It lets readers picture it and figure it out themselves, so the story is more interesting and memorable.
:::
