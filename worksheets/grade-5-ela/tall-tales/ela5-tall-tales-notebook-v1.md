## Lesson 8 · Reading between the lines

Use **Anchor note 6**. Answer each question with the frame: *I think… In paragraph __, the text says "…". This shows… because…*

**1.** Why do the people in the settlement think Sally Ann will "settle down" after the wedding (¶6)? What does that tell you about what people expected?

(lines 4)

=> I think people expected married women to stay home and act quietly. In paragraph 6 the text says "People in the settlement thought Sally Ann would settle down after the wedding. She did not." This shows they had ideas about how she should behave, and Sally Ann surprised them by staying bold.

**2.** Why doesn't Sally Ann roar back at the bear?

(lines 4)

=> I think she knew she didn't need to fight. In paragraph 8 she says "That bear was only hungry." This shows she understood the bear and used calm confidence (her smile) instead of force.

**Monitor.** Was there a sentence you had to reread? Write the ¶ number and what helped you understand it.

(lines 3)

**Points of view.** How does each character see the steam drill? *(CO11, CO12)*

## Lesson 11 · *Big Joe Mufferaw*: how a legend grows

Read Tale 4. Show how the wagon story grew each time it was told (¶3).

| First telling | Later telling | Latest telling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Joe could lift a loaded wagon]] | [[…with the horses still attached]] | [[…with the horses and driver, while drinking tea]] |

**Real or legend?** Sort these. Write **R** (probably real) or **L** (legend).

| Statement | R or L |
| --- | --- |
| Joseph Montferrand was born in Montreal in 1802. | [[R]] |
| He worked as a raftsman on the Ottawa River. | [[R]] |
| He paddled 300 km in an afternoon and beat his own shadow. | [[L]] |
| French- and English-speaking raftsmen both told stories about him. | [[R]] |

**Write about it.** Why do you think the raftsmen kept making Joe's stories bigger? Use what you know about where and when the stories were told. *(OL1, CO14)*

(lines 5)

=> I think they made the stories bigger to entertain each other on long nights by the fire (¶3) and to show pride in one of their own. Raftsmen had a hard, dangerous job, so a hero who was stronger than anything made them proud. Each teller "tried to beat the last one" (¶3), so it was also a contest.

## Lesson 18 · Show, don't tell

Use **Anchor note 8**. Rewrite each telling sentence so it shows.

**1.** *The giant was hungry.*

(lines 2)

=> Example: The giant's stomach growled so loudly that the windows rattled three towns away.

**2.** *The wind was strong.*

(lines 2)

=> Example: The wind ripped the barn roof off and delivered it to a farm in Manitoba.

**3.** *Mrs. Okafor was worried.*

(lines 2)

=> Example: Mrs. Okafor twisted her mittens and paced back and forth until she had worn a path in the snow.

**Exaggeration ladder.** Make it bigger in three steps. The top step should show something about the hero.

| Step | My hero is strong |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Real | [[She can carry two backpacks.]] |
| 2. Bigger | [[She can carry a car.]] |
| 3. Tall tale | [[She carried the whole town on her back to higher ground when the river flooded.]] |
