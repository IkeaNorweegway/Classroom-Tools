## core | LA1 — Lines, Angles & Congruence, Section 1
Leo names the three figures.
He writes: "1 is ray AB. It goes both ways. 2 is line MN. It has two endpoints."
@viz lines items="ray:B,A;line:M,N;segment:E,F" notitle=1 labels="1;2;3"
a) Name each figure. Use the right word and letters.
b) Fix Leo's two mistakes.
(lines 3)
=> a) 1 is ray BA. The endpoint B goes first. 2 is line MN (or line NM). 3 is segment EF (or segment FE).
=> b) A ray goes one way only. It has one endpoint. A line has no endpoints. It goes forever both ways.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"A ray goes both ways" or "a line has end points." Have the student count the arrowheads. Two means a line. One means a ray, and its endpoint is written first.
:::

## core | CI1 — Circles, Section 1
Ben wants a circle 10 cm across. He opens his compass to 10 cm.
The pictures show the right way to draw it.
@viz compass r=5 rlabel="[[5 cm]]" caption="The compass opening is the radius."
a) How wide will Ben's circle be?
b) How wide should he open the compass? Use the ruler.
c) A circle has a radius of 6.5 cm. Find its diameter.
@viz circle show=rd rlabel="r = 6.5 cm" dlabel="d = [[13 cm]]" size=56
(lines 3)
=> a) The compass opening is the radius. A 10 cm radius makes a circle 20 cm across.
=> b) 10 ÷ 2 = 5. Open it to 5 cm.
=> c) d = 2 × r = 2 × 6.5 = 13 cm.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"The radius and the diameter are the same." Have the student draw the diameter through the centre. Count the radii in it: two. So d = 2r.
:::

## core | VO1 — Volume, Section 1
A tent lies on the ground. It is a prism.
Maya says, "The base is the rectangle on the ground."
@viz solid kind=tri bv=4 thv=3 lv=8 ground=1 shade=0 size=200
a) Shade the two bases.
b) Name the prism.
c) How many lateral faces does it have?
(lines 3)
=> a) The two triangle ends are the bases. They match. They are on opposite ends.
=> b) A triangular prism.
=> c) 3 lateral faces. They are all rectangles. The one on the ground is one of them.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"The base of a prism is the face it sits on." Ask: which two faces match on opposite ends? Stand the tent on its triangle. It is the same prism.
:::

## core | LA3 — Lines, Angles & Congruence, Section 3
Two straight roads cross.
Raj says, "Opposite angles add to 180°. So ∠3 is 125°."
@viz angles kind=intersect a=55 labels="55°;∠2;∠3;∠4"
a) Find ∠2, ∠3 and ∠4. Give a reason for each.
b) Is Raj right? Explain.
(lines 4)
=> a) ∠2 = 180° − 55° = 125°. It sits beside 55° on a line.
=> ∠3 = 55°. It is opposite the 55° angle.
=> ∠4 = 125°. It is opposite ∠2.
=> b) No. Opposite angles are equal. Angles side by side on a line add to 180°.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Opposite angles add to 180°." Have the student trace the two angles that share a side. Those make a straight line: 180°. The angles across the X match.
:::

## core | CI2 — Circles, Section 2
A wheelchair wheel is 61 cm across.
@viz wheel dlabel="d = 61 cm" clabel="one turn = [[191.5 cm]]"
a) Estimate one turn. Use 3 × d.
b) Find the circumference. Round to one decimal place.
c) How far does the wheel roll in 10 turns? Give your answer in metres.
(lines 3)
=> a) 3 × 61 = 183. So one turn is a bit more than 183 cm.
=> b) C = πd ≈ 3.14 × 61 = 191.54. C ≈ 191.5 cm.
=> c) 191.54 × 10 = 1915.4 cm. That is about 19.2 m.

## core | VO2 — Volume, Section 2
A box of sugar cubes is full. Each cube is 1 cm³.
@viz layers l=5 w=4 h=3 llabel="one layer: [[20]] cubes" blabel="[[3]] layers"
a) How many cubes are in one layer?
b) How many layers are there?
c) Find the volume.
d) Noor writes 60 cm². What is wrong?
(lines 4)
=> a) 5 × 4 = 20 cubes.
=> b) 3 layers.
=> c) 20 × 3 = 60 cubes. V = 60 cm³.
=> d) cm² counts flat squares. Volume counts cubes. The unit is cm³.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Volume is in square units." Hold up a 1 cm cube and a 1 cm square of paper. Which one fills the box? The little 3 shows three measures: length, width and height.
:::

## core | LA2 — Lines, Angles & Congruence, Section 2
Look at the two shapes.
Leo says, "In Shape 1, AB ∥ DC. It looks like it."
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;6,3;0,3.3" names="A,B,C,D" right="1,2" sides=";3 cm;;3.3 cm" cap="Shape 1" pts2="0,0;6,0;4.5,3;0,3" names2="P,Q,R,S" right2="1,4" par2="1,0,1,0" cap2="Shape 2" unit=24
a) In Shape 2, which sides are parallel? Use the symbol ∥.
b) In Shape 2, name two sides that are perpendicular. Use ⊥.
c) Is Leo right about Shape 1? Explain.
(lines 4)
=> a) PQ ∥ SR. The matching arrowheads show it.
=> b) PQ ⊥ PS (or SR ⊥ SP). The small squares show right angles.
=> c) No. AB and DC have no arrowheads. The sides are 3 cm and 3.3 cm. So DC tilts. It is not parallel to AB.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Lines that look parallel are parallel." Only matching arrowheads, or a reason from angles, prove it. Have the student measure the gap between the lines at both ends.
:::

## core | CI3 — Circles, Section 3
A round trampoline is 4 m across.
Ava finds its area: A = 3.14 × 4 × 4 = 50.24 m².
@viz circle show=d dlabel="d = 4 m" fill=plant size=56
a) Find Ava's mistake.
b) Find the area.
c) How many times too big is Ava's answer?
(lines 4)
=> a) She used the diameter. A = πr² uses the radius.
=> b) r = 4 ÷ 2 = 2 m. A ≈ 3.14 × 2 × 2 = 12.56 m².
=> c) 50.24 ÷ 12.56 = 4. Her answer is 4 times too big.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Use the diameter in A = πr²." Have the student write the radius down before any area. Halve the diameter first, every time.
:::

## core | VO3 — Volume, Section 3
A skate ramp is a triangular prism.
Kai finds B = 1.5 m². Then he multiplies by 1 m.
@viz solid kind=tri right=1 b="3 m" th="1 m" l="4 m" bv=3 thv=1 lv=4 blabel="B" fill=earth size=200
a) Find the base area B. Show it.
b) Which length is the prism's height h?
c) Find the volume.
(lines 4)
=> a) B = 3 × 1 ÷ 2 = 1.5 m².
=> b) h = 4 m. It is the distance between the two triangle bases.
=> c) V = B × h = 1.5 × 4 = 6 m³.
=> Kai used the triangle's height twice. It is already inside B.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Use the triangle's height as the prism's height." There are two heights. The triangle's height (1 m) goes in B. The prism's height (4 m) joins the two bases.
:::

## core | LA4 — Lines, Angles & Congruence, Section 4
The two triangles are congruent.
Jo writes △ABC ≅ △DEF. She says the letter order doesn't matter.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;1,3" names="A,B,C" ticks="1,2,3" arcs="1,2,3" sides="5 cm;;" pts2="0,0;-5,0;-1,3" names2="E,D,F" ticks2="1,2,3" arcs2="1,2,3" unit=24
a) Which vertex matches A? B? C? Use the arcs.
b) Write the congruence statement in the right order.
c) AB = 5 cm. Which side of the other triangle is 5 cm?
(lines 4)
=> a) A ↔ E (one arc). B ↔ D (two arcs). C ↔ F (three arcs).
=> b) △ABC ≅ △EDF.
=> c) ED. It matches AB, with one tick.
=> Jo's order says A matches D. That is wrong.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"The letter order in △ABC ≅ △RST doesn't matter." Have the student draw an arrow from each vertex to its match first. Then write the letters in arrow order.
:::

## core | CI4 — Circles, Section 4
A round flower bed is 6 m across. It needs a fence around it. It needs soil inside.
Sam writes: fence ≈ 18.84 m².
@viz circle show=d dlabel="d = 6 m" clabel="fence: C" alabel="soil: A" fill=plant size=64
a) Find the length of fence.
b) Find the area to cover with soil.
c) Sam's unit is wrong. Explain.
(lines 4)
=> a) C = πd ≈ 3.14 × 6 = 18.84 m.
=> b) r = 3 m. A ≈ 3.14 × 3 × 3 = 28.26 m².
=> c) A fence is a length. It is in m. Area covers the inside. It is in m².
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Circumference is in square units." Ask: is the fence a line or a patch? A line is measured in m. A patch is measured in m².
:::

## ext | LA3 — Lines, Angles & Congruence, Section 3
Mia says, "∠6 matches ∠2. So ∠6 = 65° in both pictures."
@viz angles kind=transversal a=65 labels="1;65°;3;4;5;6;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t" caption="Picture A"
@viz angles kind=transversal a=65 parallel=0 marks=0 labels="1;65°;3;4;5;72°;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t" caption="Picture B"
a) In Picture A, find ∠6, ∠5 and ∠1. Give a reason for each.
b) In Picture B, find ∠1 and ∠3.
c) Is Mia right? Explain.
(lines 5)
=> a) ∠6 = 65°. It corresponds to ∠2, and m ∥ n. ∠5 = 180° − 65° = 115°. It sits beside ∠6 on a line. ∠1 = 115°. It sits beside ∠2.
=> b) ∠1 = 180° − 65° = 115°. ∠3 = 65°. It is opposite ∠2.
=> c) No. Corresponding angles match only when the lines are parallel. Picture B has no arrowheads. ∠6 = 72°, not 65°. So m and n are not parallel.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Corresponding angles are always equal." Point to the arrowheads in Picture A. Without them, the corresponding angles can be anything. Opposite and adjacent angles still work in Picture B.
:::

A tractor wheel rolls 471 cm in one turn.

c) Check your diameter.

## ext | VO4 — Volume, Section 4
A grain bin is a cylinder. It is 6 m across. It holds 113.04 m³ of canola.
@viz solid kind=cyl r="d = 6 m" h="h = ?" rv=3 hv=4 diameter=1 blabel="B" fill=cold size=140
a) Find the base area B.
b) Find the height of the bin.
c) Check your answer.
(lines 5)
=> a) r = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 m. B ≈ 3.14 × 3 × 3 = 28.26 m².
=> b) h = V ÷ B = 113.04 ÷ 28.26 = 4 m.
=> c) V = B × h = 28.26 × 4 = 113.04 m³ ✓.

## ext | CI4 — Circles, Section 4
A centre pivot waters a circle of wheat. Its arm is the radius.
Farmer Ana makes her arm twice as long. She says, "I will water twice the area."
@viz circle show=r rlabel="r = 200 m" title="A ≈ [[125 600 m²]]" fill=plant size=33 size2=66 show2=r rlabel2="r = 400 m" title2="A ≈ [[502 400 m²]]" fill2=plant
a) Find both areas.
b) How many times bigger is the new area?
c) Find both circumferences. How many times longer is the new one?
d) Is Ana right? Explain.
(lines 5)
=> a) 3.14 × 200 × 200 = 125 600 m². 3.14 × 400 × 400 = 502 400 m².
=> b) 502 400 ÷ 125 600 = 4 times.
=> c) 3.14 × 400 = 1256 m. 3.14 × 800 = 2512 m. The new one is 2 times longer.
=> d) No. Double the radius gives 4 times the area. It is 2 × 2 = 4, because r is used twice.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Double the radius, double the area." Draw the r-by-r square for each circle. The big square holds four small ones. Circumference doubles; area grows 4 times.
:::

## ext | VO4 — Volume, Section 4
A planter box is 1.2 m long and 40 cm wide. It holds 96 000 cm³ of soil.
Jay finds B = 1.2 × 40 = 48.
@viz solid kind=box l="1.2 m" w="40 cm" h="h = ?" lv=120 wv=40 hv=20 blabel="B" fill=earth size=180
a) Why is Jay's B wrong?
b) Find B in cm².
c) How deep is the soil?
d) A triangular prism has V = 60 cm³ and h = 10 cm. Find B.
(lines 5)
=> a) He mixed metres and centimetres. All lengths need the same unit.
=> b) 1.2 m = 120 cm. B = 120 × 40 = 4800 cm².
=> c) h = V ÷ B = 96 000 ÷ 4800 = 20 cm. Check: 4800 × 20 = 96 000 ✓.
=> d) B = V ÷ h = 60 ÷ 10 = 6 cm².

## stretch | CI3 + VO3 — Hay Bales
A round hay bale lies on the ground. It is 1.8 m across and 1.5 m long.
@viz solid kind=cyl lying=1 r="d = 1.8 m" h="h = 1.5 m" rv=0.9 hv=1.5 diameter=1 blabel="B" both=1 fill=sun ground=1 size=150
a) Which faces are the bases?
b) Find the base area B. Round to two decimal places.
c) Find the volume. Round to one decimal place.
d) Twine wraps once around the bale. About how long is it?
e) Which answers are in m, m² and m³? Why?
(lines 6)
=> a) The two circle ends. The bale sits on its curved surface.
=> b) r = 1.8 ÷ 2 = 0.9 m. B ≈ 3.14 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 3.14 × 0.81 = 2.5434. B ≈ 2.54 m².
=> c) V = B × h ≈ 2.5434 × 1.5 = 3.8151. V ≈ 3.8 m³.
=> d) C = πd ≈ 3.14 × 1.8 = 5.652. About 5.7 m.
=> e) Twine is a length: m. The base is an area: m². The volume fills space: m³.

## stretch | LA4 + VO4 — The Tent Ends
A tent is a triangular prism. Its two ends are congruent: △ABC ≅ △DEF.
The tent holds 6 m³ of air.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;4,0;1,3" names="A,B,C" sides="2 m;;" angles="72°;45°;63°" pts2="0,0;-4,0;-1,3" names2="D,E,F" sides2="[[2 m]];;" angles2=";[[45°]];" unit=24
@viz solid kind=tri b="2 m" th="1.5 m" l="h = ?" bv=2 thv=1.5 lv=4 blabel="B" size=200
a) Find DE and ∠E. Use the statement.
b) The triangle's height is 1.5 m. Find B.
c) How long is the tent?
d) Check your answer.
(lines 6)
=> a) D ↔ A and E ↔ B. So DE = AB = 2 m and ∠E = ∠B = 45°.
=> b) B = 2 × 1.5 ÷ 2 = 1.5 m².
=> c) h = V ÷ B = 6 ÷ 1.5 = 4 m.
=> d) V = 1.5 × 4 = 6 m³ ✓.
