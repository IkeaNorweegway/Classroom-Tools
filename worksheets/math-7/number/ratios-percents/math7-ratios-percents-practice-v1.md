## Core

### Lily's pancakes use 3 cups of flour to 2 cups of milk. Fill in the table for 2, 3 and 5 batches.
@viz dnl top="0,3,6,9,[[15]]" bottom="0,2,[[4]],[[6]],[[10]]" toplabel="flour (cups)" bottomlabel="milk (cups)"
| Flour (cups) | 3 | 6 | 9 | [[15]] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Milk (cups) | 2 | [[4]] | [[6]] | [[10]] |
=> Multiply both terms by the same factor. × 2: 6:4. × 3: 9:6. × 5: 15:10. (RP1)

### Ben says 4:5 = 6:7, because he added 2 to both terms. Look at the tapes. Is he right? Find a ratio that is equal to 4:5.
@viz tape parts="4:concentrate;5:water" caption="One batch: 4:5."
@viz tape parts="8:concentrate;10:water" caption="Two batches."
(lines 2)
=> No. Adding 2 changes the mix. Two batches of 4:5 are 8:10. (RP1)
=> Multiply (or divide) both terms by the same number. Targets the "add the same number to both terms" mix-up.

### Ava's bracelet has 3 red beads to 5 blue beads. Kai says {3/5} of the beads are red. Fix his mistake.
@viz tape parts="3:red;5:blue" total="[[8]] beads in all"
(lines 2)
=> 3:5 is part to part. There are 3 + 5 = 8 beads in all. (RP1)
=> {3/8} of the beads are red. Targets the "2:3 means 2 out of 3" mix-up.

### Find the factor for each pair of equivalent ratios.
@viz dnl top="0,3,6,9" bottom="0,4,8,12" arrow="2,3,× [[1.5]]"
a) 6:8 → 9:12. The factor is [[1.5]].
b) 10:4 → 5:2. The factor is [[0.5]].
c) 2:3 → 10:15. The factor is [[5]].
=> a) 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 and 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5. The factor is 1.5, or {3/2}. (RP1)
=> b) 5 ÷ 10 = 0.5 and 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. The factor is 0.5, or {1/2}. This is partitioning: ÷ 2.
=> c) 10 ÷ 2 = 5 and 15 ÷ 3 = 5.

### At Rogers Place, the ratio of Oilers fans to Flames fans in one section is 3:2. There are 60 Flames fans. How many Oilers fans are there?
@viz tape parts="3:Oilers fans;2:Flames fans" values="[[30]];[[30]]" total="60 Flames fans"
(work 2)
=> The Flames term goes from 2 to 60. The factor is 60 ÷ 2 = 30. (RP2)
=> 3 × 30 = 90. 3:2 = 90:60. There are 90 Oilers fans.

### Find each missing term.
a) 2:7 = 10:[[35]]
b) 6:4 = 15:[[10]]
c) 5:8 = [[25]]:40
=> a) × 5: 7 × 5 = 35. (RP2)
=> b) 6:4 = 3:2 (÷ 2). Then × 5: 15:10. Check: 6 × 10 = 60 and 4 × 15 = 60. ✓
=> c) 8 × 5 = 40, so 5 × 5 = 25.

### Juice A is 3:4 (concentrate to water). Juice B is 4:6. Zoe says B is stronger, because 4 cans is more than 3. Make a common term. Then find the stronger juice.
@viz tape parts="12:A concentrate;16:A water" caption="Juice A, 4 batches."
@viz tape parts="12:B concentrate;18:B water" caption="Juice B, 3 batches."
(lines 2)
=> Make the first terms match: 3:4 = 12:16 and 4:6 = 12:18. (RP2)
=> Same concentrate. A has less water. A is stronger. Zoe forgot that B also has more water. Targets the "more concentrate always means stronger" mix-up.

### Use benchmarks. Fill in the table for 120. Then find 35% of 120.
@viz percentbar percent=35 top="0%,10%,25%,35%,50%,100%" bottom="0,[[12]],[[30]],[[42]],[[60]],120" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="of 120"
| Percent | 50% | 25% | 10% | 5% | 1% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Of 120 | [[60]] | [[30]] | [[12]] | [[6]] | [[1.2]] |
(lines 1)
=> 50%: ÷ 2. 25%: ÷ 4. 10%: ÷ 10. 5%: half of 10%. 1%: ÷ 100. (RP3)
=> 35% = 25% + 10% = 30 + 12 = 42.

### Mia says 0.5% of 600 is 300. Use the picture to fix her mistake.
@viz hundred shaded=0.5 zoom=1 labels="0.5% of the grid"
(lines 2)
=> 300 is 50% of 600. 0.5% is half of 1%. (RP3)
=> 1% of 600 = 6. Half of 6 is 3. So 0.5% of 600 is 3. Targets the "0.5% is the same as 50%" mix-up.

### Leo says a percent can't be more than 100%. A sunflower was 40 cm tall. Now it is 150% of that height. How tall is it? Then find 250% of 40.
@viz percentbar percent=150 top="0%,50%,100%,150%" bottom="0 cm,20 cm,40 cm,[[60 cm]]" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="height"
(lines 2)
=> 150% = 100% + 50% = 40 + 20 = 60 cm. The sunflower is taller than before. (RP3)
=> 250% of 40 = 40 + 40 + 20 = 100. Targets the "a percent can't be more than 100%" mix-up.

### An Oilers hoodie costs $80. It is 25% off. Then 5% GST is added. Find the total.
@viz receipt title="Team store" items="Hoodie:$80.00;25% off:[[−$20.00]];Sale price:[[$60.00]];GST (5%):[[$3.00]];Total:[[$63.00]]"
(work 2)
=> 25% of $80 = $80 ÷ 4 = $20. Sale price: $80 − $20 = $60. (RP4)
=> GST: 10% of $60 = $6, so 5% = $3. Total: $60 + $3 = $63.

### Omar says a $100 price that goes 20% down, then 20% up, ends at $100. Fill in the steps. Is he right?
@viz flow steps="$100|20% off: [[$80]]|+ 20%: [[$16]]|[[$96]]"
(lines 2)
=> 20% of $100 = $20, so the sale price is $80. (RP4)
=> 20% of $80 = $16. $80 + $16 = $96. Omar is wrong: the second 20% is of a smaller number. Targets the "20% off, then add 20%" mix-up.

## Challenge

### Sam mixes juice in the ratio 2:5 (concentrate to water). He wants 21 cans of juice in all. How many cans of each does he use?
@viz tape parts="2:concentrate;5:water" values="[[3]]" total="21 cans in all"
(work 3)
=> 2 + 5 = 7 parts. 21 ÷ 7 = 3 cans in each part. (RP1, RP2)
=> Concentrate: 2 × 3 = 6 cans. Water: 5 × 3 = 15 cans. Check: 6:15 = 2:5 (÷ 3). ✓

### 3:4 and 6:8 are equivalent. Add the first terms and add the second terms. Is the new ratio equivalent too? Explain why.
(lines 3)
=> (3 + 6):(4 + 8) = 9:12. 9:12 = 3:4 (÷ 3), so yes. (RP2)
=> 3:4 is 1 batch and 6:8 is 2 batches. Together they are 3 batches: 9:12.

### Put these juices in order from strongest to weakest: 2:3, 3:5, 5:8 (concentrate to water).
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6,8,10" bottom="0,3,6,9,12,15" toplabel="2:3" bottomlabel="water"
(work 3)
=> Make the first terms match. 30 works: 2:3 = 30:45, 3:5 = 30:50, 5:8 = 30:48. (RP2)
=> Less water is stronger: 2:3, then 5:8, then 3:5.

### Use cross products.
a) Is 2.5:4 equal to 5:8?
b) Find the missing term: {1.5/2} = {?/10}.
(work 3)
=> a) 2.5 × 8 = 20 and 4 × 5 = 20. They match, so yes. (Also × 2: 2.5:4 = 5:8.) (RP2)
=> b) 1.5 × 10 = 2 × ?. 15 = 2 × ?. ? = 7.5. Check: 1.5:2 × 5 = 7.5:10. ✓

### A Stampede ride had 400 riders on Monday. On Tuesday, it had 150% as many riders. 0.5% of Tuesday's riders won a prize. How many riders won?
@viz percentbar percent=150 top="0%,100%,150%" bottom="0,400,[[600]]" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="riders"
(work 3)
=> 150% of 400 = 400 + 200 = 600 riders on Tuesday. (RP3)
=> 1% of 600 = 6. 0.5% is half of that: 3 riders won.

### Juice concentrate comes 3 cans for $4.50 or 5 cans for $7.00. Which is the better buy? Explain.
@viz dnl top="$0,[[$1.50]],$3.00,$4.50" bottom="0,1,2,3" toplabel="3-pack" bottomlabel="cans"
(lines 3)
=> Unit rates: $4.50 ÷ 3 = $1.50 per can. $7.00 ÷ 5 = $1.40 per can. (RP4)
=> The 5-can pack costs less for each can. It is the better buy.

### A Flames ticket cost $90. The price went up to $108.
a) By what percent did it go up?
b) Then the price drops 20%. Is it back to $90?
(work 3)
=> a) It went up $18. 10% of $90 = $9, and $18 = 2 × $9. So it went up 20%. (RP4)
=> b) No. 20% of $108 = $21.60. $108 − $21.60 = $86.40. The 20% is of a bigger number.

### Tess's bannock for 4 people uses {3/4} cup of milk.
a) How much milk for 6 people?
b) She pours 1.5 L of juice into 250 mL cups. How many cups does she fill?
(work 3)
=> a) The factor is 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. 1.5 × {3/4} = {3/2} × {3/4} = {9/8} = 1{1/8} cups. (RP4)
=> b) 1.5 L = 1500 mL. 1500 ÷ 250 = 6 cups.
