## Core

### Use the area model. Find {2/3} × {3/5}. Write the answer in simplest form.
@viz area a=2/3 b=3/5 result="2/3 × 3/5 = [[6/15]]" result2="simplest form: [[2/5]]"
(lines 1)
=> Columns: 3 of 5 shaded. Rows: 2 of 3 shaded. The square has 3 × 5 = 15 parts. (FD1)
=> The overlap is 2 × 3 = 6 parts. {2/3} × {3/5} = {6/15} = {2/5}.

### How many {1/3}s fit in 4?
@viz groups total=4 piece=1/3 numbers=0 count="4 ÷ 1/3 = [[12]]"
(lines 1)
=> Each whole has 3 thirds. 4 wholes have 4 × 3 = 12 thirds. 4 ÷ {1/3} = 12. (FD2)
=> 12 is bigger than 4. Targets the "dividing always makes it smaller" mix-up.

### Noor says 6 ÷ 0.5 = 3. Count the jumps. Then find the other quotients.
@viz numberline min=0 max=6 step=0.5 every=2 jumps="0>0.5>1>1.5>2>2.5>3>3.5>4>4.5>5>5.5>6"

### Evaluate. Show each step.
@viz flow steps="4 + 6 × 2|4 + [[12]]|[[16]]" width=420
a) 4 + 6 × 2 = [[16]]
b) (4 + 6) × 2 = [[20]]
c) 20 ÷ 4 × 5 = [[25]]
d) 3 + 2³ = [[11]]
e) −6 + 3 × (−2) = [[−12]]
f) {1/2} + {1/4} × 2 = [[1]]
g) 0.5 × 4 + 1.5 = [[3.5]]
=> a) × first: 4 + 12 = 16. Not 10 × 2 = 20. Targets the "work left to right, always" mix-up. (FD4)
=> b) Brackets first: 10 × 2 = 20. c) Left to right: 20 ÷ 4 = 5, then 5 × 5 = 25.
=> d) Cube first: 2³ = 8, then 3 + 8 = 11. e) × first: 3 × (−2) = −6, then −6 + (−6) = −12.
=> f) {1/4} × 2 = {2/4} = {1/2}. Then {1/2} + {1/2} = 1. g) 0.5 × 4 = 2. Then 2 + 1.5 = 3.5.

## Challenge

### Ava has 2{1/4} cups of flour. One batch of bannock uses {3/4} cup.
a) How many batches can she make?
b) She makes {2/3} of a batch instead. How much flour does she use?
@viz groups total=9/4 piece=3/4 numbers=0
(work 3)
=> a) 2{1/4} = {9/4}. Same bottoms: {9/4} ÷ {3/4} = 9 ÷ 3 = 3 batches. (FD2)
=> b) {2/3} × {3/4} = {6/12} = {1/2} cup. (FD1)

### There is {2/3} of a pizza left. Four friends share it equally.
a) How much of the whole pizza does each friend get?
b) What fraction of the leftover pizza does each friend get?
@viz area a=1/4 b=2/3 alabel="1/4" blabel="2/3 left" note1="2/3 left: blue columns" note2="4 shares: rows" result="each gets [[1/6]]"
(work 2)
=> a) {2/3} ÷ 4 = {1/4} × {2/3} = {2/12} = {1/6} of the pizza. (FD2)
=> b) Each friend gets {1/4} of the leftover. {1/4} of {2/3} is {1/6} of the whole.

### Kai has a board 3 m long. He cuts shelves that are {5/8} m long.
a) How many whole shelves can he cut?
b) How much board is left over?
@viz groups total=3 piece=5/8 numbers=0
(work 3)
=> a) 3 = {24/8}. {24/8} ÷ {5/8} = 24 ÷ 5 = 4{4/5}. He can cut 4 whole shelves. (FD2)
=> b) 4 shelves use 4 × {5/8} = {20/8} m. Left over: {24/8} − {20/8} = {4/8} = {1/2} m. (FD1)

### Brackets change the answer.
a) Put brackets in 6 + 4 × 3 − 1 to make it equal 29.
b) Find 2 × 5² and (2 × 5)². Explain why they are different.
(work 3)
=> a) (6 + 4) × 3 − 1 = 10 × 3 − 1 = 30 − 1 = 29. (FD4)
=> b) 2 × 5² = 2 × 25 = 50. (2 × 5)² = 10² = 100. Without brackets, only the 5 is squared.

### Omar evaluated 12 − 4 ÷ 2 × 3 and got 12. Find his mistake. Then evaluate {3/4} ÷ {1/2} − {1/2} × {1/2}.
@viz flow steps="12 − 4 ÷ 2 × 3|12 − [[2]] × 3|12 − [[6]]|[[6]]"
(work 3)
=> Omar went left to right and subtracted first. ÷ and × come before −: 4 ÷ 2 = 2, 2 × 3 = 6, 12 − 6 = 6. Targets the "work left to right, always" mix-up. (FD4)
=> {3/4} ÷ {1/2} = {3/4} × 2 = {6/4}. {1/2} × {1/2} = {1/4}. {6/4} − {1/4} = {5/4} = 1{1/4}. (FD2, FD4)

### A square rug is 1.5 m on each side. Carpet costs $8.40 per square metre.
a) Find the area of the rug.
b) Find the cost. You may use a calculator.
@viz partial cols="1:1;0.5:0.5" rows="1:1;0.5:0.5" cells="[[1]];[[0.5]];[[0.5]];[[0.25]]" total="1.5 × 1.5 = [[2.25]] m²"
(work 3)
=> a) 1.5² = 1.5 × 1.5 = 1 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.25 = 2.25 m². Check: 15 × 15 = 225, two places. (FD3)
=> b) 2.25 × 8.40 = 18.90. The rug costs $18.90. Estimate: 2 × $8 = $16, a bit more. ✓
