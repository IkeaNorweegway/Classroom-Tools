## core | PN1 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 1
Use the number line.
a) Find |−6|, |6| and |−2.5|.
b) Name two numbers that are 4 steps from 0.
@viz numberline min=-7 max=7 points="-6:−6;-2.5:−2.5;6:6"
(lines 2)
=> a) |−6| = 6, |6| = 6, |−2.5| = 2.5. Each one is a distance from 0.
=> b) 4 and −4.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"|−5| = −5" or "absolute value makes it negative." Have the student count the steps from 0. A distance is never negative.
:::

## core | IN1 — Integers, Section 1
At 7 am it is −5 °C in Calgary. By noon it is 2 degrees colder.
Sam writes (−5) + (−2) = 7. "Two negatives make a positive."
Use the number line. Find the right answer.
@viz numberline min=-8 max=8 points="-5:start" ends="colder,warmer"
(lines 2)
=> (−5) + (−2) = −7. Start at −5 and move 2 to the left. It is −7 °C at noon.
=> "Two negatives make a positive" is a rule for × and ÷. It is not for adding.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Two negatives make a positive, so −3 + (−4) = 7." Ask: did the temperature go up or down?
:::

## core | FD1 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 1
A pan has {3/4} of the bannock left. Ava eats {2/3} of what is left.
a) Use the area model. Find {2/3} × {3/4}.
b) Is the answer more or less than {3/4}? Why?
@viz area a=2/3 b=3/4 alabel="2/3 of it" blabel="3/4 pan" result="2/3 × 3/4 = [[6/12]] = [[1/2]]"
(lines 2)
=> a) 6 of 12 parts overlap. {2/3} × {3/4} = {6/12} = {1/2} of the pan.
=> b) Less. Ava eats only part of the {3/4}. Part of something is less than all of it.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Multiplying always makes a number bigger." Point to the overlap: it sits inside the {3/4} strip.
:::

## core | PN3 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 3
Kai writes {1/4} = 1.4. The grid shows {1/4}.

b) Write {1/4} as a decimal.
c) Is Kai right? Explain.
@viz hundred shaded=25 labels="1/4 of the grid"
(lines 2)

## core | IN2 — Integers, Section 2
At night it is −3 °C. At noon it is 4 °C.
Ben says subtracting always gives a smaller number.
a) Use the chips. Find 4 − (−3).
b) Is Ben right? Explain.
@viz chips pos=4 add=3 xneg=3 result="4 − (−3) = [[7]]"
(lines 2)
=> a) Add 3 zero pairs. Take away the 3 negative chips. 7 positive chips are left: 4 − (−3) = 7. Noon is 7 °C warmer.
=> b) No. 7 is bigger than 4. Taking away a negative makes it bigger: 4 − (−3) = 4 + 3.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Subtracting always makes it smaller." The chip mat shows the answer growing.
:::

## core | FD2 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 2
Zoe has 4 cups of oats. One batch of muffins uses {2/3} cup.
Zoe thinks the answer must be less than 4.
a) How many batches can she make? Use the picture.
b) Why is the answer more than 4?
@viz groups total=4 piece=2/3 numbers=0 count="4 ÷ 2/3 = [[6]]"

## core | RP3 — Ratios & Percents, Section 3
200 people ride the Ferris wheel on Monday.
a) Find 1% of 200.
b) Find 0.5% of 200.
c) Tuesday has 150% of Monday's riders. How many is that?
@viz percentbar percent=150 top="0%,50%,100%,150%" bottom="0,[[100]],200,[[300]]" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="riders"
(lines 3)
=> a) 1% of 200 = 200 ÷ 100 = 2.
=> b) 0.5% is half of 1%. Half of 2 is 1 rider.
=> c) 100% is 200 and 50% is 100. So 150% = 200 + 100 = 300 riders.

## core | PN2 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 2
Mark each number on the number line.
a) −1.5
b) −{3/4}, {3/−4} and −({3/4})
c) Lily says −1.5 is between −1 and 0. Is she right?
@viz numberline min=-2 max=1 step=0.25 every=1 keypoints="-1.5:−1.5;-0.75:−3/4"
(lines 3)
=> a) −1.5 is half way from −1 to −2.
=> b) All three sit at the same point, −0.75. One negative sign anywhere gives the same number.
=> c) No. −1.5 is left of −1. It is between −2 and −1.
::: teacher

## core | SC3 — Squares & Cubes, Section 3
Omar says 3³ = 9.
a) How many small cubes make the big cube?
b) Find ∛27 and ∛64.
c) What did Omar do wrong?
@viz cube n=3 label="3 × 3 × 3 = [[27]]"
(lines 3)
=> a) 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 small cubes.
=> b) ∛27 = 3, because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27. ∛64 = 4, because 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.
=> c) He did 3 × 3. That is one layer. 3³ means three 3s multiplied: three layers of 9.
::: teacher

"2³ = 6" or "∛27 = 9." Have the student build or count the layers. Watch for ∛27 = 9 in part b.

## core | FD3 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 3
Leo says 0.3 × 0.4 = 1.2. Use the hundred grid.
a) How many small squares overlap?
b) Find 0.3 × 0.4.
c) How do you know Leo is wrong?
@viz area a=3/10 b=4/10 alabel="0.3" blabel="0.4" note1="columns: 4 tenths" note2="rows: 3 tenths" result="0.3 × 0.4 = [[0.12]]" result2="overlap: [[12]] of 100 squares"
(lines 3)
=> a) 12 of 100 small squares.
=> b) 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12. (3 × 4 = 12, and there are two decimal places in all.)
=> c) Both numbers are less than 1. So the answer must be less than each one. 1.2 is more than 1.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"0.3 × 0.2 = 0.6." The overlap is hundredths, not tenths. Count the decimal places in both factors.
:::

Ava says (−2) × 5 = 10. "5 is the bigger number, and it is positive."
a) Use the counters. Find (−2) × 5.
b) Find (−2) × (−2) × (−2). Count the negatives first.

## ext | FD1 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 1
Tess finds {2/5} × {3/4}. She writes {8/20} × {15/20} = {120/20} = 6.
a) Is 6 a sensible answer? Explain.
b) Find the right answer.
c) Do you need a common denominator to multiply?
@viz area a=2/5 b=3/4 result="2/5 × 3/4 = [[6/20]]"
(lines 4)
=> a) No. Both fractions are less than 1. So the answer must be less than 1.
=> b) Multiply tops: 2 × 3 = 6. Multiply bottoms: 5 × 4 = 20. {6/20} = {3/10}.
=> c) No. Common denominators are for + and −. To multiply, multiply tops and multiply bottoms.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"You need a common denominator to multiply fractions." Tess also kept the denominator, as in adding. The area model shows 6 of 20 parts.
:::

## ext | PN4 — Positive & Negative Numbers, Section 4
Morning lows: Edmonton −8 °C, Calgary −3 °C, Jasper −12.5 °C, Lethbridge 0.5 °C.
@viz thermo min=-15 max=5 step=5 values="-8,-3,-12.5,0.5" labels="Edmonton;Calgary;Jasper;Lethbridge"
a) Noor says −8 °C is warmer than −3 °C, because 8 > 3. Is she right?
b) Order the four lows from coldest to warmest.
c) Omar says 0.125 > 0.5 because it has more digits. Is he right?
(lines 4)
=> a) No. −8 is further left (lower) than −3. So −8 °C is colder: −8 < −3.
=> b) −12.5, −8, −3, 0.5.
=> c) No. Line up the place values: 0.500 > 0.125. 0.5 is bigger.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
Two targets. "−8 is bigger than −3 because 8 > 3": point to the thermometer. "A longer decimal is bigger": add zeros so both have three places.
:::

## ext | FD4 — Fractions & Decimals, Section 4
Leo says 2 + 3 × 4 = 20.
a) Find the right answer.
b) Find (−6) + 8 ÷ (−2).
c) Find {1/2} × (5 − 1) + 0.5.
@viz flow steps="Brackets first|× and ÷, left to right|+ and −, left to right"
(lines 4)
=> a) 3 × 4 = 12 first. 2 + 12 = 14.
=> b) 8 ÷ (−2) = −4 first. (−6) + (−4) = −10.
=> c) 5 − 1 = 4. {1/2} × 4 = 2. 2 + 0.5 = 2.5.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Work left to right, always" (2 + 3 × 4 = 20). Have the student circle the × or ÷ before doing anything.
:::

## ext | SC4 — Squares & Cubes, Section 4
Kai says a square with 4 cm sides has area = perimeter. "Both are 16."
a) Is he right? Use the units.
b) A square garden has an area of 64 m². How much fence goes around it?
@viz sqside n=8 side="?" fence=1 fill=plant labels="Area = 64 m²;Fence = ?" cell=14
(lines 4)
=> a) No. The numbers match, but the units do not. Perimeter is 16 cm (a length). Area is 16 cm² (squares).
=> b) Side = √64 = 8 m. Fence = 4 × 8 = 32 m.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Area and perimeter are the same thing." Perimeter is the fence (m). Area is the grass inside (m²).
:::

## ext | RP1 — Ratios & Percents, Section 1
Lily's pancakes use 2 cups of flour for 3 eggs. For a bigger batch she writes 4:5.
a) Is 4:5 equal to 2:3? Explain.
b) Fill in the double number line.
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6" bottom="0,3,[[6]],[[9]]" toplabel="flour (cups)" bottomlabel="eggs"
(lines 3)
=> a) No. She added 2 to both. Equal ratios multiply both terms: 2:3 = 4:6 (× 2).
=> b) 4 cups: 6 eggs. 6 cups: 9 eggs.

## stretch | RP4 + FD3 — Ratios & Percents and Decimals
A jacket at West Edmonton Mall costs $80. It is 25% off. Then add 5% GST.
a) Fill in the receipt.
b) Sam says this is the same as 20% off. Check.
@viz receipt items="Jacket:$80.00;25% off:[[−$20.00]];Sale price:[[$60.00]];GST (5%):[[$3.00]];Total:[[$63.00]]"
(lines 3)
=> a) 25% of 80 = 20, so the sale price is $60.00. 5% of 60 = 3, so GST is $3.00. Total $63.00.
=> b) No. 20% off $80 is $64.00. The 5% GST is taken of $60, not of $80.

## stretch | IN4 + FD4 — Integers and Order of Operations
The lows in Red Deer for 4 days were −9 °C, −5 °C, 2 °C and −4 °C.
a) Write one expression for the mean. Use brackets.
b) Find the mean low. Does it make sense?
@viz numberline min=-10 max=4 points="-9;-5;2;-4"
(lines 4)
=> a) (−9 + (−5) + 2 + (−4)) ÷ 4.
=> b) The sum is −16. (−16) ÷ 4 = −4 °C. It sits between the lowest (−9) and the highest (2), so it makes sense.
=> Without brackets, only the −4 would be divided by 4.
