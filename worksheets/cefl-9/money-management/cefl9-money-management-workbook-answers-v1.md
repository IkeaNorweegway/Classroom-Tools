::: teacher
**Listen for:** "making the minimum payment means I'm keeping up" (Moment 2, Question 3, the main misconception target); "avoiding credit entirely gives a great score"; "a payday loan is just a quick loan." Keep the tone non-judgmental: some students' families use payday loans because they have few options.
:::

## Moment 1 — Debt and credit

**1.** (a) **buy now, pay later** or phone financing (b) **student loan** (c) **personal (car) loan**

**2.** **Helps:** for example, paying for school or a car needed for work, building a credit history, handling an emergency. **Hurts:** for example, high interest on unpaid balances, debt that keeps growing, stress, a damaged credit score.

**3.** Most cards have a grace period: interest is charged only on balances carried past the due date. Paying in full means nothing is carried over, so no interest builds.

::: answer
**E1.** For example: What's the total cost? What happens if I miss a payment (fees, interest)? Can I really afford every payment? Do I need it now?

**E2.** Lines of credit usually charge much lower interest (for example 9.5% vs. about 21%).
:::

## Moment 2 — The minimum-payment trap

**1.** 2,400 × 0.2099 ÷ 12 = **$41.98**.

**2.** $1,614 − $109 = **about $1,505** saved ($1,613.90 − $108.87 = $1,505.03).

::: misconception
**3.** Minimum payments keep the account in good standing, but most of each payment goes to interest. On $1,000 at 20.99%, paying only the minimum takes about **15 years** and costs **$1,614** in interest, more than the amount borrowed. That isn't keeping up; it's barely moving.
:::

::: answer
**E1.** It's the same compounding: interest is charged on the balance, including past interest. With savings it grows your money; with debt it grows what you owe.

**E2.** Pay the full statement balance by the due date every month (and only buy what you can afford to pay off).
:::

## Moment 3 — Your credit score

**1.** 300 ÷ 2,000 = 0.15, so **15%**.

**2.** Any two: pay every bill on time; keep balances low compared with limits; keep old accounts open; only apply for credit you need; check your credit report for errors.

**3.** **Not quite.** With no credit history, lenders have nothing to judge, so you may not have a score at all, or a weak one. A good score comes from using credit **responsibly** over time (for example, one card used lightly and paid in full each month).

::: answer
**E1.** Many new applications in a short time can lower your score and look risky to lenders.

**E2.** A landlord may check credit; a low score could mean being turned down or asked for a bigger deposit.
:::

## Moment 4 — Getting out of debt, and avoiding traps

**1.** 170 ÷ 3,000 ≈ 0.057, so about **5.7%**.

**2.** **The line of credit.** It costs about **$1.09** for two weeks instead of **$42**. The payday loan costs about 38 times more. (Other options: savings, an emergency fund, asking the mechanic about a payment plan.)

**3.** Either method with both factors. **Avalanche:** cheapest ($509 vs. $566). **Snowball:** paying off the phone first gives a quick win that can keep you going.

::: answer
**E1.** People who are short on money may have few other options. With the high fee, they often can't repay on time and take a new loan to pay the old one, so the debt keeps growing.

**E2.** For example: "I'll only charge what I can pay off in full each month." "I'll set up automatic payments so I'm never late."
:::
