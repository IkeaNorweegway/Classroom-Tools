**Listen for:** "student loans are free money" or "the same as grants"; "if I don't get into my first choice, I've failed" (Moment 4, Question 3, the main misconception target); "tuition is the whole cost"; "ROI is the only thing that matters."
:::

## Moment 1 — Student loans

**1.** A **loan** must be paid back (sometimes with interest). A **grant** does not have to be paid back.

**2.** 182.91 × 120 = **$21,949.20** (matches $21,949.29 apart from rounding of the monthly payment).

**3.** Look for a reason on each side. **Borrow less:** less to repay and less interest; less stress after graduating. **Work less, borrow more:** more time to study and better grades; the federal part is interest-free. Accept a reasoned conclusion.

::: answer
**E1.** **Less** interest. The balance shrinks faster, so there is less time for interest to build. (Payments are bigger each month.)

**E2.** A period after school ends when you don't have to make payments yet. It gives graduates time to find a job.
:::

## Moment 2 — Return on investment

**1.** Tuition 2 × $6,000 = $12,000. Lost earnings 2 × $19,200 = $38,400. True cost **$50,400**.

**2.** Extra pay: $47,812 − $31,200 = $16,612 a year. $50,400 ÷ $16,612 ≈ **3 years**.

**3.** Any two: not graduating; not finding a job in the field; lower pay where you live or when you finish; taking longer than planned; more borrowing and interest; the labour market changing.

::: answer
**E1.** Apprentices earn wages while they train, so they lose little income, and tuition is low. The true cost is small compared with the pay.

**E2.** No. Interests, values, job satisfaction, location, and the kind of work also matter. A pathway with a slower payback can still be the right choice.
:::

## Moment 3 — Learning while you earn

**1.** **Co-op programs, internships, apprenticeships.**

**2.** Any two: real work experience; references; networking; trying out a career before graduating; a better chance of being hired after.

**3.** Each province (and country) can set its own rules for some professions. Her Alberta credential may need to be checked, or extra steps completed, before she can work there.

::: answer
**E1.** For example: they may need to upgrade high school courses, study part-time while working, use mature-student admission routes, or get credit for work experience.

**E2.** For example: the program can take longer to finish, work terms may be in another city, or placements aren't always guaranteed.
:::

## Moment 4 — Getting in, and having a Plan B

**1.** Any two: portfolio, interview, references, extracurricular or volunteer experience, a personal statement.

**2.** A related contingency still moves you toward your goal (same field, shared skills), and sometimes lets you bridge to the first choice later.

::: misconception
**3.** A plan with a contingency doesn't fail. Maya's practical nursing diploma keeps her in health care, costs less, is in demand, and can bridge to an RN degree later. The goal (working in health care) is still reached.
:::

::: answer
**E1.** For example: volunteer in the field, take related CTS courses, job shadow, keep marks up in required courses, build a portfolio.

**E2.** One clear sentence linking the pathway to interests, cost or the labour market.
:::
