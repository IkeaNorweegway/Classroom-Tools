## Moment 1 — The T4 and the tax return

**1.** **Box 14** shows total employment income. **Box 22** shows income tax already deducted.

**2.** Federal: 0.14 × 15,000 = $2,100 − $2,303.28 → below zero, so **$0**. Alberta: 0.08 × 15,000 = $1,200 − $1,821.52 → **$0**. Tax owed **$0**. Riley already paid $300, so Riley gets a **$300 refund**.

::: misconception
**3.** A refund is the taxpayer's **own money** coming back. During the year, more tax was taken off paycheques than was really owed. The tax return works out the real amount, and the extra is returned.
:::

::: answer
**E1.** Paycheque deductions are estimates. The return adds up all income for the whole year, applies credits and deductions, and works out the exact tax.

**E2.** 0.0163 × 24,000 = **$391.20**. Yes, it matches.
:::

## Moment 2 — Credits and deductions

**1.** (a) **credit** (b) **deduction** (c) **credit**

**2.** Morgan's income is above both tax-free amounts, so a $500 deduction saves 14% + 8% = 22%: 0.22 × 500 = **$110** ($70 federal, $40 Alberta). Riley's tax was already $0, so lowering taxable income can't save anything this year. (Riley could still contribute and claim the deduction in a later year.)

**3.** Knowing what lowers your tax helps you plan: whether to save in an RRSP, which receipts to keep, and how much tax to expect, so your budget is accurate.

::: answer
**E1.** **$0.** Credits can lower tax to zero but not below. (Unused tuition credits can be carried forward to future years.)

**E2.** Receipts prove the credit or deduction if the CRA asks, and are needed to claim it.
:::

## Moment 3 — RRSP or TFSA?

**1.** (a) **TFSA** (b) **RRSP** (c) **TFSA**

**2.** 0.18 × 50,000 = **$9,000**.

**3.** **RRSP.** At $110,000, Dana is in a higher bracket (20.5% federal + 10% Alberta on the top dollars), so each RRSP dollar saves about 30% in tax now. In retirement Dana's income, and tax rate, will probably be lower.

::: answer
**E1.** TFSA money can be taken out any time with no tax, and the room comes back the next year. RRSP withdrawals are taxed and are meant for retirement.

**E2.** Yes. For example: an RRSP for retirement (tax break now) and a TFSA for shorter goals or extra savings.
:::

## Moment 4 — Gigs, tips and self-employment

**1.** **All four** must be reported: cash tips, gig delivery earnings, babysitting money and freelance design work are all income.

**2.** 8,000 − 3,500 = 4,500. 0.119 × 4,500 = **$535.50**.

**3.** These benefits are calculated from your tax return. If you don't file, the CRA can't pay them, even if you qualify. People with low incomes often get the most.

::: answer
**E1.** To report income correctly, claim business expenses, and prove amounts if the CRA asks.

**E2.** They could owe the unpaid tax plus interest and penalties after a CRA review.
:::
