# Grade 7 Science — Plant Systems: Two-Track Access Example
*Demonstration activity — not a full unit workbook. Shows how `research/significant-learning-gaps-research.md` and `research/udl-scaffolding-research.md` apply to one concept cluster: root/shoot systems and vascular tissue (LO 7-2-02).*

---

## Teacher note — read before choosing a track

Both versions below teach from the **same diagram and the same concept**: plants have a water pipe (xylem, moves water up from roots to leaves) and a food pipe (phloem, moves sugar down from leaves to the rest of the plant). What changes is the reading/writing load and — in Track B only — the outcome itself.

- **Track A** is for a student whose achievement is behind but whose cognitive ability is typical (a skill gap or undiagnosed/diagnosed LD). The outcome stays at grade level (LO 7-2-02, full concept). Only the access route changes.
- **Track B** is for a student with a documented intellectual/significant cognitive disability and an IPP specifying modified/alternate outcomes. The outcome itself is simplified. **Do not use Track B without an IPP team decision** — this file shows what a modified version could look like, it does not replace that process. See `research/significant-learning-gaps-research.md`, Part 6.

Both use the same plant picture so a student on either track can sit in the same class, working on the same topic, without a visibly "different" worksheet.

**Shared diagram (used in both tracks):**
`[Diagram: A simple flowering plant, side view. Roots at the bottom in soil. A single stem going up. Two to three leaves near the top. Two dotted arrow-paths run inside the stem: one going upward from roots to leaves, one going downward from leaves to roots. Plain line drawing, high contrast, large enough to write or point on.]`

## Track A — Grade-level concept, low reading/writing load

**Outcome:** LO 7-2-02, unchanged. **What's adjusted:** response format (circle/match/point/draw an arrow instead of paragraphs), sentence length, and an oral-answer option. **What's not adjusted:** the concept, including the Apply-level reasoning question at the end.

---

**1 — Find the parts**

Look at the plant picture. Circle the root. Put a box around the stem. Underline a leaf.

`[Diagram: same plant picture as above, unlabeled, ready for student to mark]`

---

**2 — Match the job**

Draw a line from each plant part to its job. Pictures next to each word.

| Plant part | | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 🌱 Roots | | 🌤️ Makes food from sunlight |
| 🌿 Stem | | 💧 Takes in water and minerals |
| 🍃 Leaves | | 🏗️ Holds the plant up, moves stuff between roots and leaves |

> Hint: Roots are underground. What's down there for a plant to take in?

---

**3 — Label the two pipes**

The plant has two pipes inside its stem. Write the correct word on each pipe using the word bank.

**Word bank:** xylem · phloem

`[Diagram: same plant with stem enlarged, showing two parallel dotted-line pipes — one arrow pointing up, one arrow pointing down — each with a blank label line right beside it]`

---

**4 — Which way?**

Circle the correct answer for each pipe.

**Xylem (water pipe):** moves water from the **roots → leaves** / **leaves → roots**

**Phloem (food pipe):** moves food from the **roots → leaves** / **leaves → roots**

---

**5 — Finish the sentence**

Circle the words that finish the sentence correctly, OR say your answer out loud to your teacher or a partner instead of writing.

"The roots take in **water and minerals / food that's already made**. The leaves make the plant's food using **sunlight / soil**."

---

**6 ★★ — Think like a scientist (Apply)**

A farmer accidentally cuts a ring all the way around a tree's bark. This cuts the phloem but leaves the xylem underneath undamaged.

Circle YES or NO, then point to the part of the diagram that shows why:

- Will the leaves still get water? **YES / NO**
- Will the roots still get food from the leaves? **YES / NO**

> Hint: Which pipe did the cut damage — the water pipe or the food pipe?

*(This question stays at full Apply-level demand — same reasoning a student answering in full paragraphs would need. Only the response format changed.)*

## Track B — Modified outcome, systematic instruction

**Sample IPP-aligned outcome (confirm with school team before use):** *Student will sort pictures of plant parts by their job (takes in water / holds plant up / makes food) with increasing independence.* This is deliberately narrower than LO 7-2-02 — it does not require naming xylem/phloem or explaining direction of flow. Vocabulary exposure to "water pipe" and "food pipe" is included as **exposure only, not assessed** — this keeps the student connected to what the class is studying (inclusion principle) without requiring the abstract vocabulary as a demonstrated outcome.

**Materials:** 3 picture cards (root, stem, leaf — same style/photos as Track A's diagram, not younger-grade clip art) + 3 category mats with an icon and 1–2 word label: **💧 Takes In Water** / **🏗️ Holds It Up** / **☀️ Makes Food**.

---

**Task analysis (teacher script — systematic prompting)**

| Step | What the student does | Prompt level (use least first, add more only if needed) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Look at the root picture | Independent → point to picture → "Look here" + point |
| 2 | Decide which mat it belongs on | Independent → "Which one takes in water?" → point to correct mat |
| 3 | Place the card on the mat | Independent → gesture toward mat → guide hand |
| 4 | Repeat for stem, then leaf | Same sequence, prompts fade as steps repeat |

Give immediate feedback after every placement — confirm correct placements right away ("Yes — roots take in water!"), and if incorrect, guide to the correct mat immediately rather than letting the error sit. Track which prompt level was needed each time; the goal across sessions is fewer prompts, not just correct final placement.

---

**Bonus exposure (not assessed, whole group together)**

Teacher points to the shared plant diagram and says: *"Scientists have a name for the water pipe — they call it xylem. And a name for the food pipe — phloem."* Student is not asked to recall or write these words. This is the same moment the rest of the class has with the vocabulary, just without an assessment demand attached.

## Why both stay on the same page

Per `_meta-context.md` → *Students significantly below grade level*: defaulting straight to "make it easier" without first knowing which track applies is the most common design error. Track A shows that "low reading and writing" does not have to mean "lower concept" — the ring-barking question (Q6) is genuine Apply-level science reasoning delivered through circling and pointing. Track B shows that a modified outcome can still keep the student in the same topic, same diagram, same classroom moment as everyone else — age-appropriate materials, not a different unit.
