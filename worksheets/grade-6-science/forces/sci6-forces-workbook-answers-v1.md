# Forces — Workbook Answer Key
Grade 6 Science · Teacher Copy — Not for Students
Model answers and marking guidance for all 25 core questions and 4 stretch questions.

## Core Questions — Q1 to Q25

**Q1 ★** — Force definition + examples
**Definition:** A force is a push or a pull acting on an object. Accept any wording that captures both ideas.
**Examples:** Any two reasonable forces from the student's morning — pushing a door open, pulling a backpack strap, gravity on their feet while walking, friction while walking, opening a locker, etc.

---

**Q2 ★** — External forces on box · identify + direction
(a) **Applied force** · Direction: forward / in the direction of the push.
(b) **Friction** · Direction: backward / opposite to the direction of motion.
Note: Also accept gravity (downward) and normal force (upward) as additional correct answers.

---

**Q3 ★** — Internal force — rope diagram
**Internal force type: Tension.**
Particles inside the rope are being **pulled apart** — forces from both ends stretch the rope, pulling its particles away from each other in opposite directions.

---

**Q4 ★** — Elastic vs. plastic deformation · clay
**Plastic deformation.** The clay kept its new (flat) shape after the force was removed — it did not return to its original shape. Elastic deformation would have meant the clay returned to its original round form.

---

**Q5 ★** — Newton's Third Law · state + properties
**Completion:** "…the second object exerts an equal and opposite force back on the first object."
**Three properties:** 1) Equal in magnitude (same size). 2) Opposite in direction. 3) Act on different objects. All three must be present.

---

**Q6 ★** — Force diagram · label + analyze
**Labels:** Up arrow = Normal force. Down arrow = Gravity / Weight. Right arrow (longer) = Applied force. Left arrow (shorter) = Friction.
(a) Vertical forces are **balanced** — normal force equals gravity, net vertical force = 0.
(b) Applied force is greater than friction → net force is to the **right** → box accelerates right.

---

**Q7 ★★** — Friction misconception · error analysis
(a) **Error 1:** Objects do not contain or "run out of" force — force is an interaction between two objects, applied from the outside, not stored inside. **Error 2:** The puck slowed because friction (an external force from the ice) acted on it — not because of something inside the puck decreasing.
(b) **Correct:** The hockey puck slowed because friction — a force from the ice surface — acted in the direction opposite to the puck's motion. Friction has a specific direction (opposite to motion) and a measurable size in Newtons. It is an external force from the ice.

---

**Q8 ★** — Internal forces · three scenarios
(a) Suspension bridge cable: **Tension** (particles pulled apart by the weight of the deck pulling down).
(b) Concrete column: **Compression** (floor weight pushes down, ground pushes up — particles squeezed together).
(c) Scissors cutting cardboard: **Shear** (two blades push layers of cardboard in opposite directions past each other).

---

**Q9 ★** — Elastic limit · paper clip
(a) **Elastic** deformation (springs back).
(b) **Plastic** deformation (stays permanently bent).
(c) The first bend kept the force below the paper clip's elastic limit — it returned to its original shape. The sharp bend exceeded the elastic limit — the particles permanently rearranged and the clip stayed in its new shape.

---

**Q10 ★** — Newton's Third Law · fish + water
(a) Reaction force: **Water pushes forward on the fish.**
(b) Object: **The fish.**
(c) Action and reaction forces act on different objects — the action (fish pushing water) acts on the water; the reaction (water pushing fish) acts on the fish. Forces only cancel when they act on the same object.

---

**Q11 ★** — Net force · calculate + predict
(a) Net force = 15 N − 6 N = **9 N · Direction: right**.
(b) Box accelerates **to the right**.
(c) If 15 N reduced to 6 N: net force = 6 − 6 = **0 N**. Box would remain at rest — balanced forces produce no acceleration.

---

**Q12 ★** — Friction direction · car braking
(a) Friction acts **south** (opposite to direction of travel).
(b) Friction always acts opposite to the direction of motion.
(c) Before braking, net force was **zero**. Steady speed = zero acceleration = balanced forces. Engine force forward was balanced by friction and air resistance backward.

---

**Q13 ★** — Internal forces · three diagrams
(a) Forces pushing inward from both sides: **Compression**.
(b) Two layers sliding in opposite directions: **Shear**.
(c) Forces pulling outward from both sides: **Tension**.

---

**Q14 ★★** — Engineering design · helmet vs. bridge cable
(a) **Plastically.**
(b) Plastic deformation absorbs impact energy permanently — the foam crushes and stays crushed, meaning it dissipated the collision force rather than bouncing it back. Elastic deformation would return all energy as a rebound, transmitting the full impact force to the rider's head.
(c) Bridge cables must deform **elastically** — opposite because the cables must return to original length under every load cycle. Permanent stretching (plastic deformation) would change bridge geometry and risk structural failure.

---

**Q15 ★** — Action-reaction pairs · valid or not
(a) **Not valid** — gravity and normal force both act on the book (same object).
(b) **Valid** — you push the wall (acts on wall); wall pushes you (acts on you). Different objects, equal, opposite. ✓
(c) **Not valid** — applied force and friction both act on the box (same object).
(d) **Valid** — Earth pulls book down (acts on book); book pulls Earth up (acts on Earth). Different objects, equal, opposite. ✓

---

**Q16 ★★** — Force diagram error · friction direction
(a) **Error:** Friction must act opposite to the direction of motion. The box is sliding right and slowing — friction must point left.
(b) Corrected diagram: Applied force → right; Friction → left (clearly labeled pointing opposite to motion).
(c) Net force = friction (left) > applied (right) → box slows and stops. Consistent with the scenario.

---

**Q17 ★** — Shear and torsion · four scenarios
(a) Wrench turning bolt: **Torsion**.
(b) River current cracking bank: **Shear**.
(c) Wringing towel: **Torsion**.
(d) Torsion involves twisting/rotating around a central axis; shear involves layers sliding past each other in parallel but opposite directions — no rotation around a central axis.

---

**Q18 ★** — Friction on bike · four-part
(a) **Friction** (from brake pads on wheel rim).
(b) Opposite to direction of motion (uphill / backward).
(c) Helpful: brake pads on rim (slowing); tires gripping road (propulsion). Against rider: chain/bearing friction (reduces pedaling efficiency).
(d) No — without friction, tires would skid on the road (no grip for steering or braking), brake pads would not grip the rim, and the rider could not even push off the ground. Friction is essential for the bike to function safely.

---

**Q19 ★★** — Newton's Third Law · truck/car misconception
(a) **No.**
(b) Newton's Third Law: the force the truck exerts on the car is exactly **equal in magnitude** and opposite in direction to the force the car exerts on the truck. Mass does not change this.
(c) The forces are equal, but the masses are very different. The same force causes much greater acceleration in the small-mass car than in the large-mass truck. Greater acceleration = greater speed change = greater structural damage to the lighter vehicle.

---

**Q20 ★** — Deformation sequence · spring diagram
"After" diagram: spring returns to **original length** (same as BEFORE).
(a) **Elastic** deformation.
(b) The spring returned to its original shape after the force was removed.
(c) After being pushed past the elastic limit: the spring would stay permanently compressed/shorter — it would not fully return to its original length.

---

**Q21 ★★** — External → internal → deformation chain · chair
(a) **Applied force** (downward).
(b) **Compression** (particles in the legs are squeezed together).
(c) **Plastic** deformation (leg cracked and stayed bent — permanent change).
(d) Full chain: the applied force (pushing down on the seat) created a compression internal force in the chair legs as particles were forced toward each other. When the force exceeded the elastic limit of the wood, the particles rearranged permanently — plastic deformation — and the leg cracked and remained bent.

---

**Q22 ★** — Draw force diagram · book on table
Diagram: two equal-length arrows — 12 N pointing **up** (normal force) and 12 N pointing **down** (gravity). Arrows must be the same length.
(a) Net force = **zero**. The two 12 N forces are equal and opposite — they balance exactly.
(b) **NOT an action-reaction pair.** Both forces act on the same object (the book). The actual Newton's Third Law pairs are: Earth pulls book down / book pulls Earth up; AND table pushes book up / book pushes table down.

---

**Q23 ★★** — Newton's Third Law error analysis · shopping cart
(a) The student identified two forces both acting on the same object (the cart). An action-reaction pair must act on different objects.
(b) Correct reaction to "my push on the cart": **the cart pushes me backward** (equal, opposite, acts on me — a different object).
(c) Any valid pair: e.g., my feet push the floor backward (on floor) / floor pushes me forward (on me).

---

**Q24 ★** — Elastic force + deformation · archery
(a) Force type: **Elastic (spring) force**.
(b) Direction: **forward** (the string restores toward its original position, pushing the arrow).
(c) Internal force in bowstring: **Tension** (string is being pulled/stretched from both ends).
(d) **Plastic** deformation (permanently changed — does not return to original shape).

---

**Q25 ★★** — Full force analysis · rock climber
(a) External forces: **Gravity** (downward) and **Tension from rope** (upward).
(b) Internal force in rope: **Tension**.
(c) Net force = **zero**. The climber is motionless = not accelerating. Balanced forces: tension up = gravity down.
(d) Valid pair: **Climber pulls down on rope** (acts on rope) / **rope pulls climber up** (acts on climber). Equal magnitude, opposite direction, different objects. ✓

## Stretch Questions — S1 to S4

*Multi-concept synthesis. Use as discussion guides or for extended assessment marking.*

**S1 ★★** — Force diagram + N3L · skateboarder
(a) **Force diagram must include:** gravity (down), normal force (up from ground), friction from ground (forward — reaction to foot pushing back). All labeled, arrows proportional.
(b) **Action-reaction pair:** Foot pushes ground backward (on ground) / Ground pushes foot (skater) forward (on skater). Equal, opposite, different objects.
(c) Net horizontal force is forward → forward acceleration. If net force were zero, skater would move at constant speed, not accelerate.

---

**S2 ★★** — External → internal → deformation · foam pad
(a) External force: **Applied force** (from the concrete block pressing down).
(b) Internal force: **Compression** (particles squeezed together).
(c) Deformation type: **Plastic** (stays crushed — permanent).
(d) Yes — same type. Helmet foam SHOULD deform plastically in a crash: plastic deformation absorbs the impact energy by permanently crushing, rather than bouncing the force back elastically toward the rider's head. A used/crashed helmet must be replaced because the foam has already plastically deformed.

---

**S3 ★★** — Collision · N3L + deformation + why damage differs
(a) Forces are **equal** during the collision. Newton's Third Law: the force the heavier car exerts on the lighter is exactly equal (and opposite) to the force the lighter car exerts on the heavier.
(b) **Plastic** deformation (permanently crushed).
(c) Plastic deformation dissipates impact energy over time and distance — "crumple zones." If the frame were elastic, it would rebound and transmit the full collision force instantly to the passenger compartment.
(d) **Correction:** By Newton's Third Law, the forces are exactly equal — the heavier car did NOT exert more force. The lighter car is more damaged because the same force produces much greater acceleration in a smaller mass (a = F/m).

---

**S4 ★★** — Shock absorber design · all five concepts
(a) **Elastically** — must return to original length after each bump to absorb the next one. Plastic deformation would leave it permanently compressed and non-functional.
(b) Internal force: **Compression**.
(c) External force: **Applied force / impact from road** (transmitted through the wheel).
(d) Small bump: force below elastic limit → elastic deformation → spring fully returns. Huge pothole: force exceeded elastic limit → partial plastic deformation → spring permanently shortened by 5 cm.
(e) **Action-reaction pair:** Damaged spring pushes up on bike frame (on frame) / bike frame pushes down on spring (on spring). Equal magnitude, opposite direction, different objects.
