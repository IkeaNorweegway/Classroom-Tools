## 2 core | Section 4 — Tariffs

A pair of boots made in the U.S. costs $40. Canada adds a 25% tariff. The same boots made in Quebec cost $45.

a) What is the tariff in dollars?
b) What do the American boots cost in Canada now?
c) Which pair will most shoppers buy? Who benefits?

(lines 3)

=> a) $10 · b) $50 · c) The Quebec boots ($45). The Quebec factory and its workers benefit; shoppers pay $45 instead of $40.

(lines 3)

=> Prairie Town $12,000; Forest Town $10,500. Prairie Town's GDP is larger. No: GDP measures what is produced in a year, not savings.

## 4 core | Section 2 — The National Policy

Name the three parts of the National Policy, and explain how one part helped another.

(lines 3)

=> Tariffs, the railway, settlement of the West. Example: the railway carried settlers west; settlers grew wheat and bought goods from tariff-protected eastern factories.

## 5 ext | Sections 1 and 3 — Calgary grows

Calgary was a small fort in 1880. By 1911 it had over 40,000 people. Use what you know about the CPR and the Prairie economy to explain why.

(lines 3)

=> The CPR arrived in 1883, so settlers, goods and businesses could reach Calgary; ranching and farming nearby needed a town to ship from and buy supplies in, so the town grew.

## 6 core | Section 3 — The CPR

List two goods that travelled east on the CPR and two that travelled west, and explain why the railway mattered to both.

(lines 3)

=> East: wheat, cattle (also timber, coal). West: tools, machinery, clothing, settlers. Both sides needed the railway to reach buyers.

## 7 core | Section 6 — Banks

Name two jobs a bank does, and one job of the Bank of Canada.

(lines 2)

=> Bank: keep savings safe, make loans, help people invest. Bank of Canada: issues banknotes, keeps the money system and prices stable.

## 8 ext | Section 1 — Why regions differ

A student says, "Every province should build car factories, because factories make the most money." Use what you know about natural resources and regional economies to respond.

(lines 4)

=> Regions build economies from their own resources: wheat and oil on the Prairies, timber and fish in BC, fishing in the Atlantic. Factories grew in Ontario and Quebec because of markets, water power and tariffs. Copying one industry everywhere ignores each region's strengths.

## 9 ext | Section 4 — Tariffs or free trade?

| | Price of a U.S. plough in Canada | Price of an Ontario plough |
|---|---|---|
| With a 30% tariff | $130 | $120 |
| With free trade | $100 | $120 |

You are a Prairie farmer in 1911. Laurier is promising freer trade with the U.S. Would you vote for it? Use the table and explain who might vote the other way.

(lines 4)

=> A farmer would likely vote for freer trade: the plough would cost $100, not $120. Ontario factory owners and workers might vote against it, because cheaper U.S. ploughs could take their customers and jobs.

## 10 ext | Sections 2 and 3 — How the plan fit together

Explain why the National Policy is described as one plan instead of three separate policies. What would happen to the plan if the railway had never been finished?

(lines 4)

=> Each part depended on the others: tariffs protected factories, the railway carried goods and settlers, settlers bought the goods and grew wheat. Without the railway, settlers couldn't reach the West and goods couldn't move, so tariffs and settlement wouldn't connect.

## 11 ext | Section 2 — Short and long term

Choose one short-term and one long-term consequence of the National Policy. Was the National Policy good for Canada overall? Justify your judgement.

(lines 4)

=> Short term: factory jobs in Central Canada / higher prices for farmers. Long term: a country linked east to west / lasting Western anger. Accept either judgement with evidence on both sides.

## 12 ext | Section 3 — The railway and the economy

"Without the CPR there would be no Prairie wheat economy." Do you agree? Give two pieces of evidence.

(lines 4)

=> Agree: wheat needed a cheap way to reach ports and world buyers, and settlers needed a way to reach the land; the CPR provided both. Towns like Calgary and Regina grew along it.

## 13 ext | Section 5 — Equalization

Alberta has not received equalization payments for decades. Some Albertans say the system is unfair; others say it keeps Canada together. Explain both points of view.

(lines 4)

=> Unfair: Albertans pay federal taxes that help fund payments to other provinces while Alberta gets none. Keeps Canada together: every Canadian can get similar schools and hospitals, wherever they live, so poorer regions aren't left behind.

## 14 ext | Section 6 — GDP and banking

Explain, with an example, how a good year for Alberta's farms and oil companies could help banks, and how banks could then help the economy grow even more.

(lines 4)

=> A good year means more sales, so people and businesses deposit more and need more payments and loans: banks grow. Banks then lend to businesses (e.g. for new equipment or a new well), which produce more, so GDP grows.

## S1 stretch | Sections 2, 4 and 5

The National Policy helped Central Canada more than the West. Equalization tries to reduce differences between regions. Compare the two: what was each trying to do, and who benefited from each?

(lines 5)

=> The National Policy aimed to build Canadian industry and connect the country; it benefited Central Canada's factories most. Equalization aims to give every province similar services; it benefits provinces that raise less money. One built regional differences, the other tries to smooth them.

## S2 stretch | Sections 1, 3 and 6

Trace the path of one bushel of Prairie wheat in 1900, from the field to a buyer in Britain, and name every part of the economy it touched: farming, the railway, banks and trade.

(lines 5)

=> A farmer (perhaps with a bank loan for land or tools) grows the wheat; the CPR carries it east to a port; it is shipped and sold to Britain (an export); the farmer deposits the money in a bank and spends it on goods from eastern factories.

## S3 stretch | All sections

Answer the guiding question in a strong paragraph: **How did the Canadian government help shape the economy after Confederation?** Use at least three government decisions and one trade-off.

(lines 6)

=> Strong answers use expansion west, the National Policy (tariffs, CPR, settlement), and equalization or the Bank of Canada, and explain at least one trade-off (e.g. tariffs helped factories but raised prices for farmers).
