## 1 core | Section 1 — Policing the West

It is 1874. You are an NWMP officer arriving at Fort Macleod. Write two sentences in your journal explaining why you were sent west and what your job is.

(lines 3)

=> E.g. "We were sent to stop American whisky traders and enforce Canada's laws in the West. Our job is to keep order and show that this land belongs to Canada."

## 2 core | Section 4 — The First World War

Match each battle to what happened there.

a) Second Ypres, 1915
b) Vimy Ridge, 1917
c) Beaumont-Hamel, 1916
d) Passchendaele, 1917

Choices: all four Canadian divisions attack together · Newfoundland Regiment loses most of its men in one morning · first German gas attack on the Western Front; Canadians hold the line · Canadians capture a village in deep mud

(lines 2)

=> a) gas attack, Canadians hold the line · b) all four divisions together · c) Newfoundland Regiment · d) deep mud.

## 3 core | Section 2 — Building a military

Put these steps in order and write the year for each: Royal Canadian Navy created, Militia Act (first), British Army leaves, Statute of Westminster, new Militia Act.

(lines 2)

=> 1855 Militia Act → 1871 British Army leaves → 1904 new Militia Act → 1910 Royal Canadian Navy → 1931 Statute of Westminster.

## 4 core | Section 6 — Sovereignty

In your own words, what is sovereignty? Give one example of Canada gaining more sovereignty between 1867 and 1931.

(lines 3)

=> A country's power to run its own affairs. Examples: the NWMP enforcing Canadian law (1873), the navy (1910), signing Versailles (1919), the Statute of Westminster (1931).

## 5 core | Section 3 — The Boer War

Explain why English Canadians and French Canadians disagreed about the Boer War, and how Laurier tried to solve the problem.

(lines 3)

=> Many English Canadians wanted to support Britain; many French Canadians (Bourassa) said Canada shouldn't fight Britain's wars. Laurier compromised by sending volunteers only.

## 6 core | Section 5 — Reading a memorial

A cenotaph in a small Alberta town lists 31 names under "1914–1918" and 18 names under "1939–1945". It was built in 1922 by the town council. At the top it says "Lest We Forget." Use the four memorial questions to describe what this source tells you.

(lines 4)

=> Who: 31 local people killed in WWI and 18 in WWII. Which wars: both world wars (the WWII names were added later). Who built it: the town council, in 1922, soon after the war. Words: "Lest We Forget" shows the town wanted the sacrifice remembered forever.

## 7 ext | Sections 1 and 2 — Order and defence

How did the NWMP and the British Army leaving in 1871 both push Canada to take responsibility for its own peace and security?

(lines 4)

=> With British soldiers gone, Canada had to defend itself and keep order; the NWMP (1873) gave Canada its own force to enforce laws in the West. Both put security in Canadian hands.

## 8 ext | Section 1 — The NWMP and First Nations

The NWMP stopped the whisky trade that was hurting First Nations communities. It also enforced government policies that First Nations had not chosen. Explain how the NWMP could be seen both as protecting First Nations and as controlling them.

(lines 4)

=> Protecting: it stopped American whisky traders and violence like the Cypress Hills Massacre. Controlling: it enforced federal rules on reserves and helped the government put down the 1885 resistance. Its job was to make Canada's authority real, whether or not First Nations agreed.

## 9 ext | Section 4 — At war automatically

In August 1914 Canada was at war the moment Britain declared war. What does this tell you about Canada's sovereignty in 1914? What could Canada still decide?

(lines 3)

=> Canada did not control its own foreign affairs; Britain did. Canada could still decide how much to contribute (how many soldiers, how much money).

## 10 ext | Section 2 — The navy debate

In 1910 some Canadians said Canada should just give Britain money for its navy. Laurier created a Canadian navy instead. Explain why having its own navy mattered for Canada's sovereignty.

(lines 3)

=> A Canadian navy was under Canadian control: Canada could defend its own coasts and decide how its ships were used, instead of relying on Britain.

## 11 ext | Section 3 — Significance of the Boer War

"The Boer War mattered more for what it showed about Canada than for what Canada did in South Africa." Do you agree? Use evidence.

(lines 4)

=> Agree: only about 7,000 served, but it showed Canada could raise its own soldiers (Paardeberg brought pride) and revealed the English–French divide over the Empire that returned in WWI. Accept disagreement with evidence about Paardeberg.

## 12 ext | Section 4 — Why Vimy?

Many battles were bigger or longer than Vimy Ridge. Explain why Vimy became a symbol for Canada.

(lines 4)

=> For the first time all four Canadian divisions attacked together as one Canadian force; they captured a ridge that British and French armies had failed to take; the success and the cost (about 3,600 killed) made it a symbol of Canada acting as a nation.

## 13 ext | Section 5 — Serving an unequal country

Black, Indigenous and Sikh Canadians served in the First World War even though they faced discrimination at home. Choose one group. Describe their service, and explain why remembering it matters today.

(lines 4)

=> E.g. No. 2 Construction Battalion: about 600 Black men, turned away from regular units, built roads and railways in France; remembering them honours their service, shows the whole history, and led to Canada's 2022 apology.

## 14 ext | Section 6 — 1914 and 1939

Compare how Canada went to war in 1914 and in 1939. Explain what changed in between and why.

(lines 4)

=> 1914: at war automatically when Britain declared war. 1939: Canada's Parliament decided for itself a week after Britain. In between, Canada's war effort earned it a voice (Versailles, the League), and the Statute of Westminster (1931) gave it control of its foreign affairs.

## S1 stretch | Sections 1, 2 and 6

"Development of armed forces advanced Canada's national sovereignty." Support this statement with three pieces of evidence from three different decades.

(lines 5)

=> 1870s: the NWMP enforced Canadian law in the West. 1910s: the Royal Canadian Navy; the Canadian Corps at Vimy under a Canadian commander. 1930s: the Statute of Westminster gave control of foreign affairs, earned partly by the war effort.

## S2 stretch | Sections 4 and 5

Research one heroic or gallant act by a Canadian or Newfoundland soldier in the First World War (a Victoria Cross recipient, Francis Pegahmagabow, a nurse, or a member of the No. 2 Construction Battalion). Write what they did and what it tells us about the war.

(lines 5)

=> Answers vary; strong answers give accurate who, where, when and what, and connect it to the cost or meaning of the war.

## S3 stretch | All sections

Answer the guiding question in a strong paragraph: **How can defence of peace and security play a role in the development of a nation?** Use at least three examples from Canada between 1873 and 1931.

(lines 6)

=> Strong answers link keeping order (NWMP), self-defence (1871, militia, navy) and wartime contribution (Boer War, Vimy) to growing control over Canada's own affairs, ending with the Statute of Westminster.
