## 2 core | Section 6 — Filling the West and new provinces

Put these in the order they joined Canada and write the year for each: Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, Newfoundland, Nunavut, Prince Edward Island.

(lines 2)

=> Manitoba 1870 → British Columbia 1871 → Prince Edward Island 1873 → Alberta 1905 → Newfoundland 1949 → Nunavut 1999.

## 3 core | Section 3 — Expanding west

It is 1869. You are a Canadian government official. Give your prime minister three reasons Canada should take over Rupert's Land from the Hudson's Bay Company.

(lines 3)

=> Any three: land for farms and settlers; resources (furs, timber, soil); stopping the U.S. from taking the region first; a route to British Columbia (which joined in 1871 on the promise of a railway).

## 4 ext | Section 6 — The National Policy and the CPR

Draw arrows to connect these four ideas into one chain, then explain the chain in two sentences: **railway**, **settlers**, **wheat**, **eastern factories**.

(lines 3)

=> The railway carried settlers west; the settlers grew wheat, which the railway carried east to sell; settlers used the money to buy goods made in eastern factories, which were protected by tariffs. Each part of the National Policy needed the others.

## 5 core | Section 2 — Why the colonies joined

A newspaper in Halifax, 1865, asks: "Why should Nova Scotia join with Canada at all?" Write a short answer that a supporter of Confederation might give. Use one event from the 1860s.

(lines 3)

=> For example: "The American Civil War has left the United States with a huge army, and some Americans talk of taking the whole continent. Alone, Nova Scotia is weak; joined with the other colonies we can defend ourselves and build a railway for trade."

## 6 core | Section 4 — The Red River Conflict

Fill in the cause-and-effect chain.

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| Canada sends surveyors to Red River in 1869 without asking the people there. | |
| | Manitoba joins Canada as a province in 1870. |
| Scrip is slow and confusing, and many Métis sell it cheaply. | |

=> Row 1 effect: Louis Riel and the Métis stop the surveyors, take Upper Fort Garry and form a provisional government.
=> Row 2 cause: the provisional government bargains with Canada; Parliament passes the Manitoba Act.
=> Row 3 effect: many Métis lose the land they were promised and move farther west, to the South Saskatchewan River.

## 7 ext | Section 6 — Settlement

The CPR is often called "the railway that built Canada." Give two pieces of evidence that support this title, and one group whose experience of the railway the title leaves out.

(lines 4)

=> Support: it connected BC (kept the 1871 promise); carried settlers west and wheat east; moved troops in 1885.
=> Left out: Chinese workers who did the most dangerous work in BC, and hundreds died; or First Nations and Métis whose land it crossed.

## 8 ext | Section 1 — Two empires

Explain how wars between Britain and France made it possible for Britain to control almost all of British North America by the 1860s. Use two events from the notes.

(lines 4)

=> Each war moved the boundaries. In 1713 Britain took over most of Acadia (renamed Nova Scotia); in 1759 it won at the Plains of Abraham and in 1763 France gave New France to Britain. With France gone, the colonies and territories were all British.

## 9 core | Section 5 — Treaties

Your school opens assemblies with a treaty land acknowledgement for Treaty 6. A classmate asks, "What even is a treaty?" Explain in your own words, using one detail from a numbered treaty.

(lines 3)

=> A treaty is an agreement between a First Nation and the Crown (later the Canadian government). For example, Treaty 6 (1876) set aside reserves, promised yearly payments and a "medicine chest", and opened land for settlement.

Read the two statements.

## 11 ext | Section 2 — Was Canada independent?

A student writes: "On July 1, 1867, Canada became a fully independent country." Identify the error and correct it with a complete explanation.

(lines 3)

=> In 1867 Canada became a self-governing Dominion, not a fully independent country. It ran its own affairs at home, but Britain still controlled foreign affairs (wars and treaties); when Britain went to war in 1914, Canada was at war too. Control of foreign policy came in 1931.

## 12 ext | Section 6 — New provinces

Why did Alberta and Saskatchewan become provinces in 1905, and not in 1870 when Canada took over the West? Use evidence about the railway and settlement.

(lines 3)

=> In 1870 few settlers lived there and there was no railway. After the CPR (1885) and the free-land campaigns, so many settlers arrived that they needed their own provincial governments, so the two provinces were carved out of the Northwest Territories in 1905.

## 13 ext | Section 3 — Was the West empty?

A poster from 1900 advertises the Prairies as "the last, best West: empty land waiting for you." Evaluate the poster. What does it get right, what does it leave out, and why might the government have made it that way?

(lines 4)

=> Right: there was farmland for settlers, and the government did offer land. Leaves out: First Nations and Métis peoples, who lived there and had treaties covering the land. Why: the government wanted settlers to come quickly to fill the West and strengthen Canada's claim, so it showed the land as empty.

## 14 ext | Section 5 — Two views of a treaty

The written Treaty 6 says First Nations would "cede, release, surrender" their land. Many Elders say the treaty was an agreement to share the land. Explain why both versions exist, and why historians use both.

(lines 4)

=> The treaty was negotiated through interpreters and written in English by the Crown; First Nations leaders relied on what was said aloud and understood the agreement as sharing the land in peace. Each version is evidence of one side's understanding, so historians compare both to understand what was agreed.

## S1 stretch | Sections 3, 4 and 5

Rank these three government decisions from most to least important for building Canada as a country: taking over Rupert's Land, creating Manitoba, signing the numbered treaties. Justify your ranking, and say who paid the biggest price for the decision you ranked first.

(lines 5)

=> Accept any ranking with reasons. Example: Rupert's Land first (it gave Canada the whole West; without it, nothing else was possible), treaties second (they opened the land for settlement), Manitoba third. The price: First Nations and Métis peoples, who were not consulted.

## S2 stretch | Sections 2 and 6

John A. Macdonald was a Father of Confederation and the prime minister behind the National Policy. Using evidence from two sections, explain how his decisions built the Canadian state, and name one group that was harmed by those same decisions.

(lines 5)

=> Built: he helped plan Confederation (1864–67), took over the West, and created the National Policy (tariffs, the CPR, settlement). Harmed: First Nations (treaties, the Indian Act, hunger on reserves), Métis (scrip, 1885), or Chinese railway workers.
