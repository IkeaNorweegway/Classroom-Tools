## 3 core | Section 2 — The homestead

Maria's family arrives from Ukraine in 1898 and claims a homestead near Vegreville. Describe the three things they must do to own the land, and how long it will take.

(lines 3)

=> Pay the $10 registration fee, live on the land, and build a house and plough part of it; after three years the 160 acres are theirs.

## 7 ext | Sections 1 and 4 — Two policies at once

In 1903 Canada was advertising free land in Europe and the U.S. In the same year it raised the head tax to $500. What does this show about how the government decided who belonged in Canada?

(lines 4)

=> The government chose immigrants partly by race and where they came from: it recruited white settlers from Britain, Europe and the U.S., and made it very hard for Chinese immigrants to come.

## 8 ext | Section 1 — Reading a poster

::: quote
A 1900s government poster: "Canada West: The Last, Best West. Free Homes for Millions. 160 acres free."
:::

a) Which pull factor is the poster using?
b) Which people does the poster leave out of its picture of the West?
c) Why might the government have designed it this way?

(lines 4)

=> a) Free land / free homes. b) First Nations and Métis peoples who lived there; also groups the government didn't want. c) To attract many farmers quickly, to fill and hold the West.

## 9 ext | Sections 2 and 3 — Land and settlement

Explain how access to land influenced where immigrants chose to settle, and why some groups settled close together.

(lines 4)

=> Homesteads were on the Prairies, so land drew settlers there (often near the railway). Groups settled in blocs to share language, faith and help, e.g. Ukrainians east of Edmonton and Francophones near Saint-Paul.

## 10 ext | Section 2 — "Free" land?

A student writes: "The government gave away free, empty land." Identify two problems with this statement and correct it.

(lines 4)

=> Not free: a $10 fee and three years of work were needed. Not empty: it was First Nations and Métis land, opened through the treaties. Correct: the government offered homesteads with conditions on land opened by treaty.

## 11 ext | Section 3 — Opportunity

Did immigration help build the Canadian state? Use two pieces of evidence about farms, towns or the economy.

(lines 4)

=> Yes: settlers farmed the West and grew wheat for export, built towns along the railway, bought eastern goods, and filled the land so Alberta and Saskatchewan could become provinces in 1905.

## 12 ext | Section 4 — Why restrict?

Explain why the Canadian government created policies that restricted immigration from certain groups. Use one example to support your conclusion about discrimination and government policy.

(lines 4)

=> Reasons given: protecting jobs and wages; underlying racist beliefs held by many politicians and voters. Example: the head tax applied only to Chinese immigrants, so discrimination was part of the law itself.

## 13 ext | Section 5 — The Komagata Maru

The Komagata Maru passengers said that, as British subjects, they had a right to enter Canada. Evaluate whether the government's response was fair. Use evidence.

(lines 4)

=> Most will judge it unfair: the passengers were British subjects; the Continuous Journey rule was written so they couldn't meet it; they were held two months and forced out by the navy; Canada apologized in 2016.

## 14 ext | Section 6 — Minority rights

In 1870 the Manitoba Act protected Catholic schools and French. In 1890 Manitoba took those protections away. Explain why Francophone Catholics saw this as unfair, and why this was called a "constitutional crisis."

(lines 4)

=> Their rights had been promised when Manitoba joined Canada, and the new English-speaking majority removed them. It was a constitutional crisis because it raised whether a province could take away rights protected when it joined, and it divided the whole country.

## S1 stretch | Sections 3, 4 and 5

"Immigration in post-Confederation Canada created opportunities for settlement and economic growth, while also leading to conflict and discrimination." Support both halves of this statement with one example each, and explain how they were connected.

(lines 5)

=> Opportunity: homesteads and the wheat economy. Conflict: the head tax or the Komagata Maru. Connection: the government chose which groups got the opportunity, so the same policy of building the West included some people and excluded others.

## S2 stretch | Section 6

Here are two sample accounts of the 1907 riot, written for this question: a Vancouver newspaper headline, "Asiatic invasion threatens our city," and a Japanese shopkeeper's letter describing broken windows and neighbours defending the street. How would you use both sources to find out what happened? Which parts are facts and which are opinions?

(lines 5)

=> Check facts in both (damage, dates, places) against other records; the headline's "invasion" is an opinion showing bias; the letter gives a first-hand account of damage (fact) and a perspective (opinion). Use both to see what happened and how different groups saw it.

## S3 stretch | All sections

Answer the guiding question in a strong paragraph: **Did post-Confederation immigration shape the development of the Canadian state?** Assess whether government policies in this period were fair, using at least three examples.

(lines 6)

=> Strong answers: yes, immigration filled the West, built the wheat economy and led to new provinces; but policies like the head tax, Continuous Journey and P.C. 1911-1324 were unfair to Asian and Black immigrants, and minority rights were taken away in Manitoba and Ontario.
