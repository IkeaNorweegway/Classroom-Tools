**Listen for:** "simple and compound are about the same" (Moment 3, Question 3, the main misconception target); "investing is just saving with a better rate" (it carries risk); forgetting to turn the percent into a decimal.

## Moment 1 — Fixed costs and variable costs

**1.** (a) **fixed** (b) **variable** (c) **fixed**

**2.** Fixed costs rise by $10 to $130; savings drop from $121 to **$111** a month.

**3.** Variable costs depend on day-to-day choices, so you can change them right away (eat out less this week). Fixed costs are set by contracts or memberships, so changing them takes time (cancel a plan, move).

::: answer
**E1.** For example. Fixed: phone plan, streaming subscription, bus pass. Variable: snacks, clothes, gifts.

**E2.** $121 × 10 = $1,210: **not enough**. $200 × 10 = $2,000: **enough**, with $500 to spare.
:::

## Moment 2 — Simple interest

**1.** I = 2,000 × 0.04 × 3 = **$240**. Total **$2,240**.

**2.** I = 800 × 0.05 × 2 = **$80**.

**3.** **Saving:** the bank pays you interest for keeping your money (for example, 3% a year on savings). **Borrowing:** you pay interest to use someone else's money (for example, interest on a loan or a credit card balance).

::: answer
**E1.** $15 a year: 150 ÷ 15 = **10 years**.

**E2.** 1,000 × 0.02 × 5 = $100 and 500 × 0.05 × 4 = $100. **They earn the same: $100.**
:::

## Moment 3 — Compound interest

**1.** Year 1: 2,000 × 1.04 = $2,080.00. Year 2: 2,080 × 1.04 = $2,163.20. Year 3: 2,163.20 × 1.04 = **$2,249.73**. (Simple would be $2,240.)

**2.** $1,628.89 − $1,500 = **$128.89** more.

::: misconception
**3.** Over a short time the results are close ($562.75 vs. $560 after 4 years). Over a long time compound interest pulls ahead, and the gap keeps widening: after 10 years it's $128.89 more on $1,000. Over 40–50 years it's thousands. It matters most for long-term goals, and for debt that isn't paid off.
:::

::: answer
**E1.** Ten extra years nearly doubles the result ($10,286 → $18,420). Starting early matters because compound growth is biggest in the later years.

**E2.** 800 × 1.05 = 840; 840 × 1.05 = **$882.00**. Simple gave $880, so compound is $2 more.
:::

## Moment 4 — Saving or investing?

**1.** (a) **saving** (b) **investing**

**2.** Investments can lose value in the short term. If you need the money soon, you might have to take it out when it's down. Saving keeps the principal safe.

**3.** Look for a choice matched to the 4–5 year timeline with risk and return explained. For example: *a GIC, because the money is safe and earns a little more than a savings account, and I don't need it until I graduate.* Accept a partly invested answer if the risk of losing value is named.

::: answer
**E1.** Investments that might earn more also have a bigger chance of losing money.

**E2.** Real rates change often (they depend on the bank and the economy). Check bank or credit union websites, or ask at a branch.
:::
