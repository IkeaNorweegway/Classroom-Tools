# Tilt Scenario Cards
*Week 5 Activity · Esports Program · Grades 7–9*

---

**How to use these cards**

Print and cut each card. Give one card per small group (3–4 students). Groups discuss the scenario and answer the three questions on the back of the card. Each group shares back to the class.

*Allow 10–12 minutes for discussion, 5 minutes for share-out.*

---

*Facilitator note: Tilt is an emotional response to a bad outcome that makes performance worse. The goal of this activity is not to tell students to "calm down" — it is to help them recognize the pattern and have a specific strategy ready before the moment arrives.*

---

---

## CARD 1 — The Blame Spiral

> Your team just lost a match that decided who makes the final. Your IGL immediately says to the team chat: "Our broadcaster was too loud and broke my concentration. That cost us the game."
>
> The broadcaster goes quiet and does not speak for the rest of the session. Two other players agree with the IGL. The Wellbeing Lead says nothing.

**Discuss:**

1. What triggered the tilt — and who is tilted? (It may be more than one person.)
2. What is the cost of this response to the *team's* next match and to the premiership points?
3. What should the IGL have said instead? Write one sentence that is honest *and* regulated.

---

---

## CARD 2 — The Early Rage Quit

> A player on your team is losing badly in the first five minutes of a match. They say: "This game is broken. The other team is cheating. There's no point playing."
>
> They stop trying — still in the game, but not making calls, not communicating, visibly disengaged. Your team loses the match.

**Discuss:**

1. Is it possible the other team was cheating? Does it matter for how this player responded?
2. What does "tilt" look like in this scenario — what are the visible signs?
3. If you were the IGL, what is one thing you could say *during the match* to bring this player back? Write the exact words.

## CARD 3 — The Post-Match Spiral

> Your team wins — but only barely. After the match, the Analyst says: "We almost threw that. Our rotations were terrible. We need to completely rework our strategy before next week."
>
> Two players hear this and feel embarrassed. One says: "Maybe we just got lucky. Maybe we are not actually that good." The team enters the next week session doubting themselves.

**Discuss:**

1. The Analyst's observation might be correct. Why is the *timing* of the feedback still a problem?
2. What is the difference between a growth mindset response and a fixed mindset response to this situation?
3. When is the right time and place for an Analyst to give hard feedback? What should happen first?

## CARD 4 — The Trash Talk Trap

> During a match, an opponent says something disrespectful in the chat — not a code of conduct violation, but clearly designed to get under your team's skin.
>
> One of your players responds in chat. The exchange escalates. Your team starts making mistakes because two players are distracted by the chat. You lose the match.
>
> At the end of the match, a fair play deduction is applied to your team — not for the original comment, but for the escalation.

**Discuss:**

1. Who is responsible for the fair play deduction — the opponent or your team? Explain.
2. What is the opponent's actual strategy here? Why does it work?
3. What is one rule your team could agree to right now that would prevent this from happening again?

## CARD 5 — The Overnight Tilt

> Your team had a rough session last week. There was an argument between two players that the Wellbeing Lead reported. Both players said the argument was "fine" by the end of the session, but they have not spoken since.
>
> This week, those two players have to coordinate in a match. The tension is obvious. They are not communicating. Your team loses.

**Discuss:**

1. Why did the conflict carry over — even though both players said it was "fine"?
2. Whose job is it to address this — the players themselves, the IGL, the Wellbeing Lead, or the officers? (There may be more than one right answer.)
3. What is one thing that could have been done *at the end of last session* to prevent this from happening this week?

---

## CARD 6 — The Pressure Build

> It is Week 8. Your team is second on the premiership ladder — only two points behind first place. Today is the last match before the showcase.
>
> Before the match, three of your five players are visibly anxious. One player keeps saying "We cannot lose this." The IGL tries to run a strategy session but nobody is focused.
>
> Your team plays worse than they ever have this season.

**Discuss:**

1. What is causing the tilt in this scenario? Is it a single moment — or has something been building?
2. What could the Wellbeing Lead have noticed and done before the match started?
3. Write the IGL's pre-match speech (3–4 sentences) that acknowledges the stakes and redirects the team's focus onto what they can control.

---

## CARD 7 — The Showcase Pressure (Stretch)

*Use this card if your group finishes early, or save it for a full-class discussion.*

> It is the morning of the showcase. The President is running the opening ceremony. The Broadcaster has never broadcast in front of parents and admin before. The Entrepreneur team's sponsorship pitch depends on a slide deck — and the slide deck will not open.
>
> Everything is happening at once. Three students are visibly panicking.

**Discuss:**

1. This is not a gaming tilt — it is a performance tilt. Are the causes and symptoms similar or different?
2. What is the single most useful thing a calm person in this group could say right now?
3. What could have been done in Weeks 7–8 to reduce the pressure at this moment?
