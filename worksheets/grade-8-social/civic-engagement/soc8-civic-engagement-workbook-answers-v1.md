# Civic Engagement — Workbook Answer Key
Grade 8 Social Studies · Teacher Copy — Not for Students
Model answers, marking guidance, and lesson-release markers for all 12 core questions and 3 stretch questions.

**Lesson-release schedule (adjust to your own pacing):**
- Q1–Q6 + midpoint check: open after Section 1 and Section 2 of the notes package are taught
- Q7–Q12: open after Section 3 (Voting) is taught
- Stretch S1–S3: open after Section 4 (Global Issues) is taught, as final consolidation

**Q1 ★** — [Forms of civic engagement] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 2]*
Any three of: volunteering, joining a community initiative, attending a rally, advocacy (letter/speaking at a meeting), starting or signing a petition, ethical purchasing (less directly relevant here but acceptable if justified). **Voting is not acceptable** — a 13-year-old cannot vote. Award full marks for any three non-voting forms with brief justification.

---

**Q2 ★** — [Engaged citizenship framework] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 1]*
(a) **Awareness** — Nadia notices/hears about the problem.
(b) **Decision-making** — she investigates the process and decides what she thinks should happen.
(c) **Action** — she writes a proposal and brings it to council; she is doing something.

**Q3 ★** — [Voting eligibility] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 3]*
(a) Jordan: **No** — fails the age requirement (17, not yet 18). Meets citizenship, ID, and residence.
(b) Priya: **No** — fails the proof-of-residence-in-this-riding requirement. Her documentation is for a different riding; she must update it before voting in her new riding.

**Q4 ★★** — [Global issues] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 4]*
Full chain expected: conflict disrupts the shipping route → ships take a longer route → shipping takes more time and costs more fuel/insurance → shipping companies raise their prices → the cost of importing goods rises → stores pass some of that cost on → Canadian shoppers pay more for the affected goods, or face shortages/delays. Award marks for a complete, logical chain ending in a concrete effect on a Canadian shopper — exact wording may vary.

**Q5 ★** — [Forms of civic engagement — misconception target: civic engagement requires being 18+] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 2]*
Any three of: volunteering, joining a community initiative, attending a rally, advocacy, ethical purchasing (if relevant to a family purchase), signing/starting a petition. Each must include the reason "does not require being 18." **[misconception target: civic engagement requires being 18+]**

**Q6 ★★** — [Engaged citizenship framework] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 1]*
Not fully an engaged citizen. Following the news is **awareness only** — the first of three parts. To be fully engaged, the classmate also needs to decide what they think should be done (decision-making) and actually do something about it (action), such as advocacy, joining a group, or changing a purchasing habit. Full marks require naming the missing stage(s) by name.

**Q7 ★** — [Voting eligibility, second appearance] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 3]*
**No.** Deng meets citizenship, age, and identity requirements, but he does not yet have valid proof of residence for his new riding. All four requirements must be met at the same time — missing even one (in this case, updated proof of residence) means he cannot vote in that riding's election yet.

**Q8 ★★** — [Forms of civic engagement — misconception target: voting is the only meaningful form of civic engagement] *[opens after Lesson covering Section 2]*
**Error:** The student assumes voting is the only way to influence the council's decision. This is false — the council vote happens next month, well before any election, and there are many non-voting forms available now. **Correction (any two):** advocacy (writing to or speaking at the council meeting), attending a rally, starting/signing a petition, joining a community initiative such as a youth advisory group. **[misconception target: voting is the only meaningful form of civic engagement]**

## Stretch Questions — S1 to S3

*Multi-concept synthesis. Use as discussion guides or for extended assessment marking.*

**S1 ★★** — [Global issues + Forms of civic engagement]
Any two forms, each correctly matched to a framework stage. Example: (1) **Advocacy** — writing to the town council to ask for a welcome program — this is **action**. (2) Attending a **community initiative** meeting to learn what newcomer families actually need — this is **awareness** (if framed as information-gathering) or **decision-making** (if framed as deciding how to help). Award marks for internally consistent reasoning, not one fixed answer — the framework mapping should be logically defensible.

---

**S2 ★★** — [Voting + Engaged citizenship framework]
Strong answers disagree with the claim, using collective action reasoning: one vote alone rarely decides an election, but every voter reasons this way, elections would have no votes at all — the claim only works if applied to one person and ignored for everyone else. Connect to the framework: voting is itself an action step that only matters after awareness and decision-making have happened; skipping it because "one vote doesn't matter" undermines the action stage for everyone collectively. Accept a well-justified "agree in part" answer if the student separates individual impact from collective impact accurately.

---

**S3 ★★** — [Multi-concept design task]
Award marks for: (1) a real, specific local issue named; (2) at least three distinct forms of civic engagement from the unit (not three variations of the same form); (3) all three framework stages represented across the plan; (4) a brief justification for why those specific forms were chosen for that specific issue (not generic). This is a design/create-level task — mark holistically using these four criteria rather than a single correct answer.
