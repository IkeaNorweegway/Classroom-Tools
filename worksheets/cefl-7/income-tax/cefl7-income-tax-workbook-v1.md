::: example
**Jordan earns $20.00 an hour and works 40 hours a week. Jordan is paid every two weeks. What is Jordan's pay before anything is taken off?**

1. Find the hours in one pay period. Two weeks × 40 hours = **80 hours**.
2. Multiply the hours by the wage. 80 × $20.00 = **$1,600.00**.
3. This is Jordan's **gross pay**: what Jordan earned before anything comes off.
:::

### The 2026 deduction card

Use this card for every pay question. It is a simple version of the real rules.

| Deduction | Who pays it | How to find it (each pay, every 2 weeks) |
|---|---|---|
| **EI** (Employment Insurance) | Every worker, any age | 1.63% of gross pay |
| **CPP** (Canada Pension Plan) | Workers 18 and older | 5.95% of (gross pay − $135) |
| **Income tax** | Workers who earn more than the tax-free amount in a year | Look it up in the table below |

| Gross pay every 2 weeks | $300–$600 | $700 | $800 | $900 | $1,000 | $1,100 | $1,200 | $1,300 | $1,400 | $1,500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Income tax** | $0 | $9.41 | $23.41 | $39.35 | $61.35 | $83.35 | $105.35 | $127.35 | $149.35 | $171.35 |

| Gross pay every 2 weeks | $1,600 | $1,700 | $1,800 | $1,900 | $2,000 | $2,100 | $2,200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Income tax** | $193.35 | $215.35 | $237.35 | $259.35 | $281.35 | $303.35 | $325.35 |

*Income tax in the table is an estimate using 2026 federal and Alberta rates. Real pay stubs can differ by a few dollars.*

::: example
**Check Jordan's pay stub. Jordan is 24 and earned $1,600 gross.**

1. **EI:** 1.63% of $1,600 → 0.0163 × 1,600 = **$26.08**.
2. **CPP:** Jordan is over 18. $1,600 − $135 = $1,465. Then 0.0595 × 1,465 = **$87.17** (rounded to the nearest cent).
3. **Income tax:** find $1,600 in the table → **$193.35**.
4. **Total deductions:** $26.08 + $87.17 + $193.35 = **$306.60**.
5. **Net pay:** $1,600.00 − $306.60 = **$1,293.40**. This matches the pay stub.
:::

::: example
**Jordan pays about $1,506 a year in Alberta income tax. If it were split the same way Alberta spends, how much would go to health?**

1. Health gets about **41%** of Alberta's spending (34.4 ÷ 84 ≈ 0.41).
2. 41% of $1,506 → 0.41 × 1,506 = **$617.46**.
3. So about **$617** of Jordan's Alberta income tax would go to health.

*This is a model. Tax money goes into one big pot, but this shows the size of each part.*

::: data
All numbers are for 2026. They change every year.
- Alberta minimum wage $15.00/hour; $13.00/hour for students under 18 (up to 28 hours in a school week, or during school breaks).
- Federal income tax: 14% on the first $58,523 of taxable income; tax-free (basic personal) amount $16,452.
- Alberta income tax: 8% on the first $61,200; tax-free (basic personal) amount $22,769.
- CPP: 5.95% of pay above $3,500 a year (about $135 every two weeks); starts the month after a worker turns 18.
- EI: 1.63% of pay, any age.
- Alberta Budget 2026–27: about $84 billion total; health $34.4 billion; education $10.8 billion.
- People and businesses in this workbook are made up; the rates are real.
:::
