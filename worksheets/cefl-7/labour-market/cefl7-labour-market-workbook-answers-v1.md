::: teacher
**Listen for:** "a career is just a job you have for a long time" (it is the whole path); "skills only count in the job where you learned them"; "changing your mind wastes time". The misconception target is Moment 4: "the highest-paying career is always the best."
:::

## Moment 1 — A job or a career?

**1.** (a) **job** (b) **career** (c) **job** (a volunteer role is still one role; it can be a step in a career)

**2.** Any step linked to the next with a reason. For example: *babysitting showed Mia she liked working with kids, so she chose to be a camp counsellor; counselling gave her experience that helped her get into the college program.*

**3.** Careers grow step by step and can change. Mia didn't pick "daycare director" at 18. She tried jobs, learned what she liked, went to college, and moved up over many years.

::: answer
**E1.** Any 4 sensible steps, labelled. For example: dishwasher (job) → line cook (job) → culinary diploma or cook apprenticeship (learning) → chef (job).

**E2.** Personal. Look for two real steps.
:::

## Moment 2 — Skills that travel

**1.** Circle: stay calm, talk clearly, show up on time, work well with other guards. Skills: **staying calm under pressure, communication, time management (reliability), teamwork.**

**2.** Personal. Look for a real activity linked to a named skill with a "how." For example: *hockey builds teamwork because we have to pass and plan plays together.*

**3.** One named skill and two specific actions. For example, communication: *ask one question in class every day; call to book my own dentist appointment.*

::: answer
**E1.** For example: an electrician solves why a light won't turn on; a nurse solves how to calm a scared patient.

**E2.** Any skill with a reason. Communication and teamwork are common answers.
:::

## Moment 3 — Interests change, and that's OK

**1.** Personal. Look for at least two interests and two values.

**2.** Any order, with a reason for number 1 linked to an interest or value.

**3.** Look for disagreement backed by transferable skills: *you don't waste time, because the skills you built (teamwork, problem solving) come with you to the next career.* Accept "partly agree" if the reasoning is sound (for example, some training costs time and money).

::: answer
**E1.** Personal.

**E2.** A career checked against both lists, with a fit decision.
:::

## Moment 4 — What shapes a career choice?

**1.** 47.38 − 19.05 = **$28.33 more per hour.**

**2.** Any three: training or education needed; cost of training; how long the training takes; whether training is close to home; interests; values; whether there are jobs (demand); working hours or conditions; family responsibilities.

**3.** Look for all three: pay (with numbers from the chart), training (type and length), and personal interest.

::: misconception
Pay matters, but it is not everything. The answer should name at least one missing factor: interests, values, training cost and time, whether there are jobs, or working conditions. A high-paying career you dislike, or can't get training for, may not be the best choice.
:::

**E2.** Accept a reasoned answer. **Electrician:** apprentices earn wages from the start while they train. **Early childhood educator:** a certificate is the shortest schooling.
:::
