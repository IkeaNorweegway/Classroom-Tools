::: teacher
**Listen for:** "a budget means no fun"; "saving isn't a trade-off"; "I'll just borrow if something happens"; mixing up needs and wants. The misconception target is Moment 2, Question 3.
:::

## Moment 1 — Every dollar is a choice

**1.** If Leo buys snacks, he gives up **$20 toward the headphones**, so they take longer to buy.

**2.** Personal. Look for a real choice **and** the thing given up.

**3.** Either answer is fine with a reason and the cost named. For example: *"Yes. The concert only happens once, and she can still save $15. She gives up $45 toward the bike, so it will take longer."*

::: answer
**E1.** $120 + $15 = $135 saved. $360 − $135 = **$225** more needed.

**E2.** Saving means giving up what you could buy **now**, such as the concert, to get something later.
:::

## Moment 2 — A plan for your money

**1.** (a) $100 − $40 − $35 − $25 = **$0**. Leo isn't saving anything.
(b) He needs $150 ÷ 5 = **$30 a month**. Any new plan where the spending lines add to $70 and savings is $30. For example: snacks $25, gaming $25, movies $20, savings $30.

**2.** Gaming is a **want**: nice to have, but he can live without it. Food is a **need**: something you must have to live. Needs come first in a budget.

**3.** Ava's new budget still has $20 for snacks, $15 for games and $15 for gifts. A budget doesn't remove fun; it **plans** for it, so you can enjoy spending **and** reach your goals.

::: answer
**E1.** She needs $360 − $20 = $340. At $80 a month: 340 ÷ 80 = 4.25, so a little over 4 months (about a week sooner than before).

**E2.** Any budget that adds up to $50 and includes a savings line.
:::

## Moment 3 — Setting a money goal

**1.** (a) **short-term** (b) **long-term** (c) **long-term**

**2.** 4,000 ÷ 12 = **$333.33 a month**. That's about a third of his $1,023. It could be realistic if his needs are covered (for example, he lives at home), but it leaves less for other spending and for surprises. Accept a reasoned yes or no.

**3.** A goal with what, how much, and by when; the weekly amount = cost ÷ number of weeks.

::: answer
**E1.** $1,023 − $250 − $60 = **$713** (or $713.04 using $1,023.04).

**E2.** For example: you remember it, you can track progress, and it's easier to say no to spending that doesn't fit.
:::

## Moment 4 — Ready for surprises

**1.** 300 ÷ 40 = 7.5, so **8 months** (after 7 months he'd have $280).

**2.** Personal. For example: **poor:** spending all your pay the day you get it, so you can't cover a surprise or reach a goal. **Effective:** saving a set amount from every pay first, so your goal and emergency fund grow.

**3.** For example: they know they can handle a surprise without borrowing or giving up their goals, so they worry less.

::: answer
**E1.** $300 − $180 = **$120** left. To refill $180 at $40 a month: 180 ÷ 40 = 4.5, so **5 months**.

**E2.** For example: a lost bus pass, a broken bike, a friend's birthday gift, a school trip fee, replacing lost earbuds.
:::
