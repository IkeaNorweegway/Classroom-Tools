::: teacher
**Listen for:** "university is the only real option"; "tuition is the whole cost"; "if I meet the minimum grade, I'm in"; "a pathway is a forever choice." The misconception target is Moment 1, Question 3.
:::

## Moment 1 — Four kinds of pathways

**1.** (a) **university** (b) **apprenticeship** (c) **college**

**2.** Any one real difference: length (4 years vs. 2 years); type of learning (more theory and research vs. more hands-on and applied); cost (a degree usually costs more in total); the credential (degree vs. diploma); starting work sooner with a diploma.

**3.** Look for **disagreement with a reason**: all pathways can lead to good careers, and different careers need different pathways. Apprenticeship offers at least one of: **getting paid while you learn**, less debt, mostly hands-on learning on a real job site, a trade certificate that is in demand.

::: answer
**E1.** For example: a student finishes a 2-year college diploma, then transfers credits into a university degree; or works for a few years, then goes back to school as an adult.

**E2.** Any real school with its type (university, college, polytechnic, or a trade school).
:::

## Moment 2 — The true cost

**1.** 4 × $7,293.60 = **$29,174.40**

**2.** 3 × $1,044 = $3,132. Then $3,132 + $1,524 = **$4,656**.

**3.** Apprentices **earn wages while they train**, so they lose little or no income. Most of a university student's true cost is lost earnings; an apprentice avoids most of that, and can pay tuition from their wages.

::: answer
**E1.** $7,293.60 + (8 × $1,000 = $8,000) + $19,200 = **$34,493.60**

**E2.** $29,174.40 − $4,656 = **$24,518.40** more.
:::

## Moment 3 — Shortages and surpluses

**1.** (a) **surplus** (b) **shortage**

**2.** Any one: a shortage can make it easier to find a job after you finish; you don't want to spend years training for a job that is hard to get; it helps you plan a backup.

**3.** Forecasts can change. By the time you finish school, the economy, technology, and the number of people training for that job may be different.

::: answer
**E1.** Any one: many people start training for that job; the economy slows and employers hire less; new technology does some of the work; workers move in from other places.

**E2.** Personal; look for a real outlook statement from alis.alberta.ca.
:::

## Moment 4 — Making the choice

**1.** Programs check high school courses and grades, so students need to take the **right courses** (prerequisites) and do well in them. Planning early keeps more pathways open.

**2.** Any two: time; income you could have earned (lost earnings); money for tuition (possible debt); flexibility (being tied to one place or schedule).

**3.** Look for a named pathway, a real trade-off (time, money, income, flexibility, distance), and a reason it is acceptable connected to the student's goals.

::: answer
**E1.** Either choice with a reason.

**E2.** A sensible criterion with both columns filled in.
:::
