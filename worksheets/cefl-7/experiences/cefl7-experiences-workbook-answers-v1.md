::: teacher
**Listen for:** "only paid jobs count"; "being your own boss means no risk and no boss"; "safety is the boss's job, not mine"; "if a boss asks, I have to do it." The misconception target is Moment 1, Question 3.
:::

## Moment 1 — Kinds of experiences

**1.** (a) **extracurricular** (b) **work-related** (entrepreneurship) (c) **curricular**

**2.** Personal. Look for a real experience **and** a link to work preferences (for example: *coaching younger kids showed me I like teaching and being on a team*).

**3.** Noah's unpaid experiences still built work skills. At the food bank he practised teamwork and following instructions; in band, practice and commitment; at the science fair, planning and presenting. Employers value all of these.

::: answer
**E1.** For example: peer reading buddy, library helper, school event set-up crew, recycling team, helping at a community clean-up or seniors' centre (with an adult).

**E2.** For example: pet sitting, dog walking, lawn mowing, making and selling crafts, tutoring younger kids. Look for a safe idea with an adult's OK.
:::

## Moment 2 — Where and how people work

**1.** Personal preferences on each line.

**2.** **Self-employed** (he runs his own small business; no employer), **seasonal** (winter only; also casual, since it depends on snowfall), **outdoor**.

**3.** Either, with one good and one hard thing. **Employee:** steady pay, someone else handles the business; but less choice about schedule or tasks. **Self-employed:** you are the boss and choose your work; but income can be uncertain and you carry the risk.

::: answer
**E1.** Virtual: for example, software developer or online customer support. In person: for example, electrician, nurse, hairstylist, lifeguard.

**E2.** Any reasonable reason: school, caring for family, health, another job, wanting more free time.
:::

## Moment 3 — Work-life balance

**1.** **Not allowed.** (1) It is **4 hours** on a school day; the limit is 2. (2) It goes **past 9 pm**.

**2.** Personal. Check the arithmetic: the student's hours should add up to no more than 168.

**3.** He might give up sleep-ins, time with friends or family, homework time, or band or food bank time. Either decision is fine with a reason about balance.

::: answer
**E1.** For example: so they get enough sleep, keep up with school, and stay safe (fewer late-night risks).

**E2.** For example: always tired; grades dropping; no time for friends or family; feeling stressed all the time.
:::

## Moment 4 — Belonging and safety at work

**1.** (a) **participate** (b) **know** (c) **refuse**

**2.** Any two: welcome new workers and introduce them to the team; train them; give them a buddy; make it OK to ask questions; treat everyone with respect; have clear rules against bullying.

**3.** **No.** Leaving someone out and joking about his accent is exclusion and disrespect. Someone could include Kai, tell the others to stop, or tell a supervisor. The organization should make clear that everyone is welcome.

::: answer
**E1.** For example: they're new, they want to make a good impression, they don't know their rights, or they're afraid of losing the job. Workplaces can train new workers, explain the three rights, and thank people who raise safety concerns.

**E2.** For example: *"I haven't been trained to do that safely. Can you show me first, or give me something else to do?"*
:::
