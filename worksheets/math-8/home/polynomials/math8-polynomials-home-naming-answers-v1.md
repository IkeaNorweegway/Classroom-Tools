::: tip
**For the adult:** The most common slip is mixing up the degree and the number of terms. x³ + 1 has two terms but degree 3. The second is reading x² as 2x. Test with a number: when x = 3, x² = 9 but 2x = 6.
:::

## Answers

**1.** **3 · x · x**. The coefficient is 3. The exponent 2 means two x's.
@viz poterm c="3" v="x" e="2" clabel="coefficient" elabel="exponent" expand="3x² = [[3 · x · x]]"
**2.** a) **x²** b) **5** c) **−x³**

**3.** x² = 3 · 3 = 9. 2x = 3 + 3 = 6. 9 ≠ 6, so **they are not the same**.

**4.** a) **Yes** b) **No.** 4/x = 4x⁻¹, and −1 is not a counting-number exponent. c) **Yes** (a constant).

**5.** **2x² − 3x + 1**. Three terms: a trinomial. Highest exponent 2: **quadratic trinomial**.
@viz potiles rows="2x2;-3x;+1" notes="[[2x² − 3x + 1]]"
**6.** a) **cubic binomial** (degree 3) b) **linear monomial** (degree 1) c) **constant monomial** (degree 0)

**7.** **No.** Degree is the highest exponent. The exponents are 1 and 3, so the degree is **3**. Two terms makes it a binomial.
