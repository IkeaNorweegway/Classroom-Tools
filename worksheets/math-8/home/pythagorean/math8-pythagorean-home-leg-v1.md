::: tip
**For the adult:** When the longest side (hypotenuse) is known, SUBTRACT the squares: missing leg² = hypotenuse² − other leg². The answer must be shorter than the hypotenuse. A ladder against a wall is the classic example: the ladder is the hypotenuse.
:::

## Worked example

::: example
**A 13 m ladder leans on a wall. Its foot is 5 m from the wall. How high does it reach?**

1. The ladder is the hypotenuse (across from the square corner where wall meets ground).
2. Subtract the squares: 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144.
3. Square root: √144 = 12. The ladder reaches 12 m up the wall.
4. Check: 12 m is shorter than the 13 m ladder ✓
:::
