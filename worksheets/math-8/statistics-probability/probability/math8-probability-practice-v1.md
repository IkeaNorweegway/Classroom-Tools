## Challenge

### Jordan says, "I rolled three 6s in a row, so I probably won't roll a 6 next." Is he right? Explain using the word independent.
(work 3)
=> No. Each roll is independent: the die has no memory. P(6) is still 1/6 on the next roll.

### Mia says P(heads and a 4) = {1/2} + {1/6} = {2/3}. Show two ways that her answer is wrong.
(work 4)
=> 1) "And" must be less likely than each event alone, but 2/3 is bigger than 1/2. 2) The table has 12 outcomes and only one (H4) works: 1/12. Multiply: 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.

### Design a game with a spinner and a coin where the probability of winning is {1/8}. Explain how you know.
(work 4)
=> Example: spinner with 4 equal parts, win if you spin red (1/4) AND flip heads (1/2). 1/4 × 1/2 = 1/8.

### The chance of rain in Calgary on Saturday is 40% and on Sunday is 20%. Assume the days are independent. What is the chance it rains both days? What is the chance it rains on neither day?
(work 4)
=> Both: 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.08 = 8%. Neither: 0.6 × 0.8 = 0.48 = 48%.

### A class rolls two dice 360 times and records "double 6." About how many double 6s should they expect? They got 14. Is that a surprise? Explain.
(work 3)
=> P(double 6) = 1/36. 360 × 1/36 = 10. 14 is a bit more, but results vary; it is not a big surprise.
