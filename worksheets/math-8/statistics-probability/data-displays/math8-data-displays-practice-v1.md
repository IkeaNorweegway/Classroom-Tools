### Look at the graph.
@viz bar data="Store A:98;Store B:95" ymin=94 ymax=99 break=1 title="Customer happiness score (sample)"
a) What does the graph seem to say? [[Store A is much better than Store B.]]
b) What is the real difference? [[3 points out of 100 — very small.]]
c) What makes it misleading? [[The axis starts at 94, not 0.]]

### A student says, "Most students walk to school," using the graph from Question 1a. Is that correct? Explain.
(lines 2)
=> No. Walk is 40%, which is less than half. "Most" means more than 50%.

## Challenge

### A company shows its sales doubled using two pictures: the second bag is twice as tall and twice as wide. Explain why this is misleading. How could you fix it?
@viz picto data="2024:1;2025:2" mode=scaled icon=square
(work 3)
=> The second picture has 4 times the area, so sales look 4 times bigger. Fix: use two same-size pictures for 2025 and one for 2024, or use a bar graph.

### Draw a fair bar graph and a misleading bar graph for the same data: Team A 30 goals, Team B 27 goals. Explain the difference.
(work 6)
=> Fair: y-axis from 0 to 35, bars almost the same height. Misleading: y-axis from 25 to 31, Team A's bar looks about 2½ times as tall.

### Which graph is better for this data, a line graph or a bar graph? Explain. "Number of cars that pass the school each hour from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m."
(work 3)
=> Either can work; a line graph shows the trend over the day best (rush times). A bar graph is also fine for comparing each hour. The reason must match the choice.

### A news story shows a graph of gas prices for only the 3 months they went up. The headline says "Gas prices keep rising!" What questions should you ask before believing it?
(work 3)
=> What happened in the other months? Where does the axis start? How big is the increase? Is 3 months enough to show a trend?

### Look at the graph. Write one conclusion that is supported and one that is NOT supported. Explain each.
@viz line data="2019:40;2020:35;2021:45;2022:50;2023:48" ymin=0 ymax=60 ystep=10 ylabel="birds" title="Birds counted at a feeder in May (sample)"
(work 4)
=> Supported: the most birds were counted in 2022 (50). Not supported: "the bird count goes up every year" (it fell in 2020 and 2023).
