## Challenge

### Noah says, "2{1/2} × 2{1/2} = 4{1/4}, because 2 × 2 = 4 and {1/2} × {1/2} = {1/4}." Show why this is wrong. Draw an area model.
@viz partial cols="2:2;0.5:1/2" rows="2:2;0.5:1/2" cells="[[4]];[[1]];[[1]];[[1/4]]"
(work 3)
=> He missed the two middle pieces: 2 × {1/2} = 1 twice. 4 + 1 + 1 + {1/4} = 6{1/4}. Check: {5/2} × {5/2} = {25/4} = 6{1/4}.

### Ana wrote {3/5} ÷ {1/10} = {5/3} × {1/10} = {5/30} = {1/6}. Find her mistake and fix it. How can an estimate show the answer must be bigger than 1?
(work 3)
=> She flipped the first fraction. Correct: {3/5} × 10 = 6. A tenth is small, so many tenths fit in {3/5}: the answer must be more than 1.

### Write a story problem for {3/4} ÷ 3 and another for 3 ÷ {3/4}. Solve both. Why are the answers so different?
(work 4)
=> Example: {3/4} of a pizza shared by 3 people → {1/4} each. 3 pizzas cut into {3/4}-pizza servings → 4 servings. Sharing gives a small answer; "how many fit" gives a bigger one.

### Evaluate: {1/2} + {2/3} × {3/4} ÷ {1/2}
(work 3)
=> Left to right for × and ÷: {2/3} × {3/4} = {1/2}; {1/2} ÷ {1/2} = 1. Then {1/2} + 1 = 1{1/2}.

### A field is {4/5} of a quarter section. {3/8} of the field is planted with canola. What fraction of the quarter section is canola? Draw it.
(work 3)
=> {3/8} × {4/5} = {12/40} = {3/10} of the quarter section.

### Without calculating, put these in order from smallest to largest. Explain your thinking.
{5/6} × 12 · 12 · 12 ÷ {5/6}
(work 3)
=> {5/6} × 12 (= 10) < 12 < 12 ÷ {5/6} (= 14{2/5}). Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes it smaller; dividing by it makes it bigger.
