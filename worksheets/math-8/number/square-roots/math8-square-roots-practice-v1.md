## Challenge

### Jordan says, "√36 = 18, because 18 + 18 = 36." Explain the mistake. Draw a picture to help.
(work 4)
=> The square root is the number that times ITSELF makes 36. 18 × 18 = 324. A 6 by 6 square has 36 tiles, so √36 = 6. Jordan found half, not the square root.

### Use prime factors to decide if each number is a perfect square. If it is, find the square root.
a) 196 = 2 × 2 × 7 × 7
(work 2)
=> Two equal groups: (2 × 7)(2 × 7) = 14 × 14. Yes, √196 = 14.
b) 180 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5
(work 2)
=> There is only one 5, so it cannot be split into two equal groups. Not a perfect square.

### A quarter section of farmland is a square with an area of about 640 000 m². A farmer walks once around the edge. About how far does she walk? Show your steps.
@viz squares shapes="8x8" labels="area ≈ 640 000 m²" cell=10
(work 3)
=> Side = √640 000 = 800 m. Perimeter = 4 × 800 = 3200 m, about 3.2 km.

### Name three whole numbers that have a square root between 8 and 9. Explain how you know.
(work 3)
=> 8² = 64 and 9² = 81, so any whole number from 65 to 80 works, for example 65, 70, 80.

### Two square tiles have areas of 25 cm² and 50 cm². Kai says the second tile has sides twice as long. Is Kai right? Explain with numbers.
(work 4)
=> No. The first side is √25 = 5 cm. The second side is √50 ≈ 7.1 cm, not 10 cm. A square with sides of 10 cm would have an area of 100 cm², four times as big.

### A square backyard rink has an area of 150 m². A hockey net needs a rink at least 12 m wide. Is this rink wide enough? Explain.
(work 3)
=> √150 is between 12 (144) and 13 (169), about 12.2 m. Yes, it is just wide enough.
