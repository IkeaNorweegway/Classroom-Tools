## core | IN1 — Integers, Section 1
The temperature in Edmonton drops 3 °C every hour for 4 hours. Write a multiplication. Use the number line to show it.
@viz numberline min=-14 max=2 step=1 every=2
(lines 1)
=> 4 × (−3) = −12. The temperature drops 12 °C. Four jumps of −3 from 0.

## core | SQ1–SQ2 — Squares and Square Roots, Sections 1–2
Look at the squares of tiles.
@viz squares shapes="4x4;7x7;9x9" labels="[[16]];[[49]];[[81]]" cell=10
a) How many tiles are in each square? Write each one as a square number.
b) Write a square root fact for each one.
(lines 2)
=> a) 4² = 16, 7² = 49, 9² = 81. b) √16 = 4, √49 = 7, √81 = 9.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
5² = 10. If a student writes 4² = 8, have them count the tiles.
:::

## core | RR1 — Ratios and Rates, Section 1
Frozen juice is 1 can of concentrate to 4 cans of water.
@viz tape parts="1:concentrate;4:water"
a) What is the ratio of concentrate to all the juice?
b) What percent of the juice is concentrate?
(lines 2)
=> a) 1:5. b) 1 out of 5 = 20%.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"1:4 means 1 out of 4." Ask: how many cans are in the jug?
:::

## core | IN2 — Integers, Section 2
Find each answer. Then explain why they have different signs.
a) (−5) + (−6)
b) (−5) × (−6)
(lines 3)
=> a) −11. b) 30. Adding two negatives goes further below zero. Multiplying two negatives gives a positive. The "two negatives make a positive" rule is only for × and ÷.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Two negatives make a positive" used for adding.
:::

## core | SQ3 — Squares and Square Roots, Section 3
Mark √30 and √60 on the number line. Between which whole numbers is each one?
@viz numberline min=4 max=9 step=0.5 every=1 keypoints="5.48:√30;7.75:√60"
(lines 2)
=> √30 is between 5 and 6 (25 < 30 < 36). √60 is between 7 and 8 (49 < 60 < 64), closer to 8.

## core | RR3 — Ratios and Rates, Section 3
Hockey tape: 4 rolls for $9.00 or 6 rolls for $12.60. Which is the better buy?
@viz bar data="4 rolls:9.00;6 rolls:12.60" values=1 ymin=0 ymax=14 ystep=2 ylabel="price ($)"
(lines 2)
=> 4 rolls: $2.25 each. 6 rolls: $2.10 each. The 6-roll pack is the better buy.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Bigger pack is always the better deal" (here it happens to be true, but students must show the unit prices).
:::

## ext | RR2 — Ratios and Rates, Section 2
Jen says 2:3 and 4:5 are equal ratios, "because I added 2 to both numbers." Is she right? Explain with a table or a picture.
(lines 4)
=> No. Equal ratios multiply both terms by the same number: 2:3 = 4:6. In 2:3 the second part is 1.5 times the first. In 4:5 it is only 1.25 times.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
Adding the same number to make equal ratios.
:::

## ext | SQ2 — Squares and Square Roots, Section 2
Leo says √64 = 32. Explain his mistake. How could he check his answer?
(lines 3)
=> He took half of 64. Check by multiplying: 32 × 32 = 1024, not 64. √64 = 8 because 8 × 8 = 64.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
√n = n ÷ 2.
:::

## ext | IN3–IN4 — Integers, Sections 3–4
The low temperatures in Red Deer for 5 days were −12 °C, −8 °C, −3 °C, 1 °C and −8 °C. Find the mean low. Show your steps.
@viz numberline min=-14 max=2 step=1 every=2 points="-12;-8;-3;1;-8"
(lines 3)
=> Sum = −30. Mean = (−30) ÷ 5 = −6 °C.

## ext | FR2 — Fractions, Section 2
Max says 2{1/2} × 2 = 4{1/2}. He multiplied 2 × 2 and kept the {1/2}. Find the right answer and explain his mistake.
(lines 3)
=> 2{1/2} × 2 = {5/2} × 2 = 5. He forgot to multiply the {1/2} by 2 (that is 1 more).
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
Multiplying mixed numbers part by part.
:::

## ext | PC4 — Percents, Section 4
A $120 jacket is 25% off. A week later the store adds 25% to the sale price. Is the jacket back to $120? Show your work.
(lines 4)
=> 120 × 0.75 = $90. 90 × 1.25 = $112.50. No: the 25% increase is of $90, which is only $22.50.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
"Down 25% then up 25% returns to the start."
:::

## ext | RR4 — Ratios and Rates, Section 4
On a map of Alberta, 1 cm = 40 km. Banff and Calgary are about 3.2 cm apart on the map. About how far apart are they? Draw a double number line.
(lines 3)
=> 3.2 × 40 = 128 km (about 130 km).

## ext | FR3 — Fractions, Section 3
Ana wrote {3/4} ÷ {1/2} = {4/3} × {1/2} = {4/6}. Find her mistake. What is the right answer? How can an estimate help?
(lines 4)
=> She flipped the first fraction. {3/4} × 2 = {3/2} = 1{1/2}. Estimate: a half fits into three quarters more than once, so the answer must be more than 1.
::: teacher
label: Misconception target
Flipping the first fraction.
:::

## ext | SQ4 — Squares and Square Roots, Section 4
A square garden has an area of 90 m². About how much fence goes around it? Show how you estimated.
(lines 3)
=> 81 < 90 < 100, so the side is about 9.5 m (√90 ≈ 9.49). Fence ≈ 4 × 9.5 = 38 m.

## stretch | PC1 + RR1 — Percents and Ratios
A town had 2000 people. Ten years later it has 2600. Write the new population as a percent of the old one. Then write the ratio old : new in simplest form.
(lines 4)
=> 2600 ÷ 2000 = 1.3 = 130%. 2000 : 2600 = 10 : 13.

## stretch | IN2 + FR2 — Integers and Fractions
Decide if each answer is positive or negative, and bigger or smaller than 1 in size. Do not calculate. Explain.
a) (−3) × (−{1/2})
b) (−{3/4}) × 8
(lines 4)
=> a) Positive (two negatives); size 1{1/2}, bigger than 1. b) Negative (one negative); size 6, bigger than 1.

## stretch | SQ3 + PC2 — Square roots and Percents
A square rink has an area of 400 m². The town makes it 21% bigger in area. About how long is a side of the new rink?
(lines 4)
=> New area = 400 × 1.21 = 484 m². Side = √484 = 22 m (the old side was 20 m).

## stretch | RR3 + FR3 — Rates and Fractions
A car uses 6 L of gas every 100 km. The tank holds 45 L. How far can it go on {3/4} of a tank?
(lines 4)
=> {3/4} × 45 = 33.75 L. 33.75 ÷ 6 = 5.625 hundreds of km = 562.5 km.
