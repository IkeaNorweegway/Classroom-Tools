# Statistics and Data Analysis — Concept Summary
Grade 9 Mathematics · Alberta Curriculum

---

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bivariate data | Data with two variables measured per subject |
| Scatter plot | Graph of bivariate data — each point is one subject |
| Correlation | The tendency of two variables to change together |
| Line of best fit | A straight line drawn to represent the trend in a scatter plot |
| Interpolation | Predicting within the data range — more reliable |
| Extrapolation | Predicting beyond the data range — less reliable |
| Outlier | A data point far from the general pattern |

---

## Types of Correlation

| Type | Description | Scatter plot appearance |
|---|---|---|
| Strong positive | Both increase together, close to a line | Points rise steeply left to right, tightly clustered |
| Weak positive | General upward trend, widely scattered | Points loosely rise left to right |
| No correlation | No pattern | Points randomly scattered |
| Weak negative | General downward trend, widely scattered | Points loosely fall left to right |
| Strong negative | One increases as the other decreases, close to a line | Points fall steeply, tightly clustered |

---

## Line of Best Fit — Rules

1. Draw through the **middle** of the data (equal points above and below)
2. Follows the **direction** of the trend
3. Does NOT need to pass through the **origin** or any specific data point
4. Choose two points **on the line** (not data points) to find the equation

**Finding the equation:**
Slope: m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) using two points on the line.
Then substitute one point into y = mx + b to find b.

---

## Worked Example

**Data:** Study time (h): 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 — Test scores (%): 52, 60, 68, 75, 81, 90

Line of best fit passes through approximately (1, 52) and (6, 90).

> m = (90 − 52)/(6 − 1) = 38/5 = **7.6**
>
> 52 = 7.6(1) + b → b = **44.4**
>
> Equation: y = 7.6x + 44.4

Predict score for 3.5 hours: y = 7.6(3.5) + 44.4 = **71.0%** (interpolation — within the 1–6 h range)

Predict score for 10 hours: y = 7.6(10) + 44.4 = **120.4%** — unreliable extrapolation (score > 100% is impossible)

---

## Correlation ≠ Causation

Two variables correlating strongly does NOT mean one causes the other. A third variable (confounder) often drives both. Always consider alternative explanations before claiming a causal relationship.

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Line must pass through the origin | The line of best fit can have any y-intercept |
| Line of best fit must touch the most points | Equal numbers above and below — trend matters, not touching points |
| Strong correlation proves causation | Correlation is one piece of evidence; controlled experiments are needed |
| Extrapolation is as reliable as interpolation | Extrapolation grows increasingly uncertain beyond the data range |
