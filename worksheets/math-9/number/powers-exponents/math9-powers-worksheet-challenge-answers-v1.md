**1.** Error analysis

**(a) (5x²y)³ = 5x⁶y³**

> **Error named:** Forgot to apply the exponent to the coefficient 5. The power of a product rule applies to ALL factors inside the bracket — including numerical coefficients.
>
> **Setup:** (5x²y)³ = 5³ × (x²)³ × y³ = 125 × x⁶ × y³
>
> **Final answer: 125x⁶y³**

**(b) a⁴ × a⁵ = a²⁰**

> **Error named:** Used the power-of-a-power rule (multiplied exponents) instead of the product-of-powers rule (add exponents). These are different operations.
>
> **Setup:** Same base → add exponents: a^(4+5)
>
> **Final answer: a⁹**

**(c) (4+x)³ = 64+x³**

> **Error named:** Distributed the exponent over addition. Exponents only distribute over multiplication and division — never over addition or subtraction.
>
> **What DOES distribute:** (4x)³ = 4³x³ = 64x³ (multiplication inside bracket). For addition, the expression must be expanded fully: (4+x)³ = 64 + 48x + 12x² + x³.
>
> **Final answer: Cannot be simplified by distributing — full expansion required.**

**(d) (−3)² = −9**

> **Error named:** Applied the negative sign as if the even power doesn't remove it. Any even power of a negative base is positive.
>
> **Setup:** (−3)² = (−3)×(−3). Negative × negative = positive.
>
> **Final answer: +9**

**2.** Explain the difference

**(a) 2³ × 2⁴ vs. (2³)⁴**

> **Setup for each:**
> 2³ × 2⁴: two separate groups of factors being combined → ADD exponents → 2⁷
> (2³)⁴: the power 2³ is itself raised to a power → MULTIPLY exponents → 2¹²
>
> **Why the difference:** In a product, the exponents count two separate sets of 2s joined together. In a power of a power, the outer exponent tells how many copies of the entire inner power you have — n copies of m factors each = m×n total factors.
>
> **Values: 2⁷ = 128 vs. 2¹² = 4,096**

**(b) −3⁴ vs. (−3)⁴**

> **Setup:**
> −3⁴: base is 3 (brackets absent). Exponent applies to 3 first, then negate: −(3⁴) = −81
> (−3)⁴: base is −3 (brackets enclose the negative). Exponent applies to the whole −3: positive because even exponent.
>
> **Values: −3⁴ = −81 vs. (−3)⁴ = +81**

**3.** Simplify completely — positive exponents only

**(a) (2x³y⁻²)⁴ ÷ (8x²y)²**

> **Setup — numerator:** 2⁴ × x¹² × y⁻⁸ = 16x¹²y⁻⁸
> **Setup — denominator:** 8² × x⁴ × y² = 64x⁴y²
> **Divide:** (16/64) × x^(12−4) × y^(−8−2) = (1/4)x⁸y⁻¹⁰
>
> **Final answer: x⁸/(4y¹⁰)**

**(b) [(−3a²b)³ × (ab²)⁴] ÷ (−3a⁵b⁶)**

> **Setup — (−3a²b)³:** (−3)³a⁶b³ = −27a⁶b³
> **Setup — (ab²)⁴:** a⁴b⁸
> **Product:** −27a¹⁰b¹¹
> **Divide by (−3a⁵b⁶):** (−27/−3) × a^(10−5) × b^(11−6) = 9a⁵b⁵
>
> **Final answer: 9a⁵b⁵**

**(c) (2⁻¹ + 4⁻¹)²**

> **Inside bracket first** (do NOT distribute the exponent over the sum):
> 2⁻¹ = **1/2** | 4⁻¹ = **1/4**
> Sum: 1/2 + 1/4 = **3/4**
> Squared: (3/4)² = **9/16**

**4.** 2^100 vs. 100^2

> 100² = 10,000
> 2^10 = 1,024 > 10,000? No — 2^10 < 10,000.
> But 2^13 = 8,192 and 2^14 = 16,384 > 10,000.
> So 2^100 >> 2^14 >> 10,000 = 100².
>
> **2^100 > 100²** — by an enormous margin. Exponential growth with a fixed base far outpaces polynomial growth for large enough exponents.

**5.** Find the value of n

**(a) 2ⁿ = 128**

> **Setup:** Express 128 as a power of 2: 2⁷ = 128
>
> **n = 7**

**(b) 3ⁿ × 3² = 3⁸**

> **Setup:** Product of powers → add: n + 2 = 8
>
> **n = 6**

**(c) (5ⁿ)³ = 5¹²**

> **Setup:** Power of power → multiply: 3n = 12
>
> **n = 4**

**(d) 4ⁿ = 1/16**

> **Setup:** 1/16 = 4⁻² (since 4² = 16)
>
> **n = −2**

---

**6.** Counter-argument: a⁰ = 0

> **Using the quotient rule:**
> Take any specific example: 5⁴ ÷ 5⁴ = 625 ÷ 625 = **1** (any nonzero number divided by itself equals 1).
> By the quotient rule: 5⁴ ÷ 5⁴ = 5^(4−4) = 5⁰.
> Therefore 5⁰ = 1, not 0.
>
> The student's claim "no power used means zero" misunderstands what an exponent does — an exponent counts repeated multiplication, it does not get multiplied into the base. There is no multiplication by zero anywhere in this expression.

**7.** Multi-step

**(a) (4x⁵)³ ÷ (2x²)⁴ × 2x**

> **(4x⁵)³** = 4³ × x¹⁵ = 64x¹⁵
> **(2x²)⁴** = 2⁴ × x⁸ = 16x⁸
> **÷:** 64x¹⁵ ÷ 16x⁸ = 4x⁷
> **× 2x:** 4x⁷ × 2x = **8x⁸**

**(b) [(a²b⁻¹)³]² ÷ (a³b)²**

> **Inner power:** (a²b⁻¹)³ = a⁶b⁻³
> **Outer power:** (a⁶b⁻³)² = a¹²b⁻⁶
> **Denominator:** (a³b)² = a⁶b²
> **Divide:** a^(12−6) × b^(−6−2) = a⁶b⁻⁸
>
> **Final answer: a⁶/b⁸**

**8.** Design a problem — model answer

*(Accept any expression using at least three different laws that correctly simplifies to 4x³.)*

> **Sample expression:** (2x²)³ ÷ (x⁰ × 2x³)
>
> **Simplification:**
> Numerator: (2x²)³ = 8x⁶ *(power of product)*
> Denominator: x⁰ × 2x³ = 1 × 2x³ = 2x³ *(zero exponent + product)*
> Quotient: 8x⁶ ÷ 2x³ = 4x³ *(quotient of powers)*
>
> **Laws used:** Power of a product, zero exponent, quotient of powers ✓
