## Explanation 1 — The Operations Staircase

Here is something most people have never been shown directly:

**Multiplication is shorthand for repeated addition.**

3 × 4 means "add 3, four times":
> 3 × 4 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12

**Exponentiation is shorthand for repeated multiplication.**

3⁴ means "multiply 3, four times":
> 3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81

Look at the staircase:

| Level | Operation | Written | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Repeated addition | 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 | Add 3, four times |
| 2 (shorthand) | Multiplication | 3 × 4 | Shorthand for level 1 |
| 3 | Repeated multiplication | 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 | Multiply 3, four times |
| 4 (shorthand) | Exponentiation | 3⁴ | Shorthand for level 3 |

**The key insight:** The 4 in both 3 × 4 and 3⁴ means the same thing — "four repetitions." What changes is *what you are repeating*. In 3 × 4 you repeat addition. In 3⁴ you repeat multiplication. Same count, different operation.

## Explanation 2 — The Growth Machine

This explanation gives you a reliable step-by-step procedure. Use it any time you need to evaluate a power and you lose track.

**The rule:**
1. Start at 1.
2. The base is the multiplier. The exponent is how many times you apply it.
3. Multiply step by step, keeping a running total.

**Example: Evaluate 3⁴**

| Step | Running total | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 1 | Neutral starting point |
| Apply ×3 once | 3 | This is 3¹ |
| Apply ×3 again | 9 | This is 3² |
| Apply ×3 again | 27 | This is 3³ |
| Apply ×3 again | **81** | This is 3⁴ |

**Why start at 1?** Because 1 is the identity element for multiplication — it doesn't change anything. Starting at 1 and multiplying zero times gives you 1. That is not a coincidence: it is why a⁰ = 1 for any nonzero base.

## Explanation 3 — Count the Factors

Here is the source of the confusion: the word "times." In everyday language, "3, four times" means 3 + 3 + 3 + 3. In exponents, we need a different word.

**Replace "times" with "factors."**

A **factor** is a number being multiplied. When you see:

> 3 · 3 · 3 · 3

...you have **four factors of three**. The exponent counts the factors, not the additions.

| Expression | Say this | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| 3⁴ | Three, as a factor, four times | Three times four |
| 2⁵ | Two, as a factor, five times | Two times five |

**Why this matters for the error:** 3 × 4 = 12 uses three as an *addend* four times (3+3+3+3). But 3⁴ = 81 uses three as a *factor* four times (3·3·3·3). The 4 has the same meaning in both — but the question is: factor of what kind of operation?
