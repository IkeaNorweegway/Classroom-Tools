## Answer Key

<!-- Teacher-facing only. One section per ticket. -->

**Ticket 1 — Engaged Citizenship**

1. Awareness → Decision-making → Action. Mark as correct if all three appear, in order, in the student's own words.
2. Noticing the bus is late = **awareness**. Deciding the schedule needs to change = **decision-making**. Award 1 mark for each correct match.
3. Full 3 marks require: naming that awareness is only the first stage; noting that decision-making and action are still required; and giving a reason why noticing alone does not change anything (nothing has actually been done yet). Award 1 mark for identifying awareness as insufficient, 1 mark for naming what's missing, 1 mark for a clear explanation tying it back to the framework.

**Ticket 2 — Forms of Civic Engagement**

1. Any one of: volunteering, community initiatives, rallies/protests, advocacy, ethical purchasing, political party membership, election volunteering. (Not voting.)
2. Any two of the forms listed above, applied plausibly to noise pollution (e.g., advocacy — writing to the school; a petition/community initiative; a rally). 1 mark each.
3. Full marks require naming two specific forms (not "do stuff") and explicitly stating that neither requires being 18. Award 1 mark per correctly used form (2 marks) + 1 mark for directly refuting the "have to be an adult" claim.

**Ticket 3 — Voting and Voter Eligibility**

1. Any one of: Canadian citizen; at least 18 years old; valid proof of residence in the riding; valid proof of identity.
2. No — they do not have valid proof of residence for their current (new) riding. Even though they are a citizen and old enough, all four requirements must be met, including residence proof matching the riding they are voting in.
3. Full marks require: stating that all four requirements must be met simultaneously with no exceptions; and using a concrete example (e.g., someone missing only one requirement, such as age or residence, still cannot vote) to show why "most" is not enough.

**Ticket 4 — Global Issues Affecting Canada**

1. Any one of: economic disruption, poverty, drought and famine, human migration, civil unrest, war.
2. Step 1: the smaller harvest reduces global wheat supply, raising the global price. Step 2: Canadian stores pay more for wheat/flour, so bread and other wheat products cost more for Canadian shoppers. Award 1 mark per correct step.
3. Full marks require a specific example (e.g., the 1979 Vietnamese refugee resettlement, or the wheat/bread price chain) and an explanation of how it created a real, traceable effect on people or prices in Canada — not just a general assertion that global issues "matter."
