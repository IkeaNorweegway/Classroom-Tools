## Answer Key

<!-- Teacher-facing only. One section per ticket. -->

**Ticket 1 — Types of Political Systems**

1. Dictatorship → Oligarchy → Monarchy → Democracy → Anarchy (accept order from either end, as long as the direction is stated).
2. Oligarchy. A small group (the generals), not one person and not the whole population, holds power without elections.
3. 3 marks: 1 = states anarchy means no central authority; 2 = notes freedom can increase without government; 3 = explains that without government, there is no one to guarantee safety, resolve disputes, or protect rights, so it can be unstable or unsafe despite the lack of control.

**Ticket 2 — Models of Democracy**

1. Direct democracy: citizens vote on laws themselves. Representative democracy: citizens elect people to vote on their behalf.
2. Representative — gathering 2 million people to vote directly on every bylaw is not realistic; elected representatives make it manageable. Award 1 mark for choosing representative, 1 for the population-based reasoning.
3. 3 marks: 1 = identifies the claim confuses direct and representative democracy; 2 = states representative democracy is still a genuine form of democracy; 3 = explains why representative democracy is the realistic choice for larger populations, so not using direct democracy doesn't disqualify a country from being a democracy.

**Ticket 3 — Parliamentary vs. Presidential Democracies**

1. Any one parliamentary term (caucus, backbencher, frontbencher, whip, Leader of the Opposition, shadow cabinet, parliamentary secretary) and any one presidential term (Congress, veto, elected Senate, elected judiciary).
2. Presidential. Direct nationwide election of the head of government and a fixed term are defining presidential features.
3. 3 marks: 1 = PM is chosen through party leadership and seat count, not a nationwide vote; 2 = PM can lose power if the party/legislature loses confidence, at any time; 3 = President is elected directly for a fixed term and normally serves the full term regardless of popularity.

**Ticket 4 — Canada's Political System**

1. Executive, legislative, judicial.
2. Correction: You cannot vote for the Prime Minister directly — you vote for your local MP in your riding. The PM is the leader of whichever party wins the most seats. Award 1 mark for stating you vote for the local MP, 1 mark for explaining how the PM is determined.
3. 3 marks: 1 = states the GG's role is mostly ceremonial (Royal Assent, opening Parliament); 2 = notes the GG acts on the advice of the PM/Cabinet by convention; 3 = explains why this looks like power (ceremonial visibility) but is not the same as holding real day-to-day political power.

**Ticket 5 — Political Parties and Party Systems**

1. Government = party with the most seats, forms the executive. Opposition = next-largest party, whose job is to question/challenge government decisions.
2. One-party: China. Two-party: United States. (Accept other accurate real-world examples if taught.)
3. 3 marks: 1 = states more parties is not automatically better; 2 = explains that more parties can mean no majority, requiring coalitions/slower decisions; 3 = notes two-party systems trade voter choice for faster decision-making — both are trade-offs, not a simple "better/worse."

**Ticket 6 — Canada's/Alberta's Parties and Party Membership**

1. Any one federal party (Liberal, Conservative, NDP, Green, Bloc Québécois) and any one Alberta party (UCP, NDP).
2. Any two of: local candidate selection, party priorities/policy direction, party leader.
3. A riding association is a party's local organization in a specific riding; it is run locally by a President and a Board of Directors, elected by the local party members.
