# Political Systems — Mid-Unit Quiz — Answer Key
Teacher use only · Keep off student copies

1. **Dictatorship.** (1 mark)
2. In a democracy, citizens elect their leaders in free elections; in an oligarchy, a small group holds the power, usually without free elections. (2 marks: one each)
3. The governor general appoints the leader of the party that has the confidence of the House of Commons, usually the party leader who won the most seats. (1 mark)
4. Voters elect MPs; the party with the most seats (or the confidence of the House) usually forms government and its leader becomes prime minister. (2 marks)
5. Any one: Canada has a prime minister who must keep the confidence of Parliament; the US has a separately elected president; US has a separation of powers. (2 marks)
6. The prime minister depends on the confidence of the House; if the House loses confidence the government falls. The US president is elected for a fixed term and can be removed only by impeachment. (3 marks: confidence, fixed term, impeachment)
7. Any one: choose candidates, develop policies, organize elections, represent voters. (1 mark)
8. Parties give voters clear choices, organize governments and opposition, develop policies, and hold leaders to account. (4 marks: each function, plus conclusion)

**Common errors to watch for:** "Canadians directly elect the prime minister" (Q3); "parties are the same as government" (Q7).
