## Answer Key

<!-- Teacher-facing only. One section per ticket. -->

**Ticket 1 — Scarcity and the Three Big Questions**

1. Scarcity is the problem that resources (land, labour, capital, time, money) are limited, so no society can produce everything everyone wants. (1 mark for a definition that includes "limited resources.")
2. What to produce, how to produce it, and for whom to produce it. (1 mark each, up to 2 — award both marks only if all three are correctly identified across the response, or 1 mark for two of three.)
3. 3 marks: 1 = names the opportunity cost correctly (the family vacation), 1 = correctly connects the decision to the "what to produce" question (or "for whom," if reasoned), 1 = explanation shows understanding that choosing one option means giving up the other, not just restating the scenario.

**Ticket 2 — Types of Economic Systems and the Economic Spectrum**

1. Traditional, command, market, and mixed. (1 mark for all four; accept partial credit at teacher discretion.)
2. Market economy: individuals and businesses (through buying, selling, and competition). Command economy: the government. (1 mark each.)
3. 3 marks: 1 = correctly states most real economies are mixed, not purely one type; 1 = gives a real or plausible example of government involvement in Canada (public healthcare, education, etc.); 1 = correctly uses "economic spectrum" to explain that countries sit at different points along a line rather than in one of two boxes.

**Ticket 3 — Measuring an Economy**

1. GDP measures the total dollar value of all goods and services a country produces in one year. (1 mark)
2. Prices are rising (inflation); this is called inflation. (1 mark for "prices rising," 1 mark for naming "inflation.")
3. 3 marks: 1 = recognizes GDP alone doesn't capture unemployment; 1 = explains that rising unemployment alongside GDP growth may mean the benefits aren't reaching everyone (or fewer people are working even as output grows); 1 = concludes that multiple measures are needed together for a complete picture.

**Ticket 4 — Government Revenue and Spending**

1. Taxes / taxation. (1 mark)
2. Any two of: healthcare, education, infrastructure, social programs, public safety. (1 mark each, up to 2.)
3. 3 marks: 1 = states the claim is inaccurate; 1 = names at least one other funded category tax dollars pay for; 1 = explains that taxes fund a broad basket of services, not just one visible thing.

**Ticket 5 — Surplus, Deficit, and Debt**

1. A deficit is the gap in one year when spending is greater than revenue. Debt is the total accumulated amount owed, built up from multiple years of deficits (minus any surpluses). (1 mark for correctly distinguishing both.)
2. $5 billion. (2 marks: 1 for correct calculation, 1 for correct units/direction — spending exceeds revenue.)
3. 3 marks: 1 = recognizes a deficit is not automatically bad management; 1 = connects the reasoning to the emergency circumstances (pandemic) and what the money was used for; 1 = shows understanding that intent and circumstances matter in judging a deficit, not just its existence.
