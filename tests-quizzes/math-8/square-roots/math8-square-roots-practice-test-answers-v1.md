## Part B — Show Your Work

**9.** (a) A 4 by 4 square of tiles. (b) **16** (c) **No.** 4 × 2 = 8. 4² means 4 × 4 = 16.
- 1 mark each

**10.** (a) **1 × 18, 2 × 9, 3 × 6** (b) **No.** None of the rectangles has equal sides, so 18 tiles can't make a square. (4 × 4 = 16 and 5 × 5 = 25.)
- (a) 2 marks: all three (1 mark for two)
- (b) 1 mark: no, with a reason

**11.** Factor pairs of 144 include 12 × 12 (or 144 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = (2 × 2 × 3) × (2 × 2 × 3)). **√144 = 12.** Check: 12 × 12 = 144.
- 1 mark: factor pairs or prime factors
- 1 mark: 12
- 1 mark: check by multiplying

**12.** 18 × 18 = 324, not 36, so 18 is wrong. **√36 = 6.** Check: 6 × 6 = 36. A square root is not half.
- 1 mark each part

**13.** √10 ≈ 3.2 (just past 3). √30 ≈ 5.5 (between 5 and 6). √60 ≈ 7.7 (between 7 and 8, closer to 8).
- 1 mark each: in the right whole-number gap and labelled

**14.** 25 < 27 < 36, so √27 is between 5 and 6, close to 5. Estimate **5.2**. Check: 5.2 × 5.2 = 27.04.
- 1 mark: 25 and 36
- 1 mark: 5.2 (accept 5.1 or 5.3 with a check)
- 1 mark: checks by squaring

**15.** Each side of the poster is √196 = **14 cm**. 14 cm is less than 15 cm, so **yes, it fits**.
- 1 mark: √196
- 1 mark: 14 cm
- 1 mark: yes, comparing 14 with 15

(b) 49 < 50 < 64, so the side is between 7 and 8, very close to 7. About **7.1 m**.

(c) Side = √36 = **6 m**. Around it: 4 × 6 = **24 m** of wood.

## Part D — Explain Your Thinking

**17.** Model answer: Omar is not right. Some numbers have whole-number square roots: √25 = 5. But 20 is not a perfect square. 4 × 4 = 16 and 5 × 5 = 25, so √20 is between 4 and 5. It is not a whole number.
