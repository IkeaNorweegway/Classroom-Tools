### [SD1 · 2] Are these rectangles similar? Show your work.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;5,2;0,2" sides="5 cm;2 cm;;" pts2="0,0;7.5,0;7.5,3;0,3" sides2="7.5 cm;3 cm;;" unit=16
(lines 2)
=> 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 and 7.5 ÷ 3 = 2.5 (1 mark). Same ratio, so similar (1 mark).

### [SD1 · 2] Which pair is always similar: two rectangles, two circles, or two triangles? Explain.
(lines 2)
=> Two circles (1 mark). Every circle has the same shape; only the size changes (1 mark).

### [SD1 · 3] △ABC ~ △PQR. Match each angle and name the side that matches BC.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;4,0;1,3" names="A,B,C" arcs="1,2,3" pts2="0,0;0,6;-4.5,1.5" names2="P,Q,R" arcs2="1,2,3" unit=16
(lines 2)
=> ∠A ↔ ∠P, ∠B ↔ ∠Q, ∠C ↔ ∠R (2 marks; 1 mark for two correct). BC ↔ QR (1 mark).

### [SD2 · 2] A drawing of a beetle is 6 cm long. The real beetle is 1.5 cm long. Find k. Is the drawing an enlargement or a reduction?
(lines 2)
=> k = 6 ÷ 1.5 = 4 (1 mark). k > 1, so an enlargement (1 mark).

### [SD2 · 3] △DEF ~ △ABC with k = 0.5. AB = 10 cm and EF = 3.5 cm. Find DE and BC.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;10,0;4.5,4.33" names="A,B,C" sides="10 cm;;" pts2="0,0;5,0;2.25,2.17" names2="D,E,F" unit=12
(lines 3)
=> DE = 10 × 0.5 = 5 cm (1 mark). BC = 3.5 ÷ 0.5 = 7 cm (2 marks: 1 for dividing by k).

### [SD2 · 3] Are these triangles similar? Use scale factors to decide.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;0,4" right="1" sides="6 m;;4 m" pts2="0,0;9,0;0,5" right2="1" sides2="9 m;;5 m" unit=14
(lines 2)
=> 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 (1 mark). 5 ÷ 4 = 1.25 (1 mark). Different, so not similar (1 mark).

### [SD3 · 3] A square tile has sides 10 cm. A similar tile has sides 30 cm. How many times bigger is its area? Show two ways.
@viz squares shapes="1x1;3x3" labels="10 cm tile;30 cm tile"
(lines 3)
=> Areas: 100 cm² and 900 cm², and 900 ÷ 100 = 9 (1 mark). k = 3, k² = 9 (1 mark). Answer: 9 times (1 mark).

### [SD3 · 2] A trail map of Elk Island Park uses 1 cm : 1.5 km. A trail is 8 cm on the map. How long is the real trail?
(lines 1)
=> 8 × 1.5 = 12 km (2 marks; 1 mark for 8 × 1.5 with an arithmetic slip).

### [SD3 · 2] A blueprint uses 1 cm : 2 m. A deck is 4 cm by 3 cm on it. Find the real area of the deck.
(lines 2)
=> Real sides 8 m and 6 m (1 mark). Area 48 m² (1 mark).

### [SD3 · 2] Lin says doubling the sides of a garden doubles its area. Explain her mistake.
(lines 3)
=> Length and width both double, so the area is 2 × 2 = 4 times as big (1 mark). Example: 2 m by 3 m is 6 m²; 4 m by 6 m is 24 m² (1 mark).
