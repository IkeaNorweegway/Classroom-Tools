## Part A — Choose the Answer
Circle the best answer. 1 mark each.

### [SD1 · 1] Two shapes are similar. Which must be true?
- A) They are the same size.
- B) Their matching sides are equal.
- C) Their matching angles are equal.
- D) Their areas are equal.
=> **C.** Similar shapes have the same shape: matching angles are equal and every side is multiplied by the same k.
=> Common mistakes: A, B and D describe shapes that are the same size. Similar shapes can be different sizes.

### [SD1 · 1] Which pair is **not** always similar?
- A) two squares
- B) two circles
- C) two equilateral triangles
- D) two rhombuses
=> **D.** Two rhombuses can have different angles, so they can have different shapes.
=> Common mistakes: A, B and C always have the same shape. Only the size changes.

### [SD2 · 1] A photo is 4 cm by 6 cm. A copy is 10 cm by 15 cm. What is the scale factor from the photo to the copy?
- A) 2.5
- B) 6
- C) 0.4
- D) 9
=> **A.** 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 and 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5.
=> Common mistakes: B subtracts (10 − 4). C divides the wrong way (4 ÷ 10). D subtracts (15 − 6).

### [SD2 · 1] Which scale factor makes a reduction?
- A) 0.6
- B) 1
- C) 1.5
- D) 6
=> **A.** A scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the copy smaller.
=> Common mistakes: B keeps the same size. C and D make the copy bigger.

### [SD3 · 1] A square has sides of 3 cm. It is enlarged with k = 2. What is the area of the new square?
- A) 18 cm²
- B) 36 cm²
- C) 12 cm²
- D) 6 cm²
=> **B.** The new sides are 6 cm, so the area is 6 × 6 = 36 cm². That is 2² = 4 times the old area of 9 cm².
=> Common mistakes: A doubles the area. C is the old area plus 3. D is the new side length, not an area.

### [SD3 · 1] A map uses the scale 1 cm : 4 km. Two towns are 18 km apart. How far apart are they on the map?
- A) 72 cm
- B) 22 cm
- C) 4.5 cm
- D) 14 cm
=> **C.** Every 4 km is 1 cm. 18 ÷ 4 = 4.5 cm.
=> Common mistakes: A multiplies 18 × 4. B adds. D subtracts.

### [SD4 · 1] Dilate the point (4, −2) about (0, 0) with k = 3. Where is the image?
- A) (7, 1)
- B) (12, −6)
- C) (12, −2)
- D) ({4/3}, −{2/3})
=> **B.** Multiply both coordinates by 3: 4 × 3 = 12 and −2 × 3 = −6.
=> Common mistakes: A adds 3. C multiplies only the x. D divides by 3.

### [SD4 · 1] A dilation about (0, 0) takes A(2, 1) to A′(10, 5). What is k?
- A) 8
- B) {1/5}
- C) 4
- D) 5
=> **D.** 10 ÷ 2 = 5 and 5 ÷ 1 = 5. Image ÷ original.
=> Common mistakes: A subtracts the x's. B divides the wrong way. C subtracts the y's.

## Part B — Show Your Work
Show your steps.

### [SD1 · 3] A rectangle is 3 cm by 5 cm. a) Give the sides of a similar rectangle that is bigger. b) Give the sides of a similar rectangle that is smaller. c) Mia adds 2 cm to each side and gets 5 cm by 7 cm. She says it is similar. Explain why it is not.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;5,3;0,3" sides="5 cm;3 cm;;" cap="Original" pts2="0,0;7,0;7,5;0,5" sides2="7 cm;5 cm;;" cap2="Mia's rectangle" unit=16
(lines 3)
=> a) Answers vary. Multiply both sides by the same k > 1. For example, k = 2 gives 6 cm by 10 cm (1 mark).
=> b) Answers vary. Multiply both sides by the same k between 0 and 1. For example, k = 0.5 gives 1.5 cm by 2.5 cm (1 mark).
=> c) 5 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.67 but 7 ÷ 5 = 1.4. The sides are not multiplied by the same number, so the shape changes. Adding does not keep the shape (1 mark).

### [SD1 · 3] Every side of the rhombus doubles to make the square. Kai says the two shapes are similar. Is he right? Explain.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;4,0;6,3.46;2,3.46" sides="4 cm;4 cm;4 cm;4 cm" angles="60°;120°;60°;120°" cap="Rhombus" pts2="0,0;8,0;8,8;0,8" sides2="8 cm;8 cm;8 cm;8 cm" angles2="90°;90°;90°;90°" cap2="Square" unit=24
(lines 3)
=> Every side is multiplied by 2: 8 ÷ 4 = 2 (1 mark).
=> The angles do not match: 60° and 120° in the rhombus, 90° in the square (1 mark).
=> Kai is wrong. Similar shapes need matching angles equal AND sides multiplied by the same k. Here only the sides work (1 mark).

### [SD2 · 3] △ABC ~ △DEF. a) Find the scale factor from △ABC to △DEF. b) Find EF. c) Find FD.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;8.25,8.71" names="A,B,C" sides="6 cm;9 cm;12 cm" pts2="0,0;4,0;5.5,5.81" names2="D,E,F" sides2="4 cm;EF = ?;FD = ?" unit=11
(work 2)
=> a) DE matches AB: k = 4 ÷ 6 = {2/3} (1 mark).
=> b) EF = 9 × {2/3} = 6 cm (1 mark).
=> c) FD = 12 × {2/3} = 8 cm (1 mark).

### [SD2 · 3] A Calgary Stampede poster is 60 cm wide and 90 cm tall. A similar postcard is 12 cm wide. a) Find the scale factor from the poster to the postcard. b) Find the postcard's height. c) Is the postcard an enlargement or a reduction? How does k tell you?
(work 2)
=> a) k = 12 ÷ 60 = 0.2 (1 mark).
=> b) 90 × 0.2 = 18 cm (1 mark).
=> c) A reduction, because k is between 0 and 1 (1 mark).

### [SD3 · 3] A triangle has an area of 8 cm². A similar triangle has every side 3 times as long. Sam says its area is 24 cm². Find the right area. Explain Sam's mistake.
(lines 3)
=> Area grows by k² = 3² = 9 (1 mark).
=> 8 × 9 = 72 cm² (1 mark).
=> Sam multiplied by k, not k². Area has two directions (length and width), and both grow 3 times: 3 × 3 = 9 (1 mark).

### [SD3 · 3] The map shows Red Deer and Lacombe. a) Find the real distance. b) Ponoka is 40 km from Red Deer. How far apart are they on this map? c) A second map uses 1 cm : 10 km. How far apart are Red Deer and Lacombe on the second map?
@viz sdmap a="Red Deer" b="Lacombe" len=5 angle=70 dist="5 cm" scale="1 cm : 5 km" bar="0;5 km;10 km;15 km"
(work 2)
=> a) 5 × 5 = 25 km (1 mark).
=> b) 40 ÷ 5 = 8 cm (1 mark).
=> c) 25 ÷ 10 = 2.5 cm. The gap is shorter on the second map (1 mark).

### [SD4 · 3] Dilate △DEF about (0, 0) with k = 1.5. Write D′, E′ and F′. Then draw the image.
@viz sddilate pts="2,2;4,2;2,6" k=1.5 names="D,E,F" image=key rays=1 range=10
(lines 2)
=> D′(3, 3) (1 mark).
=> E′(6, 3) (1 mark).
=> F′(3, 9) (1 mark).

## Part C — Put It Together
A game designer draws a logo on a grid. It is rectangle ABCD with A(1, 1), B(4, 1), C(4, 3) and D(1, 3). She dilates it about (0, 0) with k = 2 to make a banner.
@viz sddilate pts="1,1;4,1;4,3;1,3" k=2 names="A,B,C,D" image=key rays=1 range=8

### [SD4 · 2] (a) Write the vertices of the banner A′B′C′D′.
(lines 1)
=> A′(2, 2) and B′(8, 2) (1 mark).
=> C′(8, 6) and D′(2, 6) (1 mark).

### [SD2 · 2] (b) Find the length and width of ABCD and of A′B′C′D′. Show that both sides use the same scale factor.
(work 2)
=> ABCD is 3 by 2. A′B′C′D′ is 6 by 4 (1 mark).
=> 6 ÷ 3 = 2 and 4 ÷ 2 = 2. Both sides use k = 2 (1 mark).

### [SD3 · 2] (c) Find the area of each rectangle. How many times bigger is the banner's area?
(work 2)
=> ABCD: 3 × 2 = 6 square units. A′B′C′D′: 6 × 4 = 24 square units (1 mark).
=> 24 ÷ 6 = 4 times bigger. That is k² = 2² = 4 (1 mark).

### [SD4 · 2] (d) For a small icon, she dilates ABCD about (0, 0) with k = {1/2} instead. Write the icon's vertices. Is the icon an enlargement or a reduction?
(lines 2)
=> (0.5, 0.5), (2, 0.5), (2, 1.5) and (0.5, 1.5) (1 mark).
=> A reduction, because k = {1/2} is less than 1 (1 mark).

## Part D — Explain Your Thinking

### [SD4 · 3] Ella dilates a triangle about (0, 0). The point (4, 6) moves to (2, 3). She says k = 2 because she divided each coordinate by 2. Is she right? Find k and say if the image is an enlargement or a reduction. Explain.
(lines 4)
=> Model answer: No. k is image ÷ original: 2 ÷ 4 = {1/2} and 3 ÷ 6 = {1/2}, so k = {1/2}. Dividing by 2 is the same as multiplying by {1/2}. With k = 2 the image would be (8, 12). Since k is less than 1, the image is a reduction: it is closer to (0, 0).
