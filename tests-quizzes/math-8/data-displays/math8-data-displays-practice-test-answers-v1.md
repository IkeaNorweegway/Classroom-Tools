## Part B — Show Your Work

**9.** (a) 12 + 8 + 4 + 6 = **30** (b) 12 − 4 = **8** (c) 8/30 = **4/15**
- 1 mark each

**10.** (a) 25% of $2000 = **$500** (b) 10% + 10% = 20% of $2000 = **$400** (c) The data are parts of one whole budget (they add to 100%).
- 1 mark each

**11.** (a) **Bar graph or pictograph** (compare weeks), or a line graph (change over time). (b) **Circle graph**: parts of one whole day. (c) **Line graph**: change over time.
- 1 mark each (a sensible graph with a reason)

**12.** (a) **Graph 1** (b) In Graph 1, Team A's bar is 7 units above the axis start and Team B's is 3, so it looks about **2 times** as tall (accept 2 to 2.5). (c) **Graph 2**: its axis starts at 0, so the bars show that 52 and 48 are close.
- 1 mark each

**13.** The bag is twice as tall and twice as wide, so it looks **4 times** as big, not 2 times. Fix it by drawing two bags of the same size for 2024 (or one bag the same size plus a bar graph), so the picture shows double.
- 1 mark: says it looks 4 times as big
- 1 mark: explains why (both height and width doubled)
- 1 mark: a fix

**14.** **Not supported**: Jo practised less on Wednesday (15 min) than on Tuesday (25 min). A supported conclusion: "Jo practised the most on Friday" or "Jo's practice went up overall from Monday to Friday, with a dip on Wednesday."
- 1 mark: not supported
- 1 mark: points to Wednesday
- 1 mark: a supported conclusion

**15.** The graph only shows June to August, the summer months. You can't tell what happens in the other 9 months; visitors probably drop in the fall. The conclusion goes beyond the data.
- 1 mark: notes only 3 months are shown
- 1 mark: says the rest of the year is unknown
- 1 mark: says the conclusion is not supported

## Part C — Put It Together

**16.** (a) A **line graph** (or bar graph): the data shows change over time (years).
- 1 mark: graph type
- 1 mark: reason

(b) Years on the horizontal axis, tonnes (0 to 200) on the vertical axis, five points or bars at 150, 160, 120, 165, 170, with labels.
- 1 mark: correct values
- 1 mark: axes labelled

(c) Starting at 110 makes the small rise from 150 to 170 look huge. It also makes the 2021 drop look huge. The real change from 2019 to 2023 is only 20 tonnes, about 13%.
- 1 mark: names the cut-off axis
- 1 mark: explains the effect

(d) **Not supported**: the harvest fell in 2021 (120 tonnes). A supported conclusion: "The harvest was highest in 2023" or "Apart from 2021, the harvest went up."
- 1 mark: not supported, with the 2021 drop
- 1 mark: a supported conclusion

## Part D — Explain Your Thinking

**17.** Model answer: Raj is not right. A circle graph shows how one whole is split into parts that add to 100%, at one time. Temperatures over a year are not parts of a whole, and they change over time. A **line graph** is better: it shows how the temperature goes up and down from month to month.
