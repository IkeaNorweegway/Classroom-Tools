## Section 1: Four kinds of graphs
Target: I can say what each kind of graph shows best. (DD1)
### Match: bar, line, circle. Which one shows change over time? Which shows parts of a whole? Which compares groups?
(lines 2)
=> Line: change over time. Circle: parts of a whole. Bar: compare groups.

## Section 2: Choosing a graph
Target: I can choose the best graph and explain why. (DD2)
### You surveyed 30 students about their favourite season. Which graph would you use to show the percent who chose each season? Why?
(lines 2)
=> A circle graph: it shows each season as a part (percent) of the whole class.

## Section 3: Misleading graphs
Target: I can explain how a graph can mislead. (DD3)
### Why is this graph misleading?
@viz bar data="Mon:61;Tue:63" ymin=60 ymax=64 break=1
=> The axis starts at 60, so a 2-point difference looks like Tuesday is 3 times bigger.

## Section 4: Checking conclusions
Target: I can check if a conclusion matches the data. (DD4)
### A graph shows 45% of students like pizza best. Someone says, "Most students like pizza best." Is that supported? Explain.
(lines 2)
=> No. 45% is less than half, so it is not "most."
