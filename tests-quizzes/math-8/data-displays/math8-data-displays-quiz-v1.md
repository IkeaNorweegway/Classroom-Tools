### [DD1 · 2] Name the kind of graph you would see in each case: a) a budget split into parts; b) a temperature through the day.
(lines 1)
=> a) circle graph b) line graph

### [DD1 · 2] In a pictograph, one picture = 6 people. How many people do 3 and a half pictures stand for?
(lines 1)
=> 21

### [DD2 · 2] Give one strength and one limitation of a circle graph.
(lines 2)
=> Strength: shows each part of a whole. Limitation: hard to compare small slices / does not show change over time / no exact counts unless labelled.

### [DD2 · 2] Choose a graph for "goals scored by each player on a team" and explain your choice.
(lines 2)
=> A bar graph, because it compares separate groups (players) and shows exact amounts.

### [DD3 · 4] Look at the graph.
@viz bar data="Brand X:89;Brand Y:86" ymin=85 ymax=90 break=1 title="Battery life (hours)"
a) Brand X's bar looks how many times as tall as Brand Y's? b) What is the real difference? c) How would you fix the graph?
(work 3)
=> a) About 4 times (1). b) 3 hours, about 3.5% (1). c) Start the y-axis at 0 (2).

### [DD3 · 2] Explain why making a picture twice as tall and twice as wide is misleading.
(lines 2)
=> Its area becomes 4 times as big, so it looks like 4 times as much, not 2 times.

### [DD4 · 4] Runs scored by a team in four games: 3, 8, 2, 7. a) Is "The team scores more every game" supported? b) Write a conclusion that is supported.
(work 3)
=> a) No (1); it dropped from 8 to 2 (1). b) Any supported statement, e.g. "The most runs were scored in game 2" (2).
