## Part B — Show Your Work

**9.** (a) P(red) = **1/2** (b) P(not green) = **3/4** (c) P(yellow) = **0**: there is no yellow, so it is impossible.
- 1 mark each

**10.** 6/20 = **3/10** = **0.3** = **30%**
- 1 mark each

**11.** (a)

| | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red | R1 | R2 | R3 |
| Blue | B1 | B2 | B3 |

(b) **6** outcomes; multiply 2 × 3 = 6. (c) Blue and odd: B1, B3, so **2/6 = 1/3**.
- 1 mark each

**12.** Each roll is independent. The die has no memory, so three 6s in a row do not change the next roll. Ava is wrong: 6 is not more likely. Zoe is wrong: 6 is not less likely. P(6) = **1/6**.
- 1 mark: each roll is independent (the die has no memory)
- 1 mark: explains why both are wrong
- 1 mark: P(6) = 1/6

**13.** (a) P(tails) = 1/2. P(greater than 4) = 2/6 = 1/3 (5 or 6). P(tails and greater than 4) = 1/2 × 1/3 = **1/6**.
- 1 mark: P(greater than 4) = 2/6
- 1 mark: multiplies to get 1/6

(b) "And" must be less likely than each event on its own. 5/6 is bigger than both 1/2 and 2/6, so it is too big. (Or: a table has 12 outcomes and only 2 are tails with 5 or 6.)
- 1 mark

**14.** (a) 1/10 × 1/10 = **1/100**
- 1 mark

(b) P(on time) = 9/10 each day. 9/10 × 9/10 = **81/100** (81%)
- 1 mark: P(on time) = 9/10
- 1 mark: 81/100

**15.** (a) 14/60 = **7/30** (b) **1/6**; expect **10** sixes in 60 rolls (c) It should get closer to 1/6 (about 1000 sixes), because more trials get closer to the theoretical probability.
- 1 mark each

## Part C — Put It Together

**16.** (a) A tree or table with 4 × 3 = **12** outcomes (each symbol paired with prize, blank, blank). Accept the cards as "prize, blank 1, blank 2".
- 1 mark: a full tree or table
- 1 mark: 12 outcomes

(b) P(star) = 1/4, P(prize) = 1/3. P(win) = 1/4 × 1/3 = **1/12**. The tree shows 1 winning outcome out of 12.
- 1 mark: multiply rule
- 1 mark: checks with the tree or table

(c) 1/12 × 120 = **10** winners.
- 1 mark: method
- 1 mark: 10

(d) **No.** 15/120 = 1/8 is the experimental probability. It is close to the theoretical 1/12, but results vary. 120 games is not a lot of trials, so 15 winners instead of 10 can happen by chance.
- 1 mark: names experimental vs theoretical
- 1 mark: explains that small samples vary

## Part D — Explain Your Thinking

**17.** Model answer: Theo added, but for "and" with independent events you multiply. The right answer is 1/2 × 1/6 = **1/12**. An outcome table for a coin and a die has 2 × 6 = 12 cells, and only one cell is heads and 6. So the answer is 1 out of 12. (Adding gives 4/6, which is bigger than P(heads), and that can't be right.)
