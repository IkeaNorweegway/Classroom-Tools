### [PB1 · 2] A bag has 5 blue and 15 red cubes. Find P(blue) as a fraction, a decimal and a percent.
(lines 1)
=> 5/20 = 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%

### [PB1 · 2] On a die, find P(a number less than 5).
(lines 1)
=> 4/6 = 2/3

### [PB2 · 2] How many outcomes are there when you roll a die and spin a 5-part spinner? Explain how you know without drawing.
(lines 2)
=> 6 × 5 = 30 outcomes.

### [PB2 · 2] Liam flipped heads 6 times in a row. He says the next flip will probably be tails. Explain why he is wrong.
(lines 2)
=> The flips are independent. P(tails) is still 1/2.

### [PB3 · 2] Find P(heads and a 3) when you flip a coin and roll a die.
(work 1)
=> 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12

### [PB3 · 4] A spinner is 1/3 blue. You spin it twice. Find P(blue, blue) with the multiply rule. Then check with a tree or a table.
(work 4)
=> 1/3 × 1/3 = 1/9 (2). Tree or table with 9 outcomes, 1 is blue-blue (2).

### [PB4 · 4] A class spun a spinner 50 times and got red 18 times. The spinner has 4 equal parts, 1 of them red. a) Write the experimental and theoretical P(red). b) Why are they different? c) How could the class get closer to the theory?
(work 3)
=> a) 18/50 = 36%; 1/4 = 25% (2). b) 50 spins is a small number of trials; results vary (1). c) Spin many more times (1).
