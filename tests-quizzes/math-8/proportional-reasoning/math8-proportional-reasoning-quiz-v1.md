### [PR1 · 2] Kai has 18 cards. Noor has 6. Compare them both ways.
@viz prtimes rows="Noor:1;Kai:3" value="6"
(lines 2)
=> 18 ÷ 6 = 3: Kai has 3 times as many (1 mark). 6 ÷ 18 = {1/3}: Noor has {1/3} as many (1 mark).

### [PR1 · 2] A farm has 12 ha of canola and 30 ha of wheat. How many times as much wheat is there? What decimal of the wheat is the canola?
(lines 2)
=> 30 ÷ 12 = 2.5 times as much wheat (1 mark). 12 ÷ 30 = 0.4 (1 mark).

### [PR1 · 2] Leo has 16 cards. Mo has 0.75 times as many. How many cards does Mo have?
(lines 1)
=> 0.75 × 16 (1 mark) = 12 cards (1 mark).

### [PR1 · 2] Jay says 25 is 20 times as many as 5. Use the blocks to explain his mistake.
@viz prtimes rows="Five:1;Twenty-five:5" value="5"
(lines 2)
=> 20 is how many more: 25 − 5 (1 mark). 25 ÷ 5 = 5, so 25 is 5 times as many as 5 (1 mark).

### [PR2 · 2] Find k for each ratio. a) 2 : 7 b) 7 : 2
(lines 1)
=> a) 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5 (1 mark). b) 2 ÷ 7 = {2/7} (1 mark; about 0.29).

### [PR2 · 3] Look at the ratio table. a) Find k. b) Show k is the same in every column. c) Write one more ratio with this k.
@viz prk top="3,6,9" bottom="4.5,9,13.5" toplabel="first (a)" bottomlabel="second (b)"
(lines 2)
=> a) k = 4.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5 (1 mark).
=> b) 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 and 13.5 ÷ 9 = 1.5 (1 mark).
=> c) Any a : 1.5a, such as 10 : 15 or 2 : 3 (1 mark).

### [PR2 · 2] 40 L of gas costs $56.00. Find k for litres : dollars. What does k tell you?
@viz prk top="1,40" bottom="?,$56.00" toplabel="gas (L)" bottomlabel="cost ($)"
(lines 2)
=> k = 56 ÷ 40 = 1.4 (1 mark). It is the unit rate: $1.40 for each litre (1 mark).

### [PR2 · 3] Which ratios are equivalent to 4 : 5? 8 : 10, 12 : 16, 2 : 2.5, 6 : 7
(lines 2)
=> k for 4 : 5 is 5 ÷ 4 = 1.25 (1 mark).
=> 8 : 10 (k = 1.25) and 2 : 2.5 (k = 1.25) are equivalent (1 mark each). 12 : 16 has k ≈ 1.33 and 6 : 7 has k ≈ 1.17.

### [PR2 · 2] Kim says the ratio water : rice = 6 : 3 has k = 2. Explain her mistake and give the right k.
(lines 2)
=> k = second ÷ first, so the order matters (1 mark). k = 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5 (1 mark).

### [PR1 · 2] Explain your thinking. Ava has 3 times as many cards as Ben. Ben says, "Then I have {1/3} as many as Ava." Is Ben right? How do you know?
(lines 3)
=> Yes (1 mark). Going back undoes × 3, so divide by 3: Ben has {1/3} as many. Example: 12 and 4. 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 4 ÷ 12 = {1/3} (1 mark).
