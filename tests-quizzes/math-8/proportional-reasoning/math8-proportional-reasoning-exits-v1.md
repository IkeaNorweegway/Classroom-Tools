## Section 1: Times as many
Target: I can describe one amount as a multiple of the other, both ways. (PR1)
### Zoe has 24 stamps. Leo has 8. Compare them both ways.
@viz prtimes rows="Leo:1;Zoe:3" value="8" down="× [[3]]" up="× [[1/3]]"
(lines 2)
=> 24 ÷ 8 = 3: Zoe has 3 times as many. 8 ÷ 24 = {1/3}: Leo has {1/3} as many.
### Ty ran 5 km. Al ran 1.5 times as far. How far did Al run?
(lines 1)
=> 1.5 × 5 = 7.5 km
### Raj says 30 is 20 times as many as 10. Explain how you know he is wrong.
(lines 2)
=> 30 − 10 = 20 is how many more. To compare by "times as many", divide: 30 ÷ 10 = 3. And 20 × 10 = 200, not 30.

## Section 2: The constant of proportionality
Target: I can find k for a ratio and use it to check equivalent ratios. (PR2)
### Find k for the ratio 5 : 8.
@viz prk top="5,10" bottom="8,16" toplabel="first (a)" bottomlabel="second (b)" k="× [[1.6]]"
(lines 1)
=> k = 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6
### Do 4 : 10 and 6 : 15 have the same k? Show it.
(lines 1)
=> 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 and 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5. Yes, so they are equivalent ratios.
### Sam says k for 9 : 3 is 3. Explain how you know he is wrong.
(lines 2)
=> k = second ÷ first = 3 ÷ 9 = {1/3}. Check: {1/3} × 9 = 3 ✓. He divided the bigger number by the smaller.

## Section 3: Missing quantities
Target: I can make equivalent ratios for a given k and find a missing quantity using k. (PR3)
### Gas costs $1.45 per litre. What do 50 L cost?
@viz prk top="1,50" bottom="$1.45,[[$72.50]]" toplabel="gas (L)" bottomlabel="cost ($)" k="× 1.45"
(lines 1)
=> b = 1.45 × 50 = $72.50
### A map has the scale 1 cm : 5 km. Two towns are 28 km apart. How far apart are they on the map?
(lines 1)
=> a = 28 ÷ 5 = 5.6 cm
### Explain how you know to divide, not multiply, in the map question.
(lines 2)
=> 28 km is the second term (b). To go back to the first term, undo × 5 by dividing. Check: 5 × 5.6 = 28 ✓.

## Section 4: Direct and inverse proportion
Target: I can tell direct from inverse proportion and solve problems with each. (PR4)
### 3 workers shovel the rink in 8 h. How long do 6 workers take?
@viz prwork cases="3,8;6,4" rowlabel="workers" collabel="h" labels="3 × 8 = 24;6 × [[4]] = 24"
(lines 1)
=> 3 × 8 = 24 worker-hours. 24 ÷ 6 = 4 h.
### Direct or inverse? Hours worked and pay at $15 per hour.
(lines 1)
=> Direct. Pay = 15 × hours. Twice the hours, twice the pay.
### Explain how you know the rink question is inverse proportion.
(lines 2)
=> Workers × hours stays at 24. More workers share the work, so each works fewer hours.
