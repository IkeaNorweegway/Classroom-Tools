## Section 1: Ratios
Target: I can write a ratio three ways and tell part-to-part from part-to-whole. (RR1)
### A bowl has 4 apples and 6 oranges. Write the ratio of apples to oranges three ways.
(lines 1)
=> 4 to 6, 4:6, 4/6 (or simplified 2:3)
### What is the ratio of apples to all the fruit?
(lines 1)
=> 4:10 (or 2:5)

## Section 2: Equal ratios and proportions
Target: I can make equal ratios and solve a proportion. (RR2)
### Find x: 3 : 8 = x : 40
(lines 1)
=> x = 15 (× 5)
### Is 2:3 equal to 4:5? Explain.
(lines 2)
=> No. To get 4 from 2 you multiply by 2, but 3 × 2 = 6, not 5.

## Section 3: Rates and unit rates
Target: I can find a unit rate and use it to compare. (RR3)
### Which is the better buy: 4 L of milk for $5.60 or 2 L for $3.00? Show the unit prices.
(work 2)
=> 4 L: $1.40/L. 2 L: $1.50/L. The 4 L jug is the better buy.

## Section 4: Proportion problems
Target: I can choose a strategy to solve a ratio or rate problem. (RR4)
### 5 movie tickets cost $60. How much do 8 tickets cost? Show your strategy.
(work 2)
=> $12 each (unit rate). 8 × $12 = $96.
