## Part B — Show Your Work

**9.** (a) 6 : 4 : 2 = **3 : 2 : 1** (b) 4 : 12 = **1 : 3** (c) 6 out of 12 = **50%**
- 1 mark each

**10.** Water: **10, 15, 25** (multiply both by 2, 3, 5)
- 1 mark each

**11.** **Yes.** Way 1: 4 : 6 = 2 : 3 and 10 : 15 = 2 : 3. Way 2: 4/6 ≈ 0.67 and 10/15 ≈ 0.67. (Or a table: 2 : 3, 4 : 6, 6 : 9, 8 : 12, 10 : 15.)
- 1 mark: first way
- 1 mark: second way
- 1 mark: yes

**12.** Box A: $4.20 ÷ 6 = **$0.70** a bar. Box B: $6.50 ÷ 10 = **$0.65** a bar. **Box B** is the better buy.
- 1 mark each

**13.** (a) 250 is 2.5 × 100, so 2.5 × 7 = **17.5 L**
- 1 mark: method
- 1 mark: 17.5 L

(b) A **rate**: it compares two different units (litres and kilometres).
- 1 mark

**14.** One ticket: $62.50 ÷ 5 = $12.50. Eight tickets: 8 × $12.50 = **$100.00**
- 1 mark: strategy (unit rate or table)
- 1 mark: $12.50
- 1 mark: $100.00

**15.** 3 L juice : 2 L soda. 7.5 = 3 × 2.5, so soda = 2 × 2.5 = **5 L**
- 1 mark: double number line or table set up
- 1 mark: scale factor 2.5 (or 1.5 L juice per 1 L soda)

## Part D — Explain Your Thinking

**17.** Model answer: Sam is not right. Equal ratios come from multiplying (or dividing) both numbers by the same number, not adding. 1 : 3 means the second amount is 3 times the first, but in 2 : 4 it is only 2 times. A ratio equal to 1 : 3 is **2 : 6** (or 3 : 9, 4 : 12…).

| Level | Marks | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Full | 3 | Says Sam is wrong; explains multiplying, not adding; gives an equal ratio |
| Partial | 1–2 | Gives an equal ratio and says Sam is wrong, with no reason (2); says he is wrong only (1) |
| Not shown | 0 | Agrees with Sam |
