## Part B — Show Your Work

**9.** **135%**, **135/100** (or 27/20, or 1 35/100), **1.35**
- 1 mark each

**10.** As decimals: 0.3 = 0.3, 3% = 0.03, 0.3% = 0.003, 30.5% = 0.305. Order: **0.3%, 3%, 0.3, 30.5%**
- 1 mark: changes at least three to decimals correctly
- 1 mark: 0.3% = 0.003
- 1 mark: correct order

**11.** 10% of $60 = $6. 5% of $60 = $3. 15% = $6 + $3 = **$9.00**
- 1 mark each step

**12.** 14 ÷ 40 = 0.35 = **35%**
- 1 mark: 14 ÷ 40 (or 14/40)
- 1 mark: 0.35
- 1 mark: 35%

**13.** 5% + 15% = 20%. 20% of $35 = $7. Total = $35 + $7 = **$42.00**
- 1 mark: adds the two percents (or finds $1.75 and $5.25)
- 1 mark: $7
- 1 mark: $42.00

**14.** Alberta: $240 × 1.05 = $252.00. Ontario: $240 × 1.13 = $271.20. Difference: **$19.20** (or 8% of $240 = $19.20)
- 1 mark: Alberta total
- 1 mark: Ontario total
- 1 mark: $19.20

**15.** (a) $80 × 0.75 = $60. Then $60 × 0.90 = **$54.00**
- 1 mark: $60 sale price
- 1 mark: $54.00

(b) **No.** 35% off $80 is $52. The 10% is taken off the smaller sale price, so less money comes off.
- 1 mark

## Part C — Put It Together

**16.** (a) 20% of 400 = 80. 400 + 80 = **480 visitors**
- 1 mark: 80
- 1 mark: 480

(b) 25% of 480 = 120. 480 + 120 = **600 visitors**
- 1 mark: uses 480, not 400
- 1 mark: 600

(c) 600 ÷ 400 = 1.5, so **150%** or **1.5**
- 1 mark each

(d) **No.** The fair grew from 400 to 600. That is 200 more, and 200 is 50% of 400. The 25% was taken of 480, a bigger number, so the total growth is 50%, not 45%.
- 1 mark: says Liam is wrong
- 1 mark: explains with 50% (or with the 25% being of a bigger number)

## Part D — Explain Your Thinking

**17.** Model answer: Kai is not right. 0.5% = 0.005. 0.005 × $200 = **$1.00**. Kai found 50% of $200. 0.5% is half of one percent, which is much smaller than 50%.
