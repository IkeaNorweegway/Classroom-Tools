### [TT1 · 2] Find x. Name the theorem you used.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;3.8,5.43" angles="55°;68°;x" unit=24
(lines 2)
=> x = 180° − 55° − 68° = 57° (1 mark). The angle sum theorem (1 mark).

### [TT1 · 3] This triangle is isosceles. The top angle is 24°. Find each base angle.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;3,0;1.5,7.06" ticks="0,1,1" arcs="1,1,0" angles="b;b;24°" unit=18
(lines 2)
=> b + b + 24° = 180° (1 mark). 2b = 156° (1 mark). b = 78° (1 mark).

### [TT1 · 2] Find each angle of an equilateral triangle. Explain.
(lines 2)
=> 60° (1 mark). The three angles are equal and add to 180°: 180° ÷ 3 = 60° (1 mark).

### [TT1 · 2] A right triangle has a 29° angle. Find the third angle.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;6,3.33" right="2" angles="29°;;t" unit=26
(lines 1)
=> t = 180° − 90° − 29° (1 mark) = 61° (1 mark).

### [TT2 · 2] Can 7 cm, 9 cm and 15 cm make a triangle? Show your test.
(lines 2)
=> 7 + 9 = 16, and 16 > 15 (1 mark). Yes (1 mark).

### [TT2 · 2] Can 2.5 m, 3.5 m and 6 m make a triangle? Show your test.
(lines 2)
=> 2.5 + 3.5 = 6, which is not more than 6 (1 mark). No: the sides lie flat (1 mark).

### [TT2 · 3] Two sides are 8 cm and 13 cm. Find the range for the third side x. Show it on the number line.
@viz ttrange min=0 max=22 lo=5 hi=21 show=key cell=20
(lines 2)
=> 13 − 8 = 5 (1 mark). 13 + 8 = 21 (1 mark). 5 < x < 21, with open dots at 5 and 21 (1 mark).

### [TT3 · 3] Is a triangle with sides 12 cm, 16 cm and 20 cm a right triangle?
(lines 3)
=> 12² + 16² = 144 + 256 = 400 (1 mark). 20² = 400 (1 mark). They match, so yes, by the converse (1 mark).

### [TT3 · 2] Name the hypotenuse. Say how you know.
@viz righttri a="MN" b="NP" c="MP" aval=4 bval=7 names="N,M,P"
(lines 1)
=> MP (1 mark). It is across from the right angle at N (1 mark).

### [TT3 · 3] Raj tests 5, 12, 13 like this: 5² + 13² = 12². He says it is not a right triangle. Explain his mistake and fix it.
(lines 3)
=> c must be the longest side, 13 (1 mark). 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 (1 mark). 13² = 169, so it IS a right triangle (1 mark).
