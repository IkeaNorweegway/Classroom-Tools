## Part A — Choose the Answer
Circle the best answer. 1 mark each.

### [TT1 · 1] A triangle has angles of 47° and 68°. What is the third angle?
- A) 115°
- B) 65°
- C) 245°
- D) 21°
=> **B.** 47° + 68° = 115°. 180° − 115° = 65°.
=> Common mistakes: A stops at the sum. C subtracts from 360°. D subtracts the two angles.

### [TT1 · 1] The base angles of an isosceles triangle are 52° each. What is the top angle?
- A) 76°
- B) 128°
- C) 52°
- D) 64°
=> **A.** 52° + 52° = 104°. 180° − 104° = 76°.
=> Common mistakes: B uses only one base angle. C copies a base angle. D halves 128°.

### [TT2 · 1] Which three lengths make a triangle?
- A) 3 cm, 4 cm, 8 cm
- B) 5 cm, 5 cm, 10 cm
- C) 6 cm, 7 cm, 12 cm
- D) 2 cm, 9 cm, 6 cm
=> **C.** The two shorter sides: 6 + 7 = 13, more than 12.
=> Common mistakes: A: 3 + 4 = 7, less than 8. B: 5 + 5 = 10 exactly, so it lies flat. D: 2 + 6 = 8, less than 9.

### [TT2 · 1] Two sides of a triangle are 5 cm and 9 cm. Which could be the third side?
- A) 3 cm
- B) 4 cm
- C) 14 cm
- D) 11 cm
=> **D.** The third side must be between 9 − 5 = 4 and 9 + 5 = 14.
=> Common mistakes: A is too short. B and C are the end values, where the sides lie flat.

### [TT3 · 1] In right triangle XYZ, the right angle is at Y. Which side is the hypotenuse?
- A) XZ
- B) XY
- C) YZ
- D) the longer leg
=> **A.** The hypotenuse is across from the right angle. Y is not on side XZ.
=> Common mistakes: B and C touch the right angle, so they are legs. D: the hypotenuse is not a leg.

### [TT3 · 1] Which lengths make a right triangle?
- A) 6, 8, 12
- B) 9, 12, 15
- C) 4, 5, 6
- D) 7, 8, 10
=> **B.** 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 = 15².
=> Common mistakes: A: 36 + 64 = 100, not 144. C: 16 + 25 = 41, not 36. D: 49 + 64 = 113, not 100.

### [TT4 · 1] The legs of a right triangle are 8 cm and 15 cm. How long is the hypotenuse?
- A) 23 cm
- B) 289 cm
- C) 17 cm
- D) 12.7 cm
=> **C.** c² = 64 + 225 = 289. c = √289 = 17 cm.
=> Common mistakes: A adds the legs. B forgets the square root. D subtracts the squares.

### [TT4 · 1] A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 10 m and one leg of 6 m. How long is the other leg?
- A) 16 m
- B) 11.7 m
- C) 4 m
- D) 8 m
=> **D.** a² = 100 − 36 = 64. a = √64 = 8 m.
=> Common mistakes: A adds the lengths. B adds the squares. C subtracts the lengths.

## Part B — Show Your Work
Show your steps.

### [TT1 · 3] Find x. Then find each angle. What kind of triangle is it?
@viz polygon pts="0,0;4,0;3,1.73" angles="x;2x;3x" unit=40
(work 2)
=> x + 2x + 3x = 180°, so 6x = 180° (1 mark).
=> x = 30° (1 mark).
=> The angles are 30°, 60° and 90°. It is a right triangle (1 mark).

### [TT1 · 3] An isosceles triangle has one angle of 100°. a) Find the other two angles. b) Can 100° be a base angle? Explain.
(lines 3)
=> a) The 100° angle is the top angle: 180° − 100° = 80° (1 mark).
=> 80° ÷ 2 = 40°. The other angles are 40° and 40° (1 mark).
=> b) No. Two base angles of 100° add to 200°. That is more than 180° (1 mark).

### [TT2 · 3] On a hike, the camp is 4 km from the lake. The lake is 7 km from the peak. The three places are not in a straight line. a) Find the range for the straight distance d from the camp to the peak. b) Could d be 11 km? Explain.
@viz ttrange min=0 max=14 lo=3 hi=11 show=key label="[[3]]  <  d  <  [[11]]"
(work 2)
=> a) 7 − 4 = 3 and 7 + 4 = 11 (1 mark).
=> 3 km < d < 11 km (1 mark).
=> b) No. 4 + 7 = 11 exactly, so the camp, lake and peak would be in a straight line (1 mark).

### [TT2 · 3] Can 5 cm, 8 cm and 12 cm make a triangle? Jon checks 12 + 8 > 5 and says yes. a) Is his check enough? Explain. b) Do the right test.
(lines 3)
=> a) No. The longest side plus another side is always more than the third side, so that check proves nothing (1 mark).
=> b) Add the two shorter sides: 5 + 8 = 13 (1 mark).
=> 13 > 12, so yes, they make a triangle (1 mark).

### [TT3 · 3] A triangle has sides 9 cm, 40 cm and 41 cm. Is it a right triangle? If it is, which side is across from the right angle?
(work 2)
=> 9² + 40² = 81 + 1600 = 1681 (1 mark).
=> 41² = 1681. They are equal, so it is a right triangle (1 mark).
=> The right angle is across from the 41 cm side, the hypotenuse (1 mark).

### [TT3 · 3] A carpenter checks two corners of a deck. Corner A: 60 cm along one side, 80 cm along the other, 100 cm across. Corner B: 60 cm, 80 cm, 98 cm across. Which corner is square (90°)? Show your work.
(work 2)
=> 60² + 80² = 3600 + 6400 = 10 000 (1 mark).
=> Corner A: 100² = 10 000. Equal, so A is square (1 mark).
=> Corner B: 98² = 9604. Not equal, so B is not square (1 mark).

### [TT4 · 3] Find the missing leg. Round to one decimal place.
@viz righttri a="5.0 cm" b="b" c="9.0 cm" aval=5 bval=7.48 hyp=1
(work 2)
=> 9.0 cm is the hypotenuse, so subtract: b² = 9² − 5² (1 mark).
=> b² = 81 − 25 = 56 (1 mark).
=> b = √56 ≈ 7.5 cm (1 mark).

## Part C — Put It Together
An A-frame cabin near Canmore has a front wall shaped like an isosceles triangle. The two sloped sides are 6.5 m each. The floor is 5 m wide. The height goes from the peak straight down to the middle of the floor. It splits the wall into two right triangles.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;2.5,6" ticks="0,1,1" sides="5 m;6.5 m;6.5 m" angles="67°;67°;p" unit=24

### [TT1 · 2] (a) Each base angle is about 67°. Find the angle p at the peak.
(work 1)
=> 67° + 67° = 134° (1 mark).
=> p = 180° − 134° = 46° (1 mark).

### [TT4 · 2] (b) Find the height of the cabin.
(work 2)
=> Each right triangle has hypotenuse 6.5 m and a leg of 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 m (1 mark).
=> h² = 6.5² − 2.5² = 42.25 − 6.25 = 36, so h = 6 m (1 mark).

### [TT2 · 2] (c) The builder wants a small shed with the same 5 m floor and two sloped sides of 2.4 m. Is that possible? Explain.
(lines 2)
=> 2.4 + 2.4 = 4.8 (1 mark).
=> 4.8 < 5, so the sides cannot meet. It is not possible (1 mark).

### [TT4 · 2] (d) A wire goes from the peak to a stake on the ground. The stake is 8 m from the middle of the floor. How long is the wire?
(work 2)
=> The wire is the hypotenuse: w² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 (1 mark).
=> w = √100 = 10 m (1 mark).

## Part D — Explain Your Thinking

### [TT4 · 3] A TV is sold as 55 inches. That is its diagonal. The screen is 48 inches wide. Will it fit in a cabinet opening that is 30 inches tall? Explain how you know.
@viz ttscene kind=tv w=48 h=26.85 a="h" b="48 in" c="55 in"
(lines 4)
=> Model answer: The diagonal is the hypotenuse, so subtract to find the height: h² = 55² − 48² = 3025 − 2304 = 721. h = √721 ≈ 26.9 inches. 26.9 is less than 30, so the TV fits. Adding the squares would give about 73 inches, which is longer than the diagonal, so that cannot be the height.
