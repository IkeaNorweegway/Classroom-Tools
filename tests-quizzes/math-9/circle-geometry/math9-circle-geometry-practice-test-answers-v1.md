## Part B — Short Answer

**9.** (a) **OE** (or OA or OB) (b) **AB** (c) **CD**
- 1 mark each

**10.** OM ⊥ AB, so M is the midpoint: AM = 12 cm. In right triangle OMA: OM² = 13² − 12² = 169 − 144 = 25, so **OM = 5 cm**.
- 1 mark: AM = 12 (the perpendicular bisects the chord)
- 1 mark: Pythagorean theorem with 13 as the hypotenuse
- 1 mark: 5 cm

**11.** In right triangle OMB: MB² = 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64, so MB = 8 cm. The perpendicular bisects the chord, so **AB = 16 cm**.
- 1 mark: right triangle with 10 cm as the hypotenuse
- 1 mark: MB = 8 cm
- 1 mark: doubles to 16 cm

**12.** Half of each chord is 15 cm. Distance² = 17² − 15² = 289 − 225 = 64, so each chord is **8 cm** from the centre. **Equal chords are the same distance from the centre**, so one calculation answers for both.
- 1 mark: uses half the chord (15 cm)
- 1 mark: 8 cm
- 1 mark: names the equal-chords property

**13.** (a) ∠AOB = 2 × 38° = **76°** (b) ∠ADB = **38°** (c) Inscribed angles on the same arc are equal.
- 1 mark each

**14.** AB is a diameter, so ∠ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle). The angles in triangle ABC add to 180°: ∠ABC = 180° − 90° − 32° = **58°**.
- 1 mark: ∠ACB = 90°, with the reason
- 1 mark: uses the angle sum of a triangle
- 1 mark: 58°

**15.** The student halved instead of doubling. The **central** angle is twice the inscribed angle: 2 × 50° = **100°**.
- 1 mark: names the error (halved, or mixed up which angle is bigger)
- 1 mark: states the theorem the right way round
- 1 mark: 100°

**16.** OT ⊥ PT, so triangle OTP has its right angle at T and OP is the hypotenuse. PT² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225, so **PT = 15 cm**.
- 1 mark: right angle at T (tangent ⊥ radius)
- 1 mark: subtracts (17² − 8²)
- 1 mark: 15 cm

**17.** ∠OTP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius). ∠OPT = 180° − 90° − 52° = **38°**.
- 1 mark: ∠OTP = 90°, with the reason
- 1 mark: angle sum of 180°

**18.** (a) **11 cm** (tangent segments from the same point are equal) (b) **90°** (c) 6 + 11 + 11 + 6 = **34 cm**

## Part D — Thinking

**20.** Model answer: Maria is not correct. An inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc. It is 90° only when it stands on a diameter: then the central angle is a straight line, 180°, and half of that is 90°. For any other chord it is not 90°. For example, if the central angle is 80°, the inscribed angle on that arc is 40°. So an inscribed angle is a right angle only when it stands on a diameter (an angle in a semicircle).
