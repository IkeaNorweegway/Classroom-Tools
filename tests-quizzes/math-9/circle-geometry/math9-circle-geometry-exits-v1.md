# Answer Key — Circle Geometry Exit Tickets

## Section 1
1. Chord: a line segment with both endpoints on the circle — any chord passes through two points on the circle. Tangent: a line that touches the circle at exactly one point — it is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency. Inscribed angle: an angle formed by two chords with the vertex on the circle — it equals half the central angle subtending the same arc.
2. A diameter is a chord that passes through the centre — it is the longest possible chord. diameter = 2 × radius.

## Section 2
1. The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so the half-chord = 5 cm. Using Pythagorean theorem: r² = 5² + 4² = 25 + 16 = 41. r = √41 ≈ 6.4 cm.
2. The perpendicular bisector property: if a line from the centre of a circle is perpendicular to a chord, it bisects that chord (cuts it into two equal halves). Sketch should show a circle, chord, and a line from centre meeting the chord at 90° at its midpoint.

## Section 3
1. Inscribed angle = central angle ÷ 2 = 70° ÷ 2 = 35°.
2. Yes, they are equal. Because every inscribed angle equals half the central angle subtending the same arc, and the central angle is fixed for a given arc, any inscribed angle on that arc must equal the same value (half the same central angle).

## Section 4
1. OT ⊥ PT (tangent-radius property), so triangle OTP is right-angled at T. PT² = OP² − OT² = 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64. PT = 8 cm.
2. The tangent-radius property: a tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency. It must be 90° because the tangent touches the circle at exactly one point — if it were at any other angle, it would intersect the circle at a second point, making it a secant rather than a tangent.
