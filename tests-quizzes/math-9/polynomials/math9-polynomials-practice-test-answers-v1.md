## Part B — Short Answer

**9.** (a) **3x² − 2x + 5** (b) degree **2**, leading coefficient **3** (c) The degree is the highest power of x, not the biggest number. 5 is the constant term.
- 1 mark each

**10.** (4x² − x²) + (−3x + 5x) + (6 − 8) = **3x² + 2x − 2**
- 1 mark for each correct term

**11.** (a) 3x² and 4x are unlike terms: x² and x stand for different things (an x²-tile and an x-tile are different shapes), so they can't be combined. A number check shows it too: with x = 1, 3x² + 4x = 7, but with x = 2 it is 12 + 8 = 20, while 7x³ = 56.
- 1 mark: says they are unlike terms (different powers)
- 1 mark: a reason (tiles, meaning, or a number check)

(b) **3x² + 4x**: it is already simplified.
- 1 mark

**12.** (3x² − x²) + (−2x − 4x) + (5 − 7) = **2x² − 6x − 2**
- 1 mark for each correct term

**13.** 5x² + x − 2 − 2x² + 3x − 4 = **3x² + 4x − 6**
- 1 mark: distributes the negative to every term
- 2 marks: the answer (1 mark if one term is wrong)

**14.** The student didn't change the sign of the −5. Subtracting −5 is adding 5: 6x − 1 − 2x **+ 5** = **4x + 4**.
- 1 mark: names the error
- 1 mark: correct signs after removing the brackets
- 1 mark: 4x + 4

**15.** (a) **6x² − 3x + 12** (b) **−2x³ + 6x² − 2x**
- (a) 1 mark
- (b) 2 marks (1 mark if one sign or one term is wrong)

**16.** (a) 2x(3x + 5) = **6x² + 10x** cm²
- 1 mark: sets up 2x(3x + 5)
- 1 mark: 6x² + 10x

(b) 6(9) + 10(3) = 54 + 30 = **84 cm²** (check: 6 cm × 14 cm = 84 cm²)
- 1 mark

**17.** Area model with 2x and 3 across, x and 4 down: 2x² | 3x over 8x | 12. Total **2x² + 11x + 12**.
- 1 mark: area model with the four parts labelled
- 1 mark: the four partial products 2x², 8x, 3x, 12
- 1 mark: 2x² + 11x + 12

**18.** 3x² − 3x − 2x + 2 = **3x² − 5x + 2**. Check with x = 2: (3(2) − 2)(2 − 1) = 4 × 1 = 4, and 3(4) − 5(2) + 2 = 12 − 10 + 2 = 4 ✓
- 1 mark: four partial products
- 1 mark: 3x² − 5x + 2
- 1 mark: both sides checked with x = 2

**Follow-through:** across Part B, if one early slip is carried correctly through later steps, take off only the mark for the slip.

## Part C — Extended Response

**19.** (a) 2(2x + 6) + 2(x + 3) = 4x + 12 + 2x + 6 = **6x + 18** m
- 1 mark: adds all four sides (or doubles each side)
- 1 mark: 6x + 18

(b) (2x + 6)(x + 3) = 2x² + 6x + 6x + 18 = **2x² + 12x + 18** m²
- 1 mark: four partial products
- 1 mark: 2x² + 12x + 18

(c) (2x² + 12x + 18) − x² = **x² + 12x + 18** m²
- 1 mark: subtracts x² from the area in (b)
- 1 mark: x² + 12x + 18

(d) 3x(x + 3) = **3x² + 9x** m²
- 1 mark: sets up 3x(x + 3)
- 1 mark: 3x² + 9x

Units: take off 1 mark once if units are missing throughout. Follow-through applies from (b) to (c).

## Part D — Thinking

**20.** Model answer: Nina is not correct. (x + 4)² means (x + 4)(x + 4). The area model has four parts: x², 4x, 4x and 16. So (x + 4)² = x² + 8x + 16. Nina left out the two 4x rectangles. Number check with x = 3: (3 + 4)² = 7² = 49, but 3² + 16 = 9 + 16 = 25. The correct expansion gives 9 + 24 + 16 = 49 ✓.

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|

| Substantial | 3 | Says Nina is wrong with a correct expansion and either the area model or the number check, not both |
| Partial | 1–2 | A number check showing Nina is wrong, or an incomplete area model (2); says Nina is wrong with no supporting work (1) |
