**12.** **No.** Correlation doesn't show cause. A third variable, **population**, drives both: bigger towns have more people, so they have more coffee shops and more dentists.

## Part C — Extended Response

**19.** (a) All 10 points plotted; axes labelled Temperature (°C) and Visitors.
- 1 mark: at least 9 points correct
- 1 mark: both axes labelled

(b) **Strong positive** correlation: on hotter days, more people come to the pool.
- 1 mark: strong and positive
- 1 mark: meaning in context

(c) About **v = 10t − 70**. Accept a slope from 9 to 11 with a matching intercept (predictions within about 10 visitors of the data).
- 1 mark: a reasonable line
- 1 mark: equation from two points on the line

(d) 25 °C: about 10(25) − 70 = **180 visitors**. 40 °C: about 10(40) − 70 = **330 visitors**. The 25 °C prediction is **more reliable**: it is interpolation, inside the 18 °C to 32 °C data. 40 °C is extrapolation, and the trend may not hold (the pool may be full, or it may be too hot to go out).
- 1 mark: both predictions from their equation
- 1 mark: picks 25 °C with the interpolation reason

## Part D — Thinking

**20.** Model answer: The conclusion is not justified. The data may show a correlation (students who eat breakfast tend to have higher marks), but correlation doesn't prove that one thing causes the other. A third variable could explain both: for example, students with more regular routines or more sleep may be more likely to eat breakfast and also do better in school. To test the claim, you would need a controlled experiment: randomly put similar students into two groups, give one group breakfast and not the other, and compare marks over time, keeping other things the same.

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Full | 4 | Says the conclusion is not justified; explains correlation vs causation; names a plausible third variable; describes a controlled study |
| Substantial | 3 | Three of the four parts |
| Partial | 1–2 | Correlation vs causation with a third variable (2); or says "correlation isn't causation" only (1) |
| Not demonstrated | 0 | Agrees with the headline, or no relevant reasoning |
