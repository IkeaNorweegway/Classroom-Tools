## Part B — Short Answer

**9.** (a) y = **7, 4, 1, −2**
- 2 marks: all four (1 mark for two or three)

(b) The y-values go **down by 3** each time x goes up by 1.
- 1 mark

**10.** Table, for example: (−1, −3), (0, −1), (1, 1), (2, 3), (3, 5). A straight line through these points, crossing the y-axis at −1 and rising 2 for every 1 across.
- 1 mark: table with at least three correct points
- 1 mark: points plotted correctly
- 1 mark: straight line drawn through them with a ruler

**11.** From (−2, 4) to (4, −5): rise = −5 − 4 = **−9**, run = 4 − (−2) = **6**. Slope = −9/6 = **−3/2** (or −1.5).
- 1 mark: rise −9
- 1 mark: run 6
- 1 mark: −3/2, negative because the line goes down from left to right

**12.** (a) Rise = 1800 − 2400 = −600 m. Run = 3 h. Slope = **−200 m/h**.
- 1 mark: −600 ÷ 3
- 1 mark: −200 m/h with units

(b) It is negative, so her elevation is going **down**: she drops 200 m every hour.
- 1 mark

**13.** Kim put the run on top. Slope is rise over run: (11 − 3)/(5 − 1) = 8/4 = **2**.
- 1 mark: names the error (run over rise, or x-change on top)
- 1 mark: correct set-up
- 1 mark: 2

**14.** b = −3 (the line crosses the y-axis at (0, −3)). m = (5 − (−3))/(2 − 0) = 8/2 = 4. **y = 4x − 3**
- 1 mark: b = −3
- 1 mark: m = 4
- 1 mark: equation

**15.** (a) **(0, −8)** &nbsp; (b) 0 = 2x − 8, so x = 4: **(4, 0)**
- 1 mark each

(c) The point where y = 0 is the **x**-intercept, on the x-axis. The y-intercept is where x = 0, on the y-axis, which is (0, −8).
- 1 mark

**16.** 1 = −2(3) + b, so 1 = −6 + b and **b = 7**. **y = −2x + 7**
- 1 mark: substitutes (3, 1) and m = −2
- 1 mark: b = 7
- 1 mark: equation

**17.** (a) **P is direct** (y = 0 when x = 0, so it passes through the origin). **Q is partial** (it starts at 3).
- 1 mark: both types with a reason

(b) P: **y = 4x** &nbsp; Q: **y = 4x + 3**
- 1 mark each

**18.** (a) **$20**: the cost before any time is used (a one-time fee). (b) (70 − 20)/5 = **$10 per hour**: each extra hour costs $10. (c) **Partial**: the line starts at $20, not at (0, 0).

## Part C — Extended Response

**19.** (a) Shop A: **C = 30d**. Shop B: **C = 20d + 40**.
- 1 mark each

(b)

| Days | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shop A ($) | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 | 150 |

(c) Shop A: a line from (0, 0) through (5, 150). Shop B: a line from (0, 40) through (5, 140). Both labelled.

## Part D — Thinking

**20.** Model answer: Jordan is not correct. Compare y = 2x and y = −5x. The line y = −5x is steeper (it drops 5 for every 1 across, while y = 2x only rises 2), but −5 is smaller than 2. Steepness depends on how big the slope is when you ignore its sign. The sign only tells you the direction: positive goes up to the right, negative goes down to the right. Jordan's rule only works when both slopes are positive: y = 5x is steeper than y = 2x, and 5 > 2.

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Full | 4 | Says Jordan is wrong; gives a counterexample with a steep negative slope; explains that steepness is the size of the slope and the sign is the direction; notes the rule works for two positive slopes |
| Substantial | 3 | Says Jordan is wrong with a correct counterexample, but the explanation of size and sign is incomplete |
| Partial | 1–2 | A correct comparison of two lines with some reasoning (2); or says Jordan is wrong with no example (1) |
| Not demonstrated | 0 | Agrees with Jordan, or no relevant mathematics |
