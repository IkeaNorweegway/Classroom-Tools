# How Written Answers Are Scored
Grade 9 Mathematics · Rational Numbers · Unit test, Parts C and D

<!-- Student-facing. Published on the site (Rational Numbers extras). The teacher rubric with question-specific descriptors is in the unit test answer key (test v4) and in the Google Drive tests folder. Wording follows templates/rubric-phrase-bank.md. Contains no test answers. -->

On the unit test, the written questions (Parts C and D) are scored on the same 4-point scale as your report card. **Each part is scored on one learning target.**

## The scale

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| **4 Excellent** | *I went further.* Correct and complete, **and** I checked it or explained why it makes sense. |
| **3 Proficient** | *I've got it solidly.* Correct and complete, with working, the right sign, lowest terms and units. |
| **2 Meets** | *I've met it.* The right operation and the right sign, with one slip or one missing piece. |
| **1 Has yet to meet** | *Not yet.* The operation, the sign or the key idea isn't right yet. |
| **0** | No answer yet: blank, or no math to score. |

## What each level looks like, by learning target

Start with **Meets**. Proficient and Excellent build on it.

| Learning target | 2 Meets: I've met it | 3 Proficient adds… | 4 Excellent adds… | 1 Has yet to meet often looks like… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RN1** Compare and order | I write the numbers in the same form (common denominator, decimals or a number line) and compare them. | I say which is greater **and why**, and every step is correct. | I explain an idea that is always true, e.g. there is always another rational number between two others. | Comparing −2/3 and −5/8 by looking only at the 2 and the 5, or forgetting that negatives flip the order. |
| **RN2** Add and subtract | I use a common denominator and get the sign right. One slip is OK. | Every step correct, lowest terms, units. | I check with a number line, or by going "to zero, then past it." | Adding the tops and adding the bottoms, or losing the negative sign. |
| **RN3** Multiply | I change mixed numbers to improper fractions, multiply, and get the sign right. | Every step correct, simplified, units. | I check the size with an estimate, or explain why the sign is negative or positive. | A sign error, or adding when the situation needs multiplying. |
| **RN4** Divide | I multiply by the reciprocal of the number I'm dividing by, and get the sign right. | Every step correct, simplified, units. | I check by multiplying back, or explain why the answer is bigger or smaller than I started with. | Flipping the wrong fraction, or multiplying without flipping. |
| **RN5** Order of operations | I do the steps in the right order: brackets, exponents, × and ÷, then + and −. | Every step correct, and I can **name the rule** when I spot a mistake. | I explain why the order matters in the situation. | Working left to right, or adding before multiplying. |

**A sign error is not a slip.** On this unit the sign *is* the idea, so a wrong sign keeps that part at Has yet to meet.

## How to turn a 3 into a 4

After each answer, add **one line** that shows it makes sense:

- **Estimate first,** then compare: "2⅞ is about 3, so 5 × 2⅞ should be about 15."
- **Multiply back** to check a division.
- **Use a number line** for adding and subtracting negatives.
- **Say why the sign makes sense:** "negative, because the account balance went down."
- **Put the answer back into the situation:** does it fit the question?

## Practise it

Do Part C of the **Practice Test**. For each part, find your level in the table above and write it in the margin. Then add one check to the part with the lowest level.
