## Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

*Circle the best answer. [1 mark each] /8*

**1.** A bag has 4 red marbles and 6 blue marbles. P(red) =
A) 4/6 &nbsp; B) 1/4 &nbsp; C) 3/5 &nbsp; D) 2/5

**2.** P(A) = 0.35. P(not A) =
A) 0.65 &nbsp; B) 0.35 &nbsp; C) 1.35 &nbsp; D) 0.53

**3.** A spinner landed on red 18 times in 60 spins. The experimental probability of red is
A) 0.6 &nbsp; B) 0.18 &nbsp; C) 0.3 &nbsp; D) 1/18

**4.** A coin is flipped and a standard die is rolled. How many outcomes are in the sample space?
A) 8 &nbsp; B) 12 &nbsp; C) 6 &nbsp; D) 36

**5.** Two coins are tossed. P(two heads) =
A) 1/2 &nbsp; B) 1/3 &nbsp; C) 1/4 &nbsp; D) 3/4

**6.** Which pair of events is **independent**?
A) drawing two cards without putting the first back &nbsp; B) rolling a die twice &nbsp; C) choosing two students from one class for a team &nbsp; D) drawing two marbles without replacement

**7.** A and B are independent. P(A) = 0.5 and P(B) = 0.2. P(A and B) =
A) 0.7 &nbsp; B) 0.3 &nbsp; C) 0.25 &nbsp; D) 0.1

**8.** A bag has 3 red and 2 blue marbles. A red marble is drawn and **not** put back. P(red on the next draw) =
A) 2/4 &nbsp; B) 3/5 &nbsp; C) 3/4 &nbsp; D) 2/5

## Part B — Understanding (Short Answer)

*Show your working. [3 marks each] /30*

**9.** A bag has 5 green, 3 yellow and 2 purple cubes. One cube is drawn. Find: (a) P(yellow) (b) P(not green) (c) P(orange) *[1 each]*

**10.** A student flips a coin 40 times and gets 26 heads.
(a) What is the experimental probability of heads? *[1]*
(b) What is the theoretical probability of heads? *[1]*
(c) Why are they different, and what would you expect after 1000 flips? *[1]*

**11.** A spinner has three equal sections: red, blue and green. Spin it, then flip a coin.
(a) Draw a tree diagram or list the sample space. *[2]*
(b) Find P(blue and tails). *[1]*

**12.** Two standard dice are rolled and the numbers are added. Use the table to find: (a) P(sum of 8) (b) P(doubles) (c) P(sum less than 4) *[1 each]* [Table of all 36 sums.]

**13.** A spinner with four equal sections (1, 2, 3, 4) is spun and a die is rolled. Find P(4 on the spinner and an even number on the die). *[3]*

**14.** A bag has 3 red and 7 blue marbles. A marble is drawn, put back, and a second marble is drawn. Find P(blue, then blue). *[3]*

**15.** The captain of an Edmonton hockey team has lost the opening coin toss five games in a row. He says: "I'm due to win the next one." Is he right? Explain using probability. *[3]*

**16.** A bag has 4 red and 6 blue marbles. Two marbles are drawn **without** replacement. Find P(red, then red). *[3]*

**17.** A box has 12 granola bars: 5 chocolate and 7 plain. Two bars are taken at random, one after the other, without putting the first back.
(a) Find P(both chocolate). *[2]*
(b) Would the probability be higher or lower if the first bar were put back? Explain. *[1]*

**18.** A Banff restaurant offers 3 main dishes and 4 desserts. A meal is one main and one dessert.
(a) How many different meals are possible? *[1]*
(b) Two of the mains are vegetarian and one dessert is dairy-free. If a meal is chosen at random, what is P(vegetarian main and dairy-free dessert)? *[2]*

## Part C — Application (Extended Response)

*Show all working. [8 marks] /8*

**19.** For a fundraiser, a box holds 10 tickets: 6 red, 3 gold and 1 black. A player draws tickets without looking.

(a) For one draw, find P(gold) and P(not black). *[2]*
(b) A player draws two tickets **without** replacement. Find P(gold, then gold). *[2]*
(c) Another version of the game puts the first ticket back before the second draw. Find P(gold, then gold), and explain why this answer is different from (b). *[2]*
(d) The class played the put-it-back version 50 times and got gold-gold 7 times. Compare the experimental probability with the theoretical probability from (c). Should the class be worried? Explain. *[2]*

## Part D — Thinking

*One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks] /4*

**20.** Lee says: "Taking one marble out of a bag hardly changes anything, so drawing without replacement gives the same probability as drawing with replacement."
Test Lee's claim with a bag of 2 red and 3 blue marbles, finding P(red, then red) both ways. When is Lee nearly right, and when is Lee wrong?
