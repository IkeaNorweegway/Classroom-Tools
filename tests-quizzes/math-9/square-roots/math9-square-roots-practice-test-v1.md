## Part A — Knowledge (Selected Response)

*Circle the best answer. [1 mark each] /8*

**1.** Which number is a perfect square?
A) 18 &nbsp; B) 32 &nbsp; C) 64 &nbsp; D) 200

**2.** √36 =
A) ±6 &nbsp; B) 6 &nbsp; C) 18 &nbsp; D) 1296

**3.** √55 is between which two whole numbers?
A) 5 and 6 &nbsp; B) 7 and 8 &nbsp; C) 27 and 28 &nbsp; D) 54 and 56

**4.** Which number is irrational?
A) √25 &nbsp; B) √0.16 &nbsp; C) √(9/4) &nbsp; D) √12

**5.** √(25/64) =
A) 5/8 &nbsp; B) 25/8 &nbsp; C) 5/64 &nbsp; D) 12.5/32

**6.** √0.81 =
A) 0.09 &nbsp; B) 0.9 &nbsp; C) 9 &nbsp; D) 0.405

**7.** In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is
A) the shortest side &nbsp; B) the side across from the right angle &nbsp; C) either side that touches the right angle &nbsp; D) the side that stands straight up

**8.** Which side lengths make a right triangle?
A) 4, 5, 6 &nbsp; B) 6, 8, 10 &nbsp; C) 5, 6, 7 &nbsp; D) 7, 8, 9

## Part B — Understanding (Short Answer)

*Show your working. [3 marks each] /30*

**9.** (a) List every perfect square between 100 and 200. *[2]*
(b) Is 150 a perfect square? Use two of your numbers from (a) to explain. *[1]*

**10.** Maya says √6.4 = 3.2, because half of 6.4 is 3.2.
(a) Is Maya correct? Show a check that proves your answer. *[2]*
(b) Find √0.64. *[1]*

**11.** Estimate √30 to one decimal place. Show the perfect squares you used, and check your estimate by squaring it. *[3]*

**12.** Mark and label √5, √10 and √20 on the number line (0 to 5, ticks every 0.5). *[3]*

**13.** A right triangle has legs of 5 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse. *[3]* [Diagram: right angle at bottom left; legs 5 cm (vertical) and 12 cm (horizontal); hypotenuse c.]

**14.** A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 12 cm and one leg of 6 cm. Find the other leg. Give the exact answer and an estimate to one decimal place. *[3]* [Diagram: vertical leg 6 cm, hypotenuse 12 cm, horizontal leg b.]

**15.** A student wrote: √(36 + 64) = √36 + √64 = 6 + 8 = 14.
What did the student do wrong? Give the correct answer, and show a check that proves 14 is wrong. *[3]*

**16.** Evaluate. *[1 each]*
(a) √(49/100) &nbsp; (b) √1.44 &nbsp; (c) √(4/9) + √0.25

**17.** A square garden has an area of 196 m².
(a) How long is each side? *[1]*
(b) Sam says: "If we make the garden twice as big, 392 m², each side will be twice as long, 28 m." Is Sam right? Explain. *[2]*

**18.** (a) Write a whole number whose square root is between 7 and 8. *[1]*
(b) Explain how you know. *[1]*
(c) Is the square root of your number rational or irrational? Explain. *[1]*

## Part C — Application (Extended Response)

*Show all working and write each answer with its units. [8 marks] /8*

**19.** A school field in Lethbridge is a rectangle 80 m long and 60 m wide. Students walk from corner A to corner C. [Diagram: rectangle ABCD, A bottom left, C top right, M the midpoint of the top side DC. Path 1 dashed A→C; Path 2 dashed A→M.]

(a) How long is Path 1, the diagonal from A to C? *[2]*
(b) How much shorter is Path 1 than walking around the edge from A to B to C? *[2]*
(c) Path 2 goes from A to M, the middle of the far long side. How long is Path 2? Give the exact answer. Is it closer to 70 m or 80 m? Show how you know. *[2]*
(d) The groundskeeper checks that corner A is a right angle. She measures 3 m along one side and 4 m along the other, then measures 5.2 m between the two marks. Is corner A a right angle? Explain using the Pythagorean theorem. *[2]*

## Part D — Thinking

*One question that asks you to judge a claim. [4 marks] /4*

**20.** A right triangle has legs of 30 cm and 50 cm. Two students try to find the hypotenuse without a calculator.
Jordan says: "Shrink it by 10. A triangle with legs 3 and 5 has a hypotenuse of √34, so the big triangle's hypotenuse is 10 × √34."
Riley says: "30² is 100 times bigger than 3², so the hypotenuse should be 100 × √34."
Who is correct? Find the hypotenuse the long way to check, and explain why. Then estimate the hypotenuse to the nearest centimetre.
