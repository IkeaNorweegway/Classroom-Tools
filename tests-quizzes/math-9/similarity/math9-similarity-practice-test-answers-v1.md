## Part B — Short Answer

**9.** (a) 3x = 60, so **x = 20** (b) 4(x − 1) = 60, so x − 1 = 15, so **x = 16**
- (a) 1 mark
- (b) 1 mark: cross-multiplies correctly; 1 mark: x = 16

**10.** 6/80 = x/300, so 80x = 1800, so x = **22.5 L**
- 1 mark: proportion with matching units in matching places
- 1 mark: solves it
- 1 mark: 22.5 L

**11.** (a) 10/6 = **5/3** (about 1.67) (b) FG = 9 × 5/3 = **15 cm** (c) ∠F = **110°** (corresponding angles are equal)
- 1 mark each

**12.** **No.** 6/4 = 1.5 but 8/6 ≈ 1.33. The sides are not in the same ratio, so the rectangles are not similar (even though all their angles are 90°).
- 1 mark: 6/4 = 1.5
- 1 mark: 8/6 ≈ 1.33
- 1 mark: not similar, because the ratios differ

**13.** (a) **12.5 cm, 17.5 cm, 20 cm** (b) Perimeters: 20 cm and 50 cm. 50 ÷ 20 = 2.5, the scale factor.
- (a) 2 marks: all three sides (1 mark for two)
- (b) 1 mark

**14.** ∠C = 180° − 40° − 75° = 65°. ∠E = 180° − 40° − 65° = 75°. So ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E and ∠C = ∠F. **Similar by AA: △ABC ~ △DEF.**
- 1 mark: finds the missing angles
- 1 mark: matches two pairs of angles (AA)
- 1 mark: similarity statement in the right order

**15.** ST/PQ = 4/6 = 2/3 and TU/QR = 6/9 = 2/3, and the included angles ∠Q = ∠T = 70°. **Similar by SAS.**
- 1 mark: both ratios
- 1 mark: notes the angle is between the two sides
- 1 mark: SAS

**16.** DE ∥ BC, so △ADE ~ △ABC (AA). AB = 4 + 6 = 10. BC/DE = AB/AD, so BC = 6 × 10/4 = **15 cm**.
- 1 mark: uses AB = 10 (not DB = 6)
- 1 mark: correct proportion
- 1 mark: 15 cm

**17.** 2.4 × 250 = 600 cm = **6 m**. 3.2 × 250 = 800 cm = **8 m**. The room is 6 m by 8 m.
- 1 mark: multiplies by 250
- 1 mark: converts to metres
- 1 mark: both dimensions

**18.** The sun's rays hit both at the same angle, so the triangles are similar. h/15 = 1.6/2.4, so h = 15 × 1.6 ÷ 2.4 = **10 m**.
- 1 mark: proportion with heights and shadows matched
- 1 mark: solves it
- 1 mark: 10 m

## Part D — Thinking

**20.** Model answer: Ryan is not correct. Similar polygons need equal angles **and** sides in the same ratio. Rectangles always have equal angles, so the angle condition is met, but the sides might not be in proportion. A 2 cm by 4 cm rectangle and a 2 cm by 8 cm rectangle: 2/2 = 1 but 8/4 = 2. The ratios are different, so they are not similar (one is long and thin). Some rectangles are similar, like 2 by 4 and 3 by 6 (both ratios 1.5), but not all of them.

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Full | 4 | Says Ryan is wrong; states both conditions; gives a counterexample with ratios; notes that equal angles alone are not enough for polygons |
| Substantial | 3 | Says Ryan is wrong with a correct counterexample and ratios, but doesn't name both conditions |
| Partial | 1–2 | A counterexample without ratios, or the two conditions without an example (2); says Ryan is wrong with no support (1) |
| Not demonstrated | 0 | Agrees with Ryan, or no relevant mathematics |
