## Part B — Short Answer

**9.** Rectangle: 10 × 6 = 60 cm². Semicircle: diameter 6, so r = 3: (1/2)π(3)² ≈ 14.1 cm². Total ≈ **74.1 cm²**.
- 1 mark: rectangle area
- 1 mark: semicircle area with r = 3 (not 6)
- 1 mark: total with units

**10.** SA = 2(10 × 4) + 2(10 × 3) + 2(4 × 3) = 80 + 60 + 24 = **164 cm²**
- 2 marks: all three pairs of faces (1 mark for two pairs)
- 1 mark: 164 cm²

**11.** SA = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2π(9) + 2π(3)(10) = 18π + 60π = 78π ≈ **245.0 cm²**
- 1 mark: the two circles (18π)
- 1 mark: the curved side (60π)
- 1 mark: 245.0 cm²

**12.** Base area = (6 × 4) ÷ 2 = 12 cm². V = Bh = 12 × 10 = **120 cm³**
- 1 mark: triangle area 12
- 1 mark: multiplies by the length 10
- 1 mark: 120 cm³

**13.** V = πr²h = π(1.5)²(4) = 9π ≈ **28.3 m³**
- 1 mark: formula with r = 1.5
- 1 mark: 9π
- 1 mark: 28.3 m³

**14.** Main shed: 6 × 10 × 4 = 240 m³. Roof: (6 × 3 ÷ 2) × 10 = 90 m³. Total = **330 m³**
- 1 mark: 240 m³
- 1 mark: 90 m³
- 1 mark: total with units

**15.** (a) V = (1/3)(10²)(12) = (1/3)(100)(12) = **400 cm³** (b) SA = 10² + 2(10)(13) = 100 + 260 = **360 cm²**
- (a) 1 mark: base area × height; 1 mark: divides by 3, 400 cm³
- (b) 1 mark

**16.** (a) SA = 4π(12)² = 576π ≈ **1809.6 cm²** (b) V = (4/3)π(12)³ = 2304π ≈ **7238.2 cm³**
- (a) 1 mark
- (b) 1 mark: (4/3)π × 1728; 1 mark: 7238.2 cm³

**17.** (a) l = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = **13 cm** (b) SA = π(5)² + π(5)(13) = 25π + 65π = 90π ≈ **282.7 cm²**
- (a) 1 mark
- (b) 1 mark: base 25π and side 65π; 1 mark: 282.7 cm²

**18.** Cylinder: π(3)²(10) = 90π. Cone: (1/3)π(3)²(4) = 12π. Total = 102π ≈ **320.4 m³**
- 1 mark: cylinder volume
- 1 mark: cone volume (with the one-third)
- 1 mark: total with units

## Part C — Extended Response

**19.** (a) A: π(4)²(10) = 160π ≈ **502.7 cm³**. B: 7 × 7 × 10 = **490 cm³**.
- 1 mark each

(b) A: 2π(4)² + 2π(4)(10) = 32π + 80π = 112π ≈ **351.9 cm²**. B: 2(7 × 7) + 4(7 × 10) = 98 + 280 = **378 cm²**.
- 1 mark each

(c) **Container A** holds more (502.7 cm³ vs 490 cm³) **and** uses less material (351.9 cm² vs 378 cm²), so A is the better choice. (Accept B if the reason is about packing or stacking boxes, as long as the numbers are compared correctly.)
- 1 mark: compares both volume and surface area correctly
- 1 mark: a recommendation with a reason

(d) Cone: (1/3)π(4)²(10) = (1/3)(160π) ≈ **167.6 cm³**. That is one-third of Container A, not half.
- 1 mark: 167.6 cm³
- 1 mark: says it is one-third, not half

## Part D — Thinking

**20.** Model answer: Owen is not correct. Take a box 2 cm × 3 cm × 4 cm: V = 24 cm³. Double every edge: 4 cm × 6 cm × 8 cm, V = 192 cm³. 192 ÷ 24 = 8, so the volume is 8 times as big, not 2 times. Each of the three dimensions is doubled, so the volume is multiplied by 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. To double the volume, you would double only one of the dimensions.

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Full | 4 | Says Owen is wrong; a correct example with both volumes; states the volume is 8 times as big; explains 2 × 2 × 2 |
| Substantial | 3 | Correct example and "8 times", but no explanation of why |
| Partial | 1–2 | A correct example showing it is more than double (2); or says Owen is wrong with no example (1) |
| Not demonstrated | 0 | Agrees with Owen, or no relevant mathematics |
