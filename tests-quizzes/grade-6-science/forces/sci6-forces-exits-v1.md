# Grade 6 Science — Forces
## Exit Tickets
*One per section · Collected at end of class · Not for marks*
*3–4 minutes | Independent — no notes*

---

## How to use

Give students the questions verbally, project them, or copy them onto slips to cut and hand out. Collect at the door. Read before the next class and note any patterns before planning the retrieval warm-up.

**What to look for:** Not how many students got it right — which specific wrong answers are clustered. One common error shared by a third of the class is a re-teach moment; scattered errors are individual gaps.

*(Tension · Compression · Shear · Torsion)*

---

**Teacher read:**
- **Q1:** All four required — tension, compression, shear, torsion. Students who give three have a gap worth tracking.
- **Q2:** Answer: tension. Particles are pulled apart / away from each other. Students who write "shear" have confused the two — the rope is being pulled along its axis, not sliding in layers sideways.
- **Q3 answers:** scissors → **shear** | cable → **tension** | wet cloth → **torsion** | chair leg → **compression**
- **Most common errors:** Scissors labeled as torsion (blade motion looks rotational but cuts linearly — that's shear). Wet cloth labeled as shear (wringing is a twist around an axis — that's torsion). If 3+ students share either error, run the foam block demonstration again at the start of the next class before moving to Section 4.

**3.** Why does helmet foam need to deform plastically in a collision — not elastically? *(1 mark)*

_______________________________________________

_______________________________________________

---

**Teacher read:**
- **Q1:** Elastic deformation — returns to original shape when force is removed. Key word: temporary.
- **Q2(a):** Elastic limit.
- **Q2(b):** Plastic deformation — the material stays deformed after the force is removed. Permanent change.
- **Q3:** Plastic deformation keeps the foam crushed, absorbing the impact force. Elastic deformation would cause the foam to spring back — bouncing the force into the skull rather than absorbing it. Students who write "absorbs energy" without explaining why plastic is better than elastic: prompt "what would elastic deformation do instead?"
- **Vocabulary flag:** If students use "plastic" to mean the material (plastic bag, plastic bottle), they may have the vocabulary collision flagged in Section 4 Beat 3. A quick individual redirect: "In physics, plastic means permanently shaped — from the Greek word for shaping. The material 'plastic' got its name from this property, not the other way around."

_______________________________________________

_______________________________________________

_______________________________________________

**3.** A student says: "A truck hits a bicycle — the truck pushed harder on the bicycle, that's why the bicycle got destroyed." What error is the student making? *(1 mark)*

_______________________________________________

_______________________________________________

---

**Teacher read:**
- **Q1:** All three properties required for full marks: equal in magnitude / opposite in direction / acting on different objects. Students who write only two lose 1 mark. The most commonly omitted property is "different objects" — which is also the most important one. Students who miss this will fail Q2.
- **Q2:** Forces can only cancel when they act on the SAME object. The force on the wall acts ON the wall. The force on you acts ON you. Two different objects — nothing to cancel. Students who write "they cancel but friction holds you in place" are confusing force pairs with balance — address individually before the test.
- **Q3:** Newton's Third Law has no exceptions for size or mass. The forces ARE equal. The difference in outcome (bicycle destroyed, truck fine) is about mass and acceleration: same force, much smaller mass on the bicycle → much larger acceleration → much more damage. If students say "the truck is heavier so it pushes harder" — that is exactly the misconception flagged in Section 5 Beat 4. Flag for direct re-teach if more than a third write this.
