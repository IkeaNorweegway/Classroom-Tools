## Section 1: Equivalent ratios
Target: I can make equal ratios by multiplying or dividing both terms. (RP1)
### Juice is 1 can of concentrate to 4 cans of water. Fill in the double number line.
@viz dnl top="0,1,2,3" bottom="0,4,[[8]],[[12]]" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water"
(lines 1)
=> 1:4 = 2:8 = 3:12. Multiply both terms by 2, then by 3.
### Write one ratio equal to 6:9 by multiplying and one by dividing. What is the factor from 6:9 to 9:13.5?
(lines 2)
=> × 2: 12:18. ÷ 3: 2:3 (or ÷ by any common factor).
=> 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 and 13.5 ÷ 9 = 1.5. The factor is 1.5.
### Raj says 2:5 = 3:6, because he added 1 to both terms. Explain how you know he is wrong.
(lines 2)
=> Adding changes the mix. Equal ratios multiply both terms by the same number: 2:5 = 4:10. Also 3:6 = 1:2, which has less water for each can.

## Section 2: Missing terms and comparing ratios
Target: I can find a missing term and compare two ratios. (RP2)
### The ratio of Oilers fans to Flames fans is 4:3. There are 27 Flames fans. How many Oilers fans?
@viz tape parts="4:Oilers fans;3:Flames fans" values="[[9]]" total="27 Flames fans"
(lines 1)
=> 27 ÷ 3 = 9 in each part. 4 × 9 = 36 Oilers fans.
### Find the missing term. 6:8 = 15:?
(lines 1)
=> 6:8 = 3:4 (÷ 2). Then × 5: 15:20. Check: 6 × 20 = 120 = 8 × 15.
### Which juice is stronger: 2:5 or 3:8? Explain how you know.
(lines 2)
=> Make the first terms match: 2:5 = 6:15 and 3:8 = 6:16. Same concentrate, and 2:5 has less water. So 2:5 is stronger.

## Section 3: Percents, including less than 1% and more than 100%
Target: I can find percents of numbers, even below 1% and above 100%. (RP3)
### Find 35% of 40. Fill in the bar.
@viz percentbar percent=35 top="0%,10%,25%,35%,100%" bottom="0,[[4]],[[10]],[[14]],40" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="of 40"
(lines 1)
=> 25% of 40 = 10. 10% of 40 = 4. 35% = 10 + 4 = 14.
### Find 0.5% of 200 and 150% of 200.
(lines 1)
=> 1% of 200 = 2, so 0.5% = 1. 150% = 200 + 100 = 300.
### Noor says 120% of 50 is less than 50. Explain how you know she is wrong.
(lines 2)
=> 100% is all of 50. 120% is more than one whole: 50 + 10 = 60. So it is more than 50.

## Section 4: Percent and ratio problems
Target: I can solve problems with discounts, tax, recipes, unit rates and units. (RP4)
### A $40 cap is 25% off. Then 5% GST is added. Find the total.
@viz receipt items="Cap:$40.00;25% off:[[−$10.00]];Sale price:[[$30.00]];GST (5%):[[$1.50]];Total:[[$31.50]]"
(lines 2)
=> 25% of $40 = $10. $40 − $10 = $30. 5% of $30 = $1.50. Total $31.50.
### Which is the better buy: 2 L of juice for $3.60 or 3 L for $5.10? Change 1.5 m to cm.
(lines 2)
=> $3.60 ÷ 2 = $1.80 per L. $5.10 ÷ 3 = $1.70 per L. The 3 L jug is the better buy.
=> 1.5 × 100 = 150 cm.
### A $50 game goes 10% off, then the price goes up 10%. Kai says it is back to $50. Explain how you know he is wrong.
(lines 2)
=> 10% of $50 = $5, so $45. 10% of $45 = $4.50, so $49.50. The second 10% is of a smaller number.
