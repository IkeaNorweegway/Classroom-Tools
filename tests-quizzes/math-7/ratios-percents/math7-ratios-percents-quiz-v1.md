### [RP1 · 2] Write the ratio of concentrate to water. Then write the ratio of concentrate to all the juice.

=> 2:7 (1 mark). 2:9, or {2/9} of the juice (1 mark).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Milk (cups) | 6 | ___ | ___ | ___ |
(lines 1)
=> 12 (× 2), 3 (÷ 2), 15 (× 2.5). 1 mark each.

### [RP1 · 2] Find the factor. 8:6 → 12:9
@viz dnl top="0,4,8,12" bottom="0,3,6,9" toplabel="first" bottomlabel="second"
(lines 1)
=> 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 and 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 (1 mark). The factor is 1.5, or {3/2} (1 mark).

### [RP1 · 2] Write 2.5:1 and {1/2}:3 as ratios with whole-number terms.
(lines 1)
=> 5:2 (× 2) (1 mark). 1:6 (× 2) (1 mark).

### [RP2 · 2] At Rogers Place, the ratio of adults to kids in one row is 5:2. There are 8 kids. How many adults are in the row?
@viz tape parts="5:adults;2:kids" total="8 kids"
(lines 1)
=> 8 ÷ 2 = 4, so the factor is 4 (1 mark). 5 × 4 = 20 adults (1 mark).

### [RP2 · 3] Find each missing term.
a) 3:7 = 12:?
b) 9:6 = 15:?
c) 4:5 = ?:35
(lines 2)
=> a) 28 (1 mark). b) 9:6 = 3:2, then × 5: 10 (1 mark). c) 28 (× 7) (1 mark).

### [RP2 · 2] 2:3 and 6:9 are equivalent. Add the first terms and the second terms. Show that the new ratio is equivalent.
(lines 1)
=> 8:12 (1 mark). 8:12 ÷ 4 = 2:3 (1 mark).

### [RP2 · 2] Use cross products. Is 4:6 = 6:9? Is 4:6 = 5:8?
(lines 1)
=> 4 × 9 = 36 and 6 × 6 = 36: yes (1 mark). 4 × 8 = 32 and 6 × 5 = 30: no (1 mark).

### [RP2 · 5] Juice A is 3:5 (concentrate to water). Juice B is 4:7. Leo says B is stronger, because 4 cans is more than 3. Is he right? Explain your thinking. Use a picture or a table.
(lines 4)
=> Make a common first term: 3:5 = 12:20 and 4:7 = 12:21 (2 marks).
=> Same concentrate; A has less water (1 mark). So A is stronger, and Leo is wrong (1 mark).
=> He forgot that B also has more water (1 mark).
