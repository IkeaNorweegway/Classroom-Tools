## Section 1: Lines, rays, segments, angles, polygons
Target: I can tell apart lines, rays, segments, angles and polygons. I can name them with symbols. (LA1)
### Name each object.
@viz lines items="segment:P,Q;ray:R,S;angle:T,U,V" notitle=1
(lines 1)
=> Segment PQ. Ray RS. ∠TUV (or ∠VUT or ∠U).
### Is this shape a polygon? Explain how you know.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;4,0;3,3;0,2" unit=22
(lines 2)
=> Yes. It is closed. Its 4 sides are segments that meet only at their ends.

## Section 2: Parallel and perpendicular
Target: I can show parallel and perpendicular lines with marks and symbols. (LA2)
### Write one parallel pair and one perpendicular pair with symbols.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;5,3;0,3" names="J,K,L,M" right="1,2,3,4" par="1,2,1,2" unit=24
(lines 1)
=> JK ∥ ML (or JM ∥ KL). JK ⊥ KL (or any two sides that meet at a corner).
### Two roads look parallel on a map. There are no arrowheads. Are they parallel? Explain how you know.
(lines 2)
=> We can't tell. Lines that look parallel may meet far away. We need arrowheads or a measurement.

## Section 3: Angles where lines cross
Target: I can find angles where two lines cross. I can use angles to check if lines are parallel. (LA3)
### Find ∠2, ∠3 and ∠4.
@viz angles kind=intersect a=55 labels="55°;2;3;4"
(lines 2)
=> ∠2 = 180° − 55° = 125°. ∠3 = 55° (opposite the 55° angle). ∠4 = 125° (opposite ∠2).
### Is m ∥ n? Explain how you know.
@viz angles kind=transversal a=60 marks=0 labels="1;60°;3;4;5;60°;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t"
(lines 2)
=> Yes. ∠2 and ∠6 are corresponding angles. Both are 60°, so the lines are parallel.

## Section 4: Congruent polygons
Target: I can match the parts of congruent polygons. I can write a congruence statement. (LA4)
### △ABC ≅ △RST. Find RS and ∠T.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;2,3" names="A,B,C" sides="6 cm;;" angles=";;87°" pts2="0,0;6,0;2,3" names2="R,S,T" sides2="[[6 cm]];;" angles2=";;[[87°]]" unit=22
(lines 1)
=> RS matches AB: 6 cm. ∠T matches ∠C: 87°.
### Leo writes △ABC ≅ △TSR for the triangles above. Is he right? Explain how you know.
(lines 2)
=> No. △ABC ≅ △TSR says A matches T. But A matches R. The letter order must show the match: △ABC ≅ △RST.
