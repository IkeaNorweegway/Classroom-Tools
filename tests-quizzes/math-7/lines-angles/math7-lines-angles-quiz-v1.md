### [LA1 · 3] Name each object with its letters.
@viz lines items="line:A,B;ray:C,D;segment:E,F"
(lines 1)
=> Line AB (or BA). Ray CD (endpoint C first). Segment EF (or FE). 1 mark each.

### [LA1 · 2] Give two names for this angle.
@viz lines items="angle:P,Q,R,60" notitle=1
(lines 1)
=> Any two of ∠PQR, ∠RQP and ∠Q. 1 mark each. No mark for a name with Q not in the middle (except ∠Q).

### [LA1 · 2] Draw ray ST. Then draw segment UV. Label the points.
(work 3)
=> Ray: starts at S, goes through T, one arrowhead past T (1 mark). Segment: dots at U and V, no arrowheads (1 mark).

### [LA1 · 2] Is a shape with a gap in it a polygon? Explain.
(lines 2)
=> No (1 mark). A polygon must be closed (1 mark).

### [LA2 · 3] Write the parallel and perpendicular sides with symbols.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;5,3;0,3" names="A,B,C,D" right="1,2,3,4" par="1,2,1,2" unit=24
(lines 2)
=> AB ∥ DC (1 mark). AD ∥ BC (1 mark). Any perpendicular pair, like AB ⊥ BC (1 mark).

### [LA2 · 2] What does each mark mean? a) a small square in a corner b) matching arrowheads on two lines
(lines 2)
=> a) A right angle, so the lines are perpendicular (1 mark). b) The lines are parallel (1 mark).

### [LA3 · 3] Two lines cross. Find ∠2, ∠3 and ∠4.
@viz angles kind=intersect a=50 labels="50°;2;3;4"
(lines 2)
=> ∠2 = 180° − 50° = 130°. ∠3 = 50°. ∠4 = 130°. 1 mark each.

### [LA3 · 3] m ∥ n. ∠2 = 75°. Find ∠6, ∠1 and ∠5.
@viz angles kind=transversal a=75 labels="1;75°;3;4;5;6;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t"
(lines 2)
=> ∠6 = 75° (corresponds to ∠2). ∠1 = 180° − 75° = 105°. ∠5 = 105° (corresponds to ∠1). 1 mark each.

### [LA3 · 2] Are m and n parallel? Say why.
@viz angles kind=transversal a=60 parallel=0 marks=0 labels="1;60°;3;4;5;55°;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t"
(lines 2)
=> No (1 mark). ∠2 and ∠6 are corresponding, but 60° ≠ 55° (1 mark).

### [LA3 · 2] Explain your thinking. Mia says opposite angles add to 180°. Is she right? Use a drawing or numbers.
(lines 3)
=> No (1 mark). Opposite angles are equal, like 50° and 50°. Angles side by side on a line add to 180° (1 mark).
