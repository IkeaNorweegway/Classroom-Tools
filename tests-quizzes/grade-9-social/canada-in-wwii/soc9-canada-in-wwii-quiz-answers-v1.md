# Canada in the Second World War — Mid-Unit Quiz — Answer Key
Teacher use only · Keep off student copies

1. **1939.** (1 mark)
2. Parliament chose to declare war for itself, a week after Britain, rather than being brought into the war by Britain's declaration. (2 marks)
3. Any one: Dieppe, Ortona, Juno Beach, the Scheldt, Hong Kong. (1 mark)
4. Dieppe (1942) was a costly raid on the French coast; it taught the Allies lessons used later in the D-Day landings. (2 marks)
5. Any one group: Chinese Canadians or Cree code talkers, with an explanation (e.g. discrimination, secrecy of the work). (2 marks)
6. Cree code talkers used Cree language in radio communications, which was hard for the enemy to decode; their work helped secure messages. (3 marks: role, method, effect)
7. Any one: Canada became a founding member of the UN and NATO, and a more independent voice. (1 mark)
8. Canada's wartime role gave it a seat at international talks and a commitment to collective security, which shaped NATO, the UN and its foreign policy after 1945. (4 marks: wartime role, international standing, a post-war institution, conclusion)

**Common errors to watch for:** "Canada was part of the war only because Britain declared it" (Q2); "code talkers were only in the Second World War in the United States" (Q5).
