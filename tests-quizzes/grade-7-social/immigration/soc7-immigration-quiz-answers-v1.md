# Immigration After Confederation — Mid-Unit Quiz: Answer Key
Grade 7 Social Studies · Teacher copy

**Total:** /17 · Covers notes Sections 1–4.

**1.** [IM2] 160 acres. *(1)*

**2.** [IM2] Homesteads of 160 acres for a $10 fee were offered on the Prairies, so families who could not own land at home moved there; many settled near the railway or near people of their own background. *(1 for the land offer, 1 for how it shaped the choice)*

**3.** [IM1] Any two, with a place: Ukrainians (east of Edmonton, Vegreville, Mundare); Francophones (Saint-Paul, Bonnyville, Legal); Black settlers from Oklahoma (Amber Valley); British or American settlers (across the Prairies). *(1 each)*

**4.** [IM1] They farmed and grew wheat for export, built towns, kept the railway busy, bought goods from eastern factories. *(1 for the idea, 1 for explaining how it helped)*

**5.** [IM4] Chinese immigrants. *(1)*

**6.** [IM4] Immigrants had to arrive by a non-stop trip from their home country; there were no non-stop ships from India; so most immigrants from India were kept out. *(1 each)*

**7.** [IM4] A one-year ban on Black immigrants (1911); white settlers and newspapers protested Black farmers from Oklahoma; racist beliefs about who belonged. It was cancelled before taking effect. *(1 for the ban, 1 for the context, 1 for the reason)*

**8.** [IM1] Opportunity: homesteads, farms, the wheat economy, towns. Conflict: laws like the head tax and the Continuous Journey Regulation, protests against Black settlers. Top marks connect both to the government's choices about who could come. *(1 + 1 + 1)*
