Project-Based Learning: What the Evidence Says

Research one-pager · Oct 2026 · full summary: research/project-based-learning-research.md
Core finding: PBL lifts achievement and engagement when the project is the main vehicle for learning and the teacher builds explicit instruction, scaffolds, and critique-and-revision cycles into it. Unstructured "go research it" projects drift into minimal-guidance discovery, which underperforms.

Project-based ≠ problem-based. Hattie's 0.15 is mostly medical-school problem-based learning. Pooled PBL estimates clear the 0.40 hinge, but the spread is wide. The near-zero North American subgroup is why the US trials above are the better guide.

Best practice: do / don't

Do

  • Make the project the vehicle for the content
  • Open with a driving question that creates a "need to know"
  • Schedule mini-lessons + worked examples inside the project
  • Build in critique → revise cycles (multiple drafts)
  • Teams of 4–5 with roles
  • Checkpoint milestones with feedback students act on
  • Present to a real audience, then reflect

Don't

  • "Go research it" with minimal guidance
  • Use projects to replace knowledge-building
  • Run "dessert" projects after the unit test
  • Make groups larger than 5
  • Grade only the final product
  • Assume students see it as authentic. Ask them
  • Stretch one project past ~18 weeks

Read the evidence honestly

Implementation is everything. MDRC (2017): "promising but not yet proven." Gains depend on strong curriculum and sustained teacher support. No Alberta RCT exists, though Galileo's Focus on Inquiry (36 Alberta schools) found widespread misunderstanding of what inquiry actually is. AI-supported PBL looks promising, but the studies are small and short.
