# Vertical Non-Permanent Surfaces (VNPS) in Mathematics
*Research summary — classroom delivery practice*

---

## Topic and Why It Matters

Vertical non-permanent surfaces (VNPS) are wall-mounted whiteboards, windows, or any erasable vertical surface used for collaborative student problem-solving in small groups. The practice is central to Peter Liljedahl's *Building Thinking Classrooms* framework and has become one of the most widely adopted structural changes in K-12 mathematics in Canada over the past decade. Understanding what the evidence actually supports — and what it doesn't — matters before embedding this in curriculum design or teacher notes.

---

## Core Finding

Students working at vertical erasable surfaces begin tasks faster, stay on task longer, discuss more, and make their thinking visible in ways that improve formative assessment. The effects are most robustly documented for engagement proxies. No peer-reviewed study has established a link between VNPS use and improved test scores, retention, or long-term learning outcomes.

The practical conclusion: VNPS is a high-value structure for thinking and formative assessment — not a proven achievement intervention.

---

## Key Studies

**Liljedahl, P. (2016).** Building thinking classrooms: Conditions for problem solving. In Felmer, Kilpatrick, & Pekhonen (Eds.), *Posing and Solving Mathematical Problems.* Springer.
- Design-based research across 5 high school classes comparing five surface conditions: VNPS, horizontal non-permanent (HNPS), vertical permanent (chart paper on wall), horizontal permanent (chart paper on desk), and notebooks.
- Measured 8 engagement proxies: time to task, time to first notation, discussion, eagerness, participation, persistence, knowledge mobility, and non-linearity of work.
- **VNPS outperformed all other conditions on 7 of 8 measures.** Students at VNPS reportedly spent approximately 50% more time on task than the next-best condition (HNPS).
- **Limitation:** n = 5 classes; no learning outcome measures; book chapter, not peer-reviewed journal.

**Liljedahl, P. (2019).** Conditions for supporting problem solving: Vertical non-permanent surfaces. In Felmer et al. (Eds.), Springer. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-030-10472-6_13
- Extends the VNPS findings and articulates the mechanisms (erasability, visibility, posture, knowledge mobility) with additional classroom observation data.

**Liljedahl, P. (2020).** *Building Thinking Classrooms in Mathematics, Grades K-12: 14 Teaching Practices for Enhancing Learning.* Corwin Press.
- Full practitioner synthesis of 15 years of design research with 400+ K-12 teachers across North America. The broader research program is referenced here; the controlled surface comparison is a subset.

**No independent replication exists.** As of 2025, there is no published randomized or quasi-experimental study by researchers outside Liljedahl's group that measures student learning outcomes attributable to VNPS.

---

## Effect Sizes and Outcome Data

No standardized effect sizes are reported in the VNPS literature. The data that exists is:

- **Time on task:** VNPS groups reportedly ~50% more time on task than HNPS groups (Liljedahl, 2016). Exact figures are observational, not measured with standardized instruments.
- **Participation:** 100% participation reported in VNPS conditions vs. substantially lower in seated/paper conditions across observation data.
- **Knowledge mobility:** Documented qualitatively — groups at visible vertical surfaces scan and incorporate strategies from other groups at rates not observed in seated work.
- **Teacher uptake data** (n = 300 teachers, ResearchGate): broad adoption, but uptake ≠ learning outcome data.

There are no published effect sizes on academic achievement (assessment scores, retention, transfer) from any VNPS study.

## Why Vertical and Why Non-Permanent — The Mechanisms

**Vertical matters because:**
- Upright posture correlates with alertness (embodied cognition research, though direct classroom evidence is sparse)
- Work is publicly visible — the teacher can do formative assessment by scanning the room rather than circulating to read notebooks
- Knowledge mobility: students can look around and see other groups' approaches — not copying solutions, but borrowing strategies they must then reconstruct
- Standing promotes movement, gesture, and discussion in ways sitting does not

**Non-permanent matters because:**
- Paper commits work. Writing something down on paper (especially in a notebook) has permanent-record quality — students delay writing until confident, measurably slowing engagement
- An erasable surface signals that drafts, attempts, and errors are normal — students begin writing sooner and revise more freely
- The shared, impermanent nature of board work reduces personal exposure from errors — the mistake belongs to the group's draft, not the student's permanent record
- Even vertical permanent surfaces (chart paper on wall) underperformed VNPS substantially, confirming erasability is a distinct and significant factor

## What It Looks Like in Practice

**Group size:** Three students is the research-recommended size. Two gives insufficient idea diversity; four risks one person disengaging with the marker held by someone else.

**Grouping method:** Visibly random grouping (VRG) — card draws, random name generators — Liljedahl found breaks existing social norms ("studenting" behaviors) within roughly six weeks of consistent use. Assigned groups by the teacher are associated with faster settling of group dynamics.

**Surface types that work:**
- Wall-mounted whiteboards (most effective)
- Windows with dry-erase markers (effective and zero cost)
- Chalkboards
- Shower board / tileboard (home improvement stores, ~$15/sheet — can cover a wall)
- Wipebook-style reusable sheets adhered to walls

**Room setup:** Enough surface area for every group to work simultaneously. In rooms without full whiteboard walls, windows and tileboard panels supplement. Groups don't sit — they stand at their surface.

**Teacher moves:**
- Monitor by scanning the room from a position that sees all surfaces simultaneously — formative assessment in real time
- Use hints rather than explanations when groups are stuck (preserve thinking)
- Levelled questions: ask finished groups to extend; ask stuck groups a simpler version of the same problem
- Use focusing questions ("What are you thinking here?") rather than funnelling questions ("Try multiplying those two numbers...")

**Task type matters:** VNPS works best with problems that have multiple entry points and approaches. Pure procedural drill on whiteboards gains some engagement benefit but misses the knowledge mobility and reasoning benefits. Problems should be accessible to start but cognitively demanding to complete.

## What It Doesn't Support

These are honest limitations — important before citing this research in teacher-facing materials.

**No learning outcome evidence.** Every VNPS study measures engagement proxies (time on task, participation, notation rate). No study has shown that VNPS use improves quiz scores, unit test results, long-term retention, or transfer to novel problems. Longer thinking time creates *conditions* for better learning — it does not guarantee it.

**Small sample, non-peer-reviewed.** The core VNPS comparison was five high school classes. No peer-reviewed journal has published and gatekept the VNPS findings. All primary publications are book chapters and conference proceedings.

**Novelty effects may be real.** Most observations occur in weeks/months following implementation. Whether engagement effects persist long-term is undocumented.

**Individual accountability is harder to track.** One capable student at a shared surface can do most of the work while others observe. VNPS changes the structure but does not eliminate free-rider dynamics.

**Cognitive load concerns for novice learners.** The BTC framework assumes productive struggle drives learning for all students. Cognitive load theory research indicates novices benefit more from worked examples and direct instruction than from open-ended problem-solving. VNPS is a surface, not a pedagogy — but it is most commonly embedded in a discovery/problem-based context that may disadvantage students with gaps in prior knowledge.

**Potential for copying rather than knowledge mobility.** Public visibility of work can facilitate groups copying solutions rather than thinking through strategies. The distinction between copying and genuine knowledge mobility depends on task design and teacher monitoring — it is not automatic.

## Alberta / Canada Context

Peter Liljedahl is a Canadian researcher at Simon Fraser University (SFU, Burnaby, BC). His 15-year design research program was conducted primarily in K-12 classrooms across Canada. Alberta's revised K-6 Mathematics curriculum (2022) and the ongoing 7-9 renewal both emphasize conceptual understanding, reasoning, and communication — goals that VNPS is directly designed to support structurally. Alberta is an active BTC adopter, though no published Alberta-specific VNPS outcome data exists.

## Implications for This Workspace

**Where VNPS fits:**
- **Lesson plans:** Include VNPS as a recommended structure for practice and problem-solving phases. Note group size (3), grouping method (VRG), and teacher monitoring position.
- **Workbooks and worksheets:** Task design matters more than the surface. Problems that support VNPS well have multiple entry points and non-routine approaches — identify these explicitly when they appear.
- **Teacher notes:** Frame VNPS honestly — high value for engagement and formative assessment; unproven for test scores. Teachers can use it confidently for the engagement benefits without overselling it as an achievement tool.

**Tier 1 status:** VNPS qualifies as a Tier 1 classroom structure for engagement and formative assessment. It should not be classified as a Tier 1 cognitive strategy (retrieval practice, spaced practice, worked examples), which have direct learning outcome evidence.
