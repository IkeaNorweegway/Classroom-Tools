1# Math 9 — Course Context
*Alberta Mathematics Grade 9 (2023 Curriculum)*

---

## Course Overview

Math 9 builds the algebraic and geometric reasoning students need for senior math. The course runs on two parallel tracks: symbolic fluency (manipulating expressions and equations) and relational reasoning (recognizing and interpreting relationships in tables, graphs, and equations). Neither track is more important — students who can only manipulate symbols without reading relationships hit a ceiling in Grade 10; students who can only reason relationally hit a wall on algebra-heavy tests.

**Four curriculum strands (as organized in these materials):**

| Strand | Topics | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Rational numbers, Powers & exponents, Square roots | Tier 1 |
| Patterns & Relations | Linear relations, Linear equations, Linear inequalities | Tier 1 |
| Shape & Space | Circle geometry, Surface area & volume, Similarity & scale | Tier 2 |
| Statistics & Probability | Scatter plots & correlation, Probability (independent/dependent) | Tier 2 |

---

## Depth Ceilings by Strand

**Number:**
- Rational numbers: operations with fractions and decimals including negative rational numbers; no complex fractions (fraction within fraction)
- Powers: integer exponents only; no rational exponents in this course (√ and x^(1/2) are connected but the generalization stays at the concept level)
- Square roots: principal square root; estimating non-perfect squares; Pythagorean theorem applications; irrational numbers as a concept (not formal proof)

**Patterns & Relations:**
- Linear relations: y = mx + b form; graphing; slope as rise/run and as a rate of change; direct and partial variation; NOT systems of equations (Grade 10)
- Equations: multi-step linear equations with rational coefficients; NOT quadratic equations
- Inequalities: one-variable linear inequalities; graphing on number line; NOT compound inequalities

**Shape & Space:**
- Circle geometry: chord properties, central and inscribed angle theorem, tangent-radius property; NO formal proof of theorems (descriptive/investigative)
- Measurement: right prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, spheres; composite objects; NO oblique prisms or advanced solids
- Similarity: AA/SSS/SAS criteria for triangles; scale factors; indirect measurement; NOT trigonometry (Grade 10)

**Statistics & Probability:**
- Scatter plots: by-inspection line of best fit; positive/negative/no correlation; interpolation and extrapolation; NOT r-values or regression formulas
- Probability: two-event sample spaces; independent and dependent events; NOT permutations or combinations

## Key Misconceptions — By Strand

### Number

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| "Add fractions: add numerators AND add denominators" (1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5) | Need common denominator; only numerators add once denominator is equal |
| "Dividing by a fraction makes it smaller" | Dividing by a fraction < 1 gives a larger result |
| "√9 = ±3" | Principal square root is the positive root only; √9 = 3 |
| "√(a + b) = √a + √b" | False — demonstrate numerically: √(9+16) ≠ √9 + √16 |
| "A negative exponent means a negative answer" | Negative exponent = reciprocal; the sign of the result depends on the base |
| "3⁰ = 0" | Any non-zero base to the 0 power = 1; shown via the division pattern |

### Patterns & Relations

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| "Slope is the y-intercept" | Slope = steepness (rise/run); y-intercept = where line crosses the y-axis |
| "y-intercept is where the line crosses the x-axis" | That is the x-intercept; the y-intercept is on the y-axis |
| "A steeper line always has a larger slope value" | Only when both slopes are positive; a very steep negative slope is a large negative number |
| "When you divide by a negative, flip the sign in equations too" | In equations, the equality holds regardless of sign; flipping only applies to inequalities |
| "An equation must have exactly one solution" | Identities (0 = 0) have infinitely many; contradictions (0 = 5) have none |

### Shape & Space

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| "All angles inscribed in a circle equal 90°" | Only angles inscribed in a semicircle (subtending a diameter) equal 90° |
| "A chord is the same as a diameter" | A diameter is a chord through the centre; not all chords are diameters |
| "Volume and surface area measure the same thing" | SA = total outer area (2D); Volume = space inside (3D) |
| "A cone has twice the volume of the matching cylinder" | Cone = ⅓ of cylinder; same relationship as pyramid to prism |
| "Similar means identical" | Similar = same shape, different size; identical = congruent |
| "Congruent and similar are the same" | Congruent requires equal size AND shape; similar only requires equal shape |

### Statistics & Probability

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| "Correlation means causation" | Two variables can vary together for reasons unrelated to one causing the other |
| "A strong correlation = exact relationship" | Correlation shows tendency, not certainty; the line of best fit is a model |
| "Getting tails 5 times means heads is 'due'" (Gambler's Fallacy) | Independent events have no memory; prior outcomes do not affect future ones |
| "Theoretical probability tells you what WILL happen" | It tells you what SHOULD happen in the long run; individual results vary |
| "Removing an item from a group doesn't change probability" | Sampling without replacement makes events dependent — the sample space shrinks |

## Learning Sequence Recommendations

Build in this order within each strand to respect conceptual dependencies:

**Number:** Rational numbers → Powers & exponents → Square roots
*(Rational numbers first because exponent laws involve rational operations; square roots connect to both)*

**Patterns & Relations:** Linear relations → Linear equations → Linear inequalities
*(Relations provide the graphical/conceptual foundation; equations are algebraic tools; inequalities extend equations)*

**Shape & Space:** Circle geometry → Surface area & volume → Similarity & scale
*(Circle geometry is self-contained; measurement builds on area formulas; similarity applies proportional reasoning from Number)*

**Statistics & Probability:** Scatter plots → Probability
*(Scatter plots reinforce linear relations; probability is independent)*

## Math 9 Specific Design Notes

**Rule of Four applies throughout:**
Every major algebraic concept must appear in at least two of: symbolic, tabular, graphical, verbal forms. Linear relations should appear in all four. Circle geometry theorems appear in geometric and verbal forms. Measurement appears in algebraic formula and concrete diagram form.

**Recording vs. Processing:**
The biggest risk in math notes is transcription. Every blank in a Math 9 package requires generation — students derive the pattern, complete the step, explain the reasoning. No blank should be fillable by copying from the board without thinking.

**Self-explanation inserts:**
After every worked example, include a self-explanation prompt: "Explain what happened at Step [X] and why that step was necessary." This is the highest-yield single move for math notes (g ≈ 0.55 for transfer).

**Alberta contexts:**
Anchor problems use recognizable Alberta settings: Fort McMurray, Banff, Edmonton, Calgary, canola fields, pipeline, hockey rinks, oil sands. These are not cosmetic — contexts should be genuine (removing the context makes the math problem incoherent) rather than pseudo-context (the scenario is decorative and students ignore it).
