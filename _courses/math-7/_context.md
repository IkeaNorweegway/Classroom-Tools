# Math 7 — Course Context
*Alberta Mathematics Grade 7, **June 2026 draft curriculum** (pilot 2026–27, mandatory September 2027). Outcome wording from the numbered draft (APLC, "Math Grades 7–9 Numbered Outcomes", https://aplc.ca/pd-resource/math-grades-7-9-numbered-outcomes/, PDF August 2026) and the APLC 6–9 scope and sequence. Local copies are not committed.*
*Built 2026-10-29. Same layout and renderer as Math 8 (`_courses/math-8/_context.md`), with an even lower reading level and more pictures.*

---

## Course overview

The draft organizes Grade 7 under seven organizing ideas: Number, Algebra, Geometry, Measurement, Functions, Statistics and Probability. These materials group them into the site's four strands.

| Strand (site) | Topics | Draft outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Positive & Negative Numbers · Integers · Squares & Cubes · Fractions & Decimals (× ÷) · Ratios & Percents | 7N1, 7N2.1–7N2.2, 7N3.1–7N3.2, 7N4.1–7N4.4, 7N5 |
| Algebra & Functions | Linear Equations · Functions | 7A2, 7F1 |
| Geometry & Measurement | Lines, Angles & Congruence · Circles · Volume | 7G1.1–7G1.3, 7M1, 7M2 |
| Statistics & Probability | Samples & Statistics · Probability | 7ST1.1–7ST1.2, 7PR1.1–7PR1.2 |

Grade 7 has no 7A1 (covered in Grades 5–6), no Cartesian-plane outcome (6CG1 ends in Grade 6) and no 7G2.

**Big change from the 2016 program:** integer × and ÷, fraction × and ÷, percents below 1% and above 100%, and volume of prisms and cylinders all move *down* into Grade 7. Several of the 2016 Math 8 notes packages (`notes-packages/math-8/`) cover this content and can be mined for examples, but every Math 7 file is written fresh at the Grade 7 reading level.

## Who these materials are for — reading level and pictures

Many Grade 7 students read below grade level. The math should be hard; the reading should not. **This course sits one step lower than Math 8.**

**Reading level (all student-facing text):** Fountas & Pinnell N–P, about a Grade 3–4 reading level.
- Short sentences: 6–12 words. One idea per sentence. No sentence over 15 words.
- Everyday words: "find", "show", "check", "use", "about". No "determine", "demonstrate", "verify", "represent", "approximately" in instructions (but *verify* can be taught as a math word in Linear Equations).
- Math words stay. They are the learning (integer, absolute value, opposite, perfect square, reciprocal, ratio, percent, equation, function, domain, range, ray, transversal, congruent, radius, diameter, circumference, π, prism, cylinder, sample, census, mean, median, mode, range, outcome, probability). Each is **bold** the first time, in a vocabulary table, with a picture or example right beside it.
- Instructions are numbered steps, never paragraphs. Word problems: at most two short sentences before the question, plus a picture.
- Names in problems: short, varied, easy to read (Ava, Sam, Noor, Leo, Mia, Raj, Kai, Zoe, Ben, Lily, Omar, Tess).
- Teacher notes, answer-key marking notes and this file are for adults and are exempt.

**Pictures everywhere.** Students should be able to follow a worked example from the picture alone.
- Notes: **at least three diagrams per section**, and every worked example has its own diagram.
- Core worksheet: a diagram on **at least half** the questions. Challenge: at least a third.
- Exit tickets: at least one diagram per ticket.
- Home sheets: a diagram in the worked example and on at least two practice questions.
- Diagrams use `@viz` (see `scripts/Math8Viz.pm` %DOC, which now includes the Grade 7 types below). Labels go on the picture, never in a legend. The student copy never shows the answer: a `[[blank]]` label is a dashed box.

**Grade 7 diagram types (added 2026-10-29):** `circle` (radius, diameter, circumference labels), `circlearea` (circle cut into slices and rearranged into a parallelogram), `lines` (line, ray, segment, angle with points), `angles` (two crossing lines; parallel lines and a transversal, numbered 1–8; perpendicular; parallel marks), `polygon` (one or two polygons with hash marks, arcs, right-angle marks, side and angle labels — congruence), `tiles` (algebra tiles, or two sides of an equation), `dotplot` (with mean/median marks), `mapping` (mapping diagram for a relation), `people` (population and sample icons), `cube` (n × n × n), `thermo` (thermometers), `layers` (one layer of cubes, then the stacked box). `coord` now also takes `vline=` (vertical line test) and `open="x,y"` (open circles). The Math 8 types are all still there (numberline, counters, bars, area, partial, groups, tape, dnl, percentbar, hundred, balance, pattern, coord, transform, prism, triprism, cylinder, net, blocks, bar, line, pie, picto, tree, spinner, outcomes, flow, squares, receipt, scale, dotgrid).

## Outcomes (June 2026 draft, Grade 7)

| Code | Outcome | Main skills (from the draft) |
|---|---|---|
| 7N1 | Students analyze positive and negative numbers. | Relate absolute value of positive and negative numbers (including decimals and fractions) to their positions on the number line. Convert between fractions and decimals, including negatives. Compare and order positive and negative numbers, including decimals and fractions. Discuss real-world negative decimals (debt, stock changes, sea level, temperature). Knowledge: additive inverses have the same absolute value and opposite signs; −a/b = a/(−b) = −(a/b). |
| 7N2.1 | Students apply operations to positive and negative numbers (+, −). | Distinguish the meanings of the symbol − (negative / subtract). Add and subtract any two integers. Assess the reasonableness of a sum or difference. Solve problems. Knowledge: a − b = a + (−b); adding doesn't always give a bigger number, subtracting doesn't always give a smaller one. |
| 7N2.2 | (×, ÷) | Multiply and divide any two integers. Investigate the sign of a product or quotient of three or more integers. Create different expressions of the same product. Assess reasonableness. Solve problems. Knowledge: sign rules; a × b, a · b, a(b), (a)(b); −a = −1(a). |
| 7N3.1 | Students interpret perfect squares and perfect cubes (squares). | Identify base and exponent in a perfect square. Model a perfect square as a square region. Write perfect squares and square roots with symbols (a × a = a², √). Recall perfect squares within 144 and their roots (natural numbers). Solve perimeter and area problems for squares with natural-number sides. |
| 7N3.2 | (cubes) | Identify base and exponent in a perfect cube. Model a perfect cube as a cubic region. Write a perfect cube as repeated multiplication and a power. Recall perfect cubes within 125 and their cube roots (∛). Solve volume problems for cubes with natural-number edges. |
| 7N4.1 | Students interpret multiplication and division of positive fractions and of positive decimal numbers (× fractions). | Model fraction × fraction (area model; part of a part). Multiply two fractions (a/b × c/d = ac/bd). Compare products in equivalent forms, including simplest form. Solve problems (recipes). Knowledge: the product of two proper fractions is less than each factor. |
| 7N4.2 | (÷ fractions) | Relate a number to its reciprocal; show a fraction × its reciprocal = 1. Divide a natural number by a fraction and a fraction by a natural number. Divide a fraction by a fraction. Investigate how many fraction-sized groups make a quantity. Solve problems (sharing food, cutting material). Knowledge: dividing by a fraction = multiplying by its reciprocal; common denominators: (a/b) ÷ (c/b) = a ÷ c. |
| 7N4.3 | (decimals) | Multiply and divide decimals, including a natural number ÷ a decimal. Solve real-world problems. Knowledge: decimals as fractions, standard algorithms, area models; equivalent divisions by multiplying divisor and dividend by the same factor. |
| 7N4.4 | (order of operations) | Evaluate numerical expressions with integers, fractions and decimals using the order of operations. |
| 7N5 | Students analyze multiplicative relationships between equivalent ratios. | Make equivalent ratios by iterating (× both terms) or partitioning (÷ both terms). Find the factor relating equivalent ratios. Find an unknown in equivalent ratios. Make an equivalent ratio from two existing ones (add first terms, add second terms). Compare two ratios with a common first or second term. Find a percentage of a natural number by iterating or partitioning benchmark percents (1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%). Percentages less than 1% and greater than 100%. Solve real-world proportional problems (discounts, percent increase/decrease, taxes, scaling recipes, unit rates, unit conversion). Knowledge: a/b = c/d is equivalent to ad = bc; a percentage is a ratio a/100. |
| 7A2 | Students apply equivalence to solving linear equations, limited to integers. | Simplify algebraic expressions on one or both sides of an equation (like terms, distributive property). Solve linear equations with algebraic terms on both sides. Verify a solution by substituting into any equivalent equation. Solve real-world problems with linear equations. |
| 7F1 | Students interpret functions through domain and range. | Distinguish discrete and continuous functions. Describe domain and range (in words). Describe restrictions on domain and range in a real-world situation. Graph a function from a table of values. Decide whether a relation is a function (each input has exactly one output; vertical line test). |
| 7G1.1 | Students analyze the structure and relationships of geometric objects (objects and notation). | Tell apart lines, rays, segments, angles and polygons. Model them with materials or digital geometry. Name them with symbols (line CD, ray AC, segment AB, ∠CAB or ∠A, △ABC). Show relationships with symbols (right-angle square, matching arrowheads for parallel, ⊥, ∥). |
| 7G1.2 | (angles at intersections) | Investigate angles where two lines cross: opposite angles are congruent, adjacent angles are supplementary. Identify corresponding angles where a transversal crosses parallel lines. Verify two lines are parallel using angles. Solve problems. |
| 7G1.3 | (congruence) | Identify corresponding sides and angles of congruent polygons. Read hash marks and arcs. Verify congruence with notation (∠ABC ≅ ∠RST, AB ≅ RS, △ABC ≅ △RST). Solve problems with congruent polygons. |
| 7M1 | Students interpret and explain area and circumference of circles. | Draw circles from a radius (compass or digital). Investigate C ÷ d. Find the diameter from the circumference (π ≈ 3.14). Derive A = πr² from a parallelogram made of sectors. Calculate area and circumference from radius or diameter (π ≈ 3.14). Solve problems. |
| 7M2 | Students analyze volume of right prisms and right cylinders. | Name a prism by its base. Tell bases from lateral faces/surfaces in any orientation. Model volume of a rectangular prism as layers of unit cubes. Calculate volume of rectangular prisms, triangular prisms and cylinders (V = Bh). Find the base area given volume and height, and the height given volume and base area. Same units for all dimensions. |
| 7ST1.1 | Students interpret sample data (samples). | Identify the population for a statistical question. Justify a sample or a census. Describe a representative sample. Explain how to get one with a random method (simple random, systematic random). |
| 7ST1.2 | (statistics) | Find mean, median, mode and range of quantitative data from a representative sample. Compare statistics from two samples of the same population. Draw conclusions about a population. Knowledge: discrete vs continuous data. |
| 7PR1.1 | Students interpret theoretical and experimental probability (likelihood). | Describe favourable outcomes for an event. Say one outcome is more or less likely than another. Describe situations where events are not equally likely. Explain certain (1) and impossible (0) events. |
| 7PR1.2 | (theoretical and experimental) | Express theoretical probability in different ways (ratio, fraction, decimal, percent). Predict experimental probability from theory. Collect data from many trials. Simulate situations. Find experimental probability. Compare experimental to theoretical. Relate probability to decisions (games, weather forecasts). Knowledge: sample space; probabilities in a sample space add to 1; trials don't affect each other. |

## Depth ceilings by topic

- **Positive & Negative Numbers:** fractions, decimals and integers on the number line, opposites, absolute value |x| (introduce the bars), −a/b = a/(−b) = −(a/b). Conversions use terminating decimals; 1/3 = 0.333… may appear as "keeps going" but bar notation and "repeating decimal" as a term are Grade 8. No rational/irrational classification (Grade 8).
- **Integers:** +, −, ×, ÷ of integers. Products of three or more integers for the sign pattern. Chips (zero pairs) and number lines first, then rules. Division of 2-digit by 1-digit by hand; larger with a calculator. Decimals and fractions with signs are Grade 8.
- **Squares & Cubes:** natural numbers only. Perfect squares to 144 (12²), cubes to 125 (5³), roots of those. Principal root only. Non-perfect squares and estimating roots are Grade 8.
- **Fractions & Decimals:** positive fractions and decimals. Mixed numbers may appear in problems (change to improper fractions first). Order of operations with integers, fractions and decimals (brackets, then × and ÷ left to right, then + and − left to right; powers only as squares and cubes). Multi-digit decimal division with a calculator.
- **Ratios & Percents:** two-term ratios with whole, decimal or fraction terms; ratio tables, double number lines, tape diagrams before ad = bc. Percent of a natural number by benchmarks (1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%). Percents below 1% and above 100%. GST is 5% in Alberta (no PST). The constant of proportionality k is Grade 8: say "the factor" or "times ___".
- **Linear Equations:** integer coefficients and constants; integer solutions. Like terms, distributive property with an integer factor, x on both sides. Algebra tiles and the balance first. No fractions as coefficients and no inequalities (Grade 8).
- **Functions:** input/output, tables, ordered pairs, mapping diagrams, graphs in the first quadrant (or all four if a context needs negatives). Domain and range **in words** ("whole numbers from 0 to 8", "any number from 0 to 3"). Discrete = dots, continuous = a line. Vertical line test. No f(x) notation, set-builder notation or slope (Grade 8–9).
- **Lines, Angles & Congruence:** opposite (vertically opposite) angles are congruent; adjacent angles on a line are supplementary (add to 180°); corresponding angles are congruent when lines are parallel. Alternate and co-interior angles, triangle angle sum and similarity are not in Grade 7 (angle sum is 8G2).
- **Circles:** π ≈ 3.14. Given r or d find C and A; given C find d. Finding r from an area is a ★★ stretch only (perfect squares). No arcs or sectors beyond the area derivation.
- **Volume:** right rectangular prisms, right triangular prisms, right cylinders. V = Bh. Find B or h when the other two are known. Same units first. No surface area (Grade 8) and no pyramids, cones, spheres or composite objects (Grade 9).
- **Samples & Statistics:** population, census, sample, representative, simple random and systematic random sampling. Mean, median, mode, range. Discrete vs continuous data. Dot plots and tables (histograms, circle graphs and distribution shape are Grade 8). Outliers are not an outcome, but "one big value pulls the mean" may be noticed in a ★★ question.
- **Probability:** one experiment at a time (coin, die, spinner, bag of counters, cards). Sample space as a list. P(event) as ratio, fraction, decimal and percent; probabilities add to 1. Experiments and simulations (many trials; class data pooled). Two-event (compound) probability is Grade 8.

## Key misconceptions — refutation-first boxes

Every one gets a `::: misconception` box in the notes (WRONG / WHY / RIGHT) and at least one worksheet or workbook question that targets it.

| Misconception | Correction | Section |
|---|---|---|
| "−8 is bigger than −3 because 8 > 3" | On the number line, −8 is further left. Further left is smaller. | PN4 |
| "Absolute value means make it negative" or "|−5| = −5" | Absolute value is distance from 0. Distance is never negative: \|−5\| = 5. | PN1 |
| "−3/4, −(3/4) and 3/(−4) are three different numbers" | One negative sign anywhere makes the same number. All three sit at the same point. | PN2 |
| "−1.5 is between −1 and 0" | −1.5 is half way from −1 to −2. Draw it. | PN2 |
| "A longer decimal is bigger: 0.125 > 0.5" | Line up the place values: 0.500 > 0.125. | PN4 |
| "1/4 = 1.4" | 1/4 means 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25. | PN3 |
| "Two negatives make a positive, so −3 + (−4) = 7" | That rule is for × and ÷. Adding two negatives goes further left: −7. | IN1, IN3 |
| "Subtracting always makes it smaller" | 2 − (−3) = 2 + 3 = 5. Taking away a negative makes it bigger. | IN2 |
| "The answer takes the sign of the bigger number" (for ×) | For × and ÷, count the negatives: odd → negative, even → positive. | IN3 |
| "5² = 10" | 5² = 5 × 5 = 25. Draw the 5-by-5 square. | SC1 |
| "√16 = 8" | √16 asks: what times itself is 16? 4 × 4 = 16. | SC2 |
| "2³ = 6" or "∛27 = 9" | 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. ∛27 = 3 because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27. | SC3 |
| "Area and perimeter are the same thing" | Perimeter is the fence (cm). Area is the grass inside (cm²). | SC4 |
| "Multiplying always makes a number bigger" | ½ × 8 = 4. Taking part of something gives less. | FD1 |
| "You need a common denominator to multiply fractions" | Multiply tops, multiply bottoms. Common denominators are for + and −. | FD1 |
| "Dividing always makes a number smaller" | 3 ÷ ½ = 6. There are 6 halves in 3. | FD2 |
| "To divide, flip the first fraction" | Keep the first. Flip the one you divide BY. Then multiply. | FD2 |
| "0.3 × 0.2 = 0.6" | 3 × 2 = 6, and there are two decimal places in all: 0.06. Draw the hundred grid. | FD3 |
| "6 ÷ 0.5 = 3" | How many halves in 6? Twelve. 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12. | FD3 |
| "Work left to right, always": 2 + 3 × 4 = 20 | Multiply before adding: 2 + 12 = 14. | FD4 |
| "To make an equal ratio, add the same number to both terms" (2:3 → 4:5) | Equal ratios multiply (or divide) both terms by the same number: 2:3 = 4:6. | RP1 |
| "Ratio 2:3 means 2 out of 3" | 2 parts to 3 parts is 5 parts in all: 2 out of 5. | RP1 |
| "A percent can't be more than 100%" | 100% is one whole. 150% is one and a half wholes. | RP3 |
| "0.5% is the same as 50% (or 0.5)" | 0.5% is half of 1%: 0.005. | RP3 |
| "20% off, then add 20%, gets you back to the start" | The second 20% is of a smaller number: $100 → $80 → $96. | RP4 |
| "2x + 3x = 5x²" or "3x + 2 = 5x" | Like terms add the count only: 2x + 3x = 5x. x-terms and numbers don't combine. | LE1 |
| "2(x + 3) = 2x + 3" | Multiply BOTH terms in the bracket: 2x + 6. | LE1 |
| "Do it to one side only" | A balance stays level only if you do the same thing to both sides. | LE2 |
| "Move a term across and keep its sign" | Subtracting 3x from both sides: 5x − 3x and 3x − 3x. The term leaves one side as its opposite. | LE3 |
| "Every relation is a function" | A function gives each input exactly ONE output. Check for one input with two outputs. | FN1 |
| "Two inputs can't have the same output in a function" | That's allowed: (1, 5) and (2, 5) is still a function. | FN1 |
| "Always connect the dots" | Counts (people, tickets) stay as dots: discrete. Time, distance, money that can be any amount: a line. | FN2 |
| "Domain and range are the same thing" / swapped | Domain = all the inputs (x). Range = all the outputs (y). | FN3 |
| "A ray goes both ways" / "a line has end points" | A line goes forever both ways. A ray has one end. A segment has two ends. | LA1 |
| "Lines that look parallel are parallel" | Only matching arrowheads (or angle proof) show parallel. | LA2 |
| "Opposite angles add to 180°" | Opposite angles are equal. Angles side by side on a line add to 180°. | LA3 |
| "Corresponding angles are always equal" | Only when the lines are parallel. | LA3 |
| "The letter order in △ABC ≅ △RST doesn't matter" | Order shows the match: A ↔ R, B ↔ S, C ↔ T. | LA4 |
| "The radius and the diameter are the same" | The diameter is two radii: d = 2r. | CI1 |
| "Use the diameter in A = πr²" | Area uses the radius. Halve the diameter first. | CI3 |
| "Circumference is in square units" | Circumference is a length (cm). Area is in cm². | CI4 |
| "Double the radius, double the area" | Area grows 4 times: (2r)² = 4r². | CI4 |
| "The base of a prism is the face it sits on" | The base is the shape that matches on both ends. A triangular prism lying down still has triangle bases. | VO1 |
| "Volume is in square units" | Volume counts cubes: cm³. | VO2 |
| "Use the triangle's height as the prism's height" | Two heights: the triangle's height (inside B) and the prism's length (h). | VO3 |
| "Ask your friends — that's a fair sample" | Friends are alike. A random sample gives everyone a chance. | ST2 |
| "A census is always better" | A census can cost too much or take too long (every fish in a lake). | ST1 |
| "The median is the middle number in the list as given" | Put the numbers in order first. | ST3 |
| "The mode is the biggest number" / "the range is the biggest number" | Mode = most often. Range = biggest − smallest. | ST3 |
| "A sample tells us the exact answer for everyone" | A sample gives an estimate. A second sample will be a bit different. | ST4 |
| "After 5 heads, tails is due" | Each flip is new. The coin has no memory. | PB3 |
| "If P = ½, 10 flips give exactly 5 heads" | Experimental results vary. They get close to theory over many trials. | PB4 |
| "It either happens or it doesn't, so it's 50-50" | Count the equally likely outcomes. Rolling a 6 is 1/6, not 1/2. | PB2 |
| "A probability can be 3 (or 120%)" | Probability is from 0 to 1 (0% to 100%). | PB1 |

## Alberta contexts (must matter to the math)

Temperatures and chinooks (integers), Oilers and Flames stats (decimals, means), West Edmonton Mall and Calgary Stampede (money, percents, GST 5%), canola and wheat fields with centre-pivot irrigation circles (circles, area), grain bins and hay bales (cylinders, volume), Banff hot springs and Peyto Lake (data), Alberta lakes and fish counts (sampling), bus fares and phone plans (functions), Rogers Place seating (ratios), recipes for bannock and pancakes (fractions, ratios), elevation above and below sea level (negative decimals), bank balances and debt (negative decimals).

## Design notes (same as Math 8)

- **Rule of Four:** every major idea shows in at least two of picture, table, symbols, words.
- **Concrete → Pictorial → Symbolic:** chips, tiles, bars, balances and grids first; then the rule.
- **Self-explanation after every worked example:** "Why did we ___ at Step 2?" One short line.
- **Retrieval** at the end of each section mixes in the earlier sections.
- **Formative first:** exit tickets after each section; the mid-unit quiz after Section 2 or 3.
