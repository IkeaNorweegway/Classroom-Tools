# Unit Context — Invasion/Territorial Games, Grades 5–6
**Course:** Physical Education and Wellness | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 5–6

---

## What This Is

Invasion games are activities where teams move into the opponent's territory to score. The underlying tactical structure is shared across all sports in this category — the specific equipment and rules differ, but the problems students solve are the same.

**Core tactical problems:**
- How do we move the ball/object toward the goal? (attack)
- How do we prevent the other team from scoring? (defense)
- How do we transition quickly when possession changes?

---

## Sports in This Category

Soccer, basketball, floor hockey, ultimate frisbee, flag football, team handball, speedball — any lead-up or small-sided version appropriate for the grade

---

## Key Learning Outcomes (Grades 5–6)

**Movement Skills**
- Perform manipulative skills (pass, receive, dribble, shoot) with appropriate technique
- Apply movement concepts: move into space, support the ball carrier, track the opponent

**Tactical Understanding**
- Create space when attacking; deny space when defending
- Support the player with the ball; make yourself available
- Recognize transition moments (possession change) and respond

**Social and Personal Responsibility**
- Fair play and rule adherence in competitive contexts
- Communicate positively with teammates

**Safety**
- Appropriate use of equipment
- Body contact boundaries in modified games

---

## Teaching Approach: Teaching Games for Understanding (TGfU)

Do not teach technique in isolation before students understand why the skill matters. Lead with a modified game that creates the tactical problem → students experience why the skill is needed → teach the technique → return to game.

**Typical lesson sequence:**
1. Modified game (creates the problem)
2. Freeze and question ("Why did we lose the ball there?")
3. Skill focus (teach or refine the specific technique)
4. Application game (same structure, students apply the skill)
5. Reflection/exit question (tactical or self-assessment)

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- Only the player with the ball needs to move (off-ball movement creates space and options)
- Defense means following your opponent everywhere (positioning and reading the game matters more)
- A faster player always wins (decision speed — reading the game — is often more effective than foot speed)

---

## Modifications for Grade 5–6

- Small-sided games (3v3, 4v4) — more touches, more decisions
- Modified scoring (multiple goals, larger goals, lower nets)
- No-contact or limited-contact rules
- Reduced playing area — easier to read the game
- Equipment modifications (lighter balls, foam equipment)
