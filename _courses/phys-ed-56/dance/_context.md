# Unit Context — Dance and Rhythmic Activity, Grades 5–6
**Course:** Physical Education and Wellness | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 5–6

---

## What This Is

Dance develops body awareness, rhythm, creative expression, and cooperative movement. It is a physical literacy strand — not performance art. Students are moving learners, not performers being evaluated on artistry.

**Core learning problems:**
- How do we match our body movement to a rhythm or beat?
- How do we communicate ideas and emotions through movement?
- How do we work with a partner or group to create a shared sequence?

---

## Dance Forms in This Category

Creative movement, folk/cultural dance (including Indigenous and FNMI forms), hip hop fundamentals, rhythmic sequences, parachute games, line dance

---

## Key Learning Outcomes (Grades 5–6)

**Movement Skills**
- Move in time with a steady beat and respond to changes in rhythm
- Use movement elements: level, direction, pathways, speed, weight
- Perform locomotor and non-locomotor movements in sequence

**Understanding**
- Dance as a form of cultural expression — different communities express identity through movement
- Rhythm as a physical experience, not just a musical one

**Skills and Procedures**
- Learn and perform a 4–8 count sequence with a partner or small group
- Compose a short sequence using at least three movement elements
- Give and receive feedback on a partner's sequence

**Social and Personal Responsibility**
- Respect diverse cultural dance forms
- Participation without judgment — dance is not about how you look
- Encourage classmates; avoid negative comments about others' movement

---

## FNMI Connection

Indigenous dance forms carry cultural knowledge. Where possible, invite cultural resources or use teacher-vetted content. Treat Indigenous dance as cultural knowledge — not costume or performance — and always introduce with context.

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- Dance is only for girls (dance requires athletic ability, strength, and coordination — it is for all bodies and genders)
- You have to be "good" to participate (physical literacy means everyone participates; rhythm develops with practice)
- Dance is less rigorous than sport (dance is physically demanding and requires the same movement competencies as other activities)

---

## Teaching Approach

Start with body awareness and rhythm games (freeze dance, mirroring, echo clapping) before structured sequences. Use student choice in sequence creation. Perform for each other in small groups before whole-class performance, if at all.

---

## Safety

- Clear floor space — no equipment left in movement area
- Footwear appropriate for pivoting
- Partner work — check consent before contact (hand holds, partner lifts only at teacher discretion)
