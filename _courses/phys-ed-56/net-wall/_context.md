# Unit Context — Net/Wall Games, Grades 5–6
**Course:** Physical Education and Wellness | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 5–6

---

## What This Is

Net/wall games involve sending an object over a net or to a wall so the opponent cannot return it. Tactics are structurally similar across the category — placement, consistency, and reading opponent positioning.

**Core tactical problems:**
- How do we send the object to a space the opponent cannot reach?
- How do we position ourselves to cover the most return options?
- How do we create consistency before going for placement?

---

## Sports in This Category

Volleyball, badminton, pickleball, table tennis, four square, hand tennis — any net or wall activity appropriate for the grade

---

## Key Learning Outcomes (Grades 5–6)

**Movement Skills**
- Perform send skills: underhand serve, overhand strike, volley, clear, forehand/backhand hit
- Return to ready position after each shot
- Use body rotation to generate force in strikes

**Tactical Understanding**
- Send to open space (away from opponent)
- Maintain rally before attempting difficult shots
- Anticipate opponent's return based on position

**Social and Personal Responsibility**
- Honourable line calls — call what you see fairly
- Maintain patience in rallies; avoid frustration-driven risky shots

**Safety**
- Equipment control — racket swings in appropriate space
- Awareness of adjacent courts/players

---

## Teaching Approach: TGfU

Lead with a rally/placement game → ask "where should you send it to make it hard to return?" → teach the specific send skill → return to placement game.

**Typical lesson sequence:**
1. Cooperative rally (build consistency first)
2. Competitive placement task (sends to targets)
3. Freeze and question ("Where were you sending? Did it work? Why?")
4. Skill refinement (ready position, contact point, follow-through)
5. Application game

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- Hitting harder always works (placement and consistency beat power at this level)
- You play the net/wall, not the opponent (reading opponent position is the key tactical variable)
- Ready position doesn't matter (returning to base position is the most important habit to build early)

---

## Modifications for Grade 5–6

- Lower nets, beach ball or balloon for beginners
- Allow one bounce in badminton or table tennis for beginners
- Score only on serve (limits pressure, maintains cooperative spirit)
- Rally-for-rally scoring (score for longest rally, not just wins)
- Partner games before team games
