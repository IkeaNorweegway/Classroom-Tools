# Unit Context — Target Games, Grades 5–6
**Course:** Physical Education and Wellness | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 5–6

---

## What This Is

Target games involve propelling an object at a target with accuracy. The core challenge is precision and consistent technique — not reacting to an opponent.

**Core tactical problems:**
- How do we control the object to hit the target?
- How do we adjust our approach when we miss?
- How do we manage pressure and focus?

---

## Sports in This Category

Bowling, disc golf, bocce ball, curling (modified), cornhole, horseshoes, target throwing activities

---

## Key Learning Outcomes (Grades 5–6)

**Movement Skills**
- Consistent pre-shot routine (grip, stance, approach, release)
- Apply appropriate force for the distance
- Follow-through in the intended direction

**Tactical Understanding**
- Aim for the centre of the target consistently before attempting strategy
- In competitive target games (bocce, curling): block or bump as appropriate
- Track and adjust: notice the pattern of misses and correct

**Social and Personal Responsibility**
- Wait your turn; allow full concentration
- Honest scorekeeping
- Support and encouragement regardless of outcome

**Safety**
- Throwing/rolling sequence — never behind the release line during others' turns
- Equipment is not a weapon — balls and discs used only for intended purpose

---

## Teaching Approach

Target games are more self-paced — technique practice is central. Use station format with different target types (distance, size, shape). Introduce competition only after students have a consistent technique base.

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- Aim harder = aim better (excess force reduces accuracy; controlled force and consistent release matter more)
- Missing means you're bad at the game (technique is learnable; adjustment is the skill)
- Target games aren't athletic (precision sport requires physical control, mental focus, and fine motor skill)

---

## Modifications for Grade 5–6

- Larger targets, shorter distances
- Multiple attempts before scoring
- Cooperative formats (try to get all teammates on target before the other team)
- Self-improvement scoring: beat your own last score
