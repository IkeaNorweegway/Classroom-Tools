# Unit Context — Fitness and Active Living, Grades 5–6
**Course:** Physical Education and Wellness | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 5–6

---

## What This Is

This unit develops students' understanding of physical fitness and builds habits of personal goal-setting and self-monitoring. It is not athletic training — it is physical literacy for lifelong active living.

**Core learning problems:**
- What does fitness mean for me personally?
- How do I know if I'm getting fitter?
- How do I set a goal that is challenging but achievable?

---

## Fitness Components Covered

| Component | What it means | Example activity |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular endurance** | Heart and lungs work efficiently over time | Running, skipping, cycling |
| **Muscular strength** | How much force a muscle can produce | Push-ups, wall sit, weightlifting |
| **Muscular endurance** | How long a muscle can keep working | Plank hold, repeated squats |
| **Flexibility** | Range of motion in joints | Stretching, yoga poses |
| **Balance** | Control of body position and stability | Single-leg stands, beam work |

---

## Key Learning Outcomes (Grades 5–6)

**Knowledge**
- Name the five components of fitness and give an example of each
- Distinguish moderate from vigorous physical activity

**Understanding**
- Fitness is personal — compare yourself to your previous self, not to classmates
- All five components matter — fitness is not just cardio
- Rest and recovery are part of fitness, not the absence of it

**Skills and Procedures**
- Record personal fitness data across multiple measures
- Set a personal SMART goal for one fitness component
- Design a 1-week activity plan to work toward the goal
- Self-assess progress: compare start to end, not to classmates

**Social and Personal Responsibility**
- Effort is the measure — not performance outcome
- Respect diverse body types and fitness levels
- Support classmates during fitness challenges

---

## Assessment Notes

- Fitness data is **personal** — never display rankings or compare students
- Assess on: understanding of components, quality of goal-setting, evidence of self-monitoring, reflection quality
- Process portfolio or fitness journal works well across a unit or year

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- Fitness means being fast or strong (fitness is multi-dimensional and personal — flexibility and endurance count equally)
- If you're not sweating, you're not working (light-to-moderate activity has real health benefits)
- You have to exercise every day to be fit (rest days are part of a healthy pattern)
- Kids are naturally active — they don't need fitness goals (purposeful goal-setting develops self-regulation and motivation, not just fitness)

---

## Active Living Connection

This unit connects to the **Active Living organizing idea** across the whole year. Students track activity types and duration across all units — not just during this unit. The fitness unit gives them the vocabulary and framework to understand what they're doing in all other units.
