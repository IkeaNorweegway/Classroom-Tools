**Change from the 2016 program:** integer ×÷, fraction ×÷, percents and volume moved to Grade 7. New in Grade 8: rational and irrational numbers, ± square roots, the constant of proportionality, polynomials (classify, add and subtract), equations with rational coefficients and variables on both sides, inequalities, slope and y = mx + b, similarity and dilations, triangle theorems (angle sum, isosceles, triangle inequality) with the Pythagorean theorem, surface area with nets and orthographic/isometric drawings, distribution shape and histograms, and dependent events.

## Outcomes (June 2026 draft, Grade 8)

| Code | Outcome | Main skills (from the draft) |
|---|---|---|
| 8N1.1 | Students interpret rational and irrational numbers (rational). | Express rational numbers as fractions and decimals. Classify natural numbers, integers and rational numbers. Relate a rational number to its position on the number line. Compare and order rational numbers. Find a rational number between any two. Investigate fractions that give repeating decimals (bar notation). Add, subtract, multiply and divide any two rational numbers. Assess reasonableness by estimating. Evaluate expressions with the order of operations. Solve real-world problems. |
| 8N1.2 | (irrational) | Distinguish rational and irrational numbers. Classify a value as exact (√2, π) or approximate (1.41). Approximate an irrational number as a rounded decimal. Place irrational numbers on the number line. Compare and order real numbers. |
| 8N3 | Students interpret square roots of perfect and non-perfect squares. | Classify positive rationals as perfect or non-perfect squares. Express squares as repeated multiplication and powers. Justify the number of square roots of a real number (two for positive, one for 0, none real for negative); x² = a gives x = ±√a. √ is the principal (positive) root; −√ the negative root. Approximate square roots of non-perfect squares within 144. Find area from side and side from area. Perimeter and area problems with squares. |
| 8N5 | Students analyze multiplicative relationships within ratios. | Describe one quantity in a ratio as a multiple of the other. Find the constant of proportionality k for a ratio. Generate equivalent ratios for a given k. Find a missing quantity using k. Problems with direct proportion (k = b/a style "times as many") and inverse proportion (k = a × b). k is also called slope, unit rate or scale factor. |
| 8A1 | Students interpret powers in single-variable algebraic terms. | Write a term as coefficient × variable factors and back. Justify that an expression is a polynomial. Conventional forms (x not 1x, x not x¹, 5 not 5x⁰). Classify by number of terms (monomial, binomial, trinomial) and by degree (constant, linear, quadratic, cubic). Degree of a polynomial. Descending/ascending order. Add and subtract monomials (like terms). Subtracting a polynomial = adding its additive inverse. |
| 8A2 | Students solve single-variable linear equations involving rational numbers. | Rewrite an equation with rational coefficients as an equivalent one with integers (multiply both sides). Solve equations with rational coefficients, parentheses and variables on both sides. Solve problems. |
| 8A3 | (inequalities) | Identify strict (<, >) and non-strict (≤, ≥) inequalities. Model on the number line (open dot for strict, closed for non-strict; ray right for greater, left for less). Verify by substitution whether a number is a solution. Write a real-world situation as an inequality (speed limits, height and age restrictions). (Solving inequalities is Grade 9.) |
| 8F1 | Students relate linear functions to equations and graphs. | Slope as rise over run, m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). Positive, negative, zero and undefined slope. Compare slopes of parallel and perpendicular lines. How m and b in y = mx + b change the graph. Linear vs non-linear graphs. Rate of change in context (speed, hourly wage, filling a tank). x- and y-intercepts as ordered pairs; a linear function has exactly one x-intercept (if not horizontal). Write y = mx + b from a graph. Graph from an equation, or from a slope and a point. Horizontal (y = b) and vertical (x = a) lines. |
| 8G1.1 | Students interpret similarity through scale factor. | Find the scale factor of similar objects (drawing length ÷ original length). Decide if objects are similar given a scale factor. Apply a scale factor. Enlarge (k > 1), reduce (0 < k < 1), same (k = 1). All circles and all squares are similar. Areas of similar objects scale by k². Maps, blueprints, scale drawings. |
| 8G1.2 | (dilations) | Dilate a polygon on the Cartesian plane about (0, 0) by a scale factor (multiply both coordinates). Find coordinates of the image. Verify similarity using the plane or lines through the dilation centre. |
| 8G2 | Students analyze and explain triangle problems, using theorems. | Angle sum 180°; isosceles base angles equal; equilateral angles 60°. Triangle inequality (a + b > c for every pair) — decide if three lengths make a triangle. Prove a triangle is right (converse of Pythagoras, hands-on/digital). Find a missing side of a right triangle with a² + b² = c². Solve problems with triangles and theorems. |
| 8M2 | Students analyze the surface area of right 3-D shapes, using 2-D models. | Identify polyhedrons (prisms, pyramids) vs non-polyhedrons (cylinders, spheres). Nets: predict the 3-D shape; match net faces to faces; examples and non-examples of cube nets. Surface area of polyhedrons (prisms, pyramids) and cylinders from a net (cylinder's curved surface is a rectangle as long as the circumference). Orthographic (front, top, right side) and isometric drawings: recognize, create, build from them; justify which 2-D model to use. |
| 8ST1.1 | Students analyze distributions, using shape. | Describe shape: number of modes (unimodal, bimodal), symmetric, uniform, left- or right-skewed. Histograms with equal intervals for continuous data; bar graphs and double bar graphs for discrete data. Effect of interval size on a histogram's shape. Compare two samples' distributions. Justify mean, median or mode for a shape (symmetric: mean = median; right-skewed: mean > median; left-skewed: mean < median). |
| 8ST1.2 | (circle graphs) | Draw circle graphs (sector angle = percent × 360°). Analyze circle graphs to solve problems. |
| 8PR1 | Students interpret probability of independent and dependent events. | Sample space as set notation S = {H, T}, lists, tables, tree diagrams, area models. Decide if two events are independent or dependent (with or without replacement). Probability of two or more independent events, and of two dependent events, by modelling the sample space. |

## Depth ceilings

- **Rational Numbers:** set language (natural numbers, integers, rational numbers) but no set-builder notation or ⊆ (Grade 9). Repeating decimals with a bar. Operations with negative fractions and decimals; calculator for messy decimals.
- **Square Roots & Irrational Numbers:** square roots only (no cube roots in Grade 8). Approximate √n for n ≤ 144 using benchmarks, then a calculator. Exact (√2, π) vs approximate (1.41, 3.14). Real numbers = rational + irrational. No simplifying radicals.
- **Proportional Reasoning:** k as a decimal or ratio; direct (b = k × a) and inverse (a × b = k). Contexts: unit price, speed, recipes, maps (scale factor), workers and time (inverse).
- **Polynomials:** degree up to 3 in examples (degree n named). Add and subtract only (multiplying and factoring are Grade 9). Algebra tiles for degree ≤ 2.
- **Solving Equations:** rational coefficients (fractions and decimals), brackets, variables on both sides. One variable. Solutions may be fractions or decimals.
- **Inequalities:** write, graph and test; no solving by inverse operations (Grade 9).
- **Linear Functions:** slope formula with integer points; parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals (compare, don't prove). Function notation is Grade 9.
- **Similarity & Dilations:** scale factor; area scales by k² (no volume scaling). Dilations about the origin only.
- **Triangle Theorems:** angle sum, isosceles, equilateral, triangle inequality, Pythagorean theorem and its converse. Answers to one decimal place. No trigonometry.
- **Surface Area:** right prisms, right pyramids (square/rectangular/triangular base with given slant heights), right cylinders. Composite objects are Grade 9. π ≈ 3.14.
- **Distributions:** histograms with equal intervals, bar and double bar graphs; shape words; circle graphs. Box plots, quartiles and IQR are Grade 9.
- **Independent & Dependent Events:** two events (three for independent with a tree). With/without replacement. Mutually exclusive events and Venn diagrams are Grade 9.

## Key misconceptions — refutation-first boxes

| Misconception | Correction | Section |
|---|---|---|
| "Fractions aren't rational numbers; only integers are" / "integers aren't rational" | Every integer is also rational: 5 = 5/1. | RN1 |
| "0.333… is 0.3" | 1 ÷ 3 never ends: 0.3̄. 0.3 = 3/10. | RN2 |
| "There's no number between 0.5 and 0.6" | 0.55, 0.51, 0.501… there are endless numbers between. | RN2 |
| "−1/2 + 1/3 = 0/5" | Common denominator first: −3/6 + 2/6 = −1/6. | RN3 |
| "√ of a number is half of it" | √16 = 4 because 4 × 4 = 16. | SR1 |
| "√25 = ±5" (radical gives both) | √25 = 5 (principal root). x² = 25 has two answers: x = ±5. | SR2 |
| "√−9 = −3" | (−3)² = 9, not −9. No real number squares to a negative. | SR2 |
| "√50 ≈ 25" or "≈ 7.5 halfway" | 7² = 49, so √50 is just over 7 (≈ 7.07). | SR3 |
| "π = 3.14 exactly" / "1.41 is √2" | 3.14 and 1.41 are approximate. π and √2 never end or repeat. | SR4 |
| "k is always the bigger number divided by the smaller" | k depends on the order of the ratio a : b. | PR2 |
| "More workers, more days" (treating inverse as direct) | Doubling workers halves the time: a × b stays the same. | PR4 |
| "3x² + 2x = 5x³" | Only like terms combine: same variable AND same power. | PO3 |
| "The degree is the number of terms" | Degree = highest exponent. x³ + 1 has degree 3, two terms. | PO2 |
| "(3x + 2) − (x − 5) = 2x − 3" | Subtract every term: 3x + 2 − x + 5 = 2x + 7. | PO4 |
| "Multiply only the fraction term by the LCD" | Multiply EVERY term on both sides. | SE1 |
| "x < 3 includes 3" | Strict < leaves 3 out: open dot. ≤ includes it: closed dot. | IQ1, IQ2 |
| "x > 2 on a number line points left" | Greater is to the right. | IQ2 |
| "Slope is run over rise" | Slope = rise ÷ run (up over across). | LF1 |
| "A horizontal line has no slope / undefined slope" | Horizontal: slope 0. Vertical: undefined. | LF2 |
| "In y = mx + b, b is the slope" | m is the slope; b is where the line crosses the y-axis. | LF3 |
| "Similar means the same size" | Similar = same shape; sizes can differ by a scale factor. | SD1 |
| "Double the sides, double the area" | Area grows by k²: double the sides → 4 times the area. | SD3 |
| "Add the scale factor to each coordinate" | Dilation multiplies: (2, 3) × 2 = (4, 6). | SD4 |
| "Any three lengths make a triangle" | The two shorter sides must add to more than the longest. | TT2 |
| "c is whatever side is labelled c" | c is the hypotenuse, across from the right angle, the longest side. | TT3 |
| "For a leg, add the squares" | For a leg, subtract: a² = c² − b². | TT4 |
| "Surface area and volume measure the same thing" | SA is the covering (units²); volume is the filling (units³). | SA2 |
| "A cylinder's net rectangle is as long as the diameter" | It wraps around: length = circumference = πd. | SA3 |
| "Any 6 squares joined make a cube net" | Some arrangements overlap when folded. Test by folding. | SA1 |
| "A histogram is a bar graph with gaps" | Histogram bars touch: the intervals are continuous. | DI1 |
| "Skewed right means the bump is on the right" | The tail points the way of the skew. Right-skewed: long tail to the right. | DI2 |
| "The mean is always the best average" | In skewed data the median is more typical. | DI3 |
| "A circle graph can show change over time" | It shows parts of one whole at one time. | DI4 |
| "Without replacement, the second pick has the same chances" | One fewer item: the bottom number (and maybe the top) changes. | CP3 |
| "P(A and B) = P(A) + P(B)" | For "and", multiply: ½ × ⅙ = 1/12. | CP2 |
