## Course Overview

Grade 8 is the bridge between whole-number arithmetic and Grade 9 algebra. Students finish the four operations on fractions and integers, meet square roots and the Pythagorean theorem, use percents, ratios and rates, and solve their first equations with brackets. In geometry they move between 3-D objects and flat pictures (nets, views, transformations). In data they judge graphs and work out the chance of two things happening.

**Four strands, twelve topics (as organized in these materials):**

| Strand | Topics | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Squares & Square Roots · Percents · Ratios & Rates · Fractions (× and ÷) · Integers (× and ÷) | Tier 1 |
| Algebra (Patterns & Relations) | Linear Relations · Linear Equations | Tier 1 |
| Measurement & Geometry (Shape & Space) | Pythagorean Theorem · Surface Area & Volume · 3-D Views & Transformations | Tier 1 (Pythagorean, SA & V), Tier 2 (Views) |
| Statistics & Probability | Data Displays · Probability | Tier 2 |

## Who these materials are for — reading level and pictures

Many Grade 8 students read below grade level. The math should be hard; the reading should not.

**Reading level (all student-facing text):** Fountas & Pinnell P–Q, about a Grade 4 reading level, the same rule as `worksheets/workbooks-context.md`.
- Short sentences: 8–14 words. One idea per sentence.
- Common words: "find" not "determine", "show" not "demonstrate", "check" not "verify" (but teach *verify* as a math word).
- Math words stay. They are the learning (square root, ratio, rate, hypotenuse, net, congruent, independent). Each one is in a vocabulary table with a picture or example nearby.
- Instructions are numbered steps, not paragraphs.
- Teacher notes, answer-key marking notes and this file are for adults and are exempt.

**Pictures everywhere they help.** Every notes section has at least two diagrams; every worksheet and workbook has a diagram on at least a third of its questions. Diagrams are drawn by `scripts/Math8Viz.pm` (number lines, hundred grids, fraction bars, area models, counters, tape and double number line diagrams, balances, growing patterns, coordinate grids, right triangles with squares, prisms, cylinders, nets, isometric blocks with their views, transformations, bar/line/circle graphs, pictographs, tree diagrams, spinners, step flowcharts). Diagrams use the `dg-*` classes, put labels on the picture (no legends), and never show the answer on the student copy: a blank label is a dashed box.

## Depth Ceilings by Strand

**Number:**
- Square roots: whole numbers only. Principal (positive) root only. Estimating uses perfect-square benchmarks and a calculator. No square roots of fractions or decimals (Grade 9), no "irrational" as a formal term.
- Percents: fractional percents (0.5%), percents over 100%, combined percents (tax + tip), percent of a percent. Alberta has GST only (5%); other provinces' PST/HST may appear as a contrast. No compound-interest formula.
- Ratios and rates: two- and three-term ratios, part-to-part and part-to-whole, unit rates, proportions solved with tables, double number lines, or equivalent fractions. Cross-multiplying is allowed but never the first method taught.
- Fractions: positive fractions and mixed numbers only. Order of operations limited to positive answers. No negative fractions (Grade 9).
- Integers: × and ÷ of integers, order of operations with integers. 2-digit ÷ 1-digit by hand; 2-digit ÷ 2-digit with a calculator.

**Algebra:**
- Linear relations: tables, ordered pairs, graphs of discrete data, describing the relationship in words. No slope formula, no y = mx + b vocabulary (Grade 9); "goes up by ___ each time" and "starts at ___" are the Grade 8 language.
- Equations: the five forms in PR2, with integer a, b, c. Solutions may be negative or fractional. No variables on both sides (Grade 9), no inequalities.

**Measurement & Geometry:**
- Pythagorean theorem: right triangles only; find any side; test for a right triangle; triples. Answers to one decimal place.
- Surface area and volume: right rectangular prisms, right triangular prisms, right cylinders. No pyramids, cones, spheres or composite objects (Grade 9). Use π ≈ 3.14 or the π key.
- Views: objects made of cubes or rectangular prisms; rotations in 90° steps.
- Transformations: translations, reflections in a horizontal or vertical line, rotations of 90°, 180°, 270° about a point on the grid; combined transformations; congruence as "same size and shape". No dilations (Grade 9 similarity).

**Statistics & Probability:**
- Graphs: circle, line, bar, pictograph. Critique scale, intervals, bar width, picture size, missing labels, cherry-picked ranges. No scatter plots (Grade 9).
- Probability: two independent events only. Tree diagrams, tables, and P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). No dependent events (Grade 9), no permutations or combinations.

## Key Misconceptions — By Strand

Every one of these gets a refutation-first box in the notes and a dedicated question in the strand workbook.

### Number

| Misconception | Correction | Topic / section |
|---|---|---|
| "Squaring means times 2" (5² = 10) | Squaring is a number times itself: 5² = 5 × 5 = 25. Draw the square. | SQ1 |
| "The square root of a number is half of it" (√16 = 8) | √16 asks "what number times itself is 16?" 8 × 8 = 64, not 16. | SQ2 |
| "√20 is about 10" or "√20 is halfway, 4.5" | √20 is between √16 = 4 and √25 = 5, closer to 4 because 20 is closer to 16. | SQ3 |
| "A percent can't be more than 100%" | 100% is one whole. 150% means one and a half wholes (the price went up by half). | PC1 |
| "0.5% is the same as 50% (or as 0.5)" | 0.5% is half of one percent: 0.005. Draw half of one small square. | PC1 |
| "Up 10%, then up 15% is up 25%" | The second percent is of a bigger number. 100 → 110 → 126.50, a 26.5% increase. | PC4 |
| "A 20% discount then a 20% markup gets you back to the start" | The markup is 20% of a smaller number. 100 → 80 → 96. | PC4 |
| "Ratio 2:3 means 2 out of 3" | 2:3 part-to-part means 2 parts and 3 parts: 2 out of 5 in all. | RR1 |
| "To make an equal ratio, add the same number to both terms" (2:3 → 4:5) | Equal ratios multiply both terms by the same number: 2:3 = 4:6. | RR2 |
| "A bigger number in a rate is always a better deal" | Compare unit rates: price for ONE item or ONE unit. | RR3 |
| "Multiplying always makes a number bigger" | Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 gives less: ½ × 8 = 4. | FR2 |
| "Dividing always makes a number smaller" | 3 ÷ ¼ = 12. There are 12 quarters in 3. | FR3 |
| "To multiply mixed numbers, multiply the wholes and the fractions" (2½ × 1½ = 2¼) | Change to improper fractions first: 5/2 × 3/2 = 15/4 = 3¾. | FR2 |
| "To divide fractions, flip the first fraction" | Keep the first, flip the one you divide BY, then multiply. | FR3 |
| "Two negatives make a positive, so −3 + (−4) = 7" | The sign rule is for × and ÷ only. −3 + (−4) = −7. | IN2 |
| "The sign of the answer is the sign of the bigger number" (for ×) | For × and ÷, count the negatives: one negative → negative answer; two → positive. | IN2 |

### Algebra

| Misconception | Correction | Topic / section |
|---|---|---|
| "The pattern rule is the number you add each time, so the equation is y = x + 2" | Adding 2 each time means ×2 in the equation: tiles = 2n + (start). Check with the table. | LR2 |
| "Connect the dots on every graph" | Points for counts (figures, tickets, people) stay as dots: there is no figure 2½. | LR3 |
| "In 3x = 12, subtract 3" | 3x means 3 times x. Undo times with divide: x = 4. | LE1 |
| "Undo in the order you read it" (2x + 5 = 17: divide by 2 first) | Undo the last thing done first: subtract 5, then divide by 2. | LE2 |
| "2(x + 3) = 2x + 3" | Multiply BOTH terms in the bracket: 2x + 6. | LE3 |

### Measurement & Geometry

| Misconception | Correction | Topic / section |
|---|---|---|
| "a² + b² = c² works for any triangle" | Only right triangles. A 4, 5, 6 triangle: 16 + 25 ≠ 36. | PY1, PY4 |
| "c is always the side labelled c" or "the longest side is any side" | c is the hypotenuse: the side across from the right angle, always the longest. | PY2 |
| "To find a leg, add the squares" | For a leg, subtract: a² = c² − b². Check that the leg is shorter than c. | PY3 |
| "Surface area and volume measure the same thing" | Surface area is the covering (square units); volume is the filling (cubic units). | SV2, SV4 |
| "A rectangular prism has 6 faces, so the SA is 6 × one face" | Only a cube has 6 equal faces. Find the three pairs. | SV2 |
| "The rectangle in a cylinder's net is as long as the diameter" | It wraps all the way round, so its length is the circumference: πd. | SV1, SV3 |
| "Turning the box on its side changes its volume" | Volume stays the same. Any face can be the base for a rectangular prism. | SV4 |
| "The side view shows the hidden cubes in the back" | A view shows only the outline you see from that side. Height counts, depth doesn't. | VT1 |
| "A reflected or rotated shape is a different (not congruent) shape" | Translations, reflections and rotations keep size and shape. The image is congruent. | VT4 |

### Statistics & Probability

| Misconception | Correction | Topic / section |
|---|---|---|
| "The taller bar is always a lot bigger" | Check where the y-axis starts. A broken axis can make a small gap look huge. | DD3 |
| "A picture twice as tall shows twice as much" | Doubling height AND width makes the picture 4 times the area. | DD3 |
| "A circle graph can show change over time" | Circle graphs show parts of one whole at one time. Use a line graph for change. | DD1 |
| "After 5 heads in a row, tails is due" | Each flip is independent. The coin has no memory: P(tails) is still ½. | PB2 |
| "P(A and B) = P(A) + P(B)" | For "and" with independent events, multiply: ½ × ⅙ = 1/12. Adding can give more than 1. | PB3 |
| "If theoretical P is ½, I'll get exactly 5 heads in 10 flips" | Theory tells what happens in the long run. Small samples vary. | PB4 |

## Learning Sequence Recommendations

**Number:** Integers → Fractions → Squares & Square Roots → Ratios & Rates → Percents
*(Integer and fraction operations are used everywhere else; square roots feed the Pythagorean theorem; ratios lead into percents, and percents are "rates per 100".)*

**Algebra:** Linear Relations → Linear Equations
*(Tables and patterns give meaning to the letter before students solve for it; integer operations come first.)*

**Measurement & Geometry:** Pythagorean Theorem → Surface Area & Volume → 3-D Views & Transformations
*(Pythagorean theorem needs square roots; triangular prisms sometimes need a slant side; views and nets are both "3-D to 2-D".)*

**Statistics & Probability:** Data Displays → Probability
*(Probability uses fractions and percents; data displays can sit anywhere.)*

The site lists topics in strand order (Number, Algebra, M&G, S&P). Teachers can use any order; each topic defines its own vocabulary.

## Math 8 Design Notes

**Rule of Four:** every major idea shows in at least two of picture, table, symbols, words. The diagram library makes the picture cheap: use it.

**Concrete → Pictorial → Symbolic (CPA):** the 2016 outcomes say "concretely, pictorially and symbolically" for N1, N6, N7 and PR2. Notes go in that order: counters, tiles, bars and balances first, then the rule.

**Self-explanation after every worked example:** "Why did we ___ at Step 2?" One short line. This is the highest-yield move in math notes.

**Alberta contexts:** Edmonton Oilers and Calgary Flames, West Edmonton Mall, Calgary Stampede, canola and wheat, Banff and Jasper, the Calgary Tower, Rexall/Rogers Place, prairie weather (temperature drops are a natural integer context), the 5% GST. Contexts must matter to the math, not decorate it.

**Same contexts across a topic's materials:** the workbook and worksheets reuse the notes' examples (for example, the Pythagorean notes use a ladder against a wall and a soccer field diagonal; the workbook returns to both).
