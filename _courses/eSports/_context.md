## What This Course Is

A school esports program structured as an **athletic season**, not a gaming club. The distinction is not cosmetic — it determines every outcome the research documents. A gaming club is high enjoyment and low transfer. A season-structured program produces the GPA gains, attendance gains, belonging, and career-readiness that justify the program's existence.

This course draws from four sources:
- `esports-program-research.md` — school esports best practices literature
- `sport-education-model-research.md` — Siedentop's Sport Education Model (SEM)
- `NASEF-curriculum.pdf` — NASEF 9-week middle school course outline (adaptable to 18-week semester)
- `Alberta Esports Strategy.pdf` — Deloitte 2022 provincial strategy; Alberta-specific ecosystem, employers, and pathways

## Alberta Context — Why This Matters Here

Alberta has an active provincial esports ecosystem. This is directly usable with students (legitimacy, motivation) and with administrators (career pathway evidence, provincial alignment).

**Key Alberta facts (Deloitte 2022):**
- 130+ game studios, support services, and institutions operating in Alberta
- Active organizations: Alberta Esports Association (AESA), Digital Alberta, Interactive Arts Alberta
- Post-secondary esports programs: Keyano College (Fort McMurray), Mount Royal University (Calgary), SAIT (Calgary)
- Alberta employers students could work for: BioWare, Beamdog, Inflexion Games (all Edmonton), Unity Technologies, Red Iron Labs (both Calgary)
- Provincial strategy explicitly calls for interscholastic leagues and esports curriculum as medium-term goals — this program is aligned with where Alberta is heading
- Esports fan base is significantly younger than traditional sports (League of Legends average fan age: 21.2) — this is the students' industry

**Use this when:**
- Pitching the program to admin: Alberta has a provincial strategy; this is not fringe
- Showing students where pathways lead: these are real Alberta employers, not abstract possibilities
- Connecting to STEM and career education: Deloitte frames esports as a STEM talent pipeline

## Why SEM and Esports Research Converge

Siedentop's critique of traditional PE and the esports research community's critique of gaming clubs describe the same failure mode:

| Traditional PE failure (SEM) | Gaming club failure (Esports research) |
|---|---|
| Units too short to develop real understanding | Sessions too unstructured to develop real skills |
| Isolated drills disconnected from gameplay | Play without analysis, coaching, or reflection |
| No culminating event — no sense of achievement | No season arc — no stakes, no identity, no payoff |
| Students as receivers, not decision-makers | Students as consumers, not producers |

Both models solve this with the same four moves:
1. Run a **season**, not a collection of sessions
2. Give students **persistent team identity** and **rotating roles**
3. Make **fair play and conduct** part of the competition system, not a separate speech
4. Build toward a **culminating event** the whole season points at

## The NASEF Four Domains Model

NASEF's framework organises all esports careers into four domains with 15 specific career tracks. This is the role/identity model for this course — every student finds their fit regardless of playing skill.

```
                    STRATEGISTS
              Coaches · Analysts · Theory Crafters
                          |
ORGANIZERS ──────── [ PLAYERS ] ──────── CONTENT CREATORS
General Managers          |               Shoutcasters · Streamers
Event Organizers    [ GAMES ]             Journalists · Fandom Art
IT Support                |               Software Developers
Web Developers            |
                    ENTREPRENEURS
          Marketing · Business Developers · Corporate Sponsorship
```

**The four domains:**

| Domain | Core skill | Example careers |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategists** | Pattern recognition, analysis, coaching | Coach, Analyst, Theory Crafter |
| **Organizers** | Systems thinking, logistics, tech | Event Organizer, IT Support, Web Developer, General Manager |
| **Content Creators** | Communication, creativity, media | Broadcaster/Caster, Streamer, Journalist, Fandom Art |
| **Entrepreneurs** | Business, persuasion, growth mindset | Marketing, Business Developer, Corporate Sponsorship |

**Domain Sorting Survey (Week 1):** NASEF provides a 20-question self-assessment that places students in the domain they most naturally align with. This is the preseason role-assignment tool — it is not about gaming skill, it is about personal strengths. Students with no gaming experience can score high as Organizers or Entrepreneurs. Use it in Session 2 after the Domain overview in Session 1.

## The Season Arc

Every semester runs as one full season. The structure combines SEM's season phases with NASEF's domain-block progression.

| Phase | Weeks | NASEF Domain Focus | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Preseason** | 1–2 | Organizers (Forming/Norming) | Team formation, Club Charter co-created, Domain Sorting Survey, code of conduct, Pledge established, skill baseline |
| **Practice Season — Content** | 3–4 | Content Creators (Sorting/Exploring) | Broadcasting, clip production, social media; clinic-game day alternation begins |
| **Practice Season — Business** | 5–6 | Entrepreneurs (Explaining/Performing) | Growth mindset, esports business, sponsorship concepts, wellness module |
| **Regular Season** | 7–10 | Strategists (Exciting) + Full Competition | Scheduled competition, premiership ladder, VOD review, opponent scouting; all domains in play |
| **Postseason / Showcase** | 11–12 | All Roles (Culminating Event/Engaging) | Tournament or public showcase, all-domain production, season reflection, portfolio, ceremony |

**9-week to 18-week scaling (per NASEF):** Each 2-week domain block can be doubled to 4 weeks for a semester-length course, allowing deeper skill development within each domain before competition.

The showcase event is non-negotiable. SEM and NASEF are both unambiguous: the culminating event is what converts participation into identity.

## The Club Charter and Governance Structure

This course runs with formal governance — not just a code of conduct, but a full Club Charter modelled on NASEF's template. This is the SEM "team affiliation" principle operationalized.

**Charter structure (co-created in Weeks 1–2):**
- **Article I:** Name and Purpose — students articulate what the club is for
- **Article II:** Membership and Eligibility — GPA requirements, attendance, participation expectations
- **Article III:** Club Officers — President, Vice-President, Secretary (elected by vote in Week 2)
- **Article IV:** Duties of Officers — specific responsibilities per role
- **Article V:** Club Meetings — structure, frequency, quorum
- **Article VI:** Executive Committee — GM (teacher) + elected officers
- **Article VII:** Committees — Marketing, Workshop, Team Competition

**Week 1–2 protocol (from NASEF):**
- Session 1: Introduce the charter, read through it together, students self-select candidacy for office
- Session 2: Short candidate presentations (60 sec max), class votes, officers installed
- The teacher is the **General Manager (GM)** — supports, does not control day-to-day student governance

**Why this matters:** Students who hold formal office — even Secretary — show up differently. The charter creates accountability structures that the teacher does not need to personally enforce.

## The Session Cadence

Every session follows the same predictable structure (NASEF-sourced). Predictability reduces transition time and establishes routine.

```
1. Arrive / Sign-in / Assemble
2. Meet & Greet / The Pledge (whole group)
3. Healthy Gaming Exercises / Stretching (individuals, 10 min — EVERY session)
4. Execute the Session Activity (individual / small group / whole group)
5. Debrief: What did we cover? Why does it matter? How do we use it?
6. Wrap-up / Clean up
7. The Pledge / Dismissal
```

**The Pledge** (NASEF standard, recited at open and close every session):
> *"Win or lose, I pledge to play esports games as well as I know how; to obey all the rules of the game; to be a good sport at all times, and to (commit to) improving myself in body, mind and spirit. Good luck, have fun!"*

The facilitator breaks down the pledge in Session 1. From Session 2 onward, club officers lead it. The pledge is not ceremonial — it is the daily re-commitment to the code of conduct before anything else happens.

## Clinic-Game Day Session Structure

On clinic days and game days, the internal structure adapts the NASEF session cadence:

**Clinic days** (skill development, domain role work)
```
0:00–0:10  Check-in / Pledge / Healthy Gaming Exercises (wellbeing lead runs check-in)
0:10–0:35  Domain skill clinic — student-coaches or domain leads lead
0:35–0:50  Analyst segment — VOD review OR opponent scouting OR domain project work
0:50–0:55  Physical break (mandatory)
0:55–1:10  Scrimmage with debrief
1:10–1:20  Debrief + Pledge / Dismissal
```

**Game days** (competition)
```
0:00–0:10  Check-in / Pledge / Healthy Gaming Exercises
0:10–0:15  Event coordinator setup; broadcasters ready
0:15–0:50  Competition round (one team administers, one broadcasts)
0:50–0:55  Physical break
0:55–1:10  Second competition round if scheduled
1:10–1:20  Post-match debrief (IGL leads, analyst pulls one key moment) + Pledge
```

## The Role System

Roles are drawn from the NASEF Four Domains, mapped to session functions. All roles rotate across the season — no student holds the same production role for more than two consecutive weeks.

### Competition roles (domain: Strategists)
| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Player** | In-game execution, team communication |
| **In-game Leader (IGL)** | Pre-game calls, in-game strategy, post-game debrief |
| **Analyst** | VOD review, scouting, stat tracking, pre-match reports |

### Production roles (rotate weekly — domain varies)
| Role | Domain | Session responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| **Broadcaster / Caster** | Content Creator | Live commentary, highlight production |
| **Social Media Manager** | Content Creator / Entrepreneur | Game-day posts, standings updates |
| **Event Coordinator** | Organizer | Match setup, scoreboard admin, equipment |
| **Wellbeing Lead** | Organizer | Pre-session check-in, culture monitor, end-of-session cohesion report |
| **Club Officers** | Organizer / Entrepreneur | Charter governance, meeting facilitation, secretary records |

The IGL role stays stable for 3–4 week blocks to allow strategy development. All production roles rotate on a published schedule from the start of the season.

## The Competition and Points System

SEM's insight that sportsmanship must be *scored*, not just expected, applied to esports. The premiership ladder tracks more than wins.

| Category | Points | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Match win** | 3 pts | Standard result |
| **Match loss** | 1 pt | You showed up and competed |
| **Forfeit / no-show** | 0 pts | No credit |
| **On-time setup** | 1 pt/match | Team ready at scheduled start |
| **Wellbeing report — positive** | 1 pt/week | Wellbeing lead reports strong cohesion |
| **Fair play deduction** | −1 pt/incident | Code of conduct violation, umpired by admin team |
| **Quality production** | 1 pt/week | Broadcasting or coordination rated by GM |

Teams that win matches but tilt, argue, or treat opponents badly will not top the ladder. A team with average match results but strong culture, clean setup, and good production output stays competitive. This is the structural mechanism for the culture you want — it doesn't rely on the teacher lecturing about sportsmanship.

## Digital Wellness — Built In, Not Bolted On

Every session includes wellness by design. This is the direct response to the screen time concern that parents and admin will raise — it must be structural, not aspirational.

**Every session (non-negotiable):**
- Healthy Gaming Exercises at session start (10 min, follows the Pledge — NASEF-sourced routine)
- Physical break at 45–50 min mark
- Wellbeing lead check-in open and close

**Dedicated wellness sessions across the season:**
- **Week 5–6 (Entrepreneur block):** Tilt management and self-regulation — using NASEF Health and Wellness Toolkit (co-developed with UCI Health / San Diego County DOE — free)
- **Week 7:** Digital citizenship, conduct, community standards, digital footprint
- **Week 11 (Showcase week):** Personal growth reflection — not performance reflection

**Screen time boundaries:**
- Program hours defined and enforced; no expectation of practice outside school time
- Home gaming is a personal choice, never a program requirement

## Scope and Misconceptions

### What this course is
- A structured season with academic, SEL, and career-readiness outcomes
- A place for students who don't fit traditional school sport culture to find belonging and leadership
- A legitimate production environment — students create content that reaches an audience

### What this course is not
- A gaming club or free play period
- Only for students who are already good at games
- A reward for high grades (the GPA requirement is for *competition*, not *participation*)
- A justification for unlimited screen time

### Common misconceptions to address in Week 1
- "Only the best players matter." The premiership system values conduct, production, and preparation alongside match results.
- "Esports isn't a real sport." Use SEM's definition: authentic competition, skill development, meaningful team experience. Esports has all three. Alberta has a provincial strategy backing this.
- "The wellness and business stuff is filler." The mental performance practices are identical to what professional esports athletes use. This is performance training. The business and content roles map to real Alberta jobs.
