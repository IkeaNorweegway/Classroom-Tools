# Grade 5 English Language Arts and Literature — Course Context
*Source: Alberta Education, "English Language Arts and Literature (K–6) Curriculum," April 2022, implemented September 2023 (Grade 5–6 extract, downloaded 2026-10-23; kept out of the repo). Read with `_meta-context.md` and `research/literacy-reading-writing-research.md`.*

**This course does not use the math/science formats.** No progressive-blanking notes packages, no interleaved ★/★★ workbooks, no 5E inquiry arc. It follows the literacy research: short explicit mini-lessons, long reading and writing time, writing about reading every day, SRSD for writing, needs-based groups, and the school's Fountas & Pinnell and Words Their Way programs used for what they do well. The workspace principles still bind (produce don't receive, retrieval, fade supports, structured peer talk, feedback with a response, every assessment item maps to an outcome).

**Teacher decisions (2026-10-23):** 60 minutes a day, one block. Units by genre. Fountas & Pinnell (guided reading, Benchmark Assessment System) and Words Their Way are in use. Three editions of student materials (standard, scaffolded, easy read).

---

## Course Overview

**Grade:** 5 | **Age:** ~10–11
**Schedule:** one 60-minute block a day (≈ 180 blocks a year).
**Organizing ideas (Grade 5):** Text Forms and Structures, Oral Language, Vocabulary, Comprehension, Writing, Conventions. Phonological awareness, phonics and fluency are not Grade 5 organizing ideas, but fluency and decoding work continues for students who need it (research §5, §14).
**Course shape:** six genre units, each with one **anchor topic** for the texts the class reads together (research §2), one **SRSD writing strategy**, and one **published piece for a real audience**.
**Reporting:** outcome-based. Every assessment item maps to a local code below.

Reader's theatre runs as a short fluency routine in every unit, not only the drama unit.

Units are not full silos: routines, mnemonics and talk moves carry forward on purpose. Each unit re-teaches its own vocabulary and strategy.

## Daily and Weekly Structure

### The 60-minute block
| Min | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Warm-up | Recall without notes: spelling patterns, morphology, unit words, one sentence-combining item |
| 5–17 | Mini-lesson | One thing taught explicitly from the mentor text: an SRSD stage, a reading move, a craft move. Model, think aloud, try together |
| 17–52 | Work time | Whole class reads and writes about the anchor text, or drafts. Teacher runs two 10–15 min groups and quick conferences |
| 52–60 | Exit | Written summary or answer with evidence; read before the next lesson |

**Groups by need, not only level:** decoding (long-word routine), fluency (repeated reading / HELPS-style routine with F&P leveled passages), comprehension (grade-level text with heavy support), on-track (extension, extra conferences). Partner reading (PALS-style) can run in work time 3 days a week so the whole class gets fluency practice while the teacher runs a group.

### The week
| Day | Warm-up | Mini-lesson | Work time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Last week's spelling patterns | New WTW pattern taught explicitly + reading focus | Read anchor text; sort |
| Tue | Morphology | Reading move | Read and write about it |
| Wed | Sentence combining | Writing (SRSD stage) | Draft |
| Thu | Unit words | Craft move from mentor text | Draft / revise; structured peer feedback |
| Fri | Mixed review | Spelling from memory (writing sort) + reflection | Finish piece, or reader's theatre |

The week is a default. Units move days around when the SRSD stage needs it (move on at mastery, not by the calendar).

### Editions
| Edition | For | What changes | What stays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Standard** | Most of the class | Built-in supports; Core and Extended items | Grade 5 outcomes |
| **B — Scaffolded** | Students who can do grade-level thinking but need more support to show it | Sentence frames, partly filled organizers, a model beside each task, chunked steps, word box. Each support is marked with its fade step | Outcomes, texts and the ceiling question |
| **C — Easy read** | Students reading well below Grade 5 (e.g. F&P levels in the Grade 1–3 range) | `notes-packages/easy-read-context.md` layout and sentence rules (5–12 words, 70/30 support strip, line height 2); grade-level text read aloud for comprehension and writing tasks; a retold text at the student's level for decoding and fluency; circle / draw / label / dictate options | Same comprehension and writing outcomes (Track A). A modified-outcome version (Track B) only with an IPP team decision (`research/significant-learning-gaps-research.md`) |

**Access vs. cognitive scaffolds (research §14):** read-aloud and text-to-speech are access scaffolds on comprehension and writing outcomes, and remove the skill on decoding and fluency outcomes. Dictation is access when the outcome is composing; it removes the skill when the outcome is spelling or handwriting. Sentence frames are cognitive scaffolds: they must fade.

**Choosing an edition:** by task, not by child forever. A student can use Edition A for writing and Edition C for reading. Use the gap profile in research §14 (listen to grade-level text and ask questions: good listening comprehension + weak reading = decoding/fluency gap).

## Design Rules (literacy versions of the workspace rules)

1. **Mentor text = worked example.** Every craft move is shown in a real text and a teacher think-aloud before students try it. Self-explanation prompt: *"What did the author do here, and why?"*
2. **Writing about reading, every lesson** (Writing to Read: 0.40; summaries 0.79 elementary). Teach each response type before asking for it.
3. **SRSD for every writing piece** (1.17): build background → discuss → model → memorize → support → independent, with goal setting, self-talk, self-check and a progress graph. Move on at mastery.
4. **Grade-level text for everyone** in mini-lessons, shared reading and cold reads; F&P leveled text for fluency practice, guided groups and independent reading (research §4).
5. **Spelling = explicit + production.** WTW sorts stay; each pattern is taught first, and every week ends with spelling from memory and checking the pattern in drafts (research §7).
6. **Grammar inside sentences.** Mentor sentences, sentence combining, editing for a short target list. No isolated grammar worksheets (research §10).
7. **Peer talk and peer feedback are structured:** a question, talk moves or roles, a checklist, a product.
8. **Device-spotting is never the task.** "Find a simile" becomes "What does this comparison make you picture, and why did the author choose it?"
9. **Copyright (public repo):** every passage is original, public-domain folklore retold in our own words, or a reference only. No published text is reproduced.
10. **Three editions, one lesson** (above). Every scaffold is labelled access or cognitive in the teacher guide.

### TF — Text Forms and Structures
*Learning outcome: Students examine how text genres, forms, and structures support and enhance communication.*
- **TF1** Examine the purpose of a variety of digital or non-digital texts.
- **TF2** Engage with a variety of genres of literary texts.
- **TF3** Determine the form and structure of a variety of literary texts.
- **TF4** Develop reading stamina by engaging with text that is personally enjoyable.
- **TF5** Examine a variety of text features that organize content and emphasize information that is most important.
- **TF6** Include a variety of text features to organize content and to identify information that is most important.
- **TF7** Categorize texts according to a variety of fiction sub-genres.
- **TF8** Examine a variety of fictional text structures, including flash-forward.
- **TF9** Examine elements within a variety of fictional texts, including theme.
- **TF10** Describe characters based on what they say, think, or do or what others say and think about them.
- **TF11** Examine organizational structures of non-fiction texts.
- **TF12** Discuss a variety of opinions regarding the structure, content, or source of information expressed in non-fiction texts.
- **TF13** Listen to poems to identify beauty or emotion.
- **TF14** Recite or sing a poem from memory.
- **TF15** Examine figurative language that can be experienced for its beauty or emotion.
- **TF16** Investigate poetic structures that contribute to creative expression of ideas, including stanzas.
- **TF17** Experiment with creating lyric poetry.
- **TF18** Listen to, read, or view scripted dramatic works to learn about artful expression of stories and ideas.
- **TF19** Examine main characters and events in a variety of scripted dramatic works.
- **TF20** Make connections between features of land and personal experiences.

Grade 5 knowledge named in the program: fiction sub-genres include **science fiction and tall tales**; "A tall tale is an exaggerated tale that describes a central legendary character with extraordinary physical features or abilities." Narrative structure: beginning, problem, multiple events with many details, resolution of problem, ending. Round and flat characters; third-person point of view; theme; flash-forward.

## Error Patterns (the LA version of the misconception inventory)

| Error pattern | Where it shows | Move |
|---|---|---|
| "The main idea is the topic" ("It's about Paul Bunyan") | Summaries | Topic + what happens / what matters: gist sentence in 20 words |
| "Theme is one word" ("friendship") | Responses to fiction | Theme is a sentence: what the story says *about* the topic |
| "A tall tale is a lie" | Tall tales | It is a genre with rules: everyone knows it's exaggerated, and the fun is in how far it goes |
| "Exaggeration = bigger numbers" | Tall-tale writing | Exaggeration that *shows* a character trait or solves the problem |
| "Revising means fixing spelling" | Writing process | Revise = ideas, order, words; edit = spelling and punctuation. Two separate passes and checklists |
| "Longer is better" | Drafts | Goal is parts present and details that matter (SRSD self-check) |
| "Fluent means fast" | Fluency | Expression and phrasing count; the fluency scale scores four dimensions |
| "Good readers don't reread" | Comprehension | Monitoring: noticing confusion and fixing it is what good readers do |
| Run-on dialogue, no new line per speaker | Narrative | Dialogue anchor note + editing target |
| Tense shifts (past → present mid-story) | Narrative | Editing target: "my story stays in the past tense" |

## Depth Ceilings

- **Tall tales:** genre features, structure, characters, exaggeration and figurative language, oral tradition. Not: folklore theory, regional dialect analysis.
- **Figurative language:** hyperbole, simile, metaphor (program names metaphor and analogy). Personification only as an extension.
- **Grammar:** the Grade 5 list above. Clauses and complex/compound sentence labels are Grade 6; students may *build* compound sentences through sentence combining without labelling them.
- **Morphology:** the Grade 5 affix list. Greek and Latin roots are Grade 6 (light exposure only).
- **Research (information report unit):** narrowing questions, notes from 2–3 sources, citing basic information. Not formal bibliographies.

## Fountas & Pinnell and Words Their Way

Summary of research §4, §7 and §16 for planning:
- **F&P guided reading:** keep the groups; group by need (decoding, fluency, comprehension) and use leveled books for fluency practice and independent reading. Some guided sessions use grade-level text with heavy support.
- **F&P BAS:** use for a starting book level and observation, not for screening (≈ 54% correct classification vs ≈ 80% for a one-minute ORF in one study). Score running records for whether the student used the letters; no "meaning cue" guessing.
- **Words Their Way:** keep the spelling inventory and weekly sorts. Teach the pattern explicitly first; add blind and writing sorts and spelling from memory; tie to morphology; put each student's patterns on the editing checklist. The word-work booklet does this.
