# Grade 7 Science — Alberta (2007 Program of Studies)
*Current as of 2026. The new Alberta Science curriculum has been implemented K–6; Grades 7–9 remain on the 2007 PoS until the new curriculum is released for those grades.*

---

## Course Overview

**Curriculum:** Alberta Science 7, 2007 Program of Studies
**Grade:** 7 | **Age:** ~12–13
**Schedule:** 1-hour classes, 3 times per week
**Year length:** 8 months — September 1 through end of April
**Audience:** Students and teachers. Materials are print-ready and structured enough for any teacher to pick up and deliver.

**Calendar constraints:**
- Fall break: ~November 11 (1–2 days)
- Christmas break: ~December 18 – January 5 (2 weeks)
- Spring break: 1 week in March
- Available teaching weeks: ~28–29

**Reporting:** Outcome-based. Each assessment must map to a specific learning outcome.

## Learning Outcomes by Unit

### Interactions and Ecosystems
**General Outcome:** Students will describe the relationships among organisms in local ecosystems and evaluate the effects of human actions on ecosystems.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. **Ecosystems** — biotic and abiotic factors; local Alberta ecosystems (boreal forest, grassland, wetland, mountains, parkland)
2. **Energy flow** — producers, consumers (primary/secondary/tertiary), decomposers; food chains and food webs; energy pyramid (only ~10% transferred per level)
3. **Biodiversity** — species richness; habitat; keystone species; ecological niches
4. **Environmental change** — natural disturbance (fire, flood, drought) vs. human-caused (habitat loss, pollution, invasive species, climate change); species at risk
5. **Sustainability** — responsible stewardship; FNMI relationship to land; ecosystem services

Major misconceptions to address:
- Decomposers are "just bacteria" and not full ecosystem members — they are essential nutrient cyclers
- Bigger organisms have more energy available to them — energy decreases up the food chain, not increases
- Nature always recovers — some ecological changes are irreversible beyond a tipping point
- Human impact is always external to the ecosystem — humans are part of the ecosystems they affect

---

### Plants for Food and Fibre
**General Outcome:** Students will investigate plant growth and reproduction, and identify the role of plants in meeting human needs for food and fibre.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. **Plant cells** — cell theory basics (all living things made of cells; cells from pre-existing cells); plant cell structures: cell wall, cell membrane, vacuole, chloroplasts, nucleus (microscopy required)
2. **Plant systems** — root system (absorption, anchoring, storage), shoot system (support, photosynthesis, reproduction); vascular tissue: xylem (water up), phloem (sugars down)
3. **Photosynthesis** — light + water + CO₂ → glucose + oxygen; role of chlorophyll; connection to food chains
4. **Reproduction** — sexual (flowers, pollination, seeds, germination) and asexual (runners, tubers, bulbs, cuttings); advantages of each
5. **Crop plants** — food crops (grains, vegetables, fruit) and fibre crops (cotton, flax, hemp); Alberta agricultural context
6. **Biotechnology** — selective breeding; GMOs (benefits and concerns); organic farming; impact on biodiversity

Major misconceptions to address:
- Plants get their food from soil — plants *make* food via photosynthesis; soil provides water and minerals, not food
- Roots take in food — roots absorb water and dissolved minerals, not pre-made food
- Flowers exist to look nice — flowers are reproduction structures; their appearance evolved to attract pollinators
- Cloning is unnatural — vegetative reproduction (asexual) is a natural plant strategy, not just a lab technique

### Heat and Temperature
**General Outcome:** Students will investigate the properties of heat and temperature and describe the effects of heat transfer on matter.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. **Temperature vs. heat** — temperature measures average kinetic energy of particles; heat is the energy transferred; same temperature ≠ same heat content (a cup vs. a lake at 20°C)
2. **Particle model and heat** — particles move faster when heated; thermal expansion and contraction result; changes of state are explained by particle model
3. **Temperature scales** — Celsius (everyday, water at 0°/100°C), Kelvin (scientific; 0 K = absolute zero); conversion: K = °C + 273
4. **Heat transfer** — conduction (particle-to-particle, solids), convection (fluid circulation, liquids and gases), radiation (electromagnetic, no medium required); insulation slows all three
5. **Specific heat capacity** — conceptual level: some materials heat up/cool down faster (metal vs. water); why water is used as a coolant and why oceans moderate climate
6. **Applications** — insulation in buildings and clothing; thermal pollution; cooking; seasonal clothing decisions

Major misconceptions to address:
- Cold is the opposite of heat — cold is absence of heat energy; "cold" does not travel, heat does
- Metal is inherently colder than wood — metal feels colder because it conducts heat away from your hand faster; both at same room temperature
- Heat and temperature are the same thing — the lake/cup example is the standard correction
- Radiation requires a medium — radiation travels through a vacuum; this is how the Sun warms Earth

### Structures and Forces
**General Outcome:** Students will investigate forces that act on structures and evaluate the effects of these forces on structures and materials.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. **Types of structures** — solid (dam, brick), frame (building, Eiffel Tower), shell (egg, igloo, car body); combination structures
2. **Forces on structures** — dead load (structure's own weight), live load (what it carries), dynamic load (wind, earthquakes, moving loads); direction matters
3. **Internal forces** — compression (pushing in), tension (pulling out), shear (sliding/tearing), torsion (twisting); every external force creates internal forces
4. **Stability** — centre of gravity; wide base increases stability; triangles and arches distribute forces efficiently; failure modes
5. **Simple machines** — lever (three classes), wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw; mechanical advantage = output force ÷ input force
6. **Materials** — properties: strength, flexibility, hardness, elasticity; choosing materials for specific structural roles

Major misconceptions to address:
- Stronger materials always make stronger structures — structural form matters as much as material (a triangulated frame in weak material can outperform a solid block in strong material)
- More weight at the bottom always increases stability — what matters is the centre of gravity relative to the base, not absolute weight
- Simple machines create energy — machines transfer and redirect forces; mechanical advantage trades force for distance (conservation of energy)

### Planet Earth
**General Outcome:** Students will investigate the structure and geological processes of Earth and explain how these processes have shaped its surface.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. **Earth's structure** — crust (oceanic thin/dense, continental thick/less dense), mantle (solid but flows over geological time, convection currents), outer core (liquid iron-nickel, source of magnetic field), inner core (solid iron-nickel, extreme pressure)
2. **Rock cycle** — igneous (cooled magma/lava; intrusive vs. extrusive), sedimentary (compressed sediments; rock layers preserve history; Alberta fossils), metamorphic (heat + pressure transform existing rock); all three can convert to any other
3. **Plate tectonics** — Earth's crust divided into tectonic plates; plates move (1–10 cm/year) due to mantle convection; three boundary types: divergent, convergent, transform; continental drift as evidence
4. **Geological hazards** — earthquakes (faults, seismic waves, Richter/Mercalli scales); volcanoes (types, distribution along plate boundaries); Alberta is seismically quiet but not immune
5. **Surface processes** — weathering (mechanical and chemical), erosion, transportation, deposition; glaciers, rivers, wind; Alberta landforms shaped by glaciation
6. **Geological time and fossils** — deep time (Earth ~4.6 billion years); relative dating (rock layers — younger on top); index fossils; Alberta's fossil record (Badlands, Burgess Shale); life through geological time

Major misconceptions to address:
- Tectonic plates are land masses above the water — plates include both oceanic and continental crust; plate boundaries are often underwater
- Geological change is always catastrophic — most geological change is imperceptibly slow; students default to volcanic/earthquake thinking
- Alberta has no geological activity — Alberta has oil sands (ancient marine sediment), the Rockies (tectonic uplift), and occasional seismic events
- Fossils are only dinosaur bones — fossils include any preserved evidence of past life: shells, imprints, casts, footprints, plant material

## Inquiry Project Options (per unit)

### Interactions and Ecosystems
1. Local ecosystem field study — map biotic/abiotic components of one local ecosystem; compare to a second ecosystem; identify stressors and recommend one conservation action
2. Species at risk research — select one Alberta species at risk; investigate ecosystem role, threats, and recovery efforts; evaluate whether current protection is sufficient
3. Invasive species investigation — research one invasive species in Alberta (purple loosestrife, European starling, carp); model the cascading effects on the native food web

### Plants for Food and Fibre
1. Seed germination experiment — test the effect of one variable (temperature, light, soil type) on germination rate; controlled experiment with data collection and graphing
2. Plant tissue microscopy — observe and sketch cross-sections of root, stem, and leaf; connect structure to function for each
3. Alberta crop analysis — select two Alberta crops (one food, one fibre); compare growth requirements, biotechnology used, and environmental impact

### Heat and Temperature
1. Insulation design challenge — design and test an insulating container to keep ice from melting; compare materials; calculate rate of heat transfer; refine design
2. Heating and cooling curves — generate a heating curve for water as it changes state; explain plateaus using the particle model
3. Energy efficiency investigation — compare heat loss in two building designs (materials or window placement); apply to Alberta climate considerations

### Structures and Forces
1. Bridge design challenge — build a bridge from limited materials to span a gap; test with increasing loads; analyse which structural element failed first and why
2. Simple machine efficiency — test three machines (lever, pulley, inclined plane); calculate actual vs. theoretical mechanical advantage; explain friction as the discrepancy
3. Structural failure analysis — research one real structural failure (Tacoma Narrows, Quebec Bridge, Algo Centre); identify which load and force type caused failure

### Planet Earth
1. Rock cycle model — collect or identify samples of each rock type; trace each sample's probable pathway through the rock cycle; explain the conditions required at each stage
2. Plate tectonics investigation — map earthquake and volcano distribution against plate boundaries; explain the pattern; predict where a new volcanic island is most likely to form
3. Alberta fossil dig simulation — research the Badlands fossil record; create a time column of Alberta geological history from the Cretaceous to present; explain what the rock layers tell us

## Depth Ceilings by Unit

| Unit | Depth ceiling | Out of scope |
|---|---|---|
| Interactions and Ecosystems | Ecosystem-level interactions | Population dynamics equations, predator-prey modelling (Lotka-Volterra), genetic diversity |
| Plants for Food and Fibre | Cell theory + plant system level; photosynthesis at process level | Cellular respiration in detail, Calvin cycle, meiosis, DNA/genetics |
| Heat and Temperature | Conceptual particle model + transfer modes | Thermodynamic laws, entropy, specific heat calculations (formula), Kelvin scale derivation |
| Structures and Forces | Conceptual force analysis + simple machines | Vector resolution, torque calculations, moment of inertia, stress/strain formulas |
| Planet Earth | Descriptive plate tectonics + rock cycle | Radiometric dating calculations, deep plate tectonics mechanisms, seismology instrumentation |
