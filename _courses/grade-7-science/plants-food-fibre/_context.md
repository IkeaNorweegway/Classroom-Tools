## Unit at a Glance

**Strand:** Life Science
**Tier:** 1
**Suggested weeks:** 5–6
**Unit colour (for materials):** Green (#16a34a)
**Guiding question:** How do plants grow, reproduce, and meet human needs for food and fibre?

## Learning Outcomes (Alberta 2007 PoS)

**LO 7-2-01** Describe the basic principles of cell theory and identify the structures of a plant cell visible under a microscope.
**LO 7-2-02** Describe the structure and function of the root and shoot systems of a flowering plant; explain how materials move through the plant using vascular tissue.
**LO 7-2-03** Describe the process of photosynthesis and explain its importance to ecosystems.
**LO 7-2-04** Describe sexual and asexual reproduction in plants; identify advantages of each strategy.
**LO 7-2-05** Identify plants used for food and fibre; compare crop management practices; evaluate the impact of plant biotechnology on agriculture and society.

## Knowledge Clusters (Conceptual Sequence)

1. **Cell theory and plant cells** — cell theory: (1) all living things are made of cells, (2) cells are the basic unit of life, (3) all cells come from pre-existing cells; plant cell structures under microscope: cell wall, cell membrane, vacuole (large central), chloroplasts (green), nucleus; plant cell vs. animal cell differences
2. **Plant systems and vascular tissue** — root system: absorption, anchoring, storage; shoot system: stem, leaves, flowers; vascular tissue: xylem (water and minerals upward from roots), phloem (sugars downward from leaves); why vascular tissue is a major evolutionary advance
3. **Photosynthesis** — process level: light + water + CO₂ → glucose + oxygen; site: chloroplasts in green parts; chlorophyll absorbs light; glucose = energy for plant; oxygen = by-product; connection to food chains (plants are the energy entry point)
4. **Reproduction** — sexual: flowers (stamen, pistil, petals, sepals), pollination (wind vs. animal), fertilization, seed formation, germination, dispersal strategies; asexual: runners (strawberry), bulbs (tulip), tubers (potato), cuttings, grafting; advantages of each
5. **Plants for food and fibre** — major food crops: grains (wheat, canola, barley — Alberta context), vegetables, fruit; fibre crops: cotton, flax, hemp; Alberta agricultural identity; before/after processing
6. **Biotechnology** — selective breeding: humans intentionally breeding for desired traits (thousands of years); GMOs: inserting specific genes for pest resistance, drought tolerance, yield; organic farming; ethical questions; impact on biodiversity (monoculture risk)

## Misconceptions Inventory

| Misconception | Correct understanding | Where to address |
|---|---|---|
| Plants get their food from soil | Plants make their own food via photosynthesis; soil provides water and dissolved minerals, not food (pre-made energy) | Section 3 (photosynthesis) — refutation-first callout box |
| Roots absorb food for the plant | Roots absorb water and dissolved minerals; they do not absorb food | Section 2 (plant systems) — callout box |
| Flowers exist to look attractive (to humans) | Flower appearance evolved to attract specific pollinators; the "beauty" is a function of pollinator biology, not human aesthetics | Section 4 (reproduction) |
| Cloning/asexual reproduction is unnatural | Many plants reproduce asexually as a natural strategy; it is not a modern or lab-only technique | Section 4 |
| GMO food is inherently dangerous | GMOs are a technique, not a category; risk depends on the specific modification; blanket safety or danger claims are both oversimplified | Section 6 — balanced callout box |
| All cells look the same | Plant cells have specific structures not found in animal cells (cell wall, large vacuole, chloroplasts); different tissues have different cell types | Section 1 |

## Scope Boundary

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| Cell theory (3 tenets) + plant cell structures visible at light microscopy | Organelle biochemistry, electron microscopy detail, eukaryote/prokaryote distinction |
| Xylem/phloem function (what moves, which direction) | Osmosis mechanism, turgor pressure, transpiration pull (cohesion-tension) |
| Photosynthesis as a process (inputs, outputs, site, function) | Calvin cycle, light reactions, ATP, electron transport, chlorophyll wavelengths |
| Sexual and asexual reproduction — strategies and advantages | Meiosis, Mendelian genetics, gene expression |
| Alberta crop plants — food and fibre categories | Detailed agronomy, soil chemistry, crop rotation science |
| Selective breeding vs. GMO — concepts and social questions | Molecular genetics, CRISPR, gene sequencing |

## Microscopy Requirements

This unit requires hands-on microscopy — plan for at least two lab days:
1. **Elodea or onion skin cells** — observe cell wall, cell membrane, vacuole, chloroplasts (Elodea), nucleus (onion with iodine stain)
2. **Cross-sections** — prepared slides of root, stem, and leaf cross-sections; connect structure to vascular tissue function

Lab safety: standard microscope care; iodine stain safety (irritant); proper slide preparation.

## Alberta Agricultural Context

- Alberta grows ~20% of Canada's wheat and ~40% of canola — both are major world crops
- Barley is Alberta's second-largest crop; hops (beer) also grown; pulse crops (peas, lentils) expanding
- Flax and hemp are both fibre and food crops grown in Alberta
- Beekeeping is a major Alberta industry — pollination connection to food production
- GMO canola: ~95% of Alberta canola is genetically modified for herbicide resistance

## FNMI Integration

- Indigenous food plants: Three Sisters (corn, beans, squash) as a companion planting strategy; camas bulbs; saskatoon berries (significant to many Alberta First Nations)
- Traditional plant knowledge as a form of biotechnology (intentional selection over generations)
- Sacred plants — tobacco, sweetgrass, sage, cedar — roles in ceremony and ecosystem (connect to Grade 6 Living Systems if students have that background)
