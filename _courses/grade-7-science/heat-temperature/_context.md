## Learning Outcomes (Alberta 2007 PoS)

**LO 7-3-01** Distinguish between heat and temperature; describe how temperature is measured using the Celsius and Kelvin scales.
**LO 7-3-02** Use the particle model of matter to explain the effects of adding or removing heat; explain expansion and contraction.
**LO 7-3-03** Describe and provide examples of the three modes of heat transfer: conduction, convection, and radiation.
**LO 7-3-04** Explain the concept of specific heat capacity and describe its effects on everyday phenomena.
**LO 7-3-05** Identify ways that knowledge of heat and insulation are applied in technology and daily life; evaluate the environmental impact of thermal energy use.

## Knowledge Clusters (Conceptual Sequence)

1. **Temperature and measurement** — temperature = average kinetic energy of particles (how fast they move on average); Celsius scale: water freezes at 0°C, boils at 100°C; Kelvin scale: 0 K = absolute zero (particles have minimum energy); conversion: K = °C + 273; thermometers (liquid expansion, digital)
2. **Heat vs. temperature** — heat = total thermal energy transferred; same temperature ≠ same heat (a cup of water at 80°C vs. a bathtub at 80°C — bathtub has far more heat because more particles); direction of heat flow is always from hotter → cooler; heat is energy in transit, not stored in an object
3. **Particle model applied to heat** — particles vibrate/move faster when heated; thermal expansion results from particles moving farther apart; contraction when cooled; changes of state (solid → liquid → gas) explained by particles overcoming attractive forces; specific examples: railroad tracks, bridges, power lines all have expansion gaps
4. **Modes of heat transfer** — conduction: particle-to-particle collision in solids; good conductors (metals), poor conductors (wood, foam, air); convection: fluid (liquid or gas) circulation — hot fluid rises, cool fluid sinks, creates currents; radiation: electromagnetic energy, no medium required; all objects above 0 K radiate heat; this is how the Sun warms Earth through the vacuum of space
5. **Specific heat capacity** — some materials require more heat energy to raise their temperature (water requires ~4× more energy than most metals to change 1°C by 1 gram); conceptual level only — no formula calculations; why water is used as a coolant; why oceans moderate coastal climate; why sand/beaches heat up and cool down faster than water
6. **Applications and technology** — insulation: reduces all three modes of transfer; home insulation (walls, attic), clothing, double-glazed windows; thermal pollution: hot water discharge into rivers lowers oxygen levels; heat in Alberta: home heating dominates energy use; passive solar design

## Misconceptions Inventory

| Misconception | Correct understanding | Where to address |
|---|---|---|
| Cold is a thing that travels — "cold air is coming in" | Cold is not a substance; it is the absence of heat; what travels is heat energy, always from hot to cold | Section 2 (heat vs. temperature) — refutation-first callout box |
| Metal is inherently colder than wood | Both metal and wood are at the same room temperature; metal feels colder because it conducts heat away from your hand much faster — you are losing heat faster, not touching something colder | Section 4 (conduction) — callout box, hands-on demo |
| Heat and temperature are the same thing | Temperature measures how fast particles move on average; heat is total energy transferred; a bathtub at 30°C has far more heat energy than a cup of boiling water despite lower temperature | Section 2 — refutation first, lake/cup analogy |
| Radiation requires a medium | Radiation is electromagnetic energy and travels through a vacuum; if it required a medium, the Sun could not warm Earth | Section 4 (radiation) — callout box |
| Insulation keeps things warm | Insulation slows heat transfer in both directions; it keeps cold things cold and warm things warm; it does not generate or store heat | Section 6 (applications) |
| Water heats up at the same rate as everything else | Water has an unusually high specific heat capacity; it takes more energy to change its temperature, which is why it is used as a coolant and why oceans moderate climate | Section 5 |

## Scope Boundary

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| Temperature as average kinetic energy (conceptual) | Formal kinetic-molecular theory derivation |
| Celsius and Kelvin scales + conversion | Fahrenheit scale (not used in Canadian science) |
| Heat vs. temperature distinction (qualitative) | Specific heat capacity formula (Q = mcΔT) and calculations |
| Particle model explanation of expansion/contraction | Thermodynamic laws, entropy, enthalpy |
| Conduction, convection, radiation — descriptions and examples | Convection cell mathematics, blackbody radiation formulas |
| Specific heat capacity conceptually | Calorimetry calculations, specific heat values in tables |
| Insulation applications in buildings and clothing | HVAC engineering, R-values and U-values |

## Lab Opportunities

1. **Metal vs. wood feeling** — touch-and-compare activity; predict, then measure temperature of both with thermometer; explains the conduction misconception
2. **Heating and cooling curves** — heat water, track temperature over time; observe plateau at 100°C during boiling (phase change); graph results; explain plateau using particle model
3. **Convection current visualization** — food colouring drops in hot vs. cold water; or paper spiral above a heat source; observe circulation
4. **Insulation challenge** — design, build, and test an insulating cup; measure temperature loss over time; compare designs; iterate

## Alberta Connections

- Alberta climate context: -40°C winters → heating dominates energy use; insulation in buildings is directly relevant
- Natural gas for home heating: Alberta's heating fuel of choice; connects to energy resources
- Chinook winds: rapid temperature change due to heat exchange in descending air masses; particle model connection
- Permafrost in northern Alberta: warming permafrost releasing trapped gases; climate connection
- Hot springs in the Rockies: geothermal heat transfer from Earth's interior
