## Knowledge Clusters (Conceptual Sequence)

1. **Types of structures** — solid structures: all one material, no internal spaces (dam, brick, ice); frame structures: rigid skeleton + open spaces (building, bicycle, Eiffel Tower); shell structures: curved surface carries load (egg, igloo, helmet, car body); combination structures (most real structures use all three); why structure type matters for function
2. **Loads** — dead load: structure's own weight (always present, always down); live load: the people, objects, or vehicles the structure must support; dynamic load: variable, often lateral — wind, earthquakes, moving traffic, snow; dynamic loads are the most destructive because they are unpredictable in direction
3. **Internal forces** — every external force creates internal forces in the material; compression: force squeezes material together (pushing in); tension: force pulls material apart (pulling out); shear: forces acting in opposite directions on parallel planes (tearing, sliding); torsion: twisting force; concrete is strong in compression, weak in tension → reinforced with rebar
4. **Stability** — centre of gravity: the point where all gravitational force can be considered to act; lower CoG = more stable; wider base = more stable; triangles are the strongest 2D shape (distribute forces and cannot deform without changing side lengths); arches direct compression to the ground; failure modes: buckling, toppling, shear failure
5. **Simple machines** — lever (three classes: fulcrum placement relative to effort and load); wheel and axle; pulley (single fixed, single movable, compound); inclined plane; wedge; screw; mechanical advantage = output force ÷ input force; trade-off: more force = more distance traveled by effort; mechanical advantage > 1 never means you get energy for free
6. **Material properties and design** — strength: resists breaking under force; hardness: resists scratching/denting; flexibility: bends without breaking (ductile vs. brittle); elasticity: returns to original shape; choosing the right material for the right role; cost, availability, environmental impact

## Misconceptions Inventory

| Misconception | Correct understanding | Where to address |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger materials always make stronger structures | Structural form matters as much as material; a properly triangulated frame in weak material often outperforms a solid block in strong material | Section 1 (structure types) + Section 3 |
| More weight at the bottom always increases stability | What determines stability is the *centre of gravity* relative to the *base of support*; heavy objects high up destabilize even with a wide base | Section 4 (stability) — callout box |
| Simple machines create energy — you get more out than you put in | Simple machines redirect and multiply force, but energy is conserved; if the machine multiplies force by 3, the effort must travel 3× the distance; no energy is created | Section 5 (simple machines) — callout box |
| Compression and tension are opposite directions of the same force | They are different types of internal response; a beam under a load experiences both compression on the top surface and tension on the bottom surface simultaneously | Section 3 (internal forces) |
| Dynamic loads are just heavier live loads | Dynamic loads are different in kind, not just magnitude; direction is unpredictable and can change rapidly; structures must be designed for lateral forces, not just vertical ones | Section 2 (loads) |

## Scope Boundary

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| Structure types and real-world examples | Detailed structural engineering calculations |

## Engineering Design Connection

This unit is ideal for the engineering design cycle:
1. **Identify the problem** — span a gap; support a load; maximize height
2. **Research and brainstorm** — which structural form? which materials?
3. **Build** — first prototype
4. **Test** — apply loads; identify failure point
5. **Evaluate and refine** — what failed? why? what would fix it?
6. **Communicate** — what did you learn about structural design from the failure?

Failure is part of the process — frame student expectations around "the structure that tells you the most is the one that fails in an interesting way."
