*Implemented September 2024. Read alongside `_meta-context.md`.*

## Course Overview

**Curriculum:** Alberta Science K–6, March 2023 (implemented September 2024)
**Grade:** 6 | **Age:** ~11–12
**Schedule:** 1-hour classes, 3 times per week
**Year length:** 8 months — September 1 through end of April
**Audience:** Students and teachers. Materials are print-ready and structured enough for any teacher to pick up and deliver.

**Calendar constraints:**
- Fall break: ~November 11 (1–2 days)
- Christmas break: ~December 18 – January 5 (2 weeks)
- Spring break: 1 week in March
- Available teaching weeks: ~28–29

**Reporting:** Outcome-based. Each assessment must map to a specific learning outcome. No blanket grades.

## Computer Science — Embedding Map

CS is not taught as a standalone unit. Its content is distributed across units where it fits logically.

| CS Concept | Embedded In | How |
|---|---|---|
| What abstraction is — simplified version of something complex | **Matter** | The particle model is itself an abstraction. Students learn the concept through the science. |
| Abstraction in design — applying it, identifying examples in daily life | **Forces** | Force diagrams are abstractions. Engineering design requires deciding what details to keep or ignore. |
| Coding structures — sequences, conditionals, loops + block coding activity | **Living Systems** | Scratch simulation of a food web or photosynthesis process. Ecosystem logic maps directly onto conditionals. |
| Computational artifacts for societal needs — weather modelling, satellites | **Climate** | Already embedded in the curriculum: radars, weather satellites, computer modelling for extreme weather. |
| Impacts of technology — environmental, social, economic, intentional/unintentional | **Energy Resources** | Technology as both energy consumer and energy enabler. Clean tech, smart grids, EVs. |
| Natural vs artificial satellites + space exploration technology | **Space** | Telescopes, probes, rovers, Ex-Alta 1. Artificial satellite design as tech/coding lens. |

## Scientific Methods — Threading Map

Scientific Methods content is woven into every unit. It is never taught in isolation.

| SM Concept | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis formation and testing | Matter (phase change experiments), Forces (plasticity/elasticity tests), Living Systems (photosynthesis experiments) |
| Controlled experiments | Matter, Forces, Living Systems — each has at least one explicit controlled experiment in the curriculum |
| Evidence evaluation and trustworthiness | Climate (comparing historical data to current), Space (Pluto reclassification as a case study) |
| Communicating scientific explanations (graphs, tables, diagrams) | Every unit — exit tickets and inquiry projects require appropriate representation |
| FNMI ways of knowing as valid scientific knowledge | Climate (Elder/Knowledge Keeper collaboration), Living Systems (sacred plants) |
| Science as self-correcting | Space (Pluto), Scientific Methods strand — used as a discussion example in any unit |

## Learning Outcomes by Unit

### Matter
**LO:** Students investigate how particles of matter behave when heated or cooled and analyze effects on solids, liquids, and gases.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. Particle model — heating/cooling causes particles to speed up/slow down; attractive forces and spacing change
2. Phase changes — change of state; volume may change but mass is conserved
3. Thermometers — Celsius scale; expansion/contraction as measurement basis; safety practices
4. Expansion and contraction — typical material responses; water's anomalous property; implications for infrastructure design

Major misconceptions to address:
- Particles are the material, not discrete entities with space between
- Mass changes during a phase change
- Ice is denser than liquid water (students assume the solid form of everything is denser)

---

### Forces
**LO:** Students analyze forces and relate them to interactions between objects.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. Internal forces — tension, compression, shear, torsion
2. External forces — applied force, friction, elastic/spring force; external forces cause internal forces
3. Plasticity and elasticity — permanent vs temporary change; depends on material properties
4. Newton's Third Law — action and reaction forces; equal, opposite, acting on different objects

Major misconceptions to address:
- The heavier/larger object exerts more force in an interaction
- Action and reaction forces cancel each other out (they act on different objects)
- Friction is not a force with direction

---

### Energy Resources
**LO:** Students investigate energy resources and explain factors that influence their use.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. Selection factors — availability, societal, economic, environmental impacts; responsible management
2. Before/after processing — some resources used both ways (wind); classification task
3. Daily life use — heating, cooling, lighting, cooking, transportation; Alberta context

Major misconceptions to address:
- Renewable = clean (not always true)
- Before/after processing maps neatly onto renewable/non-renewable (it does not — wind is both)

---

### Climate
**LO:** Students investigate climate, changes in climate, and the impact of climate change on Earth.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. Earth systems and climate — Sun interaction, greenhouse gases, ice reflection, geographic factors (latitude, proximity to water, elevation)
2. Causes of climate change — natural (volcanic, meteoric, solar output, orbital) and human (industrialization, pollution, greenhouse gases)
3. Effects of climate change — weather events, migration, water resources, wildfires, agriculture, FNMI traditional practices
4. Measuring and observing climate change — long-term data, oral narratives, ice layers, extreme weather technologies
5. Collaboration — scientists + Elders + Knowledge Keepers; traditional knowledge as valid data source

Major misconceptions to address:
- Weather and climate are the same thing
- The greenhouse effect and the ozone hole are the same phenomenon
- Climate change is a future problem (it is measurable now and affecting Alberta)

---

### Living Systems
**LO:** Students investigate the characteristics and components of and interactions within ecosystems.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. Biotic and abiotic components — ecosystem types; characteristics; role in diversity
2. Photosynthesis — light + water + CO₂ → glucose + oxygen; chlorophyll; starch as stored glucose; three controlled experiments
3. Plant-animal relationships — interdependence; human reliance on plants; sacred plants (FNMI — sage, sweetgrass, cedar, tobacco)

Major misconceptions to address:
- Plants get food from soil (one of the most persistent misconceptions in all of biology)
- Photosynthesis and respiration are opposites that cancel out
- Animals are independent of plants beyond food (miss the oxygen/CO₂ cycle)
- Decomposers are not part of the ecosystem

**Photosynthesis scope boundary (Grade 6):**
Teach at the *process* level only — not the cellular or biochemical level.

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| CO₂ from air, water from roots, light from sun → glucose + oxygen | Chloroplasts, stomata, cell types |
| Where photosynthesis happens: green parts of the plant / leaves | Wavelengths absorbed by chlorophyll |
| Chlorophyll as the substance that captures light (why leaves are green) | ATP, electron transport, biochemical reaction steps |
| Glucose = food/energy for the plant; stored as starch | Molecular structure of glucose or starch |
| Three experiments: light required, oxygen produced, green parts required | Lab procedures beyond the logic of the experimental design |

The three experiments are taught at the *logic* level: what question is being asked, what setup tests it, what the result tells us. Step-by-step lab procedures live in teacher guide and separate lab sheets.

---

### Space
**LO:** Students analyze and represent celestial bodies of the solar system.

Key knowledge clusters:
1. Solar system components — Sun, planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, meteoroids; emit vs reflect light; scale
2. Exploration technologies — telescopes, satellites, probes, rovers, spacecraft, computer modelling; Ex-Alta 1 (Alberta connection)
3. Natural vs artificial satellites; orbits; International Space Station; barriers to living in space

Major misconceptions to address:
- The solar system is crowded (it is mostly empty space — scale is wildly counterintuitive)
- Pluto is still a planet
- Stars and planets are roughly the same size

## Inquiry Project Options (per unit)

### Matter
1. Build a liquid thermometer from scratch — test expansion of liquid, calibrate to Celsius scale
2. Bridge/sidewalk gap investigation — why do structures have gaps? Test expansion of materials with heat, apply to infrastructure design
3. Ice anomaly experiment — demonstrate ice floats, hypothesize effects on aquatic life if it didn't

### Forces
1. Structure challenge — build a bridge or tower that must withstand specific applied, compression, and tension forces; label all forces acting on it
2. Action-reaction investigation — design experiments demonstrating Newton's Third Law across multiple interaction types
3. Materials engineering — test plasticity and elasticity of materials, recommend materials for specific engineering applications with justification

### Energy Resources
1. Alberta energy audit — research which energy sources power the local community; map onto before/after processing framework; present findings
2. Design a device using one energy resource (pre or post processing) to solve a real problem
3. Energy resource debate — structured Socratic debate: which energy source should Alberta prioritize? Evidence required.

### Climate
1. Climate time-series analysis — compare 30-year weather data from a local Alberta station; identify trends; construct a scientific explanation
2. Traditional knowledge research project — how have FNMI communities in Alberta observed and documented climate change on the land?
3. Extreme weather event investigation — pick one Canadian extreme weather event; explain the climate systems that produced it; identify technologies used to predict it

### Living Systems
1. Photosynthesis experiment series — three controlled experiments from the curriculum: light importance, oxygen release, starch presence
2. Local ecosystem study — go outside, map biotic and abiotic components of one local ecosystem, compare to a contrasting ecosystem type
3. Sacred plants and ecosystem roles — research FNMI sacred plants in Alberta context; connect cultural significance to ecological role in local ecosystems

### Space
1. Scale model of the solar system — true outdoor scale; students calculate distances and sizes before building
2. Ex-Alta 1 satellite design challenge — Alberta students built a satellite; what would your satellite study and how would you design it?
3. Celestial body field guide — classify, describe, and represent all solar system objects; functions as a retrieval tool throughout the unit
