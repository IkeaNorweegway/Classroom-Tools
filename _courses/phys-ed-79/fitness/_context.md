# Unit Context — Fitness and Active Living, Grades 7–9
**Course:** Physical Education and Wellness | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 7–9

---

## What This Is

Fitness at Grades 7–9 develops the knowledge and self-regulation to design personal programs and track progress with increasing independence. Students move from knowing what fitness components are (Gr 5–6) to applying training principles (Gr 7–9) and designing their own short programs.

**Core learning problems:**
- How do I design a training session that targets a specific fitness goal?
- How do I know if my program is working? What does progress look like?
- What barriers to active living do I face, and how can I address them?

---

## Fitness Components (building on Gr 5–6)

| Component | Gr 7–9 Application |
|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular endurance** | Target heart rate zones, rate of perceived exertion (RPE) |
| **Muscular strength** | Progressive overload; resistance variations |
| **Muscular endurance** | Volume and rep progressions |
| **Flexibility** | Dynamic vs. static stretching; when to use each |
| **Balance and coordination** | Sport-specific demands; injury prevention |

---

## FITT Principle (introduced Gr 7, applied Gr 8–9)

| Variable | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Frequency** | How often per week |
| **Intensity** | How hard (% max HR, RPE scale, load) |
| **Time** | Duration of session or set |
| **Type** | Mode of activity (cardio, strength, flexibility) |

---

## Key Learning Outcomes (Grades 7–9)

**Knowledge**
- Define FITT and apply it to a personal fitness goal
- Distinguish aerobic from anaerobic activity; explain the role of each
- Identify signs of overtraining and appropriate recovery

**Understanding**
- Training must be specific to the goal — cardio training doesn't build strength
- Rest and recovery are part of a program, not a break from it
- Fitness changes are gradual — expect 4–6 weeks before measurable improvement

**Skills and Procedures**
- Design a 2-week personal fitness program using FITT
- Track progress using a fitness journal (not compared to classmates)
- Reflect: what worked, what didn't, what would I change?

**Social and Personal Responsibility**
- Respect diverse bodies and fitness starting points
- Fitness data is private — not shared or compared without consent
- Set goals for yourself, not to match someone else

---

## Grade Progression

### Grade 7
- FITT introduction; personal fitness goal setting; track one component over 2 weeks

### Grade 8
- Design a personal 2-week program using FITT; monitor and adjust; reflect on barriers

### Grade 9
- Multi-component program; longer tracking period; connect to lifelong active living habits and real-world barriers (time, access, money, culture)

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- More is better (overtraining is real; FITT includes frequency — rest days are programmed, not skipped)
- Fitness only matters for sport (cardiovascular and musculoskeletal fitness are health determinants for everyone)
- You need a gym (fitness is possible with bodyweight, outdoors, in a small space)
- Sore muscles = good workout (DOMS is a sign of muscle adaptation, not quality — not soreness as a goal)
- Results should come fast (4–6 weeks for measurable change; building program consistency takes longer than results)
