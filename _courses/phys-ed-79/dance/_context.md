# Unit Context — Dance and Rhythmic Activity, Grades 7–9
**Course:** Physical Education and Wellness | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 7–9

---

## What This Is

Dance at Grades 7–9 builds on the body awareness and rhythm skills from earlier grades, adding choreographic structure, cultural depth, and peer performance. Students compose, teach, and perform short sequences with increasing complexity.

**Core learning problems:**
- How do we use movement elements deliberately to communicate an idea or emotion?
- How do we construct a sequence that has a beginning, development, and clear end?
- How do we give and receive useful feedback on a partner's or group's movement?

---

## Dance Forms in This Category

Choreographed sequences, folk/cultural forms (including FNMI), hip hop, breakdance foundations, social dance, rhythmic gymnastics — form selected to match student interest and cultural context

---

## Key Learning Outcomes (Grades 7–9)

**Movement Skills**
- Move with rhythmic accuracy through varied tempos and time signatures
- Apply all movement elements with deliberate intent (not just as patterns)
- Perform a 16–32 count sequence individually, with a partner, or in a group

**Understanding**
- Cultural context of dance forms — dance carries identity, history, and meaning
- Choreographic principles: repetition, contrast, transition, canon

**Skills and Procedures**
- Compose an 8–16 count sequence using at least four movement elements
- Teach the sequence to a partner or group
- Give specific, movement-vocabulary-based feedback ("your level change on count 5 is stronger if you drop earlier")

**Social and Personal Responsibility**
- FNMI and cultural dance treated with respect — not costume or entertainment
- Performance anxiety is real — supportive audience expectations are explicit and enforced
- Peer feedback is structured: specific, kind, and useful

---

## FNMI Connection

Cultural dance forms — especially Indigenous — carry knowledge and identity. Consult with FNMI coordinators or cultural liaisons before teaching specific forms. Where authentic instruction isn't possible, focus on principles (rhythm, storytelling through movement, relationship to land) using materials from verified sources.

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- Dance is only for trained performers (choreography and performance are learnable skills, not innate)
- Dance is only for girls or certain body types (dance is for all bodies; hip hop, breakdance, and folk forms are historically male-dominated)
- You can't be creative if you can't dance well (creativity is about intent and structure, not technical perfection)
