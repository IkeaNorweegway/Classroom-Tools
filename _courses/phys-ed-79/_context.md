# Course Context — Physical Education and Wellness, Grades 7–9
**Subject:** Physical Education and Wellness (PEW) | Alberta 2022 | **Grades:** 7–9
**Implemented:** Full implementation following K–6 rollout (2022+); 7–9 curriculum finalized

---

## Curriculum Framework

Alberta PEW is organized around **eight organizing ideas:**

| Organizing Idea | Core Focus |
|---|---|
| Active Living | Motivation, confidence, competence in a wide range of activities; lifelong participation |
| Movement Skill Development | Tactical sophistication, modified strategies, complex movement sequences |
| Social and Personal Responsibility | Ethical decision-making, inclusivity, leadership, consent |
| Safety | Risk assessment, injury prevention, responsible activity choices |
| Healthy Eating | Food literacy, nutrient knowledge, performance and health |
| Healthy Relationships | Consent, mutual respect, sexuality, healthy communication |
| Growth and Development | Puberty, mental well-being, human sexuality, decision-making |
| Daily Physical Activity (DPA) | 30 min moderate-to-vigorous activity daily |

**Primary organizers for PhysEd class materials:** Active Living, Movement Skill Development, Social and Personal Responsibility. Safety is embedded across all units.

---

## Learning Framework — KUSP

All outcomes are written using KUSP:
- **Knowledge** — what students know (concepts, vocabulary, rules, biomechanics)
- **Understanding** — why it matters (principles, strategy rationale, health connections)
- **Skills and Procedures** — what students can do (refined technique, tactical execution)
- **Performance** — demonstrating competence in authentic activity contexts

| Concept | Grade 7–9 Application |
|---|---|
| **Body awareness** | Biomechanical efficiency, body position in dynamic contexts, safety under fatigue |
| **Space awareness** | Reading and exploiting space at game pace; transition between attack and defense |
| **Effort** | Pacing strategy, force application in strikes/passes, flow in sequences |
| **Relationship** | Positional relationships, matchups, outnumbered/overloaded situations |

## Grade-Level Emphases

### Grade 7
- Transition from lead-up to modified full games
- Apply game category principles: recognize tactical transfer across sports
- Begin connecting fitness components to sport-specific demands
- Social responsibility: leadership roles, managing conflict in activity

### Grade 8
- Refine technique under game-speed conditions
- Tactical problem-solving: outnumbered, overloaded, transition situations
- Fitness: understand FITT principle; design short personal programs
- Healthy relationships: consent, communication, respect in physical contexts

### Grade 9
- Near-full game formats with standard rules
- Peer coaching and leadership in skill development
- Evaluate personal fitness progress over a training period
- Growth and development: sexuality, mental health, long-term active living

---

## Misconceptions to Address

- Talented athletes don't need to practice tactics (elite performance requires systematic tactical development)
- Fitness training is only for athletes (FITT principle applies to everyone; design is personal)
- Bigger/stronger always wins (skill, decision-making, and positioning often outperform size)
- Rules exist to slow games down (rules create structure that enables tactics to matter)
- Rest days reduce fitness (recovery is part of training; overtraining is a real performance risk)
- Stretching is always good before activity (static stretching before activity can reduce explosive performance — dynamic warm-up is preferred)

---

## Assessment Notes

- Assessment is competency-based — tactical decision-making, skill quality in context, self-regulation, leadership
- Peer observation with structured checklists is appropriate and scalable
- Self-assessment: students evaluate own skill and tactical growth over a unit
- Avoid ranking students by athletic ability; assess learning targets specifically
- Fitness assessments are personal progress benchmarks — not compared across students
- Written/exit check questions test tactical understanding, not just physical performance
