## 1.1 — What Is an Ecosystem?

An **ecosystem** is made up of all the _________________ things and all the _________________ things in an area, along with the interactions between them.

> Ecosystems are not just lists of plants and animals. They are **systems** — every part affects every other part.

An ecosystem can be as large as a boreal forest or as small as a puddle. What matters is that it includes both living and non-living components **interacting together.**

**Alberta ecosystem examples:** boreal forest · grassland · wetland (bog, marsh, fen) · mountain alpine · prairie river

---

## 1.2 — Biotic Components (Living)

**Biotic** means _________________.

Biotic components include organisms — anything that is alive or was recently alive.

**Three roles in any ecosystem:**

| Role | What they do | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Producers** | Make their own food using sunlight — the base of the food web | Green plants, algae, phytoplankton |
| **Consumers** | Get energy by eating producers or other consumers | Insects, birds, deer, wolves, humans |
| **Decomposers** | Break down dead organic matter and return nutrients to the soil | Fungi, bacteria, earthworms |

> **Misconception check:** Are decomposers part of the ecosystem?
>
> Some students think decomposers are "outside" the ecosystem — just cleaning up. They are wrong. Decomposers are **essential**. Without them, dead matter would pile up, nutrients would stay locked in dead tissue, and producers would have nothing to grow from. Remove decomposers and the ecosystem collapses within years.

**Connection prompt:** Think of the last time you saw something rotting — a log, a piece of fruit, leaves on the ground. That was decomposers at work. What would happen if that process stopped?

## 1.3 — Abiotic Components (Non-Living)

**Abiotic** means _________________.

Abiotic components are the non-living physical conditions of the ecosystem. They determine which organisms can live there.

**The four major abiotic factors:**

| Abiotic factor | How it affects the ecosystem |
|---|---|
| **Sunlight** | Drives _________________ — without it, producers cannot make food |
| **Water** | All living cells require water; affects which organisms can survive where |
| **Temperature** | Sets the range of possible organisms; limits growth seasons |
| **Soil / substrate** | Provides nutrients and anchoring for plants; affected by decomposer activity |

**Why abiotic factors matter for biodiversity:**

# Section 2: Photosynthesis — Where Does a Plant's Food Really Come From?

## 2.1 — The Big Misconception (Address This First)

Before we explain anything — go back to your Pre-Knowledge Check answer about where plants get their food.

Most people answer: **from the soil** or **from water** or **from sunlight**.

All of these answers are wrong in an important way. Here's why:

> **The misconception:** Plants get their food from the soil (by absorbing nutrients through their roots).
>
> **Why it's wrong:** Soil provides minerals — but minerals are not food. Food means energy-containing molecules the organism uses to grow and function. Plants *make* their own food. They do not absorb it from anywhere. The food is manufactured inside the plant, using light as the energy source. The soil is not the food supply — it is the mineral supply. These are completely different things.

**The evidence:** A tree weighing 1,000 kg grows in a pot with a small amount of soil. After 20 years, the soil has barely changed in mass. Where did the 1,000 kg of tree come from?

Answer: ___________________________________________________________________

*(Hint: it came from the air — specifically, from CO₂)*

---

## 2.3 — Where Each Ingredient Comes From

| Ingredient | Where the plant gets it |
|---|---|
| **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** | From the _________________ — absorbed through the leaves |
| **Water (H₂O)** | From the _________________ — absorbed through the roots and carried up the stem |
| **Light energy** | From the _________________ — absorbed by the green parts of the plant |

**Where does photosynthesis happen?**

Photosynthesis takes place in the _________________ parts of the plant — mainly in the _________________.

> **Why are leaves green?** Leaves contain a substance called **chlorophyll**. Chlorophyll is what captures light for photosynthesis — and it happens to be green. No chlorophyll, no photosynthesis.

---

## 2.4 — What the Plant Does with Glucose

Glucose is the food the plant makes for itself. It has two uses:

**Energy:** Some glucose is used immediately to power the plant's growth and repair. This is what makes glucose the plant's food — it is a source of _________________, not just a molecule.

**Storage:** Leftover glucose is converted to _________________ and stored in:
- Roots (example: a _________________)
- Seeds (example: a grain of _________________)
- Fruit

> This stored starch is what animals — including humans — eat when they eat plants. Every carbohydrate you eat traces back to photosynthesis.

---

## 2.6 — The Photosynthesis / Respiration Misconception

> **The misconception:** Photosynthesis and respiration are opposites — they cancel each other out, so plants don't really contribute to oxygen in the air.
>
> **Why it's wrong:** During daylight, a healthy plant produces far more oxygen through photosynthesis than it uses through respiration. The net result is oxygen out and CO₂ in. A healthy plant is a net oxygen producer.
>
> **The proof:** Earth's atmosphere is 21% oxygen. That oxygen came from billions of years of photosynthesis. If the two processes cancelled perfectly, there would be no free oxygen in the atmosphere at all.

## 3.1 — Beyond Food: How Plants and Animals Depend on Each Other

Most students think of plants as food sources for animals. This is true — but it is only one layer of a much deeper relationship.

**Complete the web of dependencies:**

| Animals depend on plants for... | Plants depend on animals for... |
|---|---|
| Food (glucose stored as starches and sugars) | Pollination — animals carry _________________ between flowers |
| _________________ — produced as a byproduct of photosynthesis | Seed dispersal — animals move _________________ to new locations through eating and waste |
| Habitat — structure, shelter, nesting sites | CO₂ — animals release _________________ which plants use in photosynthesis |
| Soil stability — plant roots prevent _________________ | Decomposition — when animals die, decomposers return _________________ to the soil |

> **The missing layer:** Animals depend on plants for the oxygen they breathe. This is not just a detail — it is the reason life on land exists. Before photosynthetic organisms began releasing oxygen ~2.5 billion years ago, Earth's atmosphere had almost no free oxygen. Every breath you take is a product of photosynthesis.

## 3.3 — What Happens When You Remove a Part?

Ecosystems are **systems** — connected webs, not independent lists. Removing one organism can cascade through the whole system.

**Case study — wolves in Yellowstone:**

Wolves were removed from Yellowstone National Park in the 1920s. By the 1990s, the park had changed dramatically:
- Elk populations grew unchecked and _________________ vegetation along riverbanks
- Without vegetation, rivers began to _________________ because roots weren't holding the soil
- Songbird populations declined because there were fewer _________________ for nesting
- When wolves were reintroduced in 1995, vegetation returned, rivers stabilised, and biodiversity increased

This is called a **trophic cascade** — a change at one level of the food web that ripples through the whole system.

## 3.4 — FNMI Sacred Plants and Ecological Roles

Indigenous peoples across Canada have maintained detailed knowledge of plant species, their ecological roles, and their cultural significance for thousands of years. This is **traditional ecological knowledge** — a valid scientific data source.

**Four sacred plants in many Alberta First Nations traditions:**

| Plant | Ecological role in the ecosystem | Cultural significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Sage** | Nitrogen-fixing; provides habitat for insects; drought-resistant ground cover | Used in ceremony and smudging; cleansing and healing |
| **Sweetgrass** | Stabilises wetland soil; indicator species for healthy wetlands | Braided and used in ceremony; symbol of connection and kindness |
| **Cedar** | Large habitat provider; rot-resistant; long-lived carbon sink | Protective; used in medicine, ceremony, building |
| **Tobacco** | Attracts pollinators; fast-growing soil pioneer | Offered as a gift of respect; used in prayer and ceremony |

> These plants are not only culturally significant — they are ecologically important. Their presence, health, and distribution is tracked by Knowledge Keepers as an indicator of ecosystem health. This knowledge has been accumulated and refined across generations — often over longer timescales than scientific data collection can reach.

# Section 4: CS Connection — Conditional Logic in Ecosystems

**Learning target:** I can identify if/then conditional structures in ecosystem relationships and connect them to how computer programs use the same logic.

---

## 4.1 — If/Then Logic in a Food Web

Ecosystems follow **conditional logic** — if one thing changes, then something else changes as a result. This is the same structure used in computer programming.

**Examples:**

| Ecosystem event | Conditional structure |
|---|---|
| If no sunlight → photosynthesis cannot occur | `IF sunlight = 0 THEN photosynthesis = 0` |
| If photosynthesis stops → glucose is not produced | `IF photosynthesis = 0 THEN glucose = 0` |
| If glucose is not produced → producers cannot grow | `IF glucose = 0 THEN producer_growth = 0` |
| If producers cannot grow → primary consumers starve | `IF producer_growth = 0 THEN primary_consumers = declining` |

This is called a **cascade** — one condition triggers the next, which triggers the next.

**Write your own conditional chain:** Starting with "a severe drought reduces available water..."

```
IF water = low THEN ________________________________________________

IF __________________ THEN ________________________________________

IF __________________ THEN ________________________________________
```
