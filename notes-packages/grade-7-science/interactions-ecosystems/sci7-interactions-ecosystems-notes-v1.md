> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Decomposers are just garbage collectors — the ecosystem could work without them."*
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) are not optional cleanup crew. They are the organisms that break down dead material and return nutrients to the soil. Without them, nutrients would be locked permanently in dead matter and producers would run out of the minerals they need to grow. Decomposers complete the nutrient cycle — they are as essential as any producer or consumer.

**The problem with energy transfer:**

Where does the lost energy go? ____________ (used for movement, growth, body heat, digestion).

**The Energy Pyramid:**

An energy pyramid shows the amount of energy available at each trophic level. The base is ____________ and each level above is ____________.

```
          [Tertiary consumers]     ← very little energy
        [Secondary consumers]
      [Primary consumers]
    [Producers]                    ← most energy is here
```

*Label the pyramid above. Add approximate energy amounts if producers have 10,000 units.*

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Top predators like wolves and eagles have the most energy because they eat everything below them."*
>
> **Why this is wrong:** At each level, about 90% of the energy is lost. By the time you reach the top predator, only a tiny fraction of the original energy is available. A field of grass may contain 10,000 units of energy → the deer/hare feeding on it can access ~1,000 → the wolf/eagle feeding on them can access ~100. Top predators are energy-poor compared to producers. This is why top predators need large territories — there simply isn't much energy available at the top.

| Human impact | Mechanism | Example in Alberta |

| Climate change | ____________ | permafrost thaw, earlier springs, shifted migration |

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Nature always recovers — it's resilient."*
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Ecosystems have tipping points — thresholds beyond which recovery does not happen. Examples: once a coral reef bleaches repeatedly, the structural community collapses and does not rebuild on human timescales; once grassland is broken by cultivation, returning it to native prairie takes decades of active restoration; once a species goes extinct, it is gone permanently. Resilience is real but limited.

**Invasive species** are organisms introduced to an ecosystem where they did not evolve. Because they often lack ____________ predators, their populations can grow unchecked and outcompete or prey on native species.

Alberta at-risk invasive species:
- **Zebra mussels** — not yet established; prevention program at borders; would devastate native bivalves and clog water infrastructure
- **Purple loosestrife** — wetland plant; creates dense monocultures that eliminate native vegetation and waterfowl nesting habitat

**Alberta species at risk:**

| Species | Risk reason | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Woodland caribou | Habitat fragmentation from roads and industrial activity | Threatened |
| Burrowing owl | Grassland conversion to agriculture | Endangered |
| Swift fox | Hunting + habitat loss; reintroduction program | Threatened |
| Bull trout | Water temperature, sedimentation | Threatened |
