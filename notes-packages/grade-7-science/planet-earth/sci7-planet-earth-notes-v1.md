### Guided Notes

**The theory of plate tectonics:** Earth's outer shell (lithosphere) is broken into approximately ____________ major plates and several smaller ones. These plates ____________ at a rate of 1–10 cm per year (roughly the rate your fingernails grow). The driving force is ____________ currents in the mantle below.

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Tectonic plates are the same as continents."*
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Plates include both continental crust and oceanic crust. Many plate boundaries are ____________ (in the middle of oceans). The North American Plate, for example, includes most of North America AND the western half of the Atlantic Ocean. Some plates are almost entirely oceanic (Pacific Plate). Continents ride on plates; they are not the same thing as plates.

**Evidence for plate tectonics (how we know):**

| Evidence type | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Continental fit | Coastlines of Africa and South America ____________ like puzzle pieces |
| Matching fossils | Same species fossils found on ____________ continents now separated by thousands of km of ocean |
| Paleomagnetic striping | Magnetic reversal patterns are ____________ on both sides of mid-ocean ridges — like a tape recording of spreading |
| Earthquake/volcano distribution | Most earthquakes and volcanoes occur ____________ — along plate boundaries |
| GPS measurements | Modern technology directly measures plate ____________ |

**Three boundary types:**

**DIVERGENT BOUNDARY** — plates ____________ apart

Features: new seafloor created; mid-ocean ridges; volcanic activity (gentle, fluid lava); rift valleys on land

Example: ____________ Ridge (North America + Europe separating) — Iceland sits on this ridge

Alberta connection: ____________ (there are no divergent boundaries near Alberta)

---

**CONVERGENT BOUNDARY** — plates ____________ together

Three subtypes:
1. **Ocean + continent:** denser oceanic plate ____________ (subducts) under continental plate → trench + volcanic arc on continent
   Example: ____________ + North American plate → Cascade volcanoes

2. **Ocean + ocean:** older, denser plate subducts → deep trench + volcanic island arc
   Example: ____________ + Eurasian plate → Japan, Marianas Trench

3. **Continent + continent:** neither subducts; both crumple upward → major mountain chain
   Example: India + Eurasia → ____________ Mountains

**Alberta connection:** The Rocky Mountains formed ~100 million years ago when oceanic plates pushed under North America, and later continental collision crumpled up the layers. Alberta itself was pushed and folded. Alberta is safe from active tectonics today, but its rocks still record that ancient collision.

---

**TRANSFORM BOUNDARY** — plates ____________ sideways past each other

Features: earthquakes; no new crust, no subduction; the two plates grind and lock
Example: ____________ Fault, California — North American plate grinding past Pacific plate

### Guided Notes

**What is a fossil?** Any ____________ of past life preserved in rock or sediment. Not just bones.

**Types of fossils:**

| Type | How it forms | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement | minerals replace original material atom by atom | petrified wood, many dinosaur bones |
| Mould | organism dissolves, leaving a ____________ in rock | shell impression |
| Cast | mould fills with minerals, creating a 3D ____________ | cast of a shell |
| Compression | organism flattened under sediment pressure | leaf fossils, fern imprints |
| Amber preservation | organism trapped in tree resin, now hardened | insects in amber |
| Trace fossil | preserved ____________ of an organism, not the organism itself | footprints, burrows, coprolites (fossil dung) |

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Fossils are mostly large dinosaur bones."*
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Most fossils are ____________ — microscopic algae, pollen, bacteria. Large bones are rare because they require very specific conditions to preserve (rapid burial, mineral-rich water, no scavengers). The vast majority of the fossil record consists of shells, marine invertebrates, plant fragments, and microfossils. Alberta's Badlands fossil sites are remarkable precisely because large dinosaur fossils ARE found there — they are the exception.

**How fossils form (conditions needed):**
1. Organism must be ____________ quickly (before decomposition or scavenging)
2. Burial must occur in ____________ — the only environment where sedimentary rock forms
3. Mineral-rich groundwater slowly replaces or fills the hard parts over ____________ of years

**Dating rock layers:**

**Law of superposition:** In undisturbed rock layers, ____________ layers are older and ____________ layers are younger. This allows relative dating — we can say layer A is older than layer B without knowing the actual age in years.

**Index fossils:** fossils of organisms that were ____________ (found in many locations), ____________ (spread across different rock types), and existed for a ____________ time period. If you find the same index fossil in rocks in two different places, those rocks are the same ____________.

**Relative vs. absolute dating:**

| Method | What it tells you | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Relative dating | Which layers are ____________ or ____________ than others | Law of superposition, index fossils |
| Absolute dating | Actual ____________ of the rock or fossil | Radioactive decay (not required at Grade 7 level) |

**Geological time scale — broad eras:**

| Era | Approximate time range | Life present |
|---|---|---|
| Precambrian | 4.6 billion – 540 million years ago | simple single-celled life; late period: first multicellular life |
| Paleozoic | 540 – 252 million years ago | marine invertebrates, fish, first land plants, first reptiles |
| Mesozoic | 252 – 66 million years ago | ____________ era; mammals appear; Alberta's oil sands deposit |
| Cenozoic | 66 million years ago – present | mammals diversify; humans appear in last 300,000 years |

**Alberta's fossil record:**

Alberta is one of the world's most significant fossil locations because:
1. The ____________ era rock layers are exposed at the surface in the Badlands (erosion removed overlying rock)
2. The Cretaceous interior seaway covered Alberta ~75 million years ago → preserved marine fossils AND nearby dinosaur fossils
3. **Royal Tyrrell Museum (Drumheller):** world's largest collection of ____________ fossils; one of Alberta's major scientific assets
4. **Burgess Shale** (BC border, UNESCO World Heritage Site): Cambrian marine life (~505 million years ago) — some of the oldest animal fossils ever found
