### Notes

`[Diagram: A simple plant — roots underground, one stem, a few leaves near the top. Same style as the shared example diagram used in the class activity.]`

**Match the job to the part.**

| Part | | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 🌱 Roots | | 🏗️ Holds the plant up; moves things between roots and leaves |
| 🌿 Stem | | 💧 Takes in water and minerals; anchors the plant; stores food |

The stem has **two pipes** inside it.

**Word bank:** xylem · phloem

`[Diagram: Stem cutaway with two arrows — one pointing up labelled with a blank, one pointing down labelled with a blank]`

- The **water pipe** (___________________) moves water **up**, from roots to leaves.
- The **food pipe** (___________________) moves sugar **down**, from leaves to the rest of the plant.

> **MISCONCEPTION:** Some students think — *"Roots absorb food for the plant."*
>
> Why this fails: Plants can grow in water with only minerals added — no soil, no "food" at all. If roots absorbed food, this wouldn't work.
>
> Circle: Can a plant grow with just water + minerals + light? **YES** / NO
>
> **Correct understanding:** Roots take in **water and minerals only** — not food. The plant makes its own food later (Section 3!).

**Connection:** Have you ever seen a plant's roots (like in a clear cup, or a houseplant)? What did they look like?

## Section 3: Photosynthesis

**Learning target:** I can show what a plant needs to make food, and what it makes.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Picture | What it does (a few words) |
|---|---|---|
| photosynthesis | ☀️🍃 | |
| chlorophyll | 🟢 | |
| glucose | 🍬 | |
| oxygen | 💨 | |

### Notes

Plants make their own food. This process is called **photosynthesis**. It happens inside **chloroplasts**, in the green parts of the plant. **Chlorophyll** is the green colour that captures sunlight.

**Fill in the missing part of the equation.**

Hint: it's the plant's food — a type of sugar.

`[Diagram: Equation box — ☀️ light + 💧 water + CO₂ carbon dioxide → blank line + O₂ oxygen]`

light + water + carbon dioxide &rarr; ___________________ + oxygen

Plants are the start of food chains, because they make their own food and every other living thing depends on that — directly or indirectly.

> **MISCONCEPTION:** Some students think — *"Plants get their food from the soil."*
>
> Why this fails: A tiny seed grows into a huge tree over many years. The soil in the pot barely gets lighter. Where did all that extra tree "stuff" come from, if not the soil?
>
> Circle: **Air, water, and sunlight** / Soil
>
> **Correct understanding:** Plants build almost their whole body from **air (carbon dioxide) and water**, using energy from sunlight. That's what photosynthesis means. Soil gives water and minerals — not food-mass.

**Connection:** Think of the last meal you ate. Which part of it, even a small part, started with a plant doing photosynthesis?

## Section 4: Reproduction

**Learning target:** I can compare how plants make new plants — with seeds, or without.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Picture | What it does (a few words) |
|---|---|---|
| pollination | 🐝 | |
| fertilization | 🌸 | |
| sexual reproduction | 🌼➡️🌱 | |
| asexual reproduction | 🥔➡️🌱 | |

### Notes

**Sexual reproduction** uses seeds. **Label the flower parts.**

**Word bank:** petal · stamen · pistil · sepal

`[Diagram: A simple flower cross-section with 4 blank label lines pointing to the petals, the stamen (pollen-producing part), the pistil (seed-producing part), and the sepals at the base]`

The steps: pollination (pollen moves — by wind or by an animal like a bee) &rarr; fertilization &rarr; seed forms &rarr; seed spreads &rarr; new plant grows.

**Asexual reproduction** makes a new plant **without** a seed — it's an exact copy of the parent. **Match the method to the example.**

| Method | | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Runners | | 🍓 Strawberry |
| Bulbs | | 🌷 Tulip |
| Tubers | | 🥔 Potato |

> **MISCONCEPTION:** Some students think — *"Flowers are colourful just to look pretty for people."*
>
> Why this fails: Bees and other pollinators see colours and patterns differently than people do — many flowers have patterns invisible to us but bright to bees.
>
> Circle: Flower colour evolved to attract **pollinators** / people
>
> **Correct understanding:** Flower colour and shape evolved to attract the specific pollinator that visits it — not for human enjoyment.

> **MISCONCEPTION:** Some students think — *"Cloning/asexual reproduction is unnatural — only something scientists do."*
>
> Why this fails: Strawberry runners, potato eyes, and tulip bulbs all multiply on their own in a garden with zero human help.
>
> Circle: Asexual reproduction is **natural, and humans borrowed the idea** / only done in a lab
>
> **Correct understanding:** Many plants reproduce asexually on their own in nature. Cuttings and grafting are humans copying a natural strategy, not inventing a new one.

## Section 6: Biotechnology

**Learning target:** I can compare selective breeding and GMOs, and think about the good and bad sides of each.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Picture | What it does (a few words) |
|---|---|---|
| selective breeding | 🌽➡️🌽 | |
| GMO | 🧬 | |
| biodiversity | 🌾🌻🌿 | |
| monoculture | 🌾🌾🌾 | |

### Notes

**Compare the two methods.**

| | Selective breeding | GMO |
|---|---|---|
| How old is it? | Thousands of years | About 50 years |
| How it works | Choose parent plants with good traits, breed them together | Insert one specific gene directly |
| Speed | Slow — many generations | Fast — one generation |

About **95%** of Alberta's canola is a GMO variety (bred to resist herbicide).

**Growing the same one crop everywhere (monoculture)** raises the risk of a disease wiping out a whole harvest, because there's less **biodiversity** — fewer different types of plants to fall back on.

> **MISCONCEPTION:** Some students think — *"All GMO food is dangerous."*
>
> Why this fails: GMO is a method — like selective breeding is a method. A method isn't "safe" or "dangerous" by itself. What matters is which specific gene was changed, and why.
>
> Circle: Risk depends on the **specific change made** / the method itself
>
> **Correct understanding:** Both selective breeding and GMOs have been studied for safety in approved crops. Blanket "always safe" or "always dangerous" claims are both oversimplified.

### Retrieval Check

*Cover your notes. Answer from memory.*

1. Circle True or False: Selective breeding takes many generations; GMOs can work in one. **TRUE** / FALSE
2. Match the method to the example: bacteria engineered to produce insulin → **GMO**; a seedless watermelon bred over many generations → **Selective breeding**
3. Circle: About ___ % of Alberta's canola is GMO — 50 / 75 / **95**
4. Application — A student says: "Selective breeding is natural, so it must be safer than GMOs." Circle what's wrong: Selective breeding also changes an organism's genes over time / GMOs have never been tested
