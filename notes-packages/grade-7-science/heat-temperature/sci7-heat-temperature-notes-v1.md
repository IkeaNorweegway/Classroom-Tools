### Guided Notes

> **MISCONCEPTION — BEFORE WE START**
> *"Heat and temperature are the same thing."*
>
> **The test:** A cup of water at 90°C and a bathtub full of water at 90°C have the same ____________. But if you put your hand in each, the bathtub would take much longer to cool down. Why? The bathtub has far more ____________ — more particles, each moving at the same average speed, so total kinetic energy is much higher. Temperature tells you *how fast* particles are moving on average. Heat tells you *how much total energy* is being transferred.

**Heat** is energy ____________ from a hotter object to a cooler one. Heat is not stored in an object — it is what we call the ____________ of energy between objects of different temperatures.

**Direction of heat flow:** Heat always flows from ____________ (higher temperature) to ____________ (lower temperature) — never the other way, unless a machine forces it (like a refrigerator or air conditioner).

**Thermal equilibrium:** When two objects in contact reach the ____________ temperature, heat stops transferring. They are in thermal equilibrium.

**The lake and the cup:**

|  | Cup of water (80°C) | Lake (80°C) |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | same | same |
| Number of particles | very few | trillions × more |
| Total heat energy | ____________ | ____________ |
| Which cools your burn faster if spilled? | ____________ | — |

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Cold travels — cold air comes in through the window."*
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Cold is not a substance that travels. What happens is that ____________ leaves your warm house through the window and is lost to the cold outside air. When a cold wind "hits" you, it is not cold travelling to you — it is your body ____________ heat to the moving cold air faster. "Cold" is just the word we use for low heat energy; it doesn't move, arrive, or leave.

### Guided Notes

There are three ways heat moves:

---

**CONDUCTION** — heat transfer through ____________ contact, particle-to-particle

How it works: Fast-moving particles in the hot region bump into ____________ moving particles in the cooler region → energy transfers → heat moves through the material.

Best in: ____________ (particles are close together and touch)

Good conductors: ____________ (metals — why pans are metal)
Poor conductors (insulators): ____________ (wood, rubber, foam, air — why pot handles are not metal)

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Metal is colder than wood."*
>
> **The test:** Touch a metal desk and a wooden desk that have been in the same room for hours. Metal feels colder — but measure them with a thermometer. They are at ____________ temperature. Metal feels colder because it ____________ heat away from your hand much faster. Your hand is the warm object; the desk is the cooler one. Heat flows from your hand into the desk. Metal conducts so quickly that your hand loses heat fast — your brain reads this as "cold." Wood conducts so slowly that your hand barely loses any heat — your brain reads this as "warm."

---

**A note on "keeping things cold":**

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Insulation keeps things warm."*
>
> **More precisely:** Insulation slows heat transfer in ____________ directions. A cooler bag keeps ice cold just as well as it keeps hot food hot — by slowing the flow of heat across the temperature difference. The insulation doesn't know which side is "warm."
