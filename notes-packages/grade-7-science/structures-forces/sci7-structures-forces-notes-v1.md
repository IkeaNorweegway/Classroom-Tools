### Guided Notes

Every structure must handle multiple types of loads — forces acting on it from many sources.

**Dead load:** the ____________ weight of the structure itself. Always present. Always acts ____________ (gravity). Cannot be avoided — part of structural design is keeping dead load manageable.
Examples: weight of steel beams, concrete, glass, roof tiles.

**Live load:** the weight of ____________ the structure is designed to support. Varies — it is not always at maximum. Acts ____________ (gravity).
Examples: people, furniture, vehicles, snow on a roof.

**Dynamic load:** ____________ and often ____________ in direction. The most dangerous loads in terms of structural failure.

| Source | Direction | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Wind | ____________ (lateral) | pushes on walls, lifts roofs |
| Earthquake | ____________ (lateral and vertical) | shakes foundations, causes structural collapse |
| Moving traffic | ____________ and ____________ | rhythmic vibration; resonance danger |
| Impact (crash) | any direction | sudden concentrated force |

**Why dynamic loads fail structures:**

Static loads are predictable — engineers design for the maximum expected load. Dynamic loads are ____________ in direction and ____________ in magnitude. They can also cause ____________: if a force is applied at a structure's natural frequency, it can cause it to vibrate with increasing amplitude until it fails.

> **Historical example:** The Tacoma Narrows Bridge (1940) — collapsed not because of extreme wind speed, but because wind matched the bridge's ____________ frequency. The bridge started oscillating and tore itself apart.

**Alberta loads to consider:**
- Wind: high winds on open prairies; chinook winds in foothills
- Snow: dead load that accumulates — Alberta roofs must be designed to handle snow weight
- Frost heave: freezing ground expands (dynamic), pushing up foundations

### Guided Notes

External forces (loads) applied to a structure create ____________ forces inside the materials of the structure. Engineers must understand what type of internal force each part of a structure experiences, because different materials resist different forces.

**The four internal forces:**

**COMPRESSION:** forces that ____________ material together (squeezing inward)
- Direction: force pushes ____________
- Materials that resist well: ____________ (concrete, stone, brick)
- Failure mode: buckling (long columns under compression buckle sideways)
- Example in structures: column holding up a floor, arch bridge under load

**TENSION:** forces that ____________ material apart (pulling outward)
- Direction: force pulls ____________
- Materials that resist well: ____________ (steel cables, ropes)
- Failure mode: snapping
- Example in structures: cables in a suspension bridge, rebar in concrete

**SHEAR:** forces acting on ____________ planes in ____________ directions
- Direction: parallel to the surface, opposing each other
- Like trying to ____________ a material
- Example in structures: bolts in a joint, screws, a knife cutting through material

**TORSION:** a ____________ force
- Direction: rotational, around the long axis
- Like ____________ a wet towel
- Example in structures: a shaft in an engine, a drill bit, a bridge deck in a twisting wind

> **Key insight:** A single structural element often experiences multiple types simultaneously. A beam supporting a floor experiences ____________ on its top surface (being squashed by the floor above) and ____________ on its bottom surface (being stretched by the weight pulling down). This is why steel I-beams have flanges at top and bottom — more material where compression and tension are greatest.

### Guided Notes

**Centre of gravity (CoG):** the single point where the entire weight of an object can be considered to act. For a uniform object, the CoG is at the ____________. For an irregular object, it depends on where mass is distributed.

**The stability rule:**

A structure is stable when its ____________ of gravity is ____________ its base of support. When the CoG moves outside the base, the structure ____________.

Lower CoG → ____________ stable
Wider base → ____________ stable

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"A heavy bottom always makes a structure more stable."*
>
> **More precisely:** What determines stability is where the centre of gravity is *relative to the base of support*. A very tall structure with a wide base but all its mass near the top can still topple. A short, wide structure with mass at the bottom is highly stable. The relationship between CoG position and base area is what matters — not weight alone.

**Increasing stability in design:**
- Widen the ____________ (triangle at the base)
- Lower the centre of ____________ (heavy components at the bottom)
- Use ____________ (rigid triangular units that cannot deform)

**Why triangles?** A triangle is the only polygon that cannot change shape without changing the length of its sides. A square under force can become a rhombus (deform) — a triangle cannot deform. This is why ____________ is the fundamental structural element in bridges, cranes, and towers.

**Failure modes:**

| Failure | Cause | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Buckling | ____________ in a slender column | tall, thin column under excessive compression |
| Toppling | CoG moves ____________ base | tower crane with too much load on one side |
| Shear failure | opposing forces tear through a joint | bolt snapping in a connection |
| Fatigue | repeated loading and unloading over time | metal cracking after millions of cycles |

**Arches** redirect ____________ down and outward to the supports (abutments). They are highly efficient under compression — stone and concrete arches have lasted thousands of years. The weakness: arches fail catastrophically if the abutments move apart.

**Learning target:** I can identify the six simple machines, calculate mechanical advantage, and explain why machines do not create energy.

| Simple machine | | |

| Effort force | | |

### Guided Notes

A **simple machine** changes the ____________ or ____________ of a force. It does not create energy — it ____________ energy from input to output.

**Mechanical advantage:** MA = ____________ force ÷ ____________ force

If MA > 1: the machine multiplies your ____________ (you apply less force, but over more distance)
If MA = 1: direction of force changes only
If MA < 1: the machine multiplies your ____________ (you apply more force over less distance, but get speed)

> **MISCONCEPTION**
> *"Simple machines give you more power — you get more out than you put in."*
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Energy is conserved. If a machine multiplies your force by 3, you must push 3× the distance. The total work (force × distance) is the same on both sides — minus friction losses. You never get more energy out than you put in. What machines give you is **convenience** — the ability to apply a smaller force over a longer distance, which is often much more practical.

**The six simple machines:**

**1. LEVER** — a rigid bar that pivots on a ____________ (fulcrum)

Three classes based on where the fulcrum (F), effort (E), and load (L) are:

| Class | Arrangement | Example | MA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st class | F between E and L | ____________, crowbar | can be >1 or <1 |
| 2nd class | L between F and E | wheelbarrow, ____________ | always ____________ |
| 3rd class | E between F and L | tweezers, ____________ | always ____________ |

**2. WHEEL AND AXLE** — a large wheel attached to a smaller axle; rotating one rotates the other
MA = radius of ____________ ÷ radius of ____________
Examples: doorknob, steering wheel, screwdriver, ____________

**3. PULLEY** — a wheel with a rope over it; changes direction or magnitude of force

| Type | MA | How |
|---|---|---|
| Single fixed pulley | ____________ | changes ____________ only |
| Single movable pulley | ____________ | multiplies force by 2 |
| Compound (block and tackle) | = number of ____________ | each rope segment supports part of load |

**4. INCLINED PLANE** — a ____________ surface; reduces force required to raise an object
MA = length of ramp ÷ height of ramp
Example: ____________, ____________, ____________

**5. WEDGE** — two inclined planes back to back; converts a pushing force into a splitting force
Examples: ____________, ____________, ____________

**6. SCREW** — an inclined plane ____________ around a cylinder; converts rotational force into linear force
Examples: ____________, ____________, ____________
