## Overview
::: overview
**Big idea:** an equation is a balance, and every move that keeps it level (add or subtract the same number or x-term on both sides, multiply or divide both sides by the same non-zero integer) makes an equivalent equation with the same solution. Before that can work, students need to tidy each side: combine like terms, remove zero pairs, and distribute an integer factor across brackets. Solving with x on both sides is the same balance move applied to an x-term. Verifying by substitution in the original equation (and in any equivalent equation, to find where a mistake crept in) closes the loop, and word problems (two plans, two passes, two savers, two perimeters) are where x on both sides comes from naturally.
**Sequence:** algebra tiles, terms, coefficients and constants, like terms, zero pairs, the distributive property as groups of tiles, including a negative factor (−3(x − 2) = −3x + 6), simplifying one side of an equation (LE1) → the balance, equivalent equations, one- and two-step equations with integer (often negative) solutions, dividing by a negative, simplifying first (LE2) → x on both sides: take the smaller x-term off both sides, brackets first, a negative solution, a negative x-term (LE3) → verifying in the original and in an equivalent equation, the five-step word-problem routine, phone plans, ski passes, savings, bus fare vs. rider card, perimeters, temperatures (LE4).
**Materials:** algebra tiles (a class set; paper tiles cut from card stock work), a pan balance with small paper bags and centimetre cubes (or a drawn balance), mini whiteboards, a printed tile mat with a centre line for equations.
**Depth ceiling:** 7A2 is limited to integers: integer coefficients, constants and solutions. The distributive property uses an integer factor (no fractions, no variable outside the brackets). No fraction coefficients (x/3 = 4 or ½x) and no inequalities: both are Grade 8. In the 2016 program x on both sides was Grade 9; in the draft it is Grade 7, so it is fully in scope here. Keep x-terms on both sides to small coefficients so tiles still work; switch to symbols once the move is understood.
**Reading level:** student pages are written at about Grade 3–4. The math words (variable, term, coefficient, constant, expression, like terms, zero pair, distributive property, equation, solution, solve, equivalent equations, inverse operation, opposite of a term, substitute, verify) are the learning; teach each with tiles or the balance on the board. "Verify" is taught here as a math word.
:::

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| "2x + 3x = 5x²" | Build it with tiles: 2 tall tiles and 3 tall tiles are 5 tall tiles. An x² tile is a big square; nobody made one. Say "five x's," not "five x." |
| "3x + 2 = 5x" | Two kinds of tile can't be counted as one kind. Ask: "Is a small tile the same as a tall tile?" Put 3x + 2 on the board and leave it alone. |
| "2(x + 3) = 2x + 3" | Lay out 2 groups of (x + 3) and count the small tiles. Draw arrows from the 2 to EACH term. |
| Sign slips with a negative factor | −3(x − 2): write −3 × x and −3 × (−2) separately, and use the integer rule from topic 2 (negative × negative = positive). |
| Doing something to one side only | The balance tips. Use a real pan balance or the drawing; write the move on both sides every time ("− 3 | − 3"). |
| "Move a term across and keep its sign" | Do not teach "move it across." Say "subtract 3x from both sides" and write it under both sides. The tiles make it concrete: the crossed-out tiles appear on both sides. |
| Dividing by a negative (−4x = 12 → x = 3) | Check by substituting: −4(3) = −12, not 12. The check catches the sign. |
| Checking only the last line | Insist on substituting into the ORIGINAL. Show Leo's example: the answer works in his wrong first step but not in the original. |
| Word problems written as one-sided equations | Ask "what are the two things that are equal?" Write each side's expression first (Plan A: 20 + 5x, Plan B: 35 + 2x), then the = sign. |

## Lessons
### Lesson 1 — Simplifying expressions (60 min)
DO: Pre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1 previews substitution (LE4), Q2–3 one-step equations (LE2), Q4 integer adding (zero pairs), Q5 the distributive property with numbers, Q6 the balance.
SHOW: Algebra tiles: the tall tile is x, the small square is 1, red is negative. Build 3x + 2.
SAY: "A term is one part, split by + or −. The coefficient is the number in front of x. The constant has no x."
DO: Like terms: 2x + 3x with tiles. Students build 4x + 2x and 4x + 2 and say which one becomes one term.
WATCH: "5x²" and "3x + 2 = 5x." Misconception box: two kinds of tile can't be counted as one kind.
SHOW: Zero pairs: x with −x, and +1 with −1. Worked Example 1 (3x + 4 − x − 6 = 2x − 2): cross out the pairs.
DO: The distributive property: 2 groups of (x + 3). Count all the tiles: 2x + 6.
WATCH: "2(x + 3) = 2x + 3." Misconception box: point at the missing 3 tiles.
CUE: Worked Example 2 (−3(x − 2)): 3 groups, then flip every tile; check with the integer sign rules. Worked Example 3 simplifies one side of an equation, the bridge to Lesson 2.
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.

### Lesson 2 — Keeping the balance (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: 5x − 2 + x, 4(x − 2), −7 − (−3), 2 + 3 × 4.
SHOW: The balance with 2 bags and 3 blocks against 11 blocks. "Each bag holds the same number. How do we find it without opening a bag?"
SAY: "Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Then it stays level. The new equation is equivalent: it has the same solution."
WATCH: One side only. Misconception box: take 3 off the left only; the right side is heavier.
DO: Worked Example 1 (2x + 3 = 11): subtract 3, then divide by 2. Name the inverse operations. Check by substituting.
DO: Worked Example 2 (x − 5 = 3) with tiles: add 5 to both sides; the −5 and +5 are zero pairs.
CUE: Worked Example 3 (3x + 7 = 1, x = −2) and Worked Example 4 (−4x = 12, x = −3): negative solutions are normal. Check every one.
DO: Worked Example 5 (2(x + 4) = 14): simplify first, then solve. Then the equivalent-equations table, including adding an x-term to both sides (preview of Lesson 3).
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2. Quiz next class (LE1–LE2).

### Lesson 3 — Solving with x on both sides (60 min)
DO: Mid-unit quiz (25 min) first. Then the lesson.
SHOW: The balance with 5 bags and 2 blocks against 3 bags and 10 blocks. "Bags on both sides. What can we take off both sides?"
SAY: "Take the same number of bags off both sides. Usually take off the smaller x-term, so the x-term left over is positive."
DO: Worked Example 1 (5x + 2 = 3x + 10, x = 4) with the flow chart. Students write each move under both sides.
WATCH: "Move it across and keep its sign." Misconception box with tiles: 4x + 1 = 2x + 7 becomes 2x + 1 = 7, not 6x + 1 = 7. Check x = 1 in the original to show the wrong version fails.
DO: Worked Example 2 (4(x − 1) = 2x + 6, x = 5), Worked Example 3 (2x + 9 = 5x + 15, x = −2), Worked Example 4 (7 − 2x = x + 1: add 2x, x = 2).
CUE: Post the five-step strategy: simplify each side, x-terms to one side, numbers to the other side, divide, check.
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3.

### Lesson 4 — Checking solutions and word problems (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: −2(x − 5), 3x + 4 = −8, 30% of 60, 6x + 1 = 4x + 9.
SHOW: Verify x = 4 in 5x + 2 = 3x + 10: put 4 blocks in every bag and count each side (22 = 22). Then verify in 2x + 2 = 10.
SAY: "Verify means substitute into the original equation and work out each side. If you also check each equivalent equation, you find the step where a mistake happened."
WATCH: Checking only the last line. Misconception box (Leo): x = 7 works in his wrong first step but not in the original.
DO: The word-problem routine (let x = …, write each side, make an equation, solve, verify and answer in words). Worked Example 1 (phone plans, 5 GB, $45) with the table, Worked Example 2 (ski hills, 4 days, $220), Worked Example 3 (saving, 5 weeks, $100), Worked Example 4 (same perimeter, x = 5, perimeter 24).
CUE: Ask "what happens after that?" for the ski hills: after 4 days Sunpeak is cheaper. It gives the answer meaning.
DO: Core worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4.

## Assessment
::: box Mid-unit quiz (22 marks)
LE1 (Q1–4), LE2 (Q5–9). Given at the start of Lesson 3, after simplifying and keeping the balance. Q9 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need the idea that one side changed and the other did not (the balance is no longer level), the correct move (− 5 on both sides) and x = 4. On Q8 give ½ mark each; accept "yes" for d) only if the student can say x was added to both sides. On Q4 accept a correct count of tiles (12 small tiles) as the explanation.
:::
::: box Worksheets and exits
Core (★) covers LE1–LE4 with tiles, tile rows, a balance or a flow chart on most questions: like terms, the distributive property (including a negative factor), one- and two-step equations with negative solutions, equivalent equations, x on both sides, verifying, and one phone-plan problem. Challenge (★★) adds two brackets with a negative factor, brackets on both sides with a negative solution, an error in adding a negative x-term, ski passes with a follow-up comparison, a chinook temperature problem, and a rectangle-and-triangle perimeter problem. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it. Exit tickets: one per section, each ending with an "explain how you know" item.
:::

## Data and contexts used
- Phone plans ($20 + $5/GB vs. $35 + $2/GB; $15 + $4/GB vs. $27 + $1/GB) are made up and kept round. Real Alberta plans are sold as monthly bundles, not per-GB.
- Ski hills "Sunpeak" and "Pine Ridge" are made-up names in the style of Sunshine Village and Nakiska; the $120 or $90 card with $25 or $30 days vs. $55 or $60 days are made-up prices, lower than real day tickets.
- Streaming plans ($12 + $3 a movie vs. $24 + $1; $10 + $4 vs. $22 + $1) are made up.
- Bus fare ($3 a ride vs. a $40 card plus $1 a ride) is made up; Calgary and Edmonton transit fares and passes are priced differently.
- Temperatures (Calgary −13 °C warming 3 °C an hour, Edmonton −5 °C warming 1 °C; Lethbridge −11 °C warming 4 °C in a chinook, Red Deer −2 °C) are made-up but plausible winter mornings. Chinooks can warm southern Alberta by 20 °C or more in a few hours.
- Savings amounts (Ava, Sam, Omar, Tess) are made up.

## Connections
- **Integers (topic 2):** every negative coefficient and solution uses integer +, −, × and ÷; zero pairs of tiles are zero pairs of chips.
- **Fractions & Decimals (topic 4):** the order of operations is what "simplify each side" relies on.
- **Functions (topic 7):** each side of a word-problem equation is a function (cost vs. GB); the solution is where the two tables match.
- **Grade 8:** equations with fraction and decimal coefficients, linear inequalities, and slope (the "per GB" rate is the steepness of a line).
