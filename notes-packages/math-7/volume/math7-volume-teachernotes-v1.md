## Overview
::: overview
**Big idea:** a right prism is a polygon base slid straight along, so every slice parallel to the base is a copy of it; a cylinder is the same with a circle. Volume counts unit cubes. In a box, one layer of cubes covers the base (l × w) and there are h layers, so V = l × w × h. Seeing the layer as "one copy of the base, one unit thick" turns this into V = Bh for every right prism and cylinder, with B from the area formulas students already have (rectangle, triangle from Grade 6, circle from topic 9). Run backwards, the same fact gives h = V ÷ B and B = V ÷ h. Units must match before multiplying.
**Sequence:** what makes a prism, naming by the base, bases vs. lateral faces in any orientation (a Toblerone-style box lying down, a tent, a pencil, a box with each pair of faces as bases), cylinders and the curved surface (VO1) → filling a box with centimetre cubes, one layer × number of layers, V = l × w × h, cm³ and m³, the same box measured from two different bases, 1 cm³ = 1 mL and 1000 cm³ = 1 L (VO2) → V = B × h by stacking copies of the base; triangular prisms (B = ½ × b × triangle height) and cylinders (B ≈ 3.14 × r × r); the two heights; diameter vs. radius (VO3) → h = V ÷ B and B = V ÷ h; the cooler, pool, hay bale and trough; converting cm to m first (VO4).
**Materials:** centimetre cubes (at least 60 per pair), small boxes to fill (a tea box, a jewellery box), a Toblerone-style box or a paper triangular prism, a can, a hockey puck, a pencil, a 1 L milk carton and a 10 cm cube (or a 1 L measuring jug and a 1000 cm³ base-ten block), rulers, calculators, mini whiteboards. Optional: GeoGebra 3D Calculator ("Prism" and "Cylinder" tools) to turn a solid and show that the bases stay the same.
**Depth ceiling:** right rectangular prisms, right triangular prisms and right cylinders only. V = Bh; find B or h when the other two are known; same units first. π ≈ 3.14, round to one decimal place at the end. Pentagonal and hexagonal prisms appear only for naming (VO1), never for volume, because their base areas are beyond Grade 7. No surface area (Grade 8), no pyramids, cones or spheres, and no composite solids (Grade 9). Unit conversion stays at cm ↔ m for lengths and cm³ ↔ mL ↔ L; 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ appears once, as a ★★ question.
**Reading level:** student pages are written at about Grade 3–4. The math words (face, prism, base, lateral face, congruent, cylinder, curved surface, volume, cubic centimetre, cubic metre, layer, millilitre, litre, base area, height of a prism, height of a triangle, missing measure) are the learning; each has a picture beside it.
**Diagrams:** the notes use a new Grade 7 diagram type, `solid`, which shades both bases (the far one shows faintly through the solid) and can turn a prism to any position. Ask students to trace the two shaded faces with a finger before any calculation.
:::

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| "The base is the face it sits on" | Lay a Toblerone-style box on its rectangle face and ask "Which two faces match?" Stand it on its triangle, then lay it down again. The triangles never stop being the bases. |
| Counting the floor of a tent as a base | A base needs a matching partner at the opposite end. The floor has none. |
| "Volume is in square units" | Hold up a flat square tile and a centimetre cube. "Which one fills space?" Ask for the unit every time an answer is given aloud. |
| Adding the dimensions (4 + 3 + 2) | Build it with cubes. Count one layer, then the layers. |
| "Use the triangle's height as the prism's height" | Two heights, two jobs: the triangle's height goes inside B; h is the distance between the two triangles. Write "B = ½ × b × ___" before writing "V = B × ___". |
| Forgetting the ½ in the triangle area | Draw the rectangle around the triangle: the triangle is half of it. |
| Using the diameter as the radius | Ask "radius or diameter?" before B. Using d makes B (and V) 4 times too big: 565.2 m³ instead of 141.3 m³ for the grain bin. |
| Mixing cm and m | Circle every unit in the question before calculating. Convert to the unit the answer asks for. |
| Dividing the wrong way (B ÷ V) | Estimate: the height of a cooler should be tens of centimetres, not 0.05. Check with B × h = V. |
| Rounding B before multiplying by h | Keep all digits of B: 45.3416 × 2.5 = 113.354 → 113.4. Rounding B to 45.3 first gives 113.25 → 113.3. |

## Lessons
### Lesson 1 — Prisms and cylinders (60 min)
DO: Pre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1 previews l × w × h, Q2–Q4 the three base-area formulas (rectangle, triangle, circle), Q5 dividing (VO4), Q6 m ↔ cm.
SHOW: A Toblerone-style box standing on its triangle. "Which two faces match? Now I lay it down. Which two faces match now?"
SAY: "A prism has two bases that match and are parallel. The other faces are rectangles: lateral faces. We name a prism by its base."
DO: Sort a collection (boxes, a pencil, a can, a puck, a tent picture). For each: name it, trace the bases, count the lateral faces.
WATCH: "The base is the face it sits on." Misconception box.
CUE: Worked Example 1 (tent: triangular prism, the floor is a lateral face), Worked Example 2 (a juice box with each pair of faces as bases), Worked Example 3 (puck: two circle bases, one curved surface).
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.

### Lesson 2 — Volume by layers (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: area of a 7 by 3 rectangle; area of a triangle (6, 4); 3.14 × 5 × 5; 12 × 25.
DO: Cube packing (20 min). Pairs fill a small box with centimetre cubes: one layer first, count it, then count the layers. Record l, w, h, cubes in one layer, layers, total.
SAY: "Cubes in one layer times the number of layers. One layer is l × w. The number of layers is h. So V = l × w × h. We count cubes, so the unit is cm³."
WATCH: "Volume is in square units." Misconception box: a flat square tile vs. a cube.
CUE: Worked Example 1 (Mia's box, 5 × 3 × 4 = 60 cm³), Worked Example 2 (juice box 240 cm³ = 240 mL), Worked Example 3 (cereal box from two different bases: 4200 cm³ both ways).
SHOW: Pour 1 L of water into a 10 cm cube (or show a 1000 cm³ base-ten block next to a 1 L carton).
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2.

### Lesson 3 — Volume = base area × height (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: name the bases of a puck; volume of a 3 × 4 × 5 box; cm² or cm³; 25% of 60.
SHOW: A stack of identical paper triangles (or coins for a cylinder). "Each slice is a copy of the base. How many slices? That is the height."
SAY: "V = B × h. B is the area of one base. h is the distance between the bases. For a box, B is l × w. For a triangle, ½ × b × the triangle's height. For a circle, 3.14 × r × r."
CUE: Worked Example 1 (Toblerone-style box: B = 7 cm², V = 140 cm³), Worked Example 2 (barn attic: B = 12 m², V = 144 m³).
WATCH: "Use the triangle's height as the prism's height." Misconception box: the tent, 2.25 m³ vs. 4.5 m³.
CUE: Worked Example 3 (puck: 113.4 cm³), Worked Example 4 (grain bin 6 m across: 141.3 m³).
WATCH: Using the diameter as the radius. Misconception box.
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3. Quiz next class (VO1–VO3).

### Lesson 4 — Finding a missing measure (60 min)
DO: Mid-unit quiz (25 min) first. Then the lesson.
SAY: "V = B × h is a multiplication fact, like 24 = 6 × 4. Know two of them? Divide to find the third."
CUE: Worked Example 1 (cooler: h = 24 000 ÷ 1200 = 20 cm), Worked Example 2 (pool: B = 300 ÷ 1.5 = 200 m², then width 10 m), Worked Example 3 (hay bale: h = 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 m).
WATCH: Mixed units. Misconception box: the sandbox, 9000 vs. 0.9 m³.
CUE: Worked Example 4 (trough: 50 cm → 0.5 m, B = 1 m², h = 0.6 m).
DO: Core worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4.

## Assessment
::: box Mid-unit quiz (23 marks)
VO1 (Q1–3), VO2 (Q4–6), VO3 (Q7–9). Given at the start of Lesson 4, before missing measures (VO4). Calculators allowed; π ≈ 3.14. Q2: full marks need both triangle ends named as the bases. Q6 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need "volume counts cubes" (or three lengths multiplied) and the correct unit cm³. Q7: no mark for V = 20 × 9 (missing the ½). Q8: using d = 10 m as the radius (1256 m³) earns only the h mark.
:::
::: box Worksheets and exits
Core (★) covers VO1–VO4 with a diagram on most questions: naming a pentagonal prism, a lying Toblerone-style box (base = bottom), a hay bale's bases, counting layers, a cereal box, a cm² answer to fix, a doorstop, a tent (two heights), a grain bin given its diameter, a cooler's depth, a pool's floor area, and a sandbox in mixed units. Challenge (★★) adds a 1 L box's height, filling a fish tank part way, a stack of 10 pucks, doubling a can's radius, the length of a triangular prism from its volume, a bin's height from its diameter and volume, and a box in m³ and cm³. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it. Exit tickets: one per section, each ending with an "explain how you know" item.
:::

## Data and contexts used
- Hockey puck: regulation size is 3 in across and 1 in thick (7.62 cm and 2.54 cm); the materials use r = 3.8 cm and h = 2.5 cm, V ≈ 113.4 cm³.
- Grain bins: corrugated steel bins on Prairie farms are commonly 5–10 m across. The 6 m by 5 m bin (141.3 m³) holds roughly 4000 bushels of wheat (1 bushel ≈ 0.035 m³); the bin's cone roof is ignored. The 8 m bins in the Synthesis and Challenge are also realistic.
- Round hay bales: commonly about 1.2–1.5 m wide and 1.5–1.8 m across. The 1.6 m by 1.2 m bale (2.4 m³) is realistic; the "B = 2 m², V = 3 m³" bale is rounded for clean division.
- The Toblerone-style box (triangle 4 cm by 3.5 cm, 20 cm long) is close to a large chocolate bar box but rounded. The juice box (6 × 4 × 10 cm, 240 mL) is a little larger than a real 200 mL box, which has air space.
- The tent, barn attic, cereal box, cooler, pool, water trough, sandbox, fish tank, shed and doorstop sizes are made up but realistic.

## Connections
- **Squares & Cubes (topic 3):** a cube's volume is s³; the centimetre cube and the 10 cm litre cube.
- **Fractions & Decimals (topic 4):** decimal products (3.14 × 14.44), the ½ in the triangle area, dividing by decimals (300 ÷ 1.5).
- **Linear Equations (topic 6):** finding h from V = Bh is solving 1200h = 24 000.
- **Circles (topic 9):** the base area of a cylinder is the area of a circle, πr²; radius vs. diameter returns.
- **Grade 8:** surface area of the same prisms and cylinders; Grade 9 adds pyramids, cones, spheres and composite solids.
