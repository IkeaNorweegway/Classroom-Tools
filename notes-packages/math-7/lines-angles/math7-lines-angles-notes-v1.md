## Section 1: Lines, rays, segments, angles, polygons
Learning target: I can tell apart lines, rays, segments, angles and polygons. I can name them with symbols.

### Vocabulary
- point :: an exact spot. Name it with a capital letter. :: point A
- line :: straight, and goes on forever both ways. It has no ends. :: line CD
- ray :: starts at one endpoint and goes on forever one way :: ray AC
- segment :: the part of a line between two endpoints :: segment AB
- endpoint :: a point where a ray or a segment stops :: A is the endpoint of ray AC
- angle :: two rays (or segments) that share an endpoint :: ∠CAB
- vertex :: the shared endpoint of an angle. Also a corner of a polygon. :: A is the vertex of ∠CAB
- polygon :: a closed shape made of 3 or more segments. They meet only at their ends. :: △ABC

#### Ideas, not drawings
A **line** goes on forever. You can't draw forever! So a drawing is only a model. An arrowhead means "keeps going".
@viz lines items="line:C,D;ray:A,C;segment:A,B" sym=1 caption="A line has two arrowheads. A ray has one. A segment has none."
A **segment** is like a railway track between two stations. It stops at both ends.
A **ray** is like a flashlight beam. It starts at the flashlight and goes on and on.
An **angle** is two rays with the same endpoint. That point is the **vertex**.
@viz lines items="angle:C,A,B" sym=1 notitle=1 caption="The vertex A goes in the middle of the name."

::: key Naming with symbols
**Line CD:** use any two points on the line. Draw ↔ over CD.
**Ray AC:** the endpoint comes FIRST. Draw → over AC.
**Segment AB:** use the two endpoints. Draw a bar over AB.
**∠CAB:** the vertex goes in the MIDDLE. If only one angle is at A, write ∠A.
**△ABC:** a triangle with vertices A, B and C.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "A ray goes both ways." Or "A line has end points."
WHY: Count the arrowheads. A line has two. A ray has one. A segment has none.
@viz lines items="line:P,Q;ray:P,Q;segment:P,Q" labels="no ends;1 endpoint;2 endpoints"
RIGHT: A line goes forever both ways. A ray has one end. A segment has two ends.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Name each object
@viz lines items="ray:K,M;segment:G,H;line:E,F" notitle=1

Step 2: The second one has two endpoints. It is segment GH. Segment HG is fine too.
Step 3: The third one has two arrowheads. It is [[line EF]]. Line FE is fine too.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Three names for one angle
@viz lines items="angle:T,S,U,65" notitle=1 labels="∠TSU = [[∠UST]] = [[∠S]]"
Step 1: Find the vertex. The rays meet at S.
Step 2: Put S in the middle: ∠TSU or ∠UST.
Step 3: Only one angle is at S. So ∠S is fine too.
:::

(lines 2)

:::

## Section 2: Parallel and perpendicular
Learning target: I can show parallel and perpendicular lines with marks and symbols.

### Vocabulary
- intersect :: cross or meet at a point :: two roads meet at a corner
- parallel (∥) :: lines that never meet. They stay the same distance apart. :: railway rails: m ∥ n
- perpendicular (⊥) :: lines that meet at a right angle :: the edges of a window pane: m ⊥ n
- right angle :: an angle of exactly 90°. Mark it with a small square. :: the corner of a page
- arrowheads :: matching marks on lines. They show the lines are parallel. :: one > on each rail

#### Railway tracks and window panes
The two rails of a railway track never meet. They are **parallel**.
@viz angles kind=parallel names="rail m,rail n" caption="Matching arrowheads show m ∥ n."
The edges of a window pane meet at square corners. They are **perpendicular**.
@viz angles kind=perp names="m,n" caption="The small square shows a right angle (90°). m ⊥ n."

#### Fold it
1. Fold a sheet of paper in half.
2. Fold it in half again. Line up the first crease with itself.
3. Open it up. Look at the two creases.
4. Check the corners with the corner of a page. All four are right angles.
The two creases are perpendicular.

::: key Marks and symbols
**Small square:** a right angle (90°).
**Matching arrowheads:** the lines are parallel.
**m ∥ n** means "line m is parallel to line n".
**m ⊥ n** means "line m is perpendicular to line n".
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Lines that look parallel are parallel."
WHY: AB and DC look parallel. But they have no arrowheads. Measure the gap at each end.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;6,3;0,2.6" names="A,B,C,D" right="1,2" sides=";3 cm;;2.6 cm" unit=30
The gap is 2.6 cm on the left and 3 cm on the right. Far to the left, the lines meet.
RIGHT: Only matching arrowheads (or a measurement) show that lines are parallel.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — A window pane
Write the parallel and perpendicular sides with symbols.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;5,3;0,3" names="A,B,C,D" right="1,2,3,4" par="1,2,1,2" unit=30
Step 1: AB and DC both have one arrowhead. So AB ∥ DC.
Step 2: AD and BC both have two arrowheads. So AD ∥ [[BC]].
Step 3: The small square at B shows a right angle. So AB [[⊥]] BC.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Edmonton streets
In downtown Edmonton, avenues run east–west. Streets run north–south.
@viz angles kind=parallel names="Jasper Ave,102 Ave"
@viz angles kind=perp names="Jasper Ave,109 St"

Step 2: 109 Street meets Jasper Avenue at a right angle. So 109 St [[⊥]] Jasper Ave.
Step 3: 109 Street meets 102 Avenue at a right angle too. So 109 St [[⊥ 102 Ave]].
:::
::: explain
Why can't you tell two lines are parallel just by looking?
(lines 2)
=> They might lean a tiny bit and meet far away. You need arrowheads or a measurement.
:::

## Section 3: Angles where lines cross
Learning target: I can find angles where two lines cross. I can use angles to check if lines are parallel.

### Vocabulary
- opposite angles :: angles across from each other where two lines cross. Also called vertically opposite angles. :: ∠1 and ∠3
- adjacent angles :: angles side by side. They share a vertex and a ray. :: ∠1 and ∠2
- supplementary :: two angles that add to 180° :: 40° and 140°
- congruent (≅) :: exactly the same size. Congruent angles have the same number of degrees. :: ∠1 ≅ ∠3
- transversal :: a line that crosses two or more lines :: a diagonal road across two avenues
- corresponding angles :: angles in the same spot at each crossing :: ∠2 and ∠6 (both top right)

#### Try it with tracing paper
1. Draw two lines that cross. Number the angles 1 to 4.
2. Trace them on tracing paper.
3. Turn the tracing paper halfway around. Pin the centre with your pencil.
4. ∠1 lands on ∠3. ∠2 lands on ∠4.
@viz angles kind=intersect a=50 labels="1;2;3;4" caption="Two lines cross. They make four angles."
A straight line makes a straight angle: 180°. ∠1 and ∠2 sit side by side on a line. So ∠1 + ∠2 = 180°.

::: key Two lines cross
**Opposite angles are congruent:** ∠1 ≅ ∠3 and ∠2 ≅ ∠4.
**Adjacent angles on a line are supplementary:** they add to 180°.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Find the missing angles
@viz angles kind=intersect a=40 labels="40°;∠2;∠3;∠4"
Step 1: ∠2 sits beside the 40° angle on a line. ∠2 = 180° − 40° = [[140°]].
Step 2: ∠3 is opposite the 40° angle. So ∠3 = [[40°]].
Step 3: ∠4 is opposite ∠2. So ∠4 = [[140°]].
Check: the four angles go all the way around. They add to 360°.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Opposite angles add to 180°."
WHY: Opposite angles are the same size. 40° + 40° is only 80°.
@viz angles kind=intersect a=40 labels="40°;;40°;" highlight="1,3" caption="Opposite angles match."
RIGHT: Opposite angles are equal. Angles side by side on a line add to 180°.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Calgary's Peace Bridge
Steel bars on the Peace Bridge cross like an X. One angle is 115°.
@viz angles kind=intersect a=65 labels="∠1;115°;∠3;∠4"
Step 1: ∠1 is beside the 115° angle. ∠1 = 180° − 115° = [[65°]].
Step 2: ∠3 is opposite ∠1. ∠3 = [[65°]].
Step 3: ∠4 is opposite the 115° angle. ∠4 = [[115°]].
:::
::: explain
In Worked Example 2, why does ∠1 + 115° make 180°?
(lines 2)
=> They sit side by side on one straight line. A straight line is 180°.
:::

#### A transversal
A **transversal** crosses two lines. It makes 8 angles: 4 at each crossing.
@viz angles kind=transversal a=60 labels="1;2;3;4;5;6;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t" caption="∠2 and ∠6 are both top right. They are corresponding angles."
Corresponding pairs: ∠1 and ∠5, ∠2 and ∠6. Also ∠3 and ∠7, ∠4 and ∠8.

::: key Corresponding angles
If m ∥ n, corresponding angles are congruent: ∠2 ≅ ∠6.
To check if two lines are parallel, measure two corresponding angles.
Equal: the lines are parallel. Not equal: the lines are not parallel.
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — A diagonal road
Two avenues are parallel: m ∥ n. A diagonal road t crosses both. ∠2 = 55°.
@viz angles kind=transversal a=55 labels="1;55°;3;4;5;6;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t"
Step 1: ∠6 corresponds to ∠2. The avenues are parallel. So ∠6 = [[55°]].
Step 2: ∠1 is beside ∠2 on line m. ∠1 = 180° − 55° = [[125°]].
Step 3: ∠5 corresponds to ∠1. So ∠5 = [[125°]].
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Corresponding angles are always equal."
WHY: Here m and n are not parallel. ∠2 is 60°, but ∠6 is only 55°.
@viz angles kind=transversal a=60 parallel=0 marks=0 labels="1;60°;3;4;5;55°;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t"
RIGHT: Corresponding angles are equal only when the lines are parallel.
:::

::: example Worked Example 4 — Are the rails parallel?
Measure ∠2 and ∠6 in each picture.
@viz angles kind=transversal a=70 marks=0 labels="1;70°;3;4;5;70°;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t" caption="Picture A"
@viz angles kind=transversal a=75 parallel=0 marks=0 labels="1;75°;3;4;5;70°;7;8" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t" caption="Picture B"
Step 1: ∠2 and ∠6 are corresponding angles.
Step 2: In Picture A, both are 70°. So m and n are [[parallel]].
Step 3: In Picture B, they are 75° and 70°. So m and n are [[not parallel]].
:::

@viz angles kind=intersect a=70 labels="70°;[[110°]];[[70°]];[[110°]]"

## Section 4: Congruent polygons
Learning target: I can match the parts of congruent polygons. I can write a congruence statement.

### Vocabulary
- congruent polygons :: same size and same shape. One fits exactly on top of the other. :: two quilt triangles
- corresponding vertices :: matching corners :: A ↔ R
- corresponding sides :: matching sides. They are congruent. :: AB ≅ RS
- corresponding angles :: matching angles. They are congruent. :: ∠ABC ≅ ∠RST
- hash marks :: small ticks on sides. The same number of ticks means equal sides. :: one tick, two ticks
- arcs :: small curves in corners. The same number of arcs means equal angles. :: one arc, two arcs

#### Trace, then fit
1. Trace △ABC on tracing paper.
2. Slide it, turn it or flip it onto △RST.
3. Does it fit exactly? Then the triangles are **congruent**.
Hash marks and arcs show which parts match.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;4,0;1,3" names="A,B,C" ticks="1,2,3" arcs="1,2,3" pts2="0,0;0,4;-3,1" names2="R,S,T" ticks2="1,2,3" arcs2="1,2,3" unit=28 caption="△RST is △ABC turned. One tick matches one tick. Two arcs match two arcs."
"Corresponding" means "matching". In Section 3, it matched angles on a transversal. Here it matches parts of two shapes.

::: key Congruence statements
**△ABC ≅ △RST** says the triangles are congruent.
The letter order shows the match: A ↔ R, B ↔ S, C ↔ T.
Matching sides: AB ≅ RS, BC ≅ ST, CA ≅ TR.
Matching angles: ∠ABC ≅ ∠RST, and so on.
Four-sided shapes work the same way: ABCD ≅ RSTU.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Read the marks
Write a congruence statement for these triangles.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;2,3" names="A,B,C" ticks="1,2,3" arcs="1,2,3" pts2="0,0;-5,0;-2,3" names2="Y,X,Z" ticks2="1,2,3" arcs2="1,2,3" unit=26
Step 1: Match the angles by arcs. One arc: ∠A and ∠Y. Two arcs: ∠B and [[∠X]]. Three arcs: ∠C and [[∠Z]].
Step 2: Match the sides by hash marks. AB ≅ YX. BC ≅ [[XZ]]. CA ≅ [[ZY]].
Step 3: Write the letters in matching order: △ABC ≅ [[△YXZ]].
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "The letter order in a congruence statement doesn't matter."
WHY: Mia writes △ABC ≅ △XYZ. That says A matches X. But ∠A has one arc. ∠X has two arcs.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;2,3" names="A,B,C" arcs="1,2,3" pts2="0,0;-5,0;-2,3" names2="Y,X,Z" arcs2="1,2,3" unit=22
RIGHT: The order shows the match. Pair the letters by their marks. Then write them in that order.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Two quilt pieces
ABCD ≅ RSTU. One piece is flipped. Find ST, UR, ∠R and ∠U.
@viz polygon pts="0,0;6,0;6,4;3,4" names="A,B,C,D" sides="6 cm;4 cm;3 cm;5 cm" angles="53°;;;127°" right="2,3" pts2="0,0;-6,0;-6,4;-3,4" names2="R,S,T,U" sides2=";[[4 cm]];;[[5 cm]]" angles2="[[53°]];;;[[127°]]" right2="2,3" unit=24
Step 1: The order shows the match. A ↔ R and B ↔ S. C ↔ T and D ↔ U.
Step 2: ST matches BC. So ST = [[4 cm]]. UR matches DA. So UR = [[5 cm]].
Step 3: ∠R matches ∠A, so ∠R = [[53°]]. ∠U matches ∠D, so ∠U = [[127°]].
:::
::: explain
In Worked Example 2, how did you know ST matches BC?
(lines 2)
=> S and T are 2nd and 3rd in RSTU. B and C are 2nd and 3rd in ABCD.
:::

=> PQ = 9 cm. ∠R = 40°.
4. Sam says two lines are parallel because they look parallel. What should he check?
=> Arrowheads, or a pair of corresponding angles where a transversal crosses. Equal angles mean parallel.

## Concept Reference
::: key Lines, rays, segments, angles, polygons
Line CD: forever both ways. Ray AC: one endpoint, written first. Segment AB: two endpoints.
∠CAB or ∠A: the vertex goes in the middle. △ABC: a triangle.
A polygon is closed. Its 3 or more sides meet only at their ends.
:::
@viz lines items="line:C,D;ray:A,C;segment:A,B;angle:C,A,B" sym=1

::: key Parallel and perpendicular
Matching arrowheads: parallel, m ∥ n. They never meet.
Small square: a right angle, m ⊥ n. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°.
:::
@viz polygon pts="0,0;5,0;5,3;0,3" names="A,B,C,D" right="1,2,3,4" par="1,2,1,2" unit=24 caption="AB ∥ DC, AD ∥ BC, AB ⊥ BC."

::: key Angles where lines cross
Opposite angles are congruent. Adjacent angles on a line add to 180°.
A transversal crosses two lines. Corresponding angles are in the same spot at each crossing.
Parallel lines: corresponding angles are equal. Equal corresponding angles show the lines are parallel.
:::
@viz angles kind=intersect a=40 labels="40°;140°;40°;140°" highlight="1,3"
@viz angles kind=transversal a=60 labels="120°;60°;60°;120°;120°;60°;60°;120°" highlight="2,6" names="m,n,t"

::: key Congruent polygons
Same size and same shape. Hash marks show equal sides. Arcs show equal angles.
△ABC ≅ △RST: A ↔ R, B ↔ S, C ↔ T. So AB ≅ RS and ∠ABC ≅ ∠RST.
:::
@viz polygon pts="0,0;4,0;1,3" names="A,B,C" ticks="1,2,3" arcs="1,2,3" pts2="0,0;0,4;-3,1" names2="R,S,T" ticks2="1,2,3" arcs2="1,2,3" unit=24

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| A ray goes both ways, or a line has ends | A line has no ends. A ray has one. A segment has two. |
| Ray BA is the same as ray AB | The endpoint goes first. They point opposite ways. |
| The vertex can go anywhere in an angle's name | The vertex goes in the middle: ∠CAB. |
| Lines that look parallel are parallel | Look for arrowheads, or measure. |
| Opposite angles add to 180° | Opposite angles are equal. Side by side on a line adds to 180°. |
| Corresponding angles are always equal | Only when the lines are parallel. |
| Letter order in △ABC ≅ △RST doesn't matter | The order shows the match: A ↔ R, B ↔ S, C ↔ T. |
