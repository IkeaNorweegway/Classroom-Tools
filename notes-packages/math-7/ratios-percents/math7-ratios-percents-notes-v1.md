## Section 1: Equivalent ratios
Learning target: I can make equal ratios by multiplying or dividing both terms.

### Vocabulary
- ratio :: compares two amounts :: 1 can of juice to 3 cans of water is 1:3
- term :: one number in a ratio :: in 1:3, the terms are 1 and 3
- part-to-part :: compares one part to another part :: 2 red to 3 blue is 2:3
- part-to-whole :: compares one part to all of it :: 2 red out of 5 beads is {2/5}
- equivalent ratios :: ratios that make the same mix :: 1:3 = 2:6
- ratio table :: a table of equivalent ratios :: concentrate 1, 2, 3 and water 3, 6, 9
- factor :: the number you multiply both terms by :: 1:3 → 2:6 uses the factor 2

#### What is a ratio?
Mia makes juice. She mixes 1 can of concentrate with 3 cans of water.
@viz tape parts="1:concentrate;3:water" caption="Each box is one can."
The ratio of concentrate to water is 1:3. Say "1 to 3."
The order matters. Water to concentrate is 3:1.

Terms can be whole numbers, decimals or fractions.
The first term can be smaller or bigger than the second.
| Mix | Ratio |
|---|---|
| 1 can of concentrate to 3 cans of water | 1:3 (first term smaller) |
| 4 cups of flour to 1 cup of milk | 4:1 (first term bigger) |
| 2.5 cups of oats to 1 cup of milk | 2.5:1 (a decimal term) |
| {1/2} cup of sugar to 2 cups of flour | {1/2}:2 (a fraction term) |

#### More batches: multiply both terms
Mia needs more juice. She makes 2 batches.
@viz tape parts="2:concentrate;6:water" caption="2 batches: 2 cans to 6 cans."
2 batches use 2 cans of concentrate and 6 cans of water. So 1:3 = 2:6.
| Batches | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concentrate (cans) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Water (cans) | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
@viz dnl top="0,1,2,3,4" bottom="0,3,6,9,12" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water" arrow="1,4,× 4" caption="Both lines grow by the same factor."

::: key Equivalent ratios
Multiply both terms by the same number. 1:3 = 4:12 (× 4).
Divide both terms by the same number. 4:12 = 1:3 (÷ 4).
The number you use is the **factor**.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "To make an equal ratio, add the same number to both terms. 2:3 = 4:5."
WHY: Adding changes the mix. 2 batches of 2:3 need 6 cans of water, not 5.
@viz tape parts="2:concentrate;3:water" caption="One batch: 2:3."
@viz tape parts="4:concentrate;6:water" caption="Two batches: 4:6. Not 4:5."
RIGHT: 2:3 = 4:6. Multiply both terms by 2.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Ava's bracelet has 2 red beads to 3 blue beads. So 2 out of 3 beads are red."
WHY: 2:3 is part to part. The whole is 2 + 3 = 5 beads.
@viz tape parts="2:red;3:blue" total="5 beads in all"
RIGHT: 2 out of 5 beads are red. Red is {2/5} of the bracelet.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Juice for the team
Sam's juice mix is 2 cans of concentrate to 5 cans of water. Make 2, 3 and 4 batches.
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6,8" bottom="0,5,10,15,20" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water" arrow="1,3,× 3"
Step 1: Multiply both terms by 2: 2:5 = 4:10.
Step 2: Multiply both terms by 3: 2:5 = 6:15.
Step 3: Multiply both terms by 4: 2:5 = [[8:20]].
| Concentrate (cans) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water (cans) | 5 | 10 | 15 | [[20]] |
:::
::: explain
Why must Sam multiply BOTH terms by the same number?
(lines 2)
=> So every batch has the same mix. If only one term changes, the juice tastes different.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — A smaller batch of bannock
Lily's big bannock recipe uses 12 cups of flour and 4 tsp of baking powder. She wants a smaller batch.
@viz dnl top="0,3,6,9,12" bottom="0,1,2,3,4" toplabel="flour (cups)" bottomlabel="powder (tsp)" arrow="4,1,÷ 4"
Step 1: The ratio of flour to baking powder is 12:4.
Step 2: Divide both terms by 2: 12:4 = 6:2.
Step 3: Divide both terms by 4: 12:4 = [[3:1]].
Dividing both terms is called **partitioning**. Multiplying both terms is **iterating**.
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — Canola and wheat
Kai's farm has 4 ha of canola for every 6 ha of wheat. His neighbour has 6 ha of canola and 9 ha of wheat. Find the factor.
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6" bottom="0,3,6,9" toplabel="canola (ha)" bottomlabel="wheat (ha)" arrow="2,3,× 1.5"
Step 1: Divide to find the factor. 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. Check the other term: 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5.
Step 2: Both terms use the same factor. So 4:6 = 6:9.
Step 3: The factor 1.5 is the same as {3/2}. A factor can be a decimal or a fraction.
:::
::: explain
In Worked Example 3, why is the factor bigger than 1?
(lines 2)
=> The neighbour's numbers are bigger. Going from 4 to 6 means multiplying by more than 1.
:::

## Section 2: Missing terms and comparing ratios
Learning target: I can find a missing term and compare two ratios.

### Vocabulary
- unknown term :: the term you need to find :: in 2:5 = 8:?, the ? is the unknown term
- common term :: a term that is the same in both ratios :: 3:5 and 4:5 have the common term 5
- stronger mix :: more concentrate for the same water :: 4:5 is stronger than 3:5

#### Find a missing term
Sam's juice is 2:5. He has 8 cans of concentrate. How much water does he need?
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6,8" bottom="0,5,10,15,[[20]]" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water" arrow="1,4,× 4"
2 × 4 = 8. So multiply 5 by the same factor: 5 × 4 = [[20]].
2:5 = 8:[[20]]. Sam needs [[20]] cans of water.

::: example Worked Example 1 — Fans at Rogers Place
One section at Rogers Place has Oilers fans and Flames fans. The ratio is 5:2. There are 40 Flames fans. How many Oilers fans are there?
@viz tape parts="5:Oilers fans;2:Flames fans" values="[[20]];[[20]]" total="40 Flames fans"
Step 1: The Flames term goes from 2 to 40. The factor is 40 ÷ 2 = [[20]].
Step 2: Use the same factor: 5 × 20 = [[100]].
Step 3: 5:2 = [[100]]:40. There are [[100]] Oilers fans.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — When the factor isn't a whole number
Kai plants 4 bags of canola seed on 6 ha. How many hectares can 10 bags plant?
| Bags | 4 | 2 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hectares | 6 | [[3]] | [[15]] |
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6,8,10" bottom="0,3,6,9,12,[[15]]" toplabel="bags" bottomlabel="hectares"
Step 1: Divide both terms by 2: 4:6 = 2:3.
Step 2: Multiply both terms by 5: 2:3 = [[10:15]].
Step 3: 10 bags can plant [[15]] ha.
:::
::: explain
In Worked Example 2, why did we divide first?
(lines 2)
=> 4 does not go evenly into 10. Going down to 2 bags first makes the factor a whole number.
:::

#### Add two equivalent ratios
2:3 and 4:6 are equivalent. Add the first terms. Add the second terms.
(2 + 4):(3 + 6) = 6:9. This is equivalent too!
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6" bottom="0,3,6,9" toplabel="first" bottomlabel="second" caption="2 batches and 4 batches make 6 batches."
| | Ratio A | Ratio B | A + B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concentrate | 2 | 4 | 6 |
| Water | 3 | 6 | [[9]] |

#### Compare with a common term
Juice A is 3:5. Juice B is 4:5 (concentrate to water). Which is stronger?
@viz tape parts="3:A concentrate;5:A water" caption="Juice A: 3:5."
@viz tape parts="4:B concentrate;5:B water" caption="Juice B: 4:5."
Both use 5 cans of water. B has more concentrate. B is [[stronger]].

::: key Comparing two ratios
1. Make the first terms match, or make the second terms match.
2. Then compare the other terms.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "3:7 is stronger than 2:4, because 3 cans is more than 2 cans."
WHY: 3:7 also has more water. Make a common term first.
@viz tape parts="6:concentrate;12:water" caption="2:4 = 6:12 (× 3)."
@viz tape parts="6:concentrate;14:water" caption="3:7 = 6:14 (× 2)."
RIGHT: Both have 6 cans of concentrate. 2:4 has less water. So 2:4 is stronger.
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — Which juice is stronger?
Noor's juice is 2:3. Omar's juice is 3:5. Which is stronger?
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6" bottom="0,3,6,[[9]]" toplabel="Noor" bottomlabel="water" caption="Noor: × 3."
@viz dnl top="0,3,6" bottom="0,5,[[10]]" toplabel="Omar" bottomlabel="water" caption="Omar: × 2."
Step 1: Make the first terms match. 2 × 3 = 6 and 3 × 2 = 6.
Step 2: 2:3 = 6:[[9]]. 3:5 = 6:[[10]].
Step 3: Same concentrate. Noor uses less water. Noor's juice is [[stronger]].
:::

#### Cross products: a shortcut
A ratio a:b can be written as a fraction: {a/b}.
Equivalent ratios make equal fractions: {2/3} = {4/6}.
@viz bars bars="2/3;4/6" labels="2/3;4/6" unit=300 caption="The same amount is shaded."
Multiply across: 2 × 6 = 12 and 3 × 4 = 12. They match!
::: key Cross products
{a/b} = {c/d} is the same as a × d = b × c.
Use it to check two ratios. Use it to find a missing term.
:::
@viz flow steps="4/6 = 10/?|4 × ? = 6 × 10|4 × ? = 60|? = [[15]]" caption="The same answer as Worked Example 2."

::: try
1. Find the missing term. 3:4 = 12:[[16]]
2. Find the missing term. 6:9 = 10:[[15]]
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6,8,10" bottom="0,3,6,9,12,[[15]]"
3. Add the ratios 1:4 and 3:12. [[4:16. It is equal to 1:4.]]
4. Which is stronger: 3:8 or 3:10? [[3:8. Same concentrate, less water.]]
5. Which is stronger: 1:2 or 2:5? [[1:2 = 2:4. 2:4 has less water than 2:5. So 1:2.]]
6. Use cross products. Is 3:5 = 9:15? Is 3:5 = 5:7?
[[3 × 15 = 45 and 5 × 9 = 45. Yes. 3 × 7 = 21 and 5 × 5 = 25. No.]]
:::
::: retrieval
1. Write two ratios equal to 4:10.
=> 2:5 and 8:20 (any × or ÷ both terms).
2. Ava's bracelet has 3 red beads to 4 blue beads. What fraction is red?
=> {3/7}. There are 3 + 4 = 7 beads in all.
3. Find {3/4} ÷ {1/4}.
=> 3 ÷ 1 = 3
4. Find 2.5 × 4.
=> 10
5. ★ Stretch: The ratio of girls to boys is 4:5. There are 36 students. How many are girls?
=> 4 + 5 = 9 parts. 36 ÷ 9 = 4 in each part. Girls: 4 × 4 = 16.
:::

## Section 3: Percents, including less than 1% and more than 100%
Learning target: I can find percents of numbers, even below 1% and above 100%.

### Vocabulary
- percent :: a ratio out of 100. % means "out of 100." :: 45% = 45:100 = {45/100}
- benchmark :: a friendly percent you can use to build others :: 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%
- 100% :: one whole :: 100% of 30 is 30

#### A percent is a ratio out of 100
45 out of 100 squares are shaded.
@viz hundred shaded=45 labels="45 out of 100"
That is 45:100, or {45/100}, or 0.45, or 45%.

To change a ratio to a percent, make the second term 100.
3 out of 4 seats are full. {3/4} is [[75]] out of 100. That is [[75%]].
@viz percentbar percent=75 top="0%,25%,50%,75%,100%" bottom="0,1,2,3,4" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="seats"
You can also multiply by 100: {3/4} × 100 = [[75]]. So [[75%]] are full.

#### Benchmark percents
Start with 100%. Then halve it or divide it.
| Percent | 100% | 50% | 25% | 10% | 5% | 1% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| How to find it | the whole | ÷ 2 | ÷ 4 | ÷ 10 | half of 10% | ÷ 100 |
| Of 80 | 80 | 40 | 20 | 8 | [[4]] | [[0.8]] |
@viz percentbar percent=10 top="0%,10%,25%,50%,100%" bottom="0,8,20,40,80" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="of 80"

::: example Worked Example 1 — Mini donuts at the Stampede
60 people wait in line at the Calgary Stampede. 35% of them buy mini donuts. How many people is that?
@viz percentbar percent=35 top="0%,10%,25%,35%,100%" bottom="0,[[6]],[[15]],[[21]],60" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="people"
Step 1: Build 35% from benchmarks: 35% = 25% + 10%.
Step 2: 25% of 60 = 60 ÷ 4 = [[15]]. 10% of 60 = 60 ÷ 10 = [[6]].
Step 3: Add them: [[15 + 6 = 21]]. So [[21]] people buy mini donuts.
:::
::: explain
Why can we add 25% and 10% to get 35%?
(lines 2)
=> 25 out of 100 plus 10 out of 100 is 35 out of 100. The parts add up.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — 15% of $40
Find 15% of $40.
@viz dnl top="0%,5%,10%,15%,100%" bottom="$0,[[$2]],[[$4]],[[$6]],$40" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="money"
Step 1: 10% of $40 = [[$4]].
Step 2: 5% is half of 10%: [[$2]].
Step 3: 15% = 10% + 5% = [[$4 + $2 = $6]].
:::

#### Less than 1%
0.5% means half of 1%. It is half of one small square.
@viz hundred shaded=0.5 zoom=1 labels="0.5% of the grid"
0.5% = {0.5/100} = 0.005.
To find 0.5% of 400: 1% of 400 = 4. Half of 4 is [[2]].

::: misconception
WRONG: "0.5% is the same as 50%." Or: "0.5% is the same as 0.5."
WHY: 0.5% is less than 1%. It is not even one small square of the grid.
@viz hundred shaded=50 labels="50% is half the grid" caption="0.5% is much smaller: half of ONE square."
RIGHT: 0.5% is half of 1%. As a decimal, 0.5% = 0.005. 0.5% of 400 is 2.
:::

#### More than 100%
100% is one whole. 150% is one and a half wholes.
@viz hundred shaded=150 labels="100%;50%"
150% of 40 = 100% + 50% = 40 + [[20]] = [[60]].

::: misconception
WRONG: "A percent can't be more than 100%."
WHY: 100% is one whole. You can have more than one whole.
@viz percentbar percent=200 top="0%,100%,200%" bottom="0 cm,30 cm,[[60 cm]]" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="height"
RIGHT: A plant was 30 cm tall. Now it is 200% of that height: 60 cm.
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — 250% of 20
Zoe had 20 hockey cards. Now she has 250% as many. How many does she have?
@viz percentbar percent=250 top="0%,100%,200%,250%" bottom="0,20,[[40]],[[50]]" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="cards"
Step 1: 250% = 100% + 100% + 50%.
Step 2: 20 + 20 + [[10]] = [[50]].
Step 3: She has [[50]] cards. That is more than 20, because 250% is more than one whole.
:::

:::

## Section 4: Percent and ratio problems
Learning target: I can solve problems with discounts, tax, recipes, unit rates and units.

### Vocabulary
- discount :: money taken off the price :: 25% off $60 is $15 off
- sale price :: the price after the discount :: $60 − $15 = $45
- GST :: a 5% tax added to most things we buy in Alberta :: GST on $20 is $1
- percent increase :: the amount goes up by a percent :: $80 up 10% is $88
- percent decrease :: the amount goes down by a percent :: $80 down 10% is $72
- unit rate :: the amount for ONE :: $3.00 for 2 L is $1.50 per litre
- scale :: make a recipe bigger or smaller by a factor :: double a recipe: × 2

::: key Solve a problem
@viz flow steps="Read: what is compared?|Draw a table, tape or number line|Solve|Check: does it make sense?"
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — A hoodie on sale
An Oilers hoodie costs $60. It is 25% off. Find the sale price.
@viz percentbar percent=25 top="0%,25%,50%,75%,100%" bottom="$0,[[$15]],$30,[[$45]],$60" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="price"
Step 1: The discount is 25% of $60 = $60 ÷ 4 = [[$15]].
Step 2: Sale price: $60 − [[$15 = $45]].
Step 3: Check: 100% − 25% = 75%. 75% of $60 is [[$45]]. ✓
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — GST on a cap
A Flames cap costs $28. Add 5% GST. Find the total.
@viz receipt title="Team store" items="Flames cap:$28.00;GST (5%):[[$1.40]];Total:[[$29.40]]"
Step 1: 10% of $28 = [[$2.80]].
Step 2: 5% is half of 10%: [[$1.40]].
Step 3: Total: $28 + [[$1.40 = $29.40]].
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — Ticket prices go up
An Oilers ticket cost $80. The price goes up by 10%. Find the new price.
@viz percentbar percent=110 top="0%,50%,100%,110%" bottom="$0,$40,$80,[[$88]]" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="price"
Step 1: 10% of $80 = [[$8]].
Step 2: Up means add: $80 + [[$8 = $88]].
Step 3: The new price is 110% of the old price.
If the price went down 10%, it would be $80 − [[$8 = $72]].
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "20% off, then add 20%, gets you back to the start."
WHY: The second 20% is 20% of a smaller number.
@viz flow steps="$100|20% off → $80|add 20% of $80|[[$96]]"
RIGHT: $100 − $20 = $80. Then 20% of $80 is $16. $80 + $16 = $96, not $100.
:::

::: example Worked Example 4 — Bannock for more people
Tess's bannock for 6 people uses 3 cups of flour. How much flour for 10 people?
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6,8,10" bottom="0,1,2,3,4,[[5]]" toplabel="people" bottomlabel="flour (cups)"
Step 1: Find the flour for 2 people: 6:3 = 2:[[1]] (÷ 3).
Step 2: Multiply by 5: 2:[[1]] = 10:[[5]].
Step 3: She needs [[5]] cups of flour.
:::
::: explain
In Worked Example 4, why did Tess find the flour for 2 people first?
(lines 2)
=> 6 does not go evenly into 10. 2 goes into both 6 and 10.
:::

::: example Worked Example 5 — The better buy
A 2 L jug of apple juice costs $3.00. A 3 L jug costs $4.20. Which is the better buy?
@viz dnl top="$0,[[$1.50]],$3.00" bottom="0 L,1 L,2 L" toplabel="2 L jug" bottomlabel="litres"

Step 2: $4.20 ÷ 3 = [[$1.40]] per litre.
Step 3: The [[3 L]] jug costs less for each litre. It is the better buy.
:::

## Unit Synthesis
1. Juice A is 2 cans of concentrate to 7 cans of water. Juice B is 3:10.
a) Make three equivalent ratios for Juice A.
b) Which juice is stronger?

## Concept Reference
::: key Equivalent ratios
Multiply or divide BOTH terms by the same factor. 2:3 = 4:6 = 6:9.
Never add the same number to both terms.
Part to part 2:3 means 2 out of 5 in all.
:::
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6" bottom="0,3,6,9" toplabel="first" bottomlabel="second" arrow="1,3,× 3"

::: key Missing terms and comparing
Find the factor. Use it on the other term. 2:5 = 8:20 (× 4).
Compare: make one term match. Then compare the other term.
Cross products: {a/b} = {c/d} is the same as a × d = b × c.
:::
@viz tape parts="2:concentrate;5:water" total="2:5"

::: key Percents
A percent is a ratio out of 100. 45% = {45/100} = 0.45.
Benchmarks: 50% (÷ 2), 25% (÷ 4), 10% (÷ 10), 5% (half of 10%), 1% (÷ 100).
35% = 25% + 10%. 0.5% is half of 1%. 150% is 100% + 50%.
:::
@viz percentbar percent=35 top="0%,10%,25%,35%,100%" bottom="0,6,15,21,60" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="of 60"

::: key Problems
Discount: take it off. GST (5%): add it on.
Increase: add the percent. Decrease: subtract it.
Unit rate: the price for ONE. 1 m = 100 cm. 1 L = 1000 mL.
:::
@viz receipt items="Cap:$28.00;GST (5%):$1.40;Total:$29.40"
