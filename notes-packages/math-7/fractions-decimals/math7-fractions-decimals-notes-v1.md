## Section 1: Multiplying fractions
Learning target: I can multiply fractions with a picture and with a rule.

### Vocabulary
- product :: the answer to a multiplication :: the product of 2 and 3 is 6
- of :: in math, "of" often means × :: {1/2} of 8 = {1/2} × 8 = 4
- area model :: a square cut into rows and columns to show a product :: {1/3} × {1/2} is 1 part out of 6
- proper fraction :: the top is smaller than the bottom. It is less than 1. :: {3/4}
- simplest form :: the top and bottom have no common factor but 1 :: {4/8} in simplest form is {1/2}

#### A whole number times a fraction
3 × {2/5} means 3 groups of {2/5}.
@viz bars bars="2/5;2/5;2/5" labels="Group 1;Group 2;Group 3" unit=260 caption="3 groups of 2 fifths."
3 × {2/5} = {2/5} + {2/5} + {2/5} = {6/5}
@viz bars bars="6/5" labels="6 fifths" caption="6 fifths fill 1 whole, with 1 fifth more."
So 3 × {2/5} = {6/5} = 1{1/5}.

#### A fraction of a number
{1/2} × 8 means "half of 8."
@viz bars bars="4/8" labels="½ of 8 pieces" unit=320 caption="8 pieces. Half of them are shaded."
{1/2} × 8 = 4. The answer is less than 8.

#### A fraction of a fraction
{1/3} × {1/2} means "{1/3} of {1/2}."
1. Shade {1/2} of a square in columns (blue).
2. Shade {1/3} of the square in rows (yellow).
3. The overlap is the answer.
@viz area a=1/3 b=1/2 result="1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6" result2="overlap: 1 of 6 parts"
The square has 3 rows and 2 columns. That makes 3 × 2 = 6 parts.
The overlap is 1 × 1 = 1 part. So {1/3} × {1/2} = {1/6}.

::: key Multiplying fractions
Multiply the tops. Multiply the bottoms.
{a/b} × {c/d} = {(a × c)/(b × d)}
Then write the answer in simplest form.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Half a bannock recipe
Ava's bannock recipe uses {3/4} cup of milk. She makes half a recipe. How much milk does she need?
@viz area a=1/2 b=3/4 alabel="½ recipe" blabel="3/4 cup" result="1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8" result2="overlap: 3 of 8 parts"
Step 1: Half of {3/4} means {1/2} × {3/4}.
Step 2: Multiply the tops: 1 × 3 = 3.
Step 3: Multiply the bottoms: 2 × 4 = 8.
She needs {3/8} cup of milk.
:::
::: explain
Why is the bottom number 8 in Worked Example 1?
(lines 2)
=> The square is cut into 2 rows and 4 columns. That makes 2 × 4 = 8 equal parts.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Multiplying always makes a number bigger."
WHY: Times a proper fraction means take part of it. A part is smaller.
@viz numberline min=0 max=1 step=0.125 every=4 ticklabels="0:0;0.5:½;1:1" points="0.375:3/8;0.5:1/2;0.75:3/4" caption="3/8 is less than both 1/2 and 3/4."
RIGHT: {1/2} × 8 = 4. And {1/2} × {3/4} = {3/8}. Each answer is smaller.
:::
::: key Two proper fractions
When you multiply two proper fractions, the product is less than each one.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Simplify first or last?
Find {2/4} × {2/3} two ways.
@viz area a=2/3 b=2/4 result="2/4 × 2/3 = 4/12" result2="overlap: 4 of 12 parts"
Way 1: Multiply first. {2/4} × {2/3} = {4/12}. Simplify: {4/12} = [[{1/3}]].
Way 2: Simplify first. {2/4} = {1/2}. Then {1/2} × {2/3} = {2/6} = [[{1/3}]].
@viz area a=2/3 b=1/2 result="1/2 × 2/3 = 2/6" result2="overlap: 2 of 6 parts"
Both ways give {1/3}. Equal fractions give equal products.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "To multiply, find a common denominator first. {1/2} × {1/3} = {3/6} × {2/6} = {6/6} = 1."
WHY: Common denominators are for + and −. For ×, multiply the tops and the bottoms.
@viz area a=1/2 b=1/3 result="1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6" result2="1 part of 6, not 1 whole"
RIGHT: {1/2} × {1/3} = {1/6}. Half of a third is a sixth.
:::

@viz partial cols="1:1 batch;0.5:½ batch" rows="0.667:2/3 cup" cells="1 × 2/3 = 2/3;[[1/3]]" total="2/3 + 1/3 = [[1]] cup"

## Section 2: Dividing fractions
Learning target: I can divide with fractions using groups, common denominators and reciprocals.

### Vocabulary
- quotient :: the answer to a division :: 6 ÷ 2 = 3. The quotient is 3.
- divisor :: the number you divide by :: in 3 ÷ {1/2}, the divisor is {1/2}
- reciprocal :: the fraction flipped: switch the top and the bottom :: the reciprocal of {2/3} is {3/2}

#### How many groups fit?
3 ÷ {1/4} asks: how many {1/4}s fit in 3?
@viz groups total=3 piece=1/4 count="3 ÷ 1/4 = 12" caption="Count the quarters in 3 wholes."
Each whole has 4 quarters. 3 wholes have 3 × 4 = 12 quarters.
So 3 ÷ {1/4} = 12.

::: misconception
WRONG: "Dividing always makes a number smaller."
WHY: Dividing by a small piece counts how many pieces fit. Lots of small pieces fit.
@viz groups total=3 piece=1/2 count="3 ÷ 1/2 = 6"
RIGHT: 3 ÷ {1/2} = 6. There are 6 halves in 3.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Cutting rope
Raj has 6 m of rope. He cuts pieces that are {3/4} m long. How many pieces does he get?
@viz groups total=6 piece=3/4 numbers=0 count="6 ÷ 3/4 = [[8]]"
Step 1: Ask: how many {3/4}s fit in 6?
Step 2: Each metre has 4 quarters. So 6 m has [[24]] quarters.
Step 3: Each piece uses 3 quarters. 24 ÷ 3 = [[8]].
He gets [[8]] pieces.
:::
::: explain
Why is the answer to 6 ÷ {3/4} bigger than 6?
(lines 2)
=> Each piece is less than 1 m. So more than 6 pieces fit in 6 m.
:::

#### A fraction shared by a whole number
Three friends share {1/2} of a pan of bannock. How much does each one get?
@viz area a=1/3 b=1/2 alabel="1/3" blabel="1/2 pan" note1="1/2 pan: blue columns" note2="cut into 3 shares: rows" result="1/2 ÷ 3 = [[1/6]]"
Each friend gets {1/3} of the half.
{1/2} ÷ 3 = {1/3} × {1/2} = [[{1/6}]] of the pan.
Dividing by 3 is the same as taking {1/3} of it.

::: example Worked Example 2 — Sharing a pizza
Mia has {3/4} of a pizza. She shares it with 2 friends. All 3 get the same amount. How much does each one get?
@viz bars bars="3/4" labels="3/4 pizza" unit=320 caption="3 shaded pieces for 3 people."
Step 1: Write it: {3/4} ÷ 3.
Step 2: The picture has 3 quarters. Each person gets [[1]] quarter.
Step 3: Check with the rule: {1/3} × {3/4} = {3/12} = [[{1/4}]].
Each person gets [[{1/4}]] of the pizza.
:::

#### Fraction ÷ fraction with the same bottom
{3/4} ÷ {1/8}: write both in eighths first.
{3/4} = {6/8}. Now ask: how many {1/8}s fit in {6/8}?
@viz groups total=3/4 piece=1/8 numbers=0 count="6/8 ÷ 1/8 = 6 ÷ 1 = [[6]]"
Six eighths hold [[6]] pieces of one eighth.
::: key Same bottoms
When the bottoms match, divide the tops.
{a/b} ÷ {c/b} = a ÷ c
{6/8} ÷ {2/8} = 6 ÷ 2 = 3
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — Shelves from a board
Kai has a board 1{1/2} m long. Each shelf is {3/8} m long. How many shelves can he cut?
@viz groups total=3/2 piece=3/8 numbers=0 count="3/2 ÷ 3/8 = [[4]] shelves"
Step 1: Change the mixed number. 1{1/2} = {3/2}.
Step 2: Make the bottoms match. {3/2} = {[[12]]/8}.
Step 3: Divide the tops. {12/8} ÷ {3/8} = 12 ÷ 3 = [[4]].
He can cut [[4]] shelves.
:::

#### Reciprocals
How many {1/4}s make 1 whole? Four. So {1/4} × 4 = 1.
@viz groups total=1 piece=1/4 count="4 quarters make 1"
A number times its **reciprocal** is always 1.
| Number | Reciprocal | Product |
|---|---|---|
| {2/3} | {3/2} | {6/6} = 1 |
| 5 | {1/5} | {5/5} = 1 |
| {1/4} | [[4]] | [[1]] |
| {3/5} | [[{5/3}]] | [[1]] |

::: key Dividing by a fraction
Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.
1. Keep the first number.
2. Change ÷ to ×.
3. Flip the divisor (the second number).
3 ÷ {1/4} = 3 × 4 = 12. That matches the picture of 12 quarters.
:::

::: example Worked Example 4 — {2/3} ÷ {1/6}
@viz flow steps="Keep 2/3|÷ becomes ×|Flip 1/6 → [[6]]|2/3 × 6 = [[12/3]]"
Step 1: Keep {2/3}. Change ÷ to ×. Flip {1/6} to {6/1}.
Step 2: {2/3} × {6/1} = {[[12]]/3}.
Step 3: Simplify. {12/3} = [[4]].
Check with a picture. How many sixths fit in {2/3}?
@viz groups total=2/3 piece=1/6 numbers=0 count="[[4]] sixths fit in 2/3"
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "To divide, flip the first fraction. {1/2} ÷ {1/4} = {2/1} × {1/4} = {2/4}."
WHY: Keep the first one. Flip only the one you divide BY.
@viz groups total=1/2 piece=1/4 count="1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 2"
RIGHT: {1/2} ÷ {1/4} = {1/2} × {4/1} = {4/2} = 2. Two quarters fit in a half.
:::

## Section 3: Multiplying and dividing decimals
Learning target: I can multiply and divide decimals and check with an estimate.

### Vocabulary
- tenth :: one of 10 equal parts of 1 :: 0.3 is 3 tenths
- hundredth :: one of 100 equal parts of 1 :: 0.06 is 6 hundredths
- decimal place :: a digit after the decimal point :: 2.45 has two decimal places
- estimate :: a close answer with friendly numbers :: 2.9 × 4 is about 3 × 4 = 12
- equivalent division :: a division with the same quotient :: 6 ÷ 0.5 = 60 ÷ 5

#### A decimal times a whole number
Mia buys 3 m of ribbon. It costs $1.25 for each metre.
3 × 1.25 = 1.25 + 1.25 + 1.25 = 3.75
@viz receipt title="Craft store" items="Ribbon, 3 m at $1.25 per m:$3.75;Total:$3.75"
She pays $3.75.

#### A decimal times a decimal
0.3 × 0.2 means 3 tenths of 2 tenths.
Decimals are fractions: 0.3 = {3/10} and 0.2 = {2/10}.
@viz area a=2/10 b=3/10 alabel="0.2" blabel="0.3" note1="columns: 3 tenths" note2="rows: 2 tenths" result="0.3 × 0.2 = [[0.06]]" result2="overlap: [[6]] of 100 squares"
{3/10} × {2/10} = [[{6/100}]] = [[0.06]]
::: key Multiplying decimals
1. Multiply as if there were no decimal points: 3 × 2 = 6.
2. Count the decimal places in both numbers: 1 + 1 = 2.
3. Give the answer that many decimal places: 0.06.
4. Check with an estimate or a picture.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "0.3 × 0.2 = 0.6"
WHY: 0.6 is bigger than both numbers. Part of a part must be smaller.
@viz hundred shaded=6 labels="6 hundredths = 0.06"
RIGHT: 3 × 2 = 6. There are two decimal places in all. So 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.06.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Filling up with gas
Gas costs $1.45 per litre. Sam's dad buys 30 L. What does it cost?
@viz receipt title="Gas station" items="30 L at $1.45 per L:[[$43.50]];Total:[[$43.50]]"

Step 2: Multiply: 30 × 145 = [[4350]].
Step 3: There are 2 decimal places. So 30 × 1.45 = [[43.50]].
It costs [[$43.50]]. That is close to the estimate. ✓
:::

Step 3: Check with the rule. 24 × 15 = [[360]]. Two decimal places: [[3.60]].

::: explain
In Worked Example 2, why are there four parts?
(lines 2)
=> Each part of the length times each part of the width. 2 parts times 2 parts make 4 parts.
:::

#### A decimal shared by a whole number
Three friends share $4.50 equally.
@viz tape parts="3:$4.50 shared" values="[[$1.50]]" total="$4.50"
4.50 ÷ 3 = [[1.50]]. Each friend gets [[$1.50]].

#### A whole number divided by a decimal
6 ÷ 0.5 asks: how many halves fit in 6?
@viz groups total=6 piece=1/2 count="6 ÷ 0.5 = 12"
Each whole has 2 halves. 6 wholes have 12 halves. So 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12.

::: misconception
WRONG: "6 ÷ 0.5 = 3"
WHY: 3 is half of 6. That is 6 ÷ 2, not 6 ÷ 0.5.
@viz numberline min=0 max=6 step=0.5 every=2 jumps="0>0.5>1>1.5>2>2.5>3>3.5>4>4.5>5>5.5>6" caption="Jumps of 0.5 from 0 to 6. Count the jumps."
RIGHT: Count the jumps of 0.5. There are 12. So 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12.
:::

#### Make an equivalent division
Multiply both numbers by 10. The quotient stays the same.
@viz flow steps="6 ÷ 0.5|× 10 both|60 ÷ 5|= 12"
Think of money. $6 in 50¢ coins is 600¢ ÷ 50¢ = 12 coins.
::: key Dividing by a decimal
1. Multiply both numbers by 10 (or 100). Stop when the divisor is a whole number.
2. Divide.
3. Check with an estimate.
For long decimals, use a calculator. Then check with an estimate.
:::

## Section 4: Order of operations
Learning target: I can evaluate an expression in the right order.

### Vocabulary
- expression :: numbers and signs like + and ×, with no = sign :: 2 + 3 × 4
- evaluate :: find the value :: 2 + 3 × 4 evaluates to 14
- order of operations :: the rules for which step comes first :: brackets first, then × and ÷
- brackets :: ( ) around part of an expression. Do that part first. :: (2 + 3) × 4 = 20

#### Why we need an order
Mia is at the Calgary Stampede. She buys a $2 drink and 3 hot dogs at $4 each.
Her cost is 2 + 3 × 4.
@viz receipt title="Stampede food truck" items="1 drink:$2;3 hot dogs at $4:[[$12]];Total:[[$14]]"
Sam works left to right. 2 + 3 = 5, then 5 × 4 = 20.
Lily multiplies first. 3 × 4 = 12, then 2 + 12 = 14.
Mia pays $14. Lily is right. Everyone must get the same answer, so math has rules.

::: key Order of operations
1. **Brackets** ( ).
2. **Squares and cubes**, like 3² and 2³.
3. **× and ÷**, left to right.
4. **+ and −**, left to right.
:::
@viz flow steps="Brackets ( )|Squares and cubes|× and ÷, left to right|+ and −, left to right"

::: misconception
WRONG: "Always work left to right. 2 + 3 × 4 = 5 × 4 = 20."
WHY: × comes before +, even when + is first in the line.
@viz flow steps="2 + 3 × 4|2 + 12|14" width=420
RIGHT: 2 + 3 × 4 = 2 + 12 = 14.
:::
::: misconception
WRONG: "× always comes before ÷. 12 ÷ 2 × 3 = 12 ÷ 6 = 2."
WHY: × and ÷ are a team. Do them left to right, in the order you see them.
@viz flow steps="12 ÷ 2 × 3|6 × 3|18" width=420
RIGHT: 12 ÷ 2 = 6. Then 6 × 3 = 18.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — With integers
Evaluate −3 + 4 × (−2).
@viz flow steps="−3 + 4 × (−2)|−3 + ([[−8]])|[[−11]]" width=480
Step 1: × first: 4 × (−2) = [[−8]].
Step 2: Then +: −3 + (−8) = [[−11]].
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Same numbers, different answers

@viz flow steps="(5 − 2)² × 2|[[3]]² × 2|[[9]] × 2|[[18]]"

Step 2: The second has no brackets. Square first: 2² = [[4]]. Then 4 × 2 = [[8]]. Then 5 − 8 = [[−3]].

:::
::: explain
In Worked Example 2, why are the two answers different?
(lines 2)
=> The brackets change what we do first. With brackets, we subtract before we square.
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — With fractions

## Concept Reference
::: key Multiplying fractions
Multiply the tops. Multiply the bottoms. Simplify.
{2/3} × {3/4} = {6/12} = {1/2}
Two proper fractions: the product is less than each one.
Mixed numbers: change to improper fractions first.
:::
@viz area a=2/3 b=3/4 result="2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2" result2="part of a part"

::: key Dividing fractions
Ask: how many groups fit? 3 ÷ {1/4} = 12.
Same bottoms: divide the tops. {6/8} ÷ {2/8} = 6 ÷ 2 = 3.
Or keep, change, flip: {2/3} ÷ {1/6} = {2/3} × 6 = 4.
A number times its reciprocal is 1: {2/3} × {3/2} = 1.
:::
@viz groups total=3 piece=1/4 count="3 ÷ 1/4 = 12"

::: key Decimals
×: multiply the whole numbers. Count the decimal places in both. 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.06.
÷ by a decimal: multiply both by 10 or 100 first. 4.8 ÷ 0.6 = 48 ÷ 6 = 8.
Always check with an estimate.
:::
@viz partial cols="2:2;0.4:0.4" rows="1:1;0.5:0.5" cells="2;0.4;1;0.2" total="2.4 × 1.5 = 3.6"

::: key Order of operations
1. Brackets. 2. Squares and cubes. 3. × and ÷, left to right. 4. + and −, left to right.
:::
@viz flow steps="(5 − 2)² × 2|3² × 2|9 × 2|18"

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Multiplying always makes it bigger | {1/2} × 8 = 4. Taking part of something gives less. |
| You need a common denominator to multiply | Multiply tops and bottoms. Common denominators are for + and −. |
| Dividing always makes it smaller | 3 ÷ {1/2} = 6. There are 6 halves in 3. |
| Flip the first fraction | Keep the first. Flip the divisor. Then multiply. |
| 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.6 | 3 × 2 = 6, two decimal places: 0.06. |
| 6 ÷ 0.5 = 3 | There are 12 halves in 6: 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12. |
| 2 + 3 × 4 = 20 | × before +: 2 + 12 = 14. |
| 12 ÷ 2 × 3 = 2 | × and ÷ go left to right: 6 × 3 = 18. |
