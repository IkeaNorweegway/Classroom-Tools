## Section 1 — Circle Vocabulary

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Centre | Point equidistant from all points on the circle | O in any standard circle diagram |
| Radius | Segment from centre to any point on the circle | OA where A is on the circle |
| Diameter | The longest chord; passes through the centre; d = 2r | Line through O touching both sides |
| Chord | Line segment with both endpoints on the circle | Any segment connecting two circle points |
| Arc | A portion of the circle between two points | The curved part between A and B |
| Central angle | Angle with vertex at the centre | Angle AOB where O is centre |
| Inscribed angle | Angle with vertex on the circle, sides are chords | Angle APB where P is on the circle |
| Tangent | Line touching the circle at exactly one point | A line that just grazes the circle |
| Point of tangency | The single point where the tangent meets the circle | T where tangent touches |
| Semicircle | Exactly half the circle, cut by a diameter | The arc above or below a diameter |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Centre is ___ from all points on the circle | **equidistant** |
| Radius goes from ___ to any point on the circle | **the centre** |
| Diameter is the ___ chord; passes through the ___ | **longest; centre** |
| d = ___ × r; r = d/__ | **2 × r; 2** |
| Chord has both endpoints on the ___ | **circle** |
| Arc is ___ of the circle between two points | **a portion/part** |

**Diagram labels:**
- QR passes through centre O → **diameter**
- The vertical line at T → **tangent**
- QR is a diameter (special chord). A chord not through O would be **shorter than** the diameter.

**Central vs inscribed angle:**
- Central: vertex at **centre**
- Inscribed: vertex on the **circle**
- Arc intercepted: lies **inside/between** the angle's sides

**Tangent:** exactly **one** point. Perpendicular to the **radius** at that point.
Tangent ≠ chord: tangent meets circle at one point (one endpoint ON circle, extends to external); chord has BOTH endpoints on circle.

**Arc types:** minor = **shorter** arc; major = **longer** arc; semicircle = exactly **half** the circle, cut by a **diameter**

### Retrieval Check — Section 1

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Chord vs. diameter | Chord: both endpoints on circle, any length. Diameter: special chord passing through centre; longest chord possible. |
| 2. Central vs. inscribed angle | Central: vertex at centre. Inscribed: vertex on circle, sides are chords. |
| 3. Diameter 14 cm → radius | **r = 7 cm** |
| 4. Tangent property | Perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency (90° angle) |
| 5. (Stretch) Equal-length chords | Yes. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre — they are the same distance from the centre. |

## Section 2 — Chord Properties

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Perpendicular bisector | Line perpendicular to a segment AND cutting it exactly in half | OM ⊥ AB and AM = MB |
| Bisect | Cut into two equal halves | OM bisects AB |
| Equidistant | Same distance from a reference point | Two equal chords are equidistant from the centre |
| Distance from centre to chord | Length of the perpendicular segment from centre to chord | OM where OM ⊥ AB |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Property 1: Line from ___ perpendicular to chord ___ it | **centre; bisects** |
| AM = ___ | **MB** |
| Converse: bisecting line from centre is ___ to chord | **perpendicular** |

**Worked Example 1 — Chord AB = 16, radius = 10:**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| AM = 16/2 | **8 cm** |
| OA = ___ (radius) | **10** |
| OM² = 10² − 8² = ___ − ___ | 100 − 64 = **36** |
| OM = ___ cm | **6 cm** |

Why OA is hypotenuse: OA is the radius (longest side, from centre to circle), and the right angle is at M (OM ⊥ AB). The hypotenuse is always the side opposite the right angle → OA.

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Property 2: equal chords → ___ from centre | **equidistant** |
| Equidistant → ___ | **equal** |
| d₁ = ___ | **d₂** |

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|

### Retrieval Check — Section 2

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Perpendicular bisector chord property | If a line from the centre is perpendicular to a chord, it cuts the chord into two equal halves (bisects it at the midpoint). |
| 2. Chord 18 cm, distance 12 cm — find radius | Half-chord = 9. r² = 12²+9² = 144+81 = 225. **r = 15 cm** |
| 3. Two chords both 10 cm | They are **equidistant from the centre** (same length → same distance by Property 2). |
| 4. Radius 5, chord 3 from centre — find chord | Half-chord = √(25−9) = √16 = 4. **Full chord = 8 cm** |
| 5. (Stretch) Chord at d=8 vs. d=15, radius=17 | d=8: half=√(289−64)=√225=15, chord=**30 cm**. d=15: half=√(289−225)=√64=8, chord=**16 cm**. Chord at d=8 is longer by **14 cm**. |

## Section 3 — Central Angle and Inscribed Angle Theorem

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Inscribed angle theorem | Inscribed angle = ½ × central angle subtending same arc | Central 80° → inscribed 40° |
| Subtend | To form an angle over (stand opposite to) | AOB subtends arc AB |
| Same arc | The arc between the two points defining both angles | Arc AB for both angle AOB and APB |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Inscribed angle is ___ the central angle (same arc) | **half** |
| Central angle = ___ × inscribed angle | **2** |
| AOB (central) = ___° → APB (inscribed) = ___° | Central twice the inscribed |

**Worked Example 1:** AOB = 80°. APB = 80°/2 = **40°**
**Worked Example 2:** APB = 35°. AOB = 2 × 35° = **70°**

**Corollary:** All inscribed angles subtending the same arc are **the same** size (equal to each other).
APB = AQB = **(same value)**

**Misconception:** 90° only when inscribed angle subtends a **diameter** (semicircle). Central = 180° → inscribed = 90°.
Angles in semicircle: APB = **90°**. Because central = **180°** (straight line). Half = **90°**.

**Finding unknowns — AOB = 110°:**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| (a) APB (same minor arc) | 110°/2 = **55°** |
| (b) Major arc central = 360°−110° | = **250°** |
| AQB (major arc inscribed) | 250°/2 = **125°** |

### Retrieval Check — Section 3

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Inscribed angle theorem | An inscribed angle is half the central angle that subtends the same arc. |
| 2. Central 144° → inscribed | **72°** |
| 3. Inscribed 23° → central | **46°** |
| 4. Why equal inscribed angles? | All inscribed angles subtending the same arc equal half the same central angle — they all measure the same value regardless of where on the circle the vertex sits. |
| 5. (Stretch) CPD | Major arc CD central = 130°. CPD = 130°/2 = **65°** |

## Section 4 — Tangent-Radius Property

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Tangent | Line touching circle at exactly one point | Line PT where T is on circle |
| Point of tangency | Single point where tangent meets circle | T in diagram |
| Perpendicular | At 90° | Radius OT ⊥ tangent at T |
| Tangent from external point | Segment from point outside circle to point of tangency | Segment PT |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Tangent is ___ to the radius at point of tangency | **perpendicular** |
| Angle OTP = ___° | **90°** |
| Right angle is at ___, making right triangle | **point of tangency T** |

**Worked Example — Radius 5, OP = 13:**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Right angle at ___ | **T** |
| Hypotenuse = ___ | **PO = 13** |
| PT² = 13²−5² = ___−___ | 169−25 = **144** |
| PT = ___ cm | **12 cm** |

Why PO is hypotenuse: the right angle is at T (tangent ⊥ radius). In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle — that's PO.

**Equal tangents:** PT₁ = **PT₂** (both tangent segments from same external point are equal)

**Alberta fountain — radius 3, OP = 5:**
PT = √(25−9) = √16 = **4 m** from external point to point of tangency.

### Retrieval Check — Section 4

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Tangent-radius property | A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency (90° angle is formed). |
| 2. Radius 6, OP = 10 — find tangent length | PT = √(100−36) = √64 = **8 cm** |
| 3. One tangent = 8 cm — find other | **8 cm** (equal tangents from same external point) |
| 4. Why right triangle? | Perpendicularity (90° at T) is the definition of a right triangle. The centre, the point of tangency, and the external point form a right triangle with OP as hypotenuse. |
| 5. (Stretch) Radius 7, OP = 25 — find PT, angle | PT = √(625−49) = √576 = **24 cm**. Angle OPT: tan(OPT)=7/24 → **arctan(7/24) ≈ 16°** |

## Unit Synthesis

### Part A — Key ideas

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Two chord properties | (1) A perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord. (2) Equal chords are equidistant from the centre (and converse). |
| 2. Inscribed angle theorem + special case | Inscribed angle = ½ × central angle (same arc). Special case: when chord is a diameter (central angle = 180°), inscribed angle = 90°. |
| 3. Tangent-radius + Pythagorean theorem | Tangent is perpendicular to radius at point of tangency. This creates a right angle at T, forming a right triangle (centre-tangency-external point) with the external-to-centre segment as hypotenuse. |

### Part B — Radius 10, chord AB = 16, inscribed angle APB = 38°

**(a) Distance from O to chord AB:**

> Half-chord = 8. OM² = 10²−8² = 100−64 = 36. **OM = 6 cm**

**(b) Central angle AOB:**

> Inscribed APB = 38°. Central = 2 × 38° = **76°**
