### Guided Notes

**Property 1: The Perpendicular from the Centre Bisects the Chord**

If a line from the _______________ of a circle is perpendicular to a chord, then it _______________ that chord (cuts it into two equal halves).

```
            centre O
               |
               | (perpendicular)
    ───────────┼───────────
       half        half
           chord AB
```

In symbols: If OM ⊥ AB, then AM = ___

**The converse is also true:** If a line from the centre bisects a chord, then it is _______________ to the chord.

---

**Worked Example 1:**

A chord AB is 16 cm long. The perpendicular from centre O meets AB at M. Find AM.

> Since OM ⊥ AB and passes through the centre, it bisects AB.
>
> AM = ___/2 = ___ cm

**Now extend — use Pythagorean theorem:**

If the radius is 10 cm, find OM.

> Right triangle OMA: OA = ___ (radius), AM = ___, OM = ?
>
> OA² = OM² + AM²
>
> 10² = OM² + 8²
>
> OM² = ___ − ___ = ___
>
> OM = ___ cm

**Explain:** Which side is the hypotenuse in triangle OMA? How do you know?

_______________________________________________

**Property 2: Equal Chords Are Equidistant from the Centre**

In the same circle:
- If two chords are _______________ in length, then they are _______________ from the centre.
- Conversely: if two chords are the same distance from the centre, they are _______________.

```
   O (centre)
   |          |
   |d          |d
   |          |
  chord AB   chord CD
```

If AB = CD, then d₁ = ___

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: A longer chord is farther from the centre**
>
> Wrong idea: "The longest chord should be farthest from the centre — bigger chords must extend farther."
>
> Why it fails: It's the opposite. The chord closest to the centre is the *longest*. The diameter — the longest chord of all — passes through the centre, at distance 0. As a chord moves farther from the centre, it gets shorter.
>
> Think of it: as you move a horizontal chord up toward the top of a circle, it gets shorter and shorter until it disappears at the edge. The closer to the middle, the longer.

---

**Worked Example 2:**

Two chords PQ and RS are each 24 cm long in a circle with radius 13 cm.

> Since PQ = RS, they are equidistant from the centre.
>
> Let M be the midpoint of PQ. PM = ___/2 = ___
>
> Right triangle OMP: OP = 13 (radius), PM = 12, OM = ?
>
> 13² = OM² + 12²
>
> OM = ___
>
> Therefore both chords are ___ cm from the centre.

### Guided Notes

**The Inscribed Angle Theorem:**

The inscribed angle is _______________ the central angle that subtends the same arc.

Inscribed angle = (1/2) × Central angle
OR: Central angle = ___ × Inscribed angle

[SVG: Circle with inscribed angle APB at point P on the arc and central angle AOB at centre O, both subtending chord AB at the bottom]

Angle AOB (central) = ___°
Angle APB (inscribed, same arc AB) = ___°

---

**Worked Example 1:**

A central angle AOB = 80°. Find the inscribed angle APB subtending the same arc.

> Inscribed angle = Central angle / ___
>
> APB = 80° / 2 = ___°

**Worked Example 2:**

An inscribed angle APB = 35°. Find the central angle AOB subtending the same arc.

> Central angle = 2 × Inscribed angle
>
> AOB = 2 × 35° = ___°

**Explain:** An inscribed angle is at the circumference, while a central angle is at the centre. Why does being at the centre make it "see" the arc at twice the angle? (Think about how far each vertex is from the arc.)

---

**Corollary: Inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal.**

If angles APB and AQB are both inscribed in the same circle and subtend the same arc AB, then:

APB = AQB = (1/2) × AOB

No matter where P and Q are on the major arc, the inscribed angle is always the _______________ size.

[SVG: Circle with two inscribed angles APB and AQB from points P and Q on the major arc, both subtending the same chord AB, demonstrating they are equal]

APB = AQB = ___

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: All inscribed angles in a circle equal 90°**
>
> Wrong idea: "Any angle drawn inside a circle is 90°."
>
> Why it fails: Only inscribed angles that subtend a *diameter* (semicircle) equal 90°. That is a special case. An inscribed angle subtending an arc of 60° (central angle 60°) gives an inscribed angle of 30°. The angle depends on the arc.
>
> The special case: when AB is a diameter, the central angle AOB = 180°. So the inscribed angle = 180°/2 = ___°. This is why angles in a semicircle are 90°.

---

**Angles in a semicircle:**

If AB is a diameter, then any inscribed angle APB = ___°.

This is because the central angle for a diameter is always ___ (a straight line), so the inscribed angle is half: ___.

**Tangent-Radius Property:**

A tangent to a circle is _______________ to the radius at the point of tangency.

```
              T (point of tangency)
         ────┼──────────────── (tangent line)
              |
              | (radius)
              |
              O (centre)
```

Angle OTP = _____°

This means: if you know a line is tangent to a circle, you automatically know the angle at the point of tangency is _______________.

---

**Applying the Pythagorean theorem:**

Since the radius and tangent are perpendicular, they form a _______________ triangle with any line from the external point to the centre.

**Worked Example:**

A tangent PT touches a circle with centre O and radius 5 cm. The external point P is 13 cm from the centre. Find the length of the tangent PT.

```
    P ─────────── T
     \           |
      \          |
    13 \         | 5
        \        |
         O
```

> Right angle is at ___ (tangent ⊥ radius)
>
> Hypotenuse = PO = ___
>
> PT² = PO² − OT²
>
> PT² = 13² − 5²
>
> PT² = ___ − ___ = ___
>
> PT = ___ cm
