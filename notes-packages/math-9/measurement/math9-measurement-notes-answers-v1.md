## Section 1 — 2D Area Formulas

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Area | The amount of 2D space inside a shape | A = l × w for a rectangle |
| Perimeter | The total distance around the outside of a shape | P = 2l + 2w |
| Composite shape | A shape made of two or more simpler shapes | Rectangle + semicircle |
| π (pi) | The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter | ≈ 3.14159... |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Triangle height: ___ height | **perpendicular** |
| π to 2 decimal places | **3.14** |

**Practice table:**

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Triangle, b=8, h=5 | **20 cm²** |
| Circle, r=3 | **9π ≈ 28.27 cm²** |
| Rectangle, 12×4.5 | **54 m²** |
| Parallelogram, b=10, h=7 | **70 m²** |

**Composite example (rect 4×6 + semicircle d=4):**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Rectangle: 4×6 | **24 cm²** |
| Semicircle r=2: ½π(4) | **2π ≈ 6.28 cm²** |
| Total | **≈ 30.28 cm²** |

Why ½πr²: a semicircle is half a full circle — we use half the formula.

Height in area = always the **perpendicular** distance (NOT the slant side).

## Section 2 — Surface Area of Right Prisms and Cylinders

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Surface area | Total area of all outer faces of a 3D solid | SA of a cube side 2 = 24 cm² |
| Right prism | Prism where the lateral edges are perpendicular to the base | Cereal box |
| Cylinder | Circular prism — two circular bases + curved lateral surface | Soup can |
| Net | 2D unfolding of a 3D shape showing all faces | Cross-shaped net of a cube |
| Lateral surface | The non-base face(s) of a prism or cylinder | The curved side of a can |
| Rectangular prism | Prism with rectangular (including square) bases | Box |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| SA = 2(area of base) + ___ × perimeter | **height** |
| Lateral area = ___ of base × height | **perimeter** |

**WE1 — Rectangular prism l=5, w=3, h=4:**

| Faces | Area |
|---|---|
| Two l×w (5×3) | **30 cm²** |
| Two l×h (5×4) | **40 cm²** |
| Two w×h (3×4) | **24 cm²** |
| **SA** | **94 cm²** |

Faces come in 3 pairs because opposite faces are congruent (parallel).

**WE2 — Triangular prism (equilateral side 6, h=5.2, prism h=10):**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| One triangular base = (6×5.2)/2 | **15.6 cm²** |
| Two bases | **31.2 cm²** |
| Perimeter = 6+6+6 | **18 cm** |
| Lateral = 18×10 | **180 cm²** |
| SA = 31.2+180 | **211.2 cm²** |

**Cylinder SA = 2πr² + 2πrh:**
- 2πr² = **two** circular faces
- 2πrh = lateral (circumference × height)

**WE3 — Cylinder r=4, h=9:**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| 2π(4)² = 2π(16) | **32π** |
| 2π(4)(9) = 2π(36) | **72π** |
| Total | **104π ≈ 326.73 cm²** |

Lateral area 2πrh: unrolling the curved surface gives a rectangle with width=h and length=circumference=2πr. Area=2πrh.

**Misconception verify — cube side 2:** SA=6×4=**24 cm²**. V=8 **cm³**. Different units, different values.

**Practice:**
1. 8×3×5: 2(24+40+15) = **158 cm²**
2. r=2.5, h=6: 2π(6.25)+2π(15) = 12.5π+30π = **42.5π ≈ 133.52 m²**

### Retrieval Check — Section 2

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. SA cylinder formula | **SA = 2πr² + 2πrh** |
| 2. Square prism s=5, h=8 | 2(25)+4(5×8)=50+160=**210 cm²** |
| 3. SA=150π, r=5 — find h | 2π(25)+2π(5)h=150π → 50π+10πh=150π → h=**10 cm** |
| 4. Why draw the net? | Shows each face clearly and separately, making it impossible to miss or double-count any face |
| 5. (Stretch) Cylinder h=2r | SA=2πr²+2πr(2r)=2πr²+4πr²=**6πr²** |

## Section 3 — Volume of Right Prisms and Cylinders

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | Amount of 3D space inside a solid | V=lwh for a rectangular prism |
| Cubic units | Volume units (cm³, m³) | 1 cm³ = 1 mL |
| Base area (B) | Area of the base shape of a prism or cylinder | For cylinder: B=πr² |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| V = B × h; B = area of ___ | **base** |
| h = ___ of the solid | **height** |

**WE1 — Rectangular prism l=6, w=4, h=3:**

> B = 6×4 = **24 m²** | V = 24×3 = **72 m³**

**WE2 — Cylinder r=5, h=12:**

> B = π(25) = **25π cm²** | V = 25π×12 = **300π ≈ 942.48 cm³**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Rectangular prism: B = ___ | **l × w** |
| Cylinder: B = ___ | **πr²** |

**Triangular prism — legs 6 and 8, height 10:**

> B = (6×8)/2 = **24 cm²** | V = 24×10 = **240 cm³**

**Alberta oil tank — r=8, h=15:**

> V = π(64)(15) = **960π ≈ 3015.93 m³** ≈ **3,015,929 L**

### Retrieval Check — Section 3

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. General volume formula | **V = B × h** (base area × height) |
| 2. Cylinder r=3, h=10 | V=π(9)(10)=**90π ≈ 282.74 m³** |
| 3. Trapezoidal prism | B=(6+10)/2×4=32 cm². V=32×8=**256 cm³** |
| 4. V=500, B=25 — find h | h=500/25=**20 cm** |

## Section 4 — Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Pyramid | 3D solid with polygon base and triangular faces meeting at apex | Egyptian pyramid |
| Apex | The top point of a pyramid or cone | Tip of a cone |
| Slant height | Distance from apex to midpoint of base edge along a face | l in cone formula |
| Cone | Pyramid with circular base | Ice cream cone |
| Sphere | All points equidistant from a centre in 3D | Ball |

### Guided Notes

**Pyramid SA = base + triangular faces**
- Square pyramid: SA = s² + 2sl (uses slant height)
- Pyramid V = (1/3)Bh (uses perpendicular height)

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Pyramid fits ___ times in matching prism | **3** |
| Cone fits ___ times in matching cylinder | **3** |

**WE1 — Square pyramid: base 6×6, h=4, l=5:**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Base = 6×6 | **36 cm²** |
| SA = 36 + 2(6)(5) | 36+60 = **96 cm²** |
| V = (1/3)(36)(4) | **48 cm³** |

Slant height for SA: measures the triangular face height. Perpendicular height for volume: measures the interior height from base to apex.

**Cone: l = √(r²+h²); SA = πr²+πrl; V = (1/3)πr²h**

**WE2 — Cone r=3, h=4:**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| l = √(9+16) = √25 | **5 cm** |
| SA = 9π + 15π | **24π ≈ 75.40 cm²** |
| V = (1/3)π(9)(4) | **12π ≈ 37.70 cm³** |

**WE3 — Sphere r=6:**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| SA = 4π(36) | **144π ≈ 452.39 cm²** |
| V = (4/3)π(216) | **288π ≈ 904.78 cm³** |

**Misconception verify:** Cylinder V=54π. Cone V=**(1/3)(54π)=18π**. Ratio = **1/3**.

### Retrieval Check — Section 4

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Cone r=5, h=12 | l=13. SA=65π+25π=**90π≈282.74 cm²**. V=**100π≈314.16 cm³** |
| 2. Sphere r=9 | SA=**324π≈1017.88 m²**. V=**972π≈3053.63 m³** |
| 3. Why (1/3)Bh for pyramid? | A pyramid tapers to a point, filling only ⅓ of the prism with the same base and height — proven geometrically. |
| 4. Square pyramid 10×10, l=13 | SA=100+2(10)(13)=100+260=**360 m²** |
| 5. (Stretch) Sphere ⅔ of cylinder | Cylinder: r,h=2r → V=2πr³. Sphere: V=(4/3)πr³. Ratio=(4/3)/2=**2/3 ✓** |

## Section 5 — Composite Objects

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Composite object | 3D solid built from two or more basic shapes | Cylinder + rectangular prism |
| Hidden face | A face inside the composite, not on the outer surface | The circle where sphere meets cylinder |
| Overlapping surface | The shared area at the joint of two shapes | The touching circle between two joined shapes |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Faces at the joint are ___ (not outer) | **hidden** |
| SA strategy: subtract ___ faces | **hidden** (twice — once from each shape) |
| V strategy: ___ the volumes | **add** |

**WE — Cylinder (r=4, h=10) on prism (20×20×5):**

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| V of prism | 20×20×5 = **2000 cm³** |
| V of cylinder | π(16)(10) = **160π** |
| Total V | **2000+160π ≈ 2502.65 cm³** |
| Hidden area (circle) | π(16) = **16π cm²** |
| Prism SA | 2(400)+4(100) = **1200 cm²** |
| After subtracting circle from prism | 1200−16π |
| Cylinder SA | 32π+80π = 112π |
| After subtracting bottom circle | 112π−16π = 96π |
| Total SA | (1200−16π)+96π = **1200+80π ≈ 1451.33 cm²** |

We subtract hidden circle from BOTH shapes because each shape's full SA would include that circle — but in the composite, it's interior to both.

**Common mistake:** Adding both full SAs double-counts the two touching faces. Must subtract **2 × (hidden face area)**.

**Alberta grain elevator — prism (8×8×20) + pyramid (base 8×8, l=5):**

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| Pyramid h = √(5²−4²) = √9 | **3 m** |
| V of prism | 8×8×20 = **1280 m³** |
| V of pyramid | (1/3)(64)(3) = **64 m³** |
| Total V | **1344 m³** |
| SA: prism lateral (4 sides, no top/base) | 4(8×20) = **640 m²** |
| SA: prism bottom base | **64 m²** |
| SA: pyramid lateral (4 triangular faces) | 4×(½×8×5) = **80 m²** |
| Total SA | **784 m²** |

### Retrieval Check — Section 5

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Sphere (r=3) on cylinder (r=3, h=8) | V=36π+72π=**108π≈339.29 cm³** |
| 2. Box with cylindrical hole (r=2, l=10) | (a) V decreases by πr²l=4π(10)=40π cm³. (b) SA: lose two end circles (8π), gain inner cylinder wall (2πrl=40π). Net SA change: +32π. |
| 3. Hidden face rule | Any face at the junction of two joined shapes is interior — not part of the outer surface. Subtract its area from each component's SA. |
| 4. Hemisphere on cylinder (same radius) | Hidden: the flat circular face of the hemisphere (where it meets the cylinder's top). |
| 5. (Stretch) Cube (6cm) with cone removed (r=3, h=6) | Cone l=√(9+36)=√45=3√5. Remaining SA: 6(36)−πr²+πrl=216−9π+9π√5. Remaining V: 216−(1/3)π(9)(6)=**216−18π≈159.47 cm³** |

## Unit Synthesis

### Part A — Formulas from memory

| Solid | SA | V |
|---|---|---|

| Sphere | **4πr²** | **(4/3)πr³** |
| Cube (s) | **6s²** | **s³** |

### Part B — Rocket (cylinder r=3, h=15; cone r=3, h=4; hemisphere base exposed)

Cone slant: l=√(9+16)=5 cm.

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| V_cylinder | π(9)(15) = 135π |
| V_cone | (1/3)π(9)(4) = 12π |
| **Total V** | **147π ≈ 461.81 cm³** |
| Cylinder lateral | 2π(3)(15) = 90π |
| Cylinder bottom | π(9) = 9π |
| Cone lateral | π(3)(5) = 15π |
| Hemisphere exterior | 2π(9) = 18π |
| **Total SA** | **132π ≈ 414.69 cm²** |

*(Cone base and cylinder top are hidden at their junction)*

### Part C — Soup can r=3.5, h=11:

SA = 2π(12.25)+2π(3.5)(11) = 24.5π+77π = **101.5π ≈ 319.00 cm²**

V = π(12.25)(11) = **134.75π ≈ 423.29 cm³**

Volume (≈423 cm³) is larger than SA (≈319 cm²) — though units differ, the volume number is bigger.
