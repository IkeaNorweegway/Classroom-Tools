### Guided Notes

**Surface area = total area of all outer faces.**

**Strategy: draw the net (unfold the 3D shape into 2D).**

**Right Prism — general formula:**

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: Surface area and volume are the same measurement**
>
> Wrong idea: "A bigger box has more surface area AND more volume, so they're basically the same thing."
>
> Why it fails: Surface area measures the total *outer area* in cm² (or m²). Volume measures the *space inside* in cm³ (or m³). A flat sheet of metal can have a huge surface area but almost zero volume. A thick cube can have the same surface area as a flat box but much more volume. They measure fundamentally different things.
>
> Verify: A cube with side 2 cm. SA = 6 × 4 = ___cm². Volume = 2³ = ___cm³. Different units, different values.

## Section 4 — Surface Area and Volume of Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres

**Learning target:** I can apply the formulas for pyramids, cones, and spheres, and explain why pyramid/cone volume is one-third of the corresponding prism/cylinder.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition in your own words | One example |
|---|---|---|
| Pyramid | | |
| Apex | | |
| Slant height | | |
| Cone | | |
| Sphere | | |

### Guided Notes

**Pyramid — Surface Area:**

SA = area of _______________ + area of _______________ triangular faces

For a square-based pyramid (base side s, slant height l):
SA = s² + 4 × (1/2 × s × l) = s² + 2sl

**Pyramid — Volume:**

V = (1/3) × B × h

where B = base area, h = perpendicular height (NOT slant height)

---

> **Key insight: Volume of pyramid = ⅓ × volume of matching prism**
>
> A square pyramid with the same base and height as a square prism fits exactly ___ times inside the prism. This is not a coincidence — it is a geometric fact.
>
> Similarly, a cone fits ___ times inside a cylinder with the same base and height.

---

**Worked Example 1 — Square-based pyramid:**

Base 6 cm × 6 cm, perpendicular height 4 cm, slant height 5 cm.

> Base area = 6 × 6 = ___ cm²
>
> SA = ___ + 2(6)(5) = ___ + ___ = ___ cm²
>
> V = (1/3)(36)(4) = ___ cm³

**Explain:** The slant height (5 cm) is used for surface area but NOT for volume. Why?

_______________________________________________
_______________________________________________

---

**Cone — Surface Area and Volume:**

SA = πr² + πrl
- πr² = area of the circular ___
- πrl = lateral surface area (l = slant height)

V = (1/3)πr²h
- h = perpendicular height, NOT slant height

**Finding slant height from perpendicular height:**

l² = r² + h² (Pythagorean theorem — l is the hypotenuse)

---

**Worked Example 2 — Cone:**

r = 3 cm, h = 4 cm.

> Slant height: l = √(r² + h²) = √(9 + 16) = √___ = ___ cm
>
> SA = π(3)² + π(3)(___)
> = 9π + ___ π = ___ π ≈ ___ cm²
>
> V = (1/3)π(3)²(4) = (1/3)π(___)(___)
> = ___ π ≈ ___ cm³

---

**Sphere:**

SA = 4πr²

V = (4/3)πr³

**Worked Example 3 — Sphere:**

r = 6 cm.

> SA = 4π(6)² = 4π(36) = ___ π ≈ ___ cm²
>
> V = (4/3)π(6)³ = (4/3)π(216) = ___ π ≈ ___ cm³

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: A cone has half the volume of a cylinder**
>
> Wrong idea: "The cone fits inside the cylinder — it must take up half the space."
>
> Why it fails: A cone takes up exactly one-third of the matching cylinder's volume. You can demonstrate this by filling a cone and pouring into the cylinder: you need exactly 3 cone-fulls to fill the cylinder.
>
> The factor (1/3) in the formula is not an estimate — it is precise.
>
> Calculate: Cylinder with r = 3, h = 6. Volume = π(9)(6) = 54π. Cone with same r and h: V = (1/3)π(9)(6) = ___ π. Ratio: ___

---

**Summary table:**

| Solid | SA Formula | V Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangular prism | 2(lw + lh + wh) | lwh |
| Cylinder | 2πr² + 2πrh | πr²h |
| Square pyramid | s² + 2sl | (1/3)s²h |
| Cone | πr² + πrl | (1/3)πr²h |
| Sphere | 4πr² | (4/3)πr³ |

## Section 5 — Composite Objects

**Learning target:** I can find the surface area and volume of composite 3D objects by adding and subtracting the appropriate parts.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition in your own words | One example |
|---|---|---|
| Composite object | | |
| Hidden face | | |
| Overlapping surface | | |

### Guided Notes

**Composite objects** are built by combining or removing basic 3D shapes.

**The key rule:** When two shapes are joined, the faces at the joint are _______________ — they are inside the object and not part of the outer surface.

**Strategy for surface area:**
1. Identify each component shape
2. Calculate the full SA of each
3. Subtract the _______________ faces (the joined faces, twice — once from each shape)

**Strategy for volume:**
1. Calculate the volume of each component
2. _______________ the volumes (or subtract if one is removed)

---

**Worked Example:**

A cylinder (r = 4 cm, h = 10 cm) sits on top of a rectangular prism (20 cm × 20 cm × 5 cm).

[SVG: Composite object showing a cylinder sitting on top of a rectangular prism, both labelled]

**Volume:**
> V = Volume of prism + Volume of cylinder
> = (20)(20)(5) + π(4)²(10)
> = ___ + ___ π
> ≈ ___ cm³

**Surface area:**
> The circular base of the cylinder sits on top of the rectangular prism — that circle is hidden on BOTH shapes.
>
> Hidden area = area of one circle = π(4)² = ___ π cm²
>
> SA = SA of prism − circle on top + SA of cylinder − circle on bottom
>
> SA of prism = 2(20×20) + 4(20×5) = ___ + ___ = ___ cm²
>   Subtract top circle: ___ cm² − ___ π ≈ ___ cm²
>
> SA of cylinder = 2π(4)² + 2π(4)(10) = ___ π + ___ π = ___ π cm²
>   Subtract bottom circle: ___ π − ___ π = ___ π cm²
>
> Total SA ≈ ___ + ___ ≈ ___ cm²

**Explain:** Why do we subtract the hidden circle from BOTH the prism's surface area and the cylinder's surface area?
