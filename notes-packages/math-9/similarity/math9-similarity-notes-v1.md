## Section 2 — Similar Polygons

**Learning target:** I can determine if two polygons are similar, find corresponding sides and angles, and use proportions to find missing measurements.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition in your own words | One example |
|---|---|---|
| Similar polygons | | |
| Corresponding sides | | |
| Corresponding angles | | |
| Congruent | | |

### Guided Notes

**Two polygons are similar (~) if:**
1. All corresponding angles are _______________, AND
2. All corresponding sides are _______________ (in proportion)

Both conditions must be met. Angles alone or sides alone are NOT sufficient.

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: Similar means the same (congruent)**
>
> Wrong idea: "Similar and congruent both mean the shapes match."
>
> Why it fails: Congruent shapes are identical — same shape AND same size. Similar shapes have the same shape but may be different sizes. All congruent shapes are similar (scale factor = 1), but most similar shapes are NOT congruent.
>
> Two rectangles: one is 2 cm × 4 cm, the other is 4 cm × 8 cm. They are _______________ (same proportions). Are they congruent? ___

---

**The similarity statement:**

When polygons ABCD ~ EFGH, the ORDER of the letters shows the correspondence:
- A corresponds to E → angle A = angle ___
- AB corresponds to EF → AB/EF = ___/___

**Read the similarity statement carefully.** The first vertex of each polygon corresponds.

---

**Worked Example:**

Triangle ABC ~ Triangle DEF, with AB = 6, BC = 9, DE = 4.

> Scale factor = DE/AB = 4/6 = ___
>
> (Scale factor is "new over original" — the image over the original)
>
> EF corresponds to BC:
> EF/BC = ___
> EF = 9 × ___ = ___

**Explain:** Why did we use DE/AB to find the scale factor rather than AB/DE?

_______________________________________________
_______________________________________________

---

**Checking if two polygons are similar:**

Quadrilateral PQRS: P = 70°, Q = 110°, R = 80°, S = 100°. Sides: PQ = 4, QR = 6, RS = 5, SP = 3.

Quadrilateral WXYZ: W = 70°, X = 110°, Y = 80°, Z = 100°. Sides: WX = 8, XY = 12, YZ = 10, ZW = 6.

> Check angles: P = W? ___ Q = X? ___ R = Y? ___ S = Z? ___
>
> Check side ratios: WX/PQ = 8/4 = ___ XY/QR = 12/6 = ___ YZ/RS = 10/5 = ___ ZW/SP = 6/3 = ___
>
> All ratios equal? ___ All angles equal? ___ Therefore similar? ___

---

**Perimeter ratio:**

If two similar polygons have a scale factor of k, then the ratio of their perimeters = ___

*(The perimeter is just the sum of sides, and each side scales by k, so the perimeter also scales by k.)*

Quadrilateral PQRS perimeter = 4 + 6 + 5 + 3 = ___
Quadrilateral WXYZ perimeter = 8 + 12 + 10 + 6 = ___
Ratio: ___/___  = ___ = scale factor ✓

## Section 3 — Criteria for Similar Triangles

**Learning target:** I can use AA, SSS, and SAS similarity criteria to prove that two triangles are similar without measuring all six parts.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition in your own words | One example |
|---|---|---|
| AA (Angle-Angle) similarity | | |
| SSS (Side-Side-Side) similarity | | |
| SAS (Side-Angle-Side) similarity | | |
| Included angle | | |

### Guided Notes

**For triangles only, you do not need to check all six parts.** Three efficient shortcuts exist.

---

**AA — Angle-Angle:**

If two angles of one triangle equal two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.

Why only two angles? Because the third angle is automatically determined:
Third angle = 180° − ___ − ___

If angle A = angle D and angle B = angle E, then angle ___ = angle ___ automatically.
Therefore triangle ABC ~ triangle ___.

---

**SSS — Side-Side-Side:**

If all three pairs of corresponding sides are proportional, the triangles are similar.

a/d = b/e = c/f (all equal to the scale factor k)

**Worked Example — AA:**

Two triangles: Triangle PQR has angle P = 55°, angle Q = 75°.
Triangle XYZ has angle X = 55°, angle Z = 50°.

> In PQR: angle R = 180 − 55 − 75 = ___°
>
> In XYZ: angle Y = 180 − 55 − 50 = ___°
>
> Do any two angles match between the triangles?
> Angle P = Angle X = 55° ✓
> Angle R = Angle Z = 50°? ___ (check: PQR has R = ___°, XYZ has Z = 50°)
>
> Therefore by AA: triangle PQR ~ triangle ___

---

**SAS — Side-Angle-Side:**

If two pairs of sides are proportional AND the **included angle** (the angle between those sides) is equal, the triangles are similar.

---

**Worked Example — SSS:**

Triangle ABC: a = 6, b = 9, c = 12.
Triangle DEF: d = 4, e = 6, f = 8.

> d/a = 4/6 = ___
> e/b = 6/9 = ___
> f/c = 8/12 = ___
>
> All ratios equal? ___ → Triangles similar by ___

---

**Parallel lines create similar triangles:**

When a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, it creates a smaller triangle that is _______________ to the original.

[SVG: Triangle ABC with parallel line DE cutting across both sides, forming smaller similar triangle ADE inside the larger triangle ABC]

Triangle ADE ~ Triangle ___ (by AA, since DE ∥ BC creates equal corresponding angles)

This is the basis for many indirect measurement problems.

---

### Guided Notes

**Scale diagrams** represent real objects at a reduced (or enlarged) size.

Scale = diagram measurement : real measurement

Written as 1 : n, or as a fraction (1/n).

---

**Reading a scale:**

Scale 1 : 5000 means 1 cm on the diagram = ___ cm in real life = ___ m.

**Worked Example — reading a map:**

A map scale is 1 : 25 000.
A road on the map measures 3.2 cm.

Real length = 3.2 × 25 000 = ___ cm = ___ km

---

**Drawing to scale:**

> Diagram length = 50 m / 500 = ___ m = ___ cm
> Diagram width = 25 m / 500 = ___ m = ___ cm

---

**Indirect measurement — shadow method:**

Similar triangles let you measure tall objects by comparing shadows.

[SVG: Shadow method — tall object and measuring stick casting shadows on a ground line, with shadow lengths s₁ and s₂ labelled]

At the same time of day, the sun angle is the same → both triangles are similar (AA).

h/s₁ = 1.5/s₂

---

**Worked Example — Finding tree height:**

At 2 PM, a measuring stick 1.5 m tall casts a 0.9 m shadow. A nearby tree casts a 7.2 m shadow. Find the tree's height.

> h/7.2 = 1.5/0.9
>
> h = 7.2 × (1.5/0.9)
>
> h = 7.2 × ___ = ___ m

**Explain:** Why must we measure both shadows at the same time? What happens to the similar triangles if we measure at different times?

_______________________________________________
_______________________________________________

---

**Mirror method:**

A mirror placed flat on the ground reflects an object's top to an observer's eye. Both triangles (object-to-mirror, observer-to-mirror) are similar because of equal angles of incidence and reflection.

[SVG: Mirror method — tall object on the left, observer on the right, mirror on the ground between them, with distances d₁ and d₂ labelled]

h_object / d₁ = h_observer / d₂

**Alberta context — Measuring a grain elevator:**

A student stands 60 m from a grain elevator and places a mirror at 10 m from herself. Her eye height is 1.6 m. The mirror reflects the top of the elevator.

h/60 = 1.6/10 → h = ___ m
