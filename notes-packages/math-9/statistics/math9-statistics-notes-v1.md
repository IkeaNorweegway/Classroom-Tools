### Guided Notes

**Correlation** describes whether two variables tend to change _______________.

---

**Types of correlation:**

| Type | What it looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Both increase together — points rise left to right | Height and weight |
| **Negative** | One increases as the other decreases — points fall left to right | Hours of TV and test scores |
| **None** | No pattern — points are scattered randomly | Shoe size and test scores |

**Strength of correlation:**

| Strength | What it looks like | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | Points close to a straight line | Clear, consistent relationship |
| Moderate | Points loosely follow a trend | General pattern with more scatter |
| Weak | Points vaguely suggest a direction | Difficult to predict from one variable |

---

**Outlier on a scatter plot:**

An **outlier** is a point that falls _______________ away from the general pattern.

Outliers:
- May indicate a measurement error
- May indicate a genuinely unusual case
- Should be noted but NOT automatically removed

**How does an outlier affect the correlation description?** _______________________________________________

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: Correlation means causation**
>
> Wrong idea: "If two variables have a strong positive correlation, one must be causing the other."
>
> Why it fails: Two things can change together because a third factor drives both, or because the relationship is coincidental. Ice cream sales and drowning rates both increase in summer — but ice cream does not cause drowning. Both are caused by the third variable: hot weather.
>
> Before claiming cause, you must be able to explain a direct mechanism, rule out third variables (confounders), and ideally run a controlled experiment.
>
> Strong correlation is *necessary* but not *sufficient* for causation.

### Guided Notes

**The line of best fit (trend line)** is a straight line drawn to best represent the _______________ of points in a scatter plot.

It is used when there is a _______________ or _______________ correlation (not for no correlation).

---

**Rules for drawing a line of best fit by inspection:**

1. The line passes _______________ the middle of the data (not necessarily through any specific point)
2. Approximately equal numbers of points above and _______________ the line
3. The line should follow the general _______________ of the data
4. The line does NOT need to pass through the _______________ or any particular point

---

**Worked Example — Drawing and finding the equation:**

Using the temperature vs. visitors data from Section 1:

Step 1: Draw the line of best fit through the middle of the data.

Step 2: Choose two points ON the line (not necessarily data points):
Point 1: (20, ___) &nbsp;&nbsp; Point 2: (34, ___)

Step 3: Calculate the slope:
m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) = (___  − ___) / (34 − 20) = ___/___  ≈ ___

Step 4: Find the y-intercept using y = mx + b:
___ = ___(20) + b
b = ___

Step 5: Write the equation:
v = ___t + ___  (v = visitors, t = temperature)

**Explain Step 2:** Why do we choose points ON the line rather than actual data points to find the equation?

_______________________________________________
_______________________________________________

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: The line of best fit must pass through the most points**
>
> Wrong idea: "A good line of best fit connects as many dots as possible."
>
> Why it fails: The line of best fit is a model of the *trend*, not a connect-the-dots. It minimizes the total distance from all points to the line — this means most points will NOT be exactly on it. A line that passes through many points but ignores the overall direction is a worse fit than a line that misses all points but captures the trend.
>
> The goal: equal numbers of points above and below, following the general direction.

---

**The line of best fit in other representations (Rule of Four):**

| Form | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Graph** | The visual trend line through scattered data |
| **Equation** | y = mx + b, where m is the rate of change and b is the starting value |
| **Table** | Points that lie exactly on the line (not data points — predicted values) |
| **Verbal** | "For each degree increase in temperature, approximately ___ more visitors are expected" |

## Section 4 — Interpolation and Extrapolation

**Learning target:** I can use a line of best fit to interpolate and extrapolate values, and explain why extrapolation is less reliable.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition in your own words | One example |
|---|---|---|
| Interpolation | | |
| Extrapolation | | |
| Reliability | | |
| Domain of the data | | |

### Guided Notes

**Interpolation** means predicting a y-value for an x-value _______________ the range of the data.

**Extrapolation** means predicting a y-value for an x-value _______________ the range of the data.

---

**Example — using the temperature/visitors line:**

Data range: temperatures 20°C to 34°C.

| Prediction | x-value | Inside or outside data range? | Interpolation or Extrapolation? | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Visitors at 26°C | 26 | | | |
| Visitors at 22°C | 22 | | | |
| Visitors at 40°C | 40 | | | |
| Visitors at 18°C | 18 | | | |

---

**Worked Example — Interpolation:**

Using the equation v = 10t + (your b-value), predict visitors at 26°C.

> v = 10(26) + ___ = ___ visitors

This is interpolation because 26 is _______________ the data range (20 to 34).

---

**Worked Example — Extrapolation:**

Predict visitors at 40°C.

> v = 10(40) + ___ = ___ visitors

This is extrapolation because 40 is _______________ the data range.

**Why extrapolation is less reliable:**

The trend seen within the data might not continue outside it. At very high temperatures (45°C), the real relationship might change — perhaps fewer people go out because it's dangerously hot, or the park closes. The line of best fit is a model of what was observed, not a law of nature.

---

> **MISCONCEPTION: Extrapolation is as reliable as interpolation**
>
> Wrong idea: "The equation works for data we measured, so it works just as well beyond those boundaries."
>
> Why it fails: The relationship may only hold within the observed range. Beyond the range, additional factors come into play that we did not observe and cannot model from our data. A linear model predicting visitors at −10°C (using our splash pad equation) would give a nonsensical negative number — the linear trend clearly does not extend to freezing temperatures.
>
> Rule of thumb: interpolation is valid as long as the line of best fit fits the data well. Extrapolation should always be noted as "less certain" and the further from the data range, the less reliable.

---

**Evaluating a prediction:**

When you make a prediction, always state:

1. Whether it is interpolation or extrapolation
2. How confident you are and why
3. Any conditions that might make the prediction unreliable
