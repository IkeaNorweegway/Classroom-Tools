## Section 1 — What Is a Polynomial?

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Term | A single number, variable, or product of numbers and variables | 3x², −5x, 7 |
| Polynomial | Expression with one or more terms joined by + or −, whole-number variable exponents | 3x²+2x−1 |
| Monomial | One-term polynomial | 5x² |
| Binomial | Two-term polynomial | 3x−4 |
| Trinomial | Three-term polynomial | x²+3x−5 |
| Coefficient | The numerical factor of a term | In 3x², coefficient = 3 |
| Constant | A term with no variable (degree 0) | 7 in 3x+7 |
| Degree (term) | The exponent on the variable | Degree of 5x² = 2 |
| Degree (polynomial) | Degree of the highest-degree term | Degree of 4x³+2x = 3 |
| Leading coefficient | Coefficient of the highest-degree term | In 4x³+2x²−x+9, leading coeff = 4 |

### Guided Notes

## Section 2 — Like Terms

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Like terms | Terms with the same variable(s) raised to the same exponents | 3x² and −7x² |
| Unlike terms | Terms with different variables or different exponents | 4x and 4x² |
| Simplify | Combine like terms to write in fewest terms | 3x²+7x² = 10x² |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Like terms share same ___ raised to same ___ | **variable; exponent** |
| 3x² and −7x²: like? | Yes — both have **x²** |
| 4x and 4x²: like? | No — different **exponents** |
| 5x and 3y: like? | No — different **variables** |
| 6 and −2: like? | Yes — both **constants** |
| Combine: add/subtract ___ only | **coefficients** |
| 3x² + 7x² = ___ | **10x²** |

**Algebra tile connection:** same **size** and **shape**

**Practice:**
1. Like: {4x², −2x², 5x²} and {3x}. Simplified: **7x² + 3x**
2. Like: {7x, −x} and {4} and {2x²}. Simplified: **2x² + 6x + 4**
3. Constants: 9−5=4. x terms: −3x+x=−2x. x² terms: 2x². Simplified: **2x²−2x+4**

### Retrieval Check — Section 2

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. 4x and −4x like? 4x and 4x² like? | 4x and −4x: **YES** (same variable, same exponent). 4x and 4x²: **NO** (different exponents). |
| 2. 6x²−2x+3x²+x−4 | 9x²−x−4 |
| 3. "5x + 3 = 8x" — error? | Added unlike terms (5x and 3 are unlike — different types). Cannot combine a variable term and a constant. |
| 4. Four-term polynomial → 2x²+3 | e.g., **x²+x²+4−1** or **x²+x²+5−2** |
| 5. (Stretch) 3x and 3y — like? | **No.** The coefficient (3) is irrelevant — the variable parts (x vs y) must match. |

## Section 3 — Adding Polynomials

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sum | The result of adding two or more polynomials | (3x+2)+(x−1) = 4x+1 |
| Standard form | Terms written from highest degree to lowest | 4x²−3x+3 |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Step 1: Remove the ___ | **brackets** (addition changes no signs) |
| Step 2: Identify ___ terms | **like** |
| Step 3: Combine ___ terms | **like** |
| Standard form: from ___ degree to lowest | **highest** |

**(3x²+2x−1)+(x²−5x+4):**

| Step | Work |
|---|---|
| Remove brackets | 3x²+2x−1+x²−5x+4 |
| Group | (3x²+x²)+(2x−5x)+(−1+4) |
| Simplify | **4x²−3x+3** |

Vertical: 4x² | −3x | +3

**Alberta context:** (2x+3)+(x−1) = **3x+2** m

### Retrieval Check — Section 3

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. (4x²−x+2)+(−x²+3x−5) | **3x²+2x−3** |
| 2. (x²+2x)+(3x−4)+(−x²+1) | **5x−3** |
| 3. (5x+2)+(2x−3) | **7x−1 m** |
| 4. Why no sign changes for addition? | Addition does not change any term's sign — removing addition brackets leaves every term exactly as written. |
| 5. (Stretch) a=3, b=2 | a+2=5 → a=**3**; 3−b=1 → b=**2** |

## Section 4 — Subtracting Polynomials

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Difference | Result of subtracting two polynomials | (5x²+3x)−(2x²+x) = 3x²+2x |
| Opposite | Every term's sign flipped | Opposite of (2x²−3x+1) = −2x²+3x−1 |
| Distribute | Apply to every term inside brackets | −(2x²−x) = −2x²+x |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Subtracting means adding its ___ | **opposite** |
| Change sign of ___ term | **every** |
| Opposite of (2x²−3x+1) | **−2x²+3x−1** |

**(5x²+3x−4)−(2x²−x+6):**

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| Distribute negative: 5x²+3x−4 ___ 2x² ___ x ___ 6 | **− 2x² + x − 6** |
| Simplified | **3x²+4x−10** |

**Misconception:** −(2x²−x+6) = **−2x²+x−6** (all signs flip)

## Section 5 — Multiplying a Monomial by a Polynomial

### Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Distribute | Multiply the outside factor by each term inside | 3(x+4) = 3x+12 |
| Expand | Write a product in distributed form | 2x(x+3) → 2x²+6x |
| Product | Result of multiplication | (3x)(2x²) = 6x³ |

### Guided Notes

| Blank | Answer |
|---|---|
| a(b+c) = ___ + ___ | **ab + ac** |
| Multiply monomial by ___ term inside | **every** |
| 3x(2x²−4x+1) | **6x³−12x²+3x** |
| x^m × x^n = x^___ | **m+n** |
| x×x = x^___ (not 2x) | **x²** |
| x²×x³ = x^___ | **x⁵** |

**4x(3x²−2x+5):**
= 4x(3x²) + 4x(−2x) + 4x(5) = **12x³ − 8x² + 20x**

**Alberta context:** 2x(x²+3x−1) = **2x³+6x²−2x** m²

## Unit Synthesis

### Part A — Key ideas

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Degree and how to find it | The degree of the highest-exponent term. Find by inspecting each term's exponent and taking the largest. |
| 2. Why can't you add 3x² and 2x? | They have different exponents (2 vs 1) — unlike terms. Like adding apples to oranges; the variable parts don't match. |
| 3. Most important step in subtraction | Distribute the negative sign to **every term** in the subtracted polynomial — not just the first. |
| 4. Exponent rule for multiplying same-base powers | x^m × x^n = x^(m+n). Example: x²×x³ = x⁵. |
| 5. How many partial products for (ax+b)(cx+d)? | Always **4** — every term in first (2) times every term in second (2) = 2×2 = 4. |

### Part B — Simplify (3x²+x−2)−(x²−4x+1)+2x(x+3)

| Step | Work |
|---|---|
| Subtract | 3x²+x−2−x²+4x−1 |
| Expand 2x(x+3) | +2x²+6x |
| Collect | (3x²−x²+2x²)+(x+4x+6x)+(−2−1) |
| Final | **4x²+11x−3** |
