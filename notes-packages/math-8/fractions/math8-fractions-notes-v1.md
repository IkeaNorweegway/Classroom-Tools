## Section 1: Mixed numbers and estimating
Learning target: I can change between mixed numbers and improper fractions, and estimate an answer before I calculate.

### Vocabulary
- proper fraction :: top is smaller than the bottom; less than 1 :: {3/4}
- improper fraction :: top is bigger than (or equal to) the bottom; 1 or more :: {7/4}
- mixed number :: a whole number and a fraction :: 1{3/4}
- benchmark :: an easy number to compare with :: 0, {1/2}, 1

{7/4} means 7 quarters. Four quarters make 1 whole.

@viz bars bars="7/4" labels="7 quarters" caption="7 quarters = 1 whole and 3 quarters."

{7/4} = [[1]]{3/4}

::: key Mixed number ↔ improper fraction
Improper → mixed: divide. 7 ÷ 4 = 1 remainder 3 → 1{3/4}
Mixed → improper: 2{1/3} = {(2 × 3 + 1)/3} = {[[7]]/3}
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Change each one
a) {11/5} = 11 ÷ 5 = 2 remainder 1 = [[2{1/5}]]
b) 3{1/2} = {(3 × 2 + 1)/2} = [[{7/2}]]
@viz bars bars="7/2" labels="3 and a half"
:::

::: explain
In 3{1/2}, why do we multiply 3 × 2?
(lines 2)
=> Each whole has 2 halves, so 3 wholes have 3 × 2 = 6 halves. Add the extra 1 half to get 7 halves.
:::

#### Estimate first
Is the fraction close to 0, close to {1/2}, or close to 1?

@viz numberline min=0 max=1 step=0.125 every=1 ticklabels="0:0;0.5:½;1:1" points="0.125:1/8;0.4375:7/16;0.9:9/10" caption="1/8 is near 0. 7/16 is near ½. 9/10 is near 1."

::: example Worked Example 2 — Estimate {7/8} × {9/10}
{7/8} is close to 1. {9/10} is close to 1.
So the answer is close to [[1]], but a little less.
:::

::: try
a) {9/4} = [[2{1/4}]]
b) 1{5/6} = [[{11/6}]]
c) {13/3} = [[4{1/3}]]
d) Estimate {1/2} × {11/12}. Close to 0, {1/2} or 1? [[{1/2}]]
:::

::: retrieval
1. Write 2{3/5} as an improper fraction.
=> 13/5
2. Write {17/4} as a mixed number.
=> 4 1/4
3. Draw {5/3} with fraction bars.
(work 2)
=> One whole bar in thirds (all shaded) and a second bar with 2 thirds shaded.
4. Is {1/10} closer to 0, {1/2} or 1?
=> 0
5. ★ Stretch: Without calculating, is {4/5} × {1/9} closer to 0, {1/2} or 1? Explain.
=> Close to 0. 1/9 is close to 0, and taking 4/5 of a tiny amount is still tiny.
:::

## Section 2: Multiplying fractions
Learning target: I can multiply fractions and mixed numbers with a model and with a rule.

### Vocabulary
- product :: the answer to a multiplication :: the product of {1/2} and {1/3} is {1/6}
- of :: in fraction problems, "of" means multiply :: {1/2} of 8 = {1/2} × 8
- simplify :: write a fraction in lowest terms :: {6/12} = {1/2}

#### A whole number times a fraction
3 × {2/5} means 3 groups of {2/5}.

@viz bars bars="2/5;2/5;2/5" caption="3 groups of 2 fifths = 6 fifths."

3 × {2/5} = {2/5} + {2/5} + {2/5} = {[[6]]/5} = [[1{1/5}]]

#### A fraction times a fraction
{2/3} × {3/4} means "{2/3} of {3/4}." Use an **area model**.

::: example Worked Example 3 — {2/3} × {3/4} with an area model
@viz area a=2/3 b=3/4 result="2/3 × 3/4 = [[6/12]]" result2="overlap: [[6]] of [[12]] parts"
Shade {3/4} of the square in columns (blue).
Shade {2/3} of the square in rows (yellow).
The overlap is [[6]] parts out of [[12]].
{2/3} × {3/4} = {6/12} = [[{1/2}]]
:::

::: explain
Why is the bottom number 12?
(lines 2)
=> The square is cut into 3 rows and 4 columns, so there are 3 × 4 = 12 equal parts.
:::

::: key The rule
Multiply the tops. Multiply the bottoms. Simplify.
{a/b} × {c/d} = {(a × c)/(b × d)}
{2/3} × {3/4} = {6/12} = {1/2}
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Multiplying always makes a number bigger."
WHY: Multiplying by a number less than 1 means taking only part of it.
RIGHT: {1/2} × 8 = 4. Half of 8 is less than 8. {2/3} × {3/4} = {1/2}, smaller than both fractions.
:::

::: example Worked Example 4 — Mixed numbers: 2{1/2} × 1{1/3}
Step 1: Change to improper fractions. {5/2} × {4/3}
Step 2: Multiply. {(5 × 4)/(2 × 3)} = {[[20]]/[[6]]}
Step 3: Simplify. {20/6} = {10/3} = [[3{1/3}]]
Estimate check: about 2.5 × 1.3 ≈ 3. ✓
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "2{1/2} × 1{1/3} = (2 × 1) and ({1/2} × {1/3}) = 2{1/6}."
WHY: You forgot to multiply 2 by {1/3} and {1/2} by 1. The estimate (about 3) shows 2{1/6} is too small.
RIGHT: Change to improper fractions first: {5/2} × {4/3} = {20/6} = 3{1/3}.
@viz partial cols="2:2;0.5:1/2" rows="1:1;0.333:1/3" cells="2 × 1 = 2;1/2 × 1 = 1/2;2 × 1/3 = 2/3;1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6" total="2 + 1/2 + 2/3 + 1/6 = 3 1/3" caption="2½ × 1⅓ has four parts. Multiplying only 2 × 1 and ½ × ⅓ misses two of them."
:::

::: explain
Why change mixed numbers to improper fractions before you multiply?
(lines 2)
=> So every part gets multiplied by every part. Multiplying only wholes by wholes and fractions by fractions leaves pieces out.
:::

::: try
a) 4 × {3/8} = [[{12/8} = 1{1/2}]]
b) {1/2} × {3/5} = [[{3/10}]]
c) {4/5} × {5/6} = [[{20/30} = {2/3}]]
d) 1{1/2} × 2{2/3} = [[{3/2} × {8/3} = {24/6} = 4]]
:::

::: retrieval
1. Find {3/4} × {2/5}.
=> 6/20 = 3/10
2. Draw an area model for {1/2} × {1/3}.
(work 3)
=> A square cut into 2 rows and 3 columns; 1 row and 1 column shaded; the overlap is 1 of 6 parts = 1/6.
3. Jay says 6 × {2/3} is bigger than 6. Is he right? Explain.
=> No. 2/3 is less than 1, so 2/3 of 6 is 4, less than 6.
4. Find 1{1/4} × 2.
=> 5/4 × 2 = 10/4 = 2 1/2
5. ★ Stretch: A recipe needs {3/4} cup of oats. You make {2/3} of the recipe. How much oats? Draw it.
=> 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2 cup
:::

## Section 3: Dividing fractions
Learning target: I can divide fractions using a model, common denominators, and the multiply-by-the-reciprocal rule.

### Vocabulary
- quotient :: the answer to a division :: 3 ÷ {1/4} = 12
- reciprocal :: flip a fraction upside down :: the reciprocal of {2/3} is {3/2}
- common denominator :: the same bottom number :: {3/4} and {2/4}

#### How many groups fit?
3 ÷ {1/4} asks: **how many quarters fit into 3?**

::: misconception
WRONG: "Dividing always makes a number smaller, so 3 ÷ {1/4} must be less than 3."
WHY: Dividing asks "how many pieces fit?" Small pieces fit many times.
RIGHT: 4 quarters fit in each whole, so 12 quarters fit in 3. 3 ÷ {1/4} = 12.
@viz groups total=3 piece=1/4 count="3 ÷ 1/4 = [[12]]"
:::

::: example Worked Example 5 — Sharing: {3/4} ÷ 3
{3/4} of a pizza is shared by 3 friends. How much does each get?
@viz bars bars="3/4"
Split {3/4} into 3 equal shares. Each share is [[{1/4}]] of a pizza.
{3/4} ÷ 3 = [[{1/4}]]
:::

::: explain
Why is the answer smaller here, but bigger in 3 ÷ {1/4}?
(lines 2)
=> Here we share a fraction among 3 people, so each gets less. In 3 ÷ 1/4 we count how many small pieces fit, so we get many.
:::

::: example Worked Example 6 — Common denominators: {3/4} ÷ {1/8}
Make the bottoms the same. {3/4} = {[[6]]/8}
Now ask: how many eighths fit in 6 eighths? [[6]]
@viz groups total=3/4 piece=1/8 count="3/4 ÷ 1/8 = [[6]]"
:::

::: key The rule: multiply by the reciprocal
**Keep** the first fraction. **Change** ÷ to ×. **Flip** the second fraction.
{3/4} ÷ {1/8} = {3/4} × {8/1} = {24/4} = [[6]]
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "To divide fractions, flip the first one: {3/4} ÷ {1/8} = {4/3} × {1/8}."
WHY: That gives {4/24} = {1/6}. But the model shows 6 eighths fit in {3/4}.
RIGHT: Keep the first. Flip the one you divide BY. {3/4} × {8/1} = 6.
:::

::: example Worked Example 7 — With mixed numbers: 2{1/2} ÷ {3/4}
Change to improper: {5/2} ÷ {3/4}
Keep, change, flip: {5/2} × {[[4]]/[[3]]}
Multiply: {20/6} = [[3{1/3}]]
Meaning: 3{1/3} pieces of {3/4} fit into 2{1/2}.
:::

::: explain
Check the answer in Worked Example 7 with multiplication. What do you multiply?
(lines 2)
=> 3 1/3 × 3/4 = 10/3 × 3/4 = 30/12 = 2 1/2. ✓ It matches the number we divided.
:::

::: try
a) 4 ÷ {1/3} = [[12]]
b) {2/3} ÷ 2 = [[{1/3}]]
c) {5/6} ÷ {1/3} = [[{5/6} × 3 = {15/6} = 2{1/2}]]
d) 3{1/2} ÷ {1/2} = [[7]]
:::

::: retrieval
1. How many {1/3} cups are in 2 cups?
=> 6
2. Find {4/5} ÷ {2/5}.
=> 2 (how many 2/5 fit in 4/5)
3. Maya wrote {2/3} ÷ {1/6} = {3/2} × {1/6} = {3/12}. What did she do wrong?
=> She flipped the first fraction. Keep 2/3 and flip 1/6: 2/3 × 6 = 4.
4. Find 1{1/2} ÷ {3/8}.
=> 3/2 × 8/3 = 24/6 = 4
5. ★ Stretch: Make up a story for 6 ÷ {3/4}. Solve it.
=> Example: a 6 m board is cut into 3/4 m pieces. 6 × 4/3 = 8 pieces.
:::

## Section 4: Fraction problems
Learning target: I can choose the right operation for a fraction problem and use the order of operations.

### Vocabulary
- order of operations :: the order to do math steps: brackets, exponents, × and ÷ (left to right), + and − (left to right) :: BEDMAS
- reasonable :: makes sense for the problem :: a third of a pizza can't be 3 pizzas

@viz flow steps="Read: what happens?|Multiply (part of) or divide (how many fit / share)?|Estimate|Solve and check"

::: example Worked Example 8 — Which operation?
a) A ribbon is 4{1/2} m long. You cut pieces {3/4} m long. How many pieces? → [[divide]] (how many fit)
4{1/2} ÷ {3/4} = {9/2} × {4/3} = {36/6} = [[6 pieces]]
b) You eat {2/3} of a {3/4} kg bag of grapes. How much do you eat? → [[multiply]] ("of")
{2/3} × {3/4} = [[{1/2} kg]]
:::

::: explain
How can you tell when a fraction problem needs division?
(lines 2)
=> It asks how many pieces or groups fit, or asks to share into equal parts.
:::

::: example Worked Example 9 — Order of operations
{1/2} + {3/4} × {2/3}
× first: {3/4} × {2/3} = {6/12} = [[{1/2}]]
Then +: {1/2} + {1/2} = [[1]]
:::

::: explain
Why don't we add {1/2} + {3/4} first?
(lines 2)
=> Multiplication comes before addition in the order of operations.
:::

::: try
a) A canola field is {5/8} of a quarter section. Half of the field is sprayed today. What part of the quarter section is sprayed? [[{1/2} × {5/8} = {5/16}]]
b) How many {2/3} cup servings are in 6 cups of soup? [[6 ÷ {2/3} = 9 servings]]
c) ({1/2} + {1/4}) ÷ {3/8} = [[{3/4} ÷ {3/8} = 2]]
:::

::: retrieval
1. You have 3 cups of flour. Each batch needs {3/4} cup. How many batches?
=> 3 ÷ 3/4 = 4 batches
2. A recipe needs {2/3} cup of milk. You make 3 batches. How much milk?
=> 3 × 2/3 = 2 cups
3. Find {2/5} × 5 + {1/2}.
=> 2 + 1/2 = 2 1/2
4. Explain how you chose the operation in Question 1.
=> It asks how many 3/4 cups fit in 3 cups, so it is division.
5. ★ Stretch: Write one word problem for {1/2} × {1/3} and one for {1/2} ÷ {1/3}. Which answer is bigger? Why?
=> Answers vary. 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6; 1/2 ÷ 1/3 = 1 1/2. Dividing by a fraction less than 1 gives a bigger answer.
:::

## Concept Reference
::: key Mixed numbers
{7/4} = 1{3/4} (7 ÷ 4 = 1 R 3) · 2{1/3} = {7/3} (2 × 3 + 1 = 7)
:::

::: key Multiply
Tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms, simplify. Change mixed numbers to improper fractions first.
{2/3} × {3/4} = {6/12} = {1/2}. Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes a smaller answer.
:::
@viz area a=2/3 b=3/4 result="2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12"

::: key Divide
Ask: how many fit? 3 ÷ {1/4} = 12.
Rule: keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second. {3/4} ÷ {1/8} = {3/4} × 8 = 6.
Dividing by a fraction less than 1 makes a bigger answer.
:::
@viz groups total=3 piece=1/4 count="3 ÷ 1/4 = 12"

::: key Which operation?
"Of" or "part of" → multiply. "How many fit" or "share equally" → divide. Brackets, then × and ÷, then + and −.
:::

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Multiplying always makes bigger | {1/2} × 8 = 4 |
| 2{1/2} × 1{1/3} = 2{1/6} | Improper first: {5/2} × {4/3} = 3{1/3} |
| Dividing always makes smaller | 3 ÷ {1/4} = 12 |
| Flip the first fraction | Flip the second: {3/4} ÷ {1/8} = {3/4} × 8 |
