## Overview
::: overview
**Big idea:** a 3-D object can be modelled in 2-D three ways, and each model has a job. A net unfolds the object, so its surface area is simply the area of the net: find the area of every face and add. Prisms and pyramids (polyhedrons) unfold into rectangles and triangles; a cylinder unfolds into two circles and a rectangle whose length is the circumference, because the label wraps once around the base. Orthographic views (top, front, right side) give exact plans; an isometric drawing shows three sides in one picture. Surface area covers (square units); volume, from Grade 7, fills (cubic units).
**Sequence:** flat faces vs. curved surfaces, faces/edges/vertices, pyramids and the apex, nets as unfolded solids, cube nets and non-nets, opposite faces (SA1) → covering vs. filling, the box as 3 pairs of faces, the triangular prism, the slant height vs. the height of a pyramid, a gazebo roof with no base, a rectangle-based pyramid with two slant heights (SA2) → unrolling a can's label, SA = 2πr² + 2πrh, a grain bin's side only, a hockey puck (SA3) → three views of a cube building, isometric dot paper, hidden cubes, building from views, choosing a net, views or isometric (SA4).
**Materials:** empty boxes (cereal, tissue, Toblerone-style), a soup can with a paper label, scissors and tape, centimetre grid paper, square tiles or printed squares for cube-net tests, linking cubes (about 10 per pair), isometric dot paper, rulers, calculators. Optional: GeoGebra 3D (geogebra.org/3d) to unfold a net, or Polypad (mathigon.org/polypad) for cube nets and isometric drawing.
**Depth ceiling:** right prisms, right pyramids with a square, rectangle or triangle base, and right cylinders. Slant heights are always given; students never find a slant height with Pythagoras (that link is a ★ conversation only). π ≈ 3.14; answers to one decimal place. Open shapes (no top, no floor, walls only, side only) are in scope; composite objects are Grade 9. Cube buildings are at most 3 × 3 stacks, 3 tall.
**Reading level:** student pages are written at about Grade 3–4 (F&P N–P). The math words (polyhedron, face, edge, vertex, pyramid, apex, net, surface area, square units, lateral face, slant height, height of a pyramid, cylinder, curved surface, circumference, radius, diameter, view, orthographic, isometric, 2-D model) are the learning; each has a picture beside it.
**Notation:** SA for surface area; s for slant height and h for height (pyramid) or the height of a cylinder. Students write the area inside each face of the net before adding. The cylinder rule appears as SA = 2πr² + 2πrh and, in the worked examples, written out: 2 × 3.14 × r × r + 2 × 3.14 × r × h.
:::

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Any 6 squares joined make a cube net" | Hand out 6 paper squares and tape. Pairs build the 2 by 3 block and the cross and fold both. Rule from the notes: a 2 by 2 square never works; 5 in a row overlaps. |
| Opposite faces next to each other | Colour the loop of 4 in the cross. Opposite faces are two steps apart in the loop; the two flaps are opposite each other. |
| "Surface area and volume are the same" | Build a 3 × 2 × 1 box from cubes: 6 cubes inside (cm³), 22 squares to cover it (cm²). Ask "paper or sand?" before every problem. |
| Missing faces on a box (adding only 3) | Write a number on every face of the net first. A box has 3 pairs: SA = 2 × (front + top + side). |
| Using the pyramid's height for a triangle face | Show both on a model: a straw down the middle is h; a strip of tape up the middle of a face is s. Only s lies on the face. s is always longer than h. |
| Covering the base of a roof or the floor of a shed | Read the context aloud and cross out the faces that are not covered before computing. |
| The cylinder's rectangle as long as the diameter | Peel a can label. Lay it beside a ruler; then roll the can one turn along the ruler. The label is "a bit more than 3 diameters" long. |
| Using d in πr² or squaring as × 2 | Ask "radius or diameter?" first and write r × r. The Grade 7 circle slips return here. |
| "The front view shows every cube" | Build the building from the misconception box. Count cubes, then look at the front only. The hidden ones are behind. |
| Isometric lines drawn horizontal | On dot paper only vertical lines and the two slanted directions are allowed. Trace the dots before drawing. |

## Lessons
### Lesson 1 — Polyhedrons and nets (60 min)
DO: Pre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1–Q2 rectangle and triangle area (the faces), Q3–Q4 circle area and circumference from Grade 7 (needed in Lesson 3), Q5 volume (the contrast in Lesson 2), Q6 naming a prism.
SHOW: A box, a pyramid, a can and a ball. "Which ones could you wrap with flat pieces of card, with no bending?"
SAY: "A polyhedron has only flat faces, and every face is a polygon. Prisms and pyramids are polyhedrons. A cylinder, cone and sphere have a curved surface, so they are not."
DO: Fill the faces–edges–vertices table with real models (triangular pyramid row is the blank).
DO: Cut a cereal box along some edges and lay it flat. "This is a net. Fold it back and you get the box."
CUE: Worked Example 1 (square + 4 triangles → square-based pyramid), Worked Example 2 (Toblerone-style net, 5 faces).
WATCH: "Any 6 squares make a cube net." Misconception box, then the paper-square test.
CUE: Worked Example 3 (cross and staircase are nets; 2 by 3 block and 5 in a row are not), Worked Example 4 (opposite faces: A–C, E–F).
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.

### Lesson 2 — Surface area of prisms and pyramids (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: area of a 7 × 4 rectangle; ½ × 6 × 5; 3.14 × 10; name the solid with 2 triangles and 3 rectangles.
SHOW: The 3 × 2 × 1 cube box and its net. "Cubes fill it. Squares cover it."
WATCH: "Surface area and volume are the same." Misconception box: 6 cm³ vs. 22 cm².
SAY: "Surface area is the area of the net. Write the area in each face. Add them all. The answer is in square units."
CUE: Worked Example 1 (gift box 30 × 20 × 10 → 2200 cm²), Worked Example 2 (Toblerone-style box → 254 cm²).
SHOW: A pyramid model with a straw for h and tape for s.
CUE: Worked Example 3 (6 cm base, s = 5 cm → 96 cm²).
WATCH: Using h = 4 cm instead of s = 5 cm. Misconception box (12 cm² vs. 15 cm² per triangle).
CUE: Worked Example 4 (gazebo roof, no base → 24 m²), Worked Example 5 (rectangle base, two slant heights → 122.2 cm²).
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2. Quiz next class.

### Lesson 3 — Surface area of cylinders (60 min)
DO: Mid-unit quiz (25 min) first (SA1–SA2). Then the lesson.
SHOW: Peel the label off a soup can. "What shape is it? How long is it?"
SAY: "The label wraps once around the can. Its length is the circumference, π × d. Its height is the can's height. The net is two circles and that rectangle."
CUE: Worked Example 1 (soup can r = 4 cm, h = 10 cm → 351.7 cm²).
WATCH: "The label is as long as the diameter." Misconception box: roll the can along a ruler.
CUE: Worked Example 2 (grain bin, d = 6 m, side only → 94.2 m²), Worked Example 3 (hockey puck → 150.3 cm²).
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3.

### Lesson 4 — Orthographic and isometric drawings (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: SA of a 4 × 2 × 1 box; one triangle face (b = 4, s = 5); C for d = 6 cm; is a cone a polyhedron?
DO: Pairs build the 6-cube building from the notes with linking cubes. Each partner sketches it from the front, top and right.
SAY: "Each view is flat and shows exact sizes. Three views together are an orthographic drawing. An isometric drawing shows three sides at once on dot paper."
CUE: Worked Example 1 (draw the views: top 3 and 1, front 1, 2, 1, side 1 and 2).
WATCH: "The front view shows every cube." Misconception box: build it and count 5.
CUE: Worked Example 2 (build from the views → 4 cubes), Worked Example 3 (which model: net, orthographic, isometric).
DO: Isometric dot paper: copy the L of 3 cubes (Your turn Q2).
DO: Core worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4. Unit synthesis as review.

## Data and contexts used
- Soup can r = 4 cm, h = 10 cm and juice can r = 3 cm, h = 12 cm are close to real can sizes (a standard soup can is about 6.6 cm across and 10 cm tall); the values are rounded for clean arithmetic.
- Hockey puck: a regulation puck is 3 in (7.6 cm) across and 1 in (2.54 cm) thick; the notes use r = 3.8 cm, h = 2.5 cm (rounded).
- Toblerone-style box: equilateral triangle ends with 4 cm sides and a 3.5 cm height (exact height ≈ 3.46 cm, rounded); the 20 cm length is made up. The quiz box (6 cm sides, 5.2 cm height) is rounded the same way.
- Grain bins on Alberta farms range from about 5 m to over 10 m across; the 6 m by 5 m and 10 m by 8 m bins are plausible, not specific bins.
- Round hay bales (1.6 m across, 1.2 m long) are realistic; net wrap or plastic covers the curved side.
- Gift box, gazebo roofs, sheds, tents, the paper towel tube, the drum, the doorstop and all cube buildings are made up.
- Every slant height is given and chosen to give clean areas; some pairs (h = 4, s = 5 on a 6 cm base) are consistent with Pythagoras, others (the rectangle-based pyramid's 5.7 cm) are rounded.

## Connections
- **Grade 7 Volume and Circles:** volume fills (cm³) and is the contrast for SA; C = πd and A = πr² are the cylinder's two pieces.
- **Triangle Theorems (topic 9):** the slant height, height and half the base edge of a square pyramid form a right triangle (★ link only; slant heights are always given in this unit).
- **Similarity & Dilations (topic 8):** doubling every edge of a box multiplies its SA by 4, the k² rule.
- **Grade 9:** surface area of composite objects, and volume of pyramids, cones and spheres.
