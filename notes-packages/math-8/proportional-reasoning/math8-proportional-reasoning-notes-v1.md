## Section 1: Times as many
Learning target: I can describe one amount as a multiple of the other, both ways.

### Vocabulary
- additive comparison :: compares by subtracting: how many more :: Ava has 8 more cards than Ben
- multiplicative comparison :: compares by dividing: how many times as many :: Ava has 3 times as many cards as Ben
- multiple :: one amount is a number times another :: 12 is 3 times 4
- times as many :: the bigger amount as a multiple of the smaller :: 12 is 3 times as many as 4
- as many as (a fraction) :: the smaller amount as a part of the bigger :: 4 is {1/3} as many as 12

#### Two ways to compare
Ava has 12 hockey cards. Ben has 4.
@viz prtimes rows="Ben:1;Ava:3" value="4" total="4 cards;12 cards" caption="Each block is 4 cards."
**Subtract:** 12 − 4 = 8. Ava has 8 more cards. This is an additive comparison.
**Divide:** 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Ava has 3 times as many cards. This is a multiplicative comparison.
In Grade 8, we use the multiplicative comparison.

#### Turn it around
You can describe either amount as a multiple of the other.
@viz prtimes rows="Ben:1;Ava:3" value="4" down="× 3" up="× 1/3" caption="Down: Ben to Ava. Up: Ava to Ben."
Ben compared to Ava: 4 ÷ 12 = {4/12} = {1/3}. Ben has {1/3} as many cards as Ava.
Ava compared to Ben: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Ava has 3 times as many cards as Ben.
The two numbers 3 and {1/3} flip each other.

::: key Times as many
"A is how many times B?" Find A ÷ B.
12 ÷ 4 = 3, so 12 is 3 times 4.
4 ÷ 12 = {1/3}, so 4 is {1/3} of 12.
The multiple can be a whole number, a decimal or a fraction.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Ava has 12 cards and Ben has 4. So Ava has 8 times as many."
WHY: 8 is how many MORE. That is subtracting. 8 times 4 would be 32 cards, not 12.
@viz prtimes rows="Ben:1;Ava:3" value="4" total="4;12" caption="Three blocks of 4, not eight."
RIGHT: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Ava has 3 times as many. She also has 8 more.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "Ava has 3 times as many as Ben. So Ben has 3 times fewer, or −3 as many."
WHY: Going back, you undo × 3. You divide by 3.
@viz tape parts="1:Ben;3:Ava" values="4" caption="Ben is 1 block out of Ava's 3 blocks."
RIGHT: Ben has {1/3} as many as Ava. 12 × {1/3} = 4.
:::

## Section 2: The constant of proportionality
Learning target: I can find k for a ratio and use it to check equivalent ratios.

### Vocabulary
- proportional :: two amounts that grow by the same factor :: 2:5, 4:10 and 6:15 are proportional
- constant of proportionality (k) :: the number you multiply the first term by to get the second :: in 2:5, k = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5
- unit rate :: k when the first term is ONE of something :: $1.45 for each litre of gas

#### The factor goes across. k goes down.
Sam's juice is 2 cans of concentrate to 5 cans of water. In Grade 7, you used a factor to make equal ratios.
@viz dnl top="0,2,4,6" bottom="0,5,10,15" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water" arrow="1,3,× 3" caption="The factor × 3 makes 2:5 into 6:15."
Now look down each column. How do you get from concentrate to water?
@viz prk top="2,4,6,8" bottom="5,10,15,20" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water" k="× 2.5"
5 ÷ 2 = 2.5. 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5. 20 ÷ 8 = [[2.5]].
Every column has the same down arrow. This number is the **constant of proportionality**, k.
Water = 2.5 × concentrate.

::: key The constant of proportionality
For a ratio a : b, k = b ÷ a. So b = k × a.
k is the same for every equivalent ratio.
k can be a decimal or a fraction: k = 2.5 = {5/2}.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "k is always the bigger number divided by the smaller. For water to rice 4 : 2, k = 2."
WHY: k depends on the order of the ratio. k = second term ÷ first term.
@viz prk top="4,8" bottom="2,4" toplabel="water (cups)" bottomlabel="rice (cups)" k="× 0.5"
RIGHT: For water : rice = 4 : 2, k = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. Rice = 0.5 × water. Check: 0.5 × 8 = 4 ✓. For rice : water = 2 : 4, k would be 2.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — The price of gas
Lee pays $29.00 for 20 L of gas. Find k for litres : dollars.
@viz prk top="1,10,20" bottom="[[$1.45]],[[$14.50]],$29.00" toplabel="gas (L)" bottomlabel="cost ($)" k="× [[1.45]]" one=1
Step 1: The ratio is litres : dollars = 20 : 29.
Step 2: k = 29 ÷ 20 = [[1.45]].
Step 3: Cost = 1.45 × litres. The unit rate is [[$1.45]] per litre.
k is the cost of ONE litre. That is why it is a unit rate.
:::

## Section 3: Missing quantities
Learning target: I can make equivalent ratios for a given k and find a missing quantity using k.

### Vocabulary
- missing quantity :: the amount you need to find :: 40 L of gas costs ? dollars
- scale :: a map ratio of map length to real length :: 1 cm : 5 km
- scale factor :: k for a map or drawing :: 1 cm : 5 km has k = 5 (km for each cm)

#### Build equivalent ratios from k
Say k = 1.5. Pick any first term. Multiply it by 1.5 to get the second term.
@viz prk top="2,4,5,10" bottom="3,6,[[7.5]],[[15]]" toplabel="first (a)" bottomlabel="second (b)" k="× 1.5"
2 : 3, 4 : 6, 5 : [[7.5]] and 10 : [[15]] all have k = 1.5.

#### Find a missing quantity
@viz flow steps="Find k = b ÷ a|Know a? b = k × a|Know b? a = b ÷ k|Check it"
Down the arrow: multiply by k. Back up the arrow: divide by k.

::: example Worked Example 1 — Filling up
Gas costs $1.45 per litre. Lee fills a 40 L tank. What does it cost?
@viz prk top="1,10,40" bottom="$1.45,$14.50,[[$58.00]]" toplabel="gas (L)" bottomlabel="cost ($)" k="× 1.45" one=1
Step 1: k = 1.45 (dollars for each litre).
Step 2: Know a = 40. So b = k × a = 1.45 × 40 = [[58]].
Step 3: The gas costs [[$58.00]].
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — A map of Alberta
A map has the scale 1 cm : 5 km. So k = 5.
@viz dnl top="0 cm,1 cm,7.5 cm,[[12 cm]]" bottom="0 km,5 km,[[37.5 km]],60 km" toplabel="map" bottomlabel="real"
Step 1: Two towns are 7.5 cm apart on the map. Real distance: b = 5 × 7.5 = [[37.5]] km.
Step 2: A lake is 60 km long. Map length: a = b ÷ k = 60 ÷ 5 = [[12]] cm.
Step 3: Check: 5 × [[12]] = 60 ✓.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "The lake is 60 km long. On the map it is 60 × 5 = 300 cm."
WHY: 60 km is the second term (b). You go back up the arrow, so you divide.
@viz prk top="1,12" bottom="5,60" toplabel="map (cm)" bottomlabel="real (km)" k="× 5"
RIGHT: a = 60 ÷ 5 = 12 cm. Check: 12 × 5 = 60 ✓. A 300 cm map would be 3 m wide!
:::

## Section 4: Direct and inverse proportion
Learning target: I can tell direct from inverse proportion and solve problems with each.

### Vocabulary
- direct proportion :: b = k × a. When a doubles, b doubles :: at 100 km/h, 2 h → 200 km
- inverse proportion :: a × b = k. When a doubles, b halves :: 2 workers take 6 h, 4 workers take 3 h
- origin :: the point (0, 0) on a graph :: a direct proportion line goes through it
- slope :: how far a line goes up for each 1 across :: a line with k = 100 goes up 100 for each 1 across

#### Direct proportion: a straight line through (0, 0)
The Bremner family drives from Edmonton to Calgary. That is about 300 km. They drive at 100 km/h.
| Time (h) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distance (km) | 0 | 100 | [[200]] | [[300]] |
@viz coord xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=400 xstep=1 ystep=50 yevery=100 xlabel="time (h)" ylabel="distance (km)" points="0,0;1,100;2,200;3,300:Calgary" line=1
distance = 100 × time. So k = [[100]] km per hour.
The points make a straight line through (0, 0). For each 1 h across, the line goes up [[100]] km.
On a graph, k is the **slope**. You will use slope again in Linear Functions.

#### Inverse proportion: one goes up, the other goes down
2 workers shovel the outdoor rink in 6 h. That is 2 × 6 = 12 worker-hours of shovelling.
@viz prwork cases="2,6;3,4;4,3;6,2" rowlabel="workers" collabel="h" labels="2 × 6 = 12;3 × [[4]] = 12;4 × [[3]] = 12;6 × [[2]] = 12" caption="Each square is 1 worker for 1 hour. There are always 12 squares."
More workers share the same work. So each one works fewer hours.
@viz curve xmin=0 xmax=12 ymin=0 ymax=12 xstep=1 ystep=1 xevery=2 yevery=2 xlabel="workers" ylabel="hours" path="1,12;2,6;3,4;4,3;6,2;12,1" smooth=1 points="2,6;3,4;4,3;6,2" caption="Inverse proportion makes a curve, not a straight line."

::: key Direct or inverse?
**Direct:** b ÷ a is the same every time. b = k × a. Both go up together. Straight line through (0, 0).
**Inverse:** a × b is the same every time. a × b = k. One goes up, the other goes down. A curve.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "2 workers shovel the rink in 6 h. So 4 workers take 12 h."
WHY: That treats it as direct. More workers share the work, so the time goes DOWN.
@viz prwork cases="2,6;4,3" rowlabel="workers" collabel="h" labels="2 × 6 = 12;4 × 3 = 12"
RIGHT: 2 × 6 = 12 worker-hours. 4 workers take 12 ÷ 4 = 3 h. Doubling workers halves the time.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Edmonton to Calgary

## Concept Reference
::: key Times as many
"A is how many times B?" Find A ÷ B. 12 ÷ 4 = 3: 12 is 3 times 4.
Turn it around: 4 ÷ 12 = {1/3}. 4 is {1/3} of 12.
Divide to compare, not subtract.
:::
@viz prtimes rows="Ben:1;Ava:3" value="4" down="× 3" up="× 1/3"

::: key The constant of proportionality
For a : b, k = b ÷ a. Then b = k × a.
k is the same for every equivalent ratio. The order of the ratio matters.
Names for k: unit rate ($ per L, km per h), scale factor (maps), slope (graphs).
:::
@viz prk top="2,4,6" bottom="5,10,15" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water" k="× 2.5"

::: key Missing quantities
Know a: b = k × a. Know b: a = b ÷ k.
Multiply down the arrow. Divide back up.
:::
@viz flow steps="Find k = b ÷ a|Know a? b = k × a|Know b? a = b ÷ k|Check it"

::: key Direct and inverse
Direct: b ÷ a stays the same. b = k × a. A straight line through (0, 0).
Inverse: a × b stays the same. a × b = k. More workers, fewer hours.
:::
@viz prwork cases="2,6;4,3" rowlabel="workers" collabel="h" labels="2 × 6 = 12;4 × 3 = 12"

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| 12 is 8 times 4 (12 − 4 = 8) | Divide: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. 12 is 3 times 4. |
| k is the bigger ÷ the smaller | k = second ÷ first. For 4 : 2, k = 0.5. |
| 2 : 3 = 7 : 8 (add 5) | Use k: 7 × 1.5 = 10.5. 2 : 3 = 7 : 10.5. |
| Know b, so multiply by k | Know b: divide. 60 km ÷ 5 = 12 cm. |
| More workers, more hours | Inverse: 2 × 6 = 4 × 3. Twice the workers, half the time. |
