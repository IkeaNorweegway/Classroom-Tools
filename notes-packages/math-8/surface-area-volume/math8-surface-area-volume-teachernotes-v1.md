## Overview
::: overview
**Big idea:** surface area is the wrapping; volume is the filling. Nets make surface area visible; "layers of the base" makes volume visible.
**Sequence:** nets (SV1) → SA of prisms (SV2) → SA of cylinders (SV3) → volume = area of base × height (SV4).
**Materials:** empty cereal boxes, a small triangular box or a paper tent, cans and paper labels, centimetre cubes, scissors and tape.
**Depth ceiling:** right rectangular prisms, right triangular prisms and right cylinders only. No pyramids, cones, spheres or composite objects (Grade 9). π ≈ 3.14 or the π key.
:::

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| SA = 6 × one face | Cut a cereal box open; lay matching faces on top of each other. |
| Cylinder rectangle = diameter | Peel the label off a can and lay it beside the lid. |
| SA and volume mixed up | Every answer gets units: cm² (wrap) or cm³ (fill). |
| Using d in πr² | Write "r = d ÷ 2" as a first line in every cylinder problem. |
| "Turning changes volume" | Build a 2 × 3 × 4 block with cubes; turn it. Count again. |

## Lessons
### Lesson 1 — Nets (60 min)
DO: Pre-knowledge check. Pairs cut open a cereal box along its edges and lay it flat.
SHOW: The box net; the tent net; the can (two lids and a label).
WATCH: The cylinder's rectangle as long as the diameter. Roll the label back around the can.
DO: Which-net-folds-into-a-cube sort (6–8 printed nets). Exit ticket Section 1.

### Lesson 2 — SA of prisms (60 min)
DO: Warm-up: area of rectangles and triangles.
SHOW: Cereal box table (three pairs). Then the tent with its slant sides.
SAY: "Surface area: add up every face. Use the net so you don't miss one."
CUE: "Where does the 5 m slant come from?" (Connect to the Pythagorean theorem.)
DO: Exit ticket Section 2.

### Lesson 3 — SA of cylinders (60 min)
DO: Warm-up: circumference and area of a circle with r = 3.
SHOW: Can and label. SA = 2πr² + 2πrh.
DO: Hay bale example (plastic wrap). Exit ticket Section 3.

### Lesson 4 — Volume (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: one SA, one net, one circle area.
SHOW: Build a 3 × 2 base with cubes and stack 3 layers.
SAY: "Volume is the area of the base times how many layers."
DO: Grain-bin example; the box-turning misconception with cubes.
DO: Core worksheet; exit ticket Section 4. Quiz next class.
