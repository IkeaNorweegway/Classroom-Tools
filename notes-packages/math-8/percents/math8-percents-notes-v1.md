## Pre-Knowledge Check
1. What does 50% mean? Draw a picture.
2. A shirt is 25% off. What do you think that means?
3. Can a percent be more than 100%? Give an example if you can.

## Section 1: Percents, fractions and decimals
Learning target: I can show a percent on a hundred grid and write it as a fraction and a decimal, even when it is less than 1% or more than 100%.

### Vocabulary
- percent (%) :: "out of 100" :: 45% = 45 out of 100
- hundred grid :: a square split into 100 small squares :: each small square is 1%
- fractional percent :: a percent less than 1% :: 0.5% is half of one small square

**Percent** means "out of [[100]]." A hundred grid shows it.

@viz hundred shaded=45 labels="45 out of 100" caption="45 small squares are shaded."

45% = {45/100} = [[0.45]]

::: key Three ways to write the same amount
| Percent | Fraction | Decimal |
|---|---|---|
| 45% | {45/100} | 0.45 |
| 7% | {[[7]]/100} | [[0.07]] |
| 60% | {60/100} = {[[3]]/5} | [[0.6]] |
To change a percent to a decimal, divide by 100. Move the decimal point [[2]] places to the left.
:::

#### More than 100%
100% is one whole grid. So what is 150%?

::: misconception
WRONG: "A percent can't be more than 100%."
WHY: 100% is one whole. You can have more than one whole. If a town's population grows from 1000 to 1500, it is now 150% of what it was.
RIGHT: 150% means one whole and a half more. 150% = 1.5.
@viz hundred shaded=150 labels="100%;50%" caption="150% is one full grid and half of another."
:::

150% = {150/100} = [[1.5]]

#### Less than 1%
1% is one small square. What is 0.5%?

::: misconception
WRONG: "0.5% is the same as 50%" or "0.5% = 0.5."
WHY: 0.5% is only half of ONE small square. 50% is half the whole grid. 0.5 is half of the whole too.
RIGHT: 0.5% = 0.005. Divide by 100: move the decimal two places left.
@viz hundred shaded=0.5 zoom=1 caption="0.5% is half of one small square."
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Change each one
a) 8% → decimal: 8 ÷ 100 = [[0.08]]
b) 0.25 → percent: 0.25 × 100 = [[25%]]
c) {3/4} → percent: {3/4} = {75/100} = [[75%]]
d) 225% → decimal: 225 ÷ 100 = [[2.25]]
e) 0.3% → decimal: 0.3 ÷ 100 = [[0.003]]
:::

::: explain
Why do we divide by 100 to change a percent to a decimal?
(lines 2)
=> Percent means "out of 100", so 8% = 8 ÷ 100 = 0.08.
:::

::: try
Fill in the table.
| Percent | Fraction | Decimal |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | [[{20/100} or {1/5}]] | [[0.2]] |
| [[40%]] | {2/5} | [[0.4]] |
| [[130%]] | [[{130/100}]] | 1.3 |
| 0.8% | [[{0.8/100} or {8/1000}]] | [[0.008]] |
:::

::: retrieval
1. What does 35% mean?
=> 35 out of 100. 35% = 35/100 = 0.35.
2. Shade 120% on hundred grids. How many grids do you need?
@viz hundred shaded=0 grids=2
=> 2 grids: one full grid and 20 squares of the second.
3. Write 0.7% as a decimal.
=> 0.007
4. Raj says, "You can't score 110% on anything." Give an example where 110% makes sense.
=> A store sold 110% of last year's sales: all of last year's amount plus 10% more.
5. ★ Stretch: Which is bigger: 0.5, 5% or 0.5%? Explain with a picture or numbers.
=> 0.5 = 50%. So 0.5 is the biggest, then 5%, then 0.5%.
:::

## Section 2: Percent of a number
Learning target: I can find a percent of a number, and find what percent one number is of another.

### Vocabulary
- part :: the amount you are finding :: 20 is the part in "20 is 25% of 80"
- whole :: the full amount (100%) :: 80 is the whole
- benchmark percent :: an easy percent to find in your head :: 50% (half), 10% (÷ 10), 1% (÷ 100)

A **percent bar** lines up percents with the numbers they stand for.

::: example Worked Example 2 — Find 25% of $80
@viz percentbar percent=25 top="0%,25%,50%,75%,100%" bottom="$0,[[$20]],$40,$60,$80" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="money"
100% is $80. Split it into 4 equal parts: each part is 25%.
25% of $80 = $80 ÷ 4 = [[$20]]
Or multiply: 0.25 × 80 = [[20]]
:::

::: explain
Why can we divide by 4 to find 25%?
(lines 2)
=> 25% is one quarter of 100%. So 25% of a number is one quarter of it.
:::

::: key Benchmarks you can do in your head
- 50% → divide by [[2]]
- 10% → divide by [[10]]
- 1% → divide by [[100]]
- 5% → half of [[10%]]
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — Use benchmarks: 15% of $60
10% of 60 = [[$6]]
5% of 60 = half of $6 = [[$3]]
15% = 10% + 5% = $6 + $3 = [[$9]]
@viz dnl top="0%,5%,10%,15%,100%" bottom="$0,$3,$6,[[$9]],$60" toplabel="percent" bottomlabel="money"
:::

::: explain
Why does adding 10% and 5% give 15%?
(lines 2)
=> 15% is 10% and 5% put together, and both are percents of the same $60.
:::

#### What percent is it?
::: example Worked Example 4 — 18 out of 24 shots went in. What percent?
Write a fraction: {18/24}
Change to a decimal: 18 ÷ 24 = [[0.75]]
Change to a percent: 0.75 × 100 = [[75%]]
:::

::: try
a) 50% of 46 = [[23]]
b) 10% of 250 = [[25]]
c) 30% of 70 = [[21]]
d) 1% of 900 = [[9]]
e) A team won 12 of its 16 games. What percent did it win?
(work 2)
=> 12 ÷ 16 = 0.75 = 75%
:::

::: retrieval
1. Find 10% of $85.
=> $8.50
2. Find 20% of 45.
=> 9 (10% is 4.5, double it)
3. Ana got 21 out of 25 on a quiz. What percent is that?
=> 21 ÷ 25 = 0.84 = 84%
4. Explain how to find 5% of a number in your head.
=> Find 10% (divide by 10), then take half.
5. ★ Stretch: 12 is 30% of what number? Use a percent bar.
=> 30% is 12, so 10% is 4 and 100% is 40.
:::

## Section 3: Combined percents
Learning target: I can solve problems where two percents are added, like tax and tip.

### Vocabulary
- GST :: Goods and Services Tax, 5% in Alberta :: GST on $20 is $1
- tip :: extra money given for service, often 15% :: tip on $40 is $6
- subtotal :: the cost before tax or tip :: $40.00
- total :: the final amount you pay :: subtotal + tax + tip

In Alberta, most things have **5% GST**. Some other provinces add a provincial tax too.

::: example Worked Example 5 — Pizza dinner for $40
GST: 5% of $40 = [[$2.00]]
Tip: 15% of $40 = [[$6.00]]
Total = $40 + $2 + $6 = [[$48.00]]
@viz receipt items="Pizza dinner:$40.00;GST (5%):[[$2.00]];Tip (15%):[[$6.00]];Total:[[$48.00]]" title="Pizza Place"
:::

::: explain
Both percents are of the same $40. So you could also find 20% of $40 in one step. Why does that work?
(lines 2)
=> 5% + 15% = 20%, and both are taken from the same subtotal, so 20% of $40 = $8 gives the same extra amount.
:::

::: key Combining percents of the same amount
If both percents are of the **same** number, you can add the percents first.
5% + 15% = [[20%]] of $40 = $8
Total = 100% + 20% = [[120%]] of $40 = 1.2 × 40 = $48
:::

::: example Worked Example 6 — Two taxes
In Saskatchewan, a $30 shirt has 5% GST and 6% PST.
Total tax = 5% + 6% = [[11%]]
11% of $30 = 0.11 × 30 = [[$3.30]]
Total = [[$33.30]]
:::

::: try
a) A $60 jacket in Alberta. GST is 5%. Find the total cost.
(work 2)
=> 5% of 60 = $3. Total = $63.
b) A $25 haircut. Add 5% GST and a 20% tip. Find the total.
(work 2)
=> 25% of $25 = $6.25. Total = $31.25.
:::

::: retrieval
1. What is the GST on a $90 bike lock in Alberta?
=> 5% of $90 = $4.50
2. A $50 meal. Tax 5%, tip 15%. What is the total?
=> 20% of $50 = $10. Total $60.
3. Why can you add 5% and 15% in Question 2?
=> Both percents are of the same $50.
4. Write the total for Question 2 as a percent of $50.
=> 120%
5. ★ Stretch: In Ontario, the tax (HST) is 13%. A $20 movie ticket there costs more than in Alberta. How much more?
=> Ontario: 13% of $20 = $2.60. Alberta: 5% of $20 = $1.00. $1.60 more.
:::

## Section 4: Percent of a percent
Learning target: I can explain why a percent change after another percent change is not the same as adding the percents.

### Vocabulary
- increase :: goes up :: a price goes from $100 to $110
- decrease (discount) :: goes down :: 20% off
- original :: the starting amount :: $100

::: misconception
WRONG: "A town grows 10% one year and 15% the next. That is 25% in all."
WHY: The 15% is taken from a bigger number, because the town already grew.
RIGHT: Start with 1000 people. +10%: 1000 + 100 = 1100. +15% of 1100: 1100 + 165 = 1265. That is a 26.5% increase, not 25%.
@viz bar data="Start:1000;Year 1:1100;Year 2:1265" values=1 ymin=0 ymax=1400 ystep=200 title="Town population"
:::

::: example Worked Example 7 — Sale, then more off
A $100 hoodie at West Edmonton Mall is 20% off. Then the store takes 20% off the sale price.
First sale: 20% of $100 = $20. New price = [[$80]]
Second sale: 20% of $80 = [[$16]]. New price = [[$64]]
Total discount = $100 − $64 = $36. That is [[36%]], not 40%.
@viz flow steps="$100|−20% → $80|−20% of $80 → [[$64]]"
:::

::: explain
Why is the second discount only $16, not $20?
(lines 2)
=> The second 20% is taken from $80, not $100. 20% of a smaller number is smaller.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "A price goes down 20%, then up 20%. It is back where it started."
WHY: The 20% increase is taken from the smaller, lower price.
RIGHT: $100 → −20% → $80 → +20% of $80 = $16 → $96. It ends lower than it started.
:::

::: example Worked Example 8 — Using multipliers
Up 10% means × [[1.10]]. Down 20% means × [[0.80]].
$50 up 10%, then down 20%: 50 × 1.10 × 0.80 = [[$44]]
:::

::: explain
Why does "down 20%" mean multiply by 0.80?
(lines 2)
=> After a 20% discount you pay the 80% that is left. 80% = 0.80.
:::

::: try
a) A $200 bike is 25% off, then 10% off the sale price. What is the final price?
(work 3)
=> 200 × 0.75 = 150. 150 × 0.90 = $135.
b) Is that the same as 35% off? Explain.
(work 2)
=> No. 35% off $200 would be $130. The second discount was taken from $150, so you save less.
:::

::: retrieval
1. $100 goes up 10%, then up 10% again. What is the final amount?
=> 100 → 110 → 121. $121.
2. Why isn't the answer to Question 1 $120?
=> The second 10% is of $110, which is $11, not $10.
3. What do you multiply by for "up 25%"? For "down 25%"?
=> × 1.25 and × 0.75
4. A $40 game is 50% off, then 50% off again. Is it free? Explain.
=> No. $40 → $20 → $10. The second 50% is half of $20.
5. ★ Stretch: Canola prices drop 10% one month and rise 10% the next. Are they back where they started? Show with a starting price of $600 per tonne.
=> 600 × 0.9 = 540. 540 × 1.1 = 594. No, $6 lower than the start.
:::

## Unit Synthesis
1. Show 125% three ways: a picture, a fraction and a decimal.
=> Picture: one full hundred grid + 25 squares. 125/100 (= 5/4). 1.25.
2. Explain two ways to find 15% of $80.
=> 0.15 × 80 = 12, or 10% ($8) + 5% ($4) = $12.
3. When can you add two percents? When can't you? Give an example of each.
=> You can add them when both are percents of the same amount (tax + tip on one bill). You can't when the second percent is of a new amount (two sales in a row).

## Concept Reference
::: key Percent means "out of 100"
45% = {45/100} = 0.45
Percent → decimal: ÷ 100. Decimal → percent: × 100.
150% = 1.5 (more than one whole). 0.5% = 0.005 (half of one percent).
:::
@viz hundred shaded=150 labels="100%;50%"

::: key Percent of a number
25% of 80 = 0.25 × 80 = 20
Benchmarks: 50% → ÷ 2 · 10% → ÷ 10 · 1% → ÷ 100 · 5% → half of 10%
What percent? part ÷ whole × 100: 18 out of 24 → 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 = 75%
:::
@viz percentbar percent=25 top="0%,25%,50%,75%,100%" bottom="0,20,40,60,80"

::: key Combined percents (same amount)
Tax 5% + tip 15% on $40 = 20% of $40 = $8. Total $48 = 120% of $40.
:::

::: key Percent of a percent (new amount)
Do one change at a time. $100 → −20% → $80 → −20% → $64 (36% off, not 40%).
Up 10% = × 1.10. Down 20% = × 0.80.
:::

| 0.5% = 0.5 | 0.5% = 0.005. Divide by 100. |
| Up 10%, then up 15% = up 25% | The second percent is of a bigger number: 1.10 × 1.15 = 1.265, up 26.5%. |
| Down 20%, then up 20% = no change | $100 → $80 → $96. |
