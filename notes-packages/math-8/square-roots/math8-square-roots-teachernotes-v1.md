## Overview
::: overview
**Big idea:** a square number is an area; its square root is the side. Every lesson goes back to the tile picture.
**Sequence:** perfect squares (SQ1) → square roots (SQ2) → estimating (SQ3) → problems (SQ4). Quiz after Lesson 4. Exit ticket at the end of each lesson.
**Materials:** square tiles or grid paper, calculators (Lesson 3 only), the concept reference sheet.
**Depth ceiling:** whole numbers only, principal root only. Square roots of fractions and decimals wait for Grade 9.
:::

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| 5² = 10 (squaring as doubling) | Build 5 × 2 and 5 × 5 with tiles side by side. Only one is a square. |
| √16 = 8 (root as half) | Check every root by multiplying it by itself. Make "check it" a class habit. |
| √20 ≈ 10 or "halfway" (4.5 as a rule) | Always name the two perfect squares first, then ask which is closer. |
| Dividing area by 4 to find a side | Separate area (inside) from perimeter (around). Draw the square. |

## Lessons
### Lesson 1 — Perfect squares (60 min)
DO: Warm-up: tiles on desks. "Make a square with 9 tiles. With 10. With 16." Students sort numbers into "makes a square" and "doesn't."
SHOW: Notes Section 1 squares picture. Fill in 3 × 3 = 9 together.
SAY: "Squared means the number times itself. The little 2 says use the number twice."
WATCH: Students writing 5² = 10. Use the misconception box before Worked Example 1.
CUE: After Worked Example 2: "How did the rectangles prove 12 is not a perfect square?" Take two answers before students write.
DO: Table of squares 1–15. Students keep it; it is used all unit.
DO: Exit ticket Section 1.

### Lesson 2 — Square roots (60 min)
DO: Retrieval warm-up: 7², 9², 12², is 50 a perfect square?
SHOW: The 7 × 7 picture. "If I tell you the area is 49, what is the side?"
SAY: "The square root undoes squaring. Area in, side out."
WATCH: √16 = 8. Have the student check: 8 × 8. Let the check do the correcting.
CUE: Factor pairs: "Which pair is special?" Prime factors: "Can you split these into two equal teams?"
DO: Exit ticket Section 2.

### Lesson 3 — Estimating (60 min)
DO: Warm-up: √25, √36, √49, and "Is there a whole number whose square is 30?"
SHOW: The number line from 3 to 6 with √16, √20, √25.
SAY: "Find the perfect squares on each side first. Then ask: which one is it closer to?"
WATCH: "Halfway" as a rule. √20 is not exactly 4.5 and √24 is not 4.5 either. Compare with a calculator.
DO: Calculators for Worked Example 6. Discuss why the decimal keeps going (no formal "irrational" yet).
DO: Exit ticket Section 3.

### Lesson 4 — Problems (60 min)
DO: Warm-up from all three sections (interleaved): 13², √144, estimate √70.
SHOW: The quarter-section example. Ask first: "Is this a square-root problem or a squaring problem? How do you know?"
CUE: "Does the answer make sense? Check side × side."
DO: Start the Core worksheet. Fast finishers move to Challenge.
DO: Exit ticket Section 4. Quiz next class.

## Connections
- **Pythagorean Theorem** uses squares and square roots in every problem. Teach this topic first.
- **Grade 9:** square roots of fractions and decimals, and the idea that some square roots never end.
- Home practice: two parent sheets (perfect squares and roots, estimating) on the parent page.
