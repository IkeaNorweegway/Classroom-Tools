## Section 1: Perfect squares
Learning target: I can show a perfect square as a square of tiles, and find the square of a number.

### Vocabulary
- square (a number) :: multiply a number by itself :: 4² = 4 × 4 = 16
- perfect square :: a number you get when you square a whole number :: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36
- exponent :: the small raised number; it tells how many times to multiply :: in 5², the exponent is 2

Take some square tiles. Can you make a square with 9 tiles? Yes: 3 rows of 3.

@viz squares shapes="1x1;2x2;3x3;4x4;5x5" labels="1 × 1 = 1;2 × 2 = 4;3 × 3 = [[9]];4 × 4 = [[16]];5 × 5 = [[25]]" caption="Each picture is a square. Count the tiles in each one."

A number of tiles that makes a perfect square is called a [[perfect square]].

We write 3 × 3 as 3². We say "three squared."

::: key Squaring a number
To **square** a number, multiply it by [[itself]].
7² = 7 × 7 = [[49]]
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "5² = 10, because the 2 means times 2."
WHY: The 2 is an exponent. It says how many 5s to multiply. It does not say "times 2."
RIGHT: 5² = 5 × 5 = 25. Draw it: a 5 by 5 square has 25 tiles, not 10.
@viz squares shapes="5x2;5x5" labels="5 × 2 = 10 (not a square!);5 × 5 = 25" cell=14
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — Is 36 a perfect square?
Try to make a square with 36 tiles.
6 rows of 6 works. 6 × 6 = 36.
So 36 [[is]] a perfect square.
@viz squares shapes="6x6" labels="6 × 6 = 36"
:::

::: explain
Why does 6 × 6 make a square, but 4 × 9 does not?
(lines 2)
=> Both sides are the same length (6 and 6). A 4 by 9 rectangle has different side lengths, so it is not a square.
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Is 12 a perfect square?
List the ways to make a rectangle with 12 tiles.
@viz squares shapes="1x12;2x6;3x4" caption="None of these rectangles has equal sides."
No rectangle has equal sides. So 12 is [[not]] a perfect square.
:::

::: explain
How do the rectangles prove that 12 is not a perfect square?
(lines 2)
=> Every way to arrange 12 tiles makes a rectangle with two different side lengths. There is no square.
:::

::: try
a) 8² = [[64]]
b) 10² = [[100]]
c) 1² = [[1]]
d) 12² = [[144]]
e) Circle the perfect squares: 20 · 49 · 50 · 81 · 99 · 100
=> 49 (7 × 7), 81 (9 × 9), 100 (10 × 10)
:::

## Section 2: Square roots
Learning target: I can find the square root of a perfect square, and explain what it means.

### Vocabulary
- square root :: the number you square to get a number; the side of the square :: √49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49
- radical sign :: the symbol √ :: √81 means "the square root of 81"
- factor :: a number that divides into another number with no remainder :: 4 is a factor of 36

A square root **undoes** squaring.

@viz squares shapes="7x7" labels="area = 49 tiles, side = [[7]]" cell=13 caption="The area is 49. The square root is the length of one side."

If the area of a square is 49, each side is [[7]]. So √49 = 7.

::: key Square root
√(area of a square) = length of a [[side]]
√64 = 8, because 8 × 8 = [[64]]
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "√16 = 8, because the square root is half."
WHY: Check it: 8 × 8 = 64, not 16. Half is ÷ 2. Square root asks a different question.
RIGHT: √16 asks "what number times itself makes 16?" 4 × 4 = 16, so √16 = 4.
@viz squares shapes="4x4;8x8" labels="√16 = 4 ✓;8 × 8 = 64 ✗" cell=10
:::

#### Using factors to find a square root
A perfect square has one factor pair where both factors are the [[same]].

::: example Worked Example 3 — Find √36 using factor pairs
List the factor pairs of 36:
1 × 36 · 2 × 18 · 3 × 12 · 4 × 9 · 6 × 6
The pair with two equal factors is [[6 × 6]].
So √36 = [[6]].
@viz flow steps="List factor pairs|Find the pair with equal factors|That factor is the square root"
:::

::: explain
Why is 6 the square root of 36, but 4 is not, even though 4 is a factor?
(lines 2)
=> 4 pairs with 9, and 4 ≠ 9. The square root pairs with itself: 6 × 6 = 36.
:::

#### Using prime factors (for bigger numbers)
Break the number into prime factors. If you can split them into **two equal groups**, it is a perfect square.

::: example Worked Example 4 — Is 144 a perfect square?
144 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3
Make two equal groups: (2 × 2 × 3) × (2 × 2 × 3)
Each group = [[12]]. So 144 = 12 × 12 and √144 = [[12]].
:::

::: explain
How do two equal groups of prime factors show a perfect square?
(lines 2)
=> Each group is the side length. Two equal groups means side × side, which is a square.
:::

::: try
a) √81 = [[9]]
b) √121 = [[11]]
c) √1 = [[1]]
d) √400 = [[20]]
e) A square rug has an area of 169 cm². How long is one side?
(work 2)
=> √169 = 13 cm, because 13 × 13 = 169.
:::

::: connection
Squaring and square roots undo each other, like adding and subtracting. What is √(9²)? Why?
=> 9. Squaring 9 gives 81, and the square root of 81 brings you back to 9.
:::

::: retrieval
1. What does √49 mean in words?
=> The number that, times itself, makes 49. It is 7.
2. √100 = ?
=> 10
3. Ana says √36 = 18. Explain her mistake.
=> She divided by 2. Check: 18 × 18 = 324, not 36. √36 = 6.
4. A square photo has an area of 225 cm². How long is each side?
=> √225 = 15 cm.
5. ★ Stretch: Use prime factors to decide if 72 is a perfect square. (72 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3)
=> No. There are three 2s, so the factors cannot be split into two equal groups. 72 is not a perfect square.
:::

## Section 3: Estimating square roots
Learning target: I can estimate the square root of a number that is not a perfect square.

### Vocabulary
- benchmark :: a number you know well that helps you estimate :: 16 and 25 are benchmarks for √20
- estimate :: a close guess you can explain :: √20 is about 4.5
- approximate :: close, but not exact :: a calculator shows √20 ≈ 4.472…

Not every number is a perfect square. But we can still estimate its square root.

::: misconception
WRONG: "√20 is about 10, because 10 is half of 20."
WHY: 10 × 10 = 100. That is way bigger than 20.
RIGHT: Use the perfect squares next to 20. 16 < 20 < 25, so √20 is between 4 and 5.
:::

::: example Worked Example 5 — Estimate √20
Step 1: Find the perfect squares on each side. 16 < 20 < 25
Step 2: Take their square roots. 4 < √20 < 5
Step 3: Which is closer? 20 is 4 away from 16 and 5 away from 25. So √20 is a bit closer to [[4]].
@viz numberline min=3 max=6 step=0.1 every=1 points="4:√16;4.47:√20;5:√25" caption="√20 sits between 4 and 5, a little closer to 4."
Estimate: √20 ≈ [[4.4 or 4.5]]
Check: 4.5 × 4.5 = 20.25. Very close!
:::

::: explain
Why is √20 between 4 and 5?
(lines 2)
=> 20 is between the perfect squares 16 and 25, and √16 = 4, √25 = 5.
:::

::: example Worked Example 6 — Use a calculator
Press √ then 20. The screen shows 4.472135955…
The digits keep going. The calculator shows only some of them.
So the answer is an [[approximate]] value. We round: √20 ≈ 4.47
:::

::: explain
Why can't a calculator show the exact value of √20?
(lines 2)
=> The decimal never ends, so the screen can only show some of the digits. Any answer it shows is rounded.
:::

::: try
Between which two whole numbers is each square root?
a) √30 is between [[5]] and [[6]]
b) √50 is between [[7]] and [[8]]
c) √90 is between [[9]] and [[10]]
d) Estimate √60 to one decimal place. Then check with a calculator.
(work 2)
=> 49 < 60 < 64, so 7 < √60 < 8, closer to 8. Estimate 7.7. Calculator: 7.745… ≈ 7.7
:::

@viz numberline min=0 max=10 keypoints="5.48:√30;7.07:√50;9.49:√90" caption="Where do they go? Mark √30, √50 and √90."

::: retrieval
1. Between which two whole numbers is √40?
=> 6 and 7 (36 < 40 < 49)
2. Which is closer to √40: 6 or 7? Why?
=> 6. 40 is 4 away from 36 but 9 away from 49.
3. Leo says √80 ≈ 40. Show why that cannot be right.
=> 40 × 40 = 1600, not 80. √80 is between 8 and 9 (64 and 81), very close to 9.
4. Name a whole number whose square root is between 9 and 10.
=> Any number from 82 to 99, for example 90.
5. ★ Stretch: Why does √99 come out very close to 10?
=> 99 is only 1 less than 100, and √100 = 10. So √99 is just under 10 (about 9.95).
:::

## Section 4: Square root problems
Learning target: I can use squares and square roots to solve real problems.

### Vocabulary
- area :: the space inside a flat shape, in square units :: a 3 m by 3 m floor has an area of 9 m²
- side length :: the length of one side of a square :: √(area)

Many problems give the **area** of a square and ask for the **side**. Use the square root.

@viz flow steps="Area of a square|Take the square root|Side length|Check: side × side = area"

::: example Worked Example 7 — A quarter section of farmland
In Alberta, a "quarter section" of farmland is a square. Its area is about 640 000 m².
How long is one side?
@viz squares shapes="8x8" labels="area 640 000 m²" cell=12
Side = √640 000 = [[800]] m
Check: 800 × 800 = [[640 000]] ✓
:::

::: explain
Why do we use a square root here, not divide by 4?
(lines 2)
=> Area is side × side. To undo "side × side" we need the square root. Dividing by 4 would give a perimeter question, not an area one.
:::

::: example Worked Example 8 — Fencing a square garden
A square garden has an area of 50 m². About how much fence goes around it?
Side = √50 ≈ [[7.1]] m (49 < 50 < 64, very close to 7)
Fence = 4 sides ≈ 4 × 7.1 = [[28.4]] m
:::

::: explain
Why did we take the square root first, then multiply by 4?
(lines 2)
=> The square root gives one side. A fence goes around all 4 equal sides.
:::

::: try
a) A square tile has an area of 81 cm². What is its side length?
(work 2)
=> √81 = 9 cm
b) A square skating rink in a backyard has an area of 200 m². Estimate one side to one decimal place.
(work 2)
=> 196 < 200 < 225, so √200 is just over 14. √200 ≈ 14.1 m.
c) Is a square with an area of 45 m² bigger or smaller than a 7 m by 7 m square? Explain.
(work 2)
=> 7 × 7 = 49 m². 45 < 49, so the 45 m² square is smaller. Its side is √45 ≈ 6.7 m.
:::

::: retrieval
1. A square poster has an area of 144 cm². How long is each side?
=> √144 = 12 cm
2. A square patio has an area of 30 m². Estimate one side.
=> Between 5 and 6 (25 and 36), about 5.5 m (√30 ≈ 5.48)
3. What is the perimeter of a square with an area of 64 m²?
=> Side = 8 m, so perimeter = 4 × 8 = 32 m
4. Why do we check by multiplying side × side?
=> Squaring the side should give back the area. If it does, the answer is right.
5. ★ Stretch: One square has double the area of another square. Does it have double the side length? Try it with an area of 16 and an area of 32.
=> No. Side of 16 is 4. Side of 32 is √32 ≈ 5.7, not 8. Doubling the area does not double the side.
:::

## Unit Synthesis
1. Explain the difference between squaring a number and finding its square root. Use a picture.
=> Squaring: side → area (5 → 25). Square root: area → side (25 → 5). Picture: a 5 by 5 square.
2. Explain how to estimate √70 without a calculator.
=> 64 < 70 < 81, so √70 is between 8 and 9, closer to 8. About 8.4.
3. Make up a problem that uses a square root. Solve it.
=> Answers vary. Example: a square room has an area of 36 m². Each wall is √36 = 6 m long.

=> Answers vary.

## Concept Reference
::: key Squaring
A **square number** is a number times itself. 6² = 6 × 6 = 36.
The exponent 2 means "use the number twice as a factor." It does **not** mean × 2.
:::

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n² | 1 | 4 | 9 | 16 | 25 | 36 | 49 | 64 | 81 | 100 | 121 | 144 | 169 | 196 | 225 |

::: key Square root
√ asks: "What number times itself makes this?"
√49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49.
Picture: the **area** of a square is inside the √. The **side** is the answer.
:::

::: key Is it a perfect square?
- Make a square with tiles, or
- find a factor pair with two equal factors (36 = 6 × 6), or
- split the prime factors into two equal groups (144 = (2·2·3)(2·2·3)).
:::

::: key Estimating
1. Find the perfect squares on each side: 16 < 20 < 25
2. Take their roots: 4 < √20 < 5
3. Decide which it is closer to: 20 is closer to 16, so √20 ≈ 4.5
A calculator gives an **approximate** value: √20 ≈ 4.47
:::
