## Overview
::: overview
**Big idea:** the theorem is about areas. The two squares on the legs add up to the square on the hypotenuse. Keep the squares picture on the board all unit.
**Sequence:** squares on a right triangle (PY1) → finding the hypotenuse (PY2) → finding a leg (PY3) → testing and problems (PY4).
**Materials:** grid paper and scissors (cut out the 3, 4 and 5 squares), string with knots for a 3-4-5 triangle, measuring tapes, calculators.
**Depth ceiling:** right triangles only, answers to one decimal place. No formal proof; no 3-D diagonals (those come in Grade 9 problems).
**Prerequisite:** Squares and Square Roots.
:::

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| Using a² + b² = c² on any triangle | Test a 4, 5, 6 triangle in class. Let the numbers fail. |
| c is "the side labelled c" | Find the right angle first, every time. Circle it. |
| Adding squares to find a leg | "Should a leg be longer than the hypotenuse?" Estimate first. |
| c = a + b | Back to the squares picture: areas add, not sides. |

## Lessons
### Lesson 1 — The squares (60 min)
DO: Pre-knowledge check. Students draw a 3-4-5 right triangle on grid paper and build a square on each side.
SHOW: Count the squares: 9 + 16 = 25. Repeat with 6-8-10.
SAY: "The two small squares fill the big square exactly."
DO: Test a 4, 5, 6 triangle: 16 + 25 ≠ 36. Exit ticket Section 1.

### Lesson 2 — Finding the hypotenuse (60 min)
DO: Warm-up: 7², 12², √81, √144.
SHOW: The four-step flowchart; the TV example (TVs are sold by the diagonal).
DO: Measure a desk or door and predict its diagonal; then measure to check.
DO: Exit ticket Section 2.

### Lesson 3 — Finding a leg (60 min)
DO: Warm-up: two hypotenuse questions.
SHOW: A real ladder or a metre stick leaning on a wall. "Which side is the hypotenuse now?"
SAY: "To find a leg, subtract. Then check: the leg must be shorter than c."
DO: Exit ticket Section 3.

### Lesson 4 — Testing and problems (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: one hypotenuse, one leg, one "which side is c?"
SHOW: The carpenter's 3-4-5 check with a knotted string.
DO: Core worksheet; triples challenge. Exit ticket Section 4. Quiz next class.

## Assessment

## Connections
- **Squares and Square Roots** is the prerequisite: every problem ends with a square root.
- **Surface Area:** the slant side of a triangular prism is often found with the theorem.
- **Grade 9:** circle geometry (tangents and chords) and 3-D diagonals.
