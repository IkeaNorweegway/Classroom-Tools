## Pre-Knowledge Check
1. A recipe uses 2 cups of flour for every 1 cup of milk. What do you think "for every" means?
2. Which is a better deal: 3 granola bars for $2, or 5 for $3? How did you decide?
3. A car drives 100 km in 1 hour. What does "km per hour" mean?

## Section 1: Ratios
Learning target: I can write a ratio three ways and tell part-to-part from part-to-whole.

### Vocabulary
- ratio :: compares two or more amounts of the same kind :: 1 can of juice to 3 cans of water, 1:3
- part-to-part :: compares one part to another part :: juice : water = 1 : 3
- part-to-whole :: compares one part to the total :: juice : all = 1 : 4
- term :: each number in a ratio :: in 1:3, the terms are 1 and 3

To make frozen juice, mix **1 can of concentrate** with **3 cans of water**.

@viz tape parts="1:concentrate;3:water" caption="Each box is one can."

We can write the ratio of concentrate to water three ways:
1 to 3 · 1 : 3 · {1/3}

::: key Part-to-part or part-to-whole?
concentrate : water = 1 : 3 (part to part)
concentrate : all the juice = 1 : [[4]] (part to whole)
As a fraction, the concentrate is {1/[[4]]} of the jug. As a percent, that is [[25%]].
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "The ratio 1:3 means 1 out of 3 cans is concentrate."
WHY: 1:3 is part to part. There are 1 + 3 = 4 cans in all.
RIGHT: 1 out of 4 cans is concentrate. Part-to-whole: 1:4 or {1/4} or 25%.
:::

::: example Worked Example 1 — A three-term ratio
A trail mix has 4 cups of nuts, 2 cups of raisins and 1 cup of chocolate chips.
nuts : raisins : chips = [[4 : 2 : 1]]
Total cups = [[7]]
Raisins are {[[2]]/[[7]]} of the mix.
@viz tape parts="4:nuts;2:raisins;1:chips"
:::

::: explain
Why do we add all three terms to get the whole?
(lines 2)
=> The whole is everything in the mix, so we add every part: 4 + 2 + 1 = 7.
:::

::: try
A class has 12 girls and 15 boys.
a) girls : boys = [[12 : 15]] (or [[4 : 5]])
b) boys : whole class = [[15 : 27]]
c) What fraction of the class is girls? [[{12/27} = {4/9}]]
:::

::: retrieval
1. Write "3 red to 5 blue" in two other ways.
=> 3:5 and 3/5
2. A bag has 3 red and 5 blue marbles. What is the ratio of red to all marbles?
=> 3:8
3. Kate says the ratio 2:5 (sugar to flour) means 2 out of 5 cups are sugar. Is she right?
=> No. There are 2 + 5 = 7 cups in all, so 2 out of 7 are sugar.
4. A drink is 1 part syrup to 4 parts water. What percent is syrup?
=> 1 out of 5 = 20%
5. ★ Stretch: Write a ratio that can be written as a percent, and explain why "60 km per hour" cannot be written as a percent.
=> 1 part syrup out of 5 parts = 20%. A ratio compares the same kind of thing, so it can be "out of 100". 60 km per hour compares two different units (km and hours), so there is nothing to be "out of 100".
:::

## Section 2: Equal ratios and proportions
Learning target: I can make equal ratios with a table or a double number line, and use them to solve problems.

### Vocabulary
- equivalent ratios :: ratios that show the same comparison :: 1:3 = 2:6 = 3:9
- proportion :: a statement that two ratios are equal :: 1/3 = 2/6
- scale factor :: the number you multiply both terms by :: 1:3 × 4 → 4:12

To make **more** juice, keep the same taste. Multiply **both** terms by the same number.

@viz dnl top="0,1,2,3,4" bottom="0,3,6,[[9]],[[12]]" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water" arrow="1,3,×3" caption="Both lines are multiplied by the same number."

| Concentrate (cans) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water (cans) | 3 | 6 | [[9]] | [[12]] |

::: misconception
WRONG: "1:3 and 2:4 are equal. I just added 1 to both."
WHY: Adding changes the taste. In 1:3, water is 3 times the concentrate. In 2:4, water is only 2 times.
RIGHT: Multiply both terms by the same number: 1:3 × 2 = 2:6.
@viz tape parts="2:concentrate;4:water" caption="2:4 is a stronger mix. Water is only double, not triple."
:::

::: example Worked Example 2 — Bannock for the whole class
A bannock recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 1 tablespoon of baking powder. You want to use 12 cups of flour.
Scale factor: 12 ÷ 3 = [[4]]
Baking powder: 1 × 4 = [[4]] tablespoons
So 3 : 1 = 12 : [[4]]
:::

::: explain
Why do we multiply the baking powder by the same number as the flour?
(lines 2)
=> To keep the same ratio (the same recipe), both parts must grow by the same factor.
:::

::: example Worked Example 3 — Solve a proportion
{2/5} = {x/20}
5 × [[4]] = 20, so multiply the top by 4 too: x = 2 × 4 = [[8]]
Check: {8/20} simplifies to {2/5} ✓
:::

::: explain
How can you check that two ratios are equal?
(lines 2)
=> Simplify both, or check that the same scale factor turns one into the other.
:::

::: try
a) 2 : 7 = 6 : [[21]]
b) 5 : 4 = [[25]] : 20
c) {3/8} = {x/40}, x = [[15]]
d) Paint is 2 parts blue to 3 parts white. How much white goes with 10 L of blue?
(work 2)
=> 10 ÷ 2 = 5, so 3 × 5 = 15 L of white.
:::

::: retrieval
1. Write two ratios equal to 3:4.
=> 6:8, 9:12 (any multiples)
2. Is 4:6 equal to 6:8? Explain.
=> No. 4:6 = 2:3 and 6:8 = 3:4.
3. Solve: 5/6 = x/18
=> x = 15
4. A juice is 1:3. Ben makes 3:5. Whose juice is stronger? Use a picture or a table.
=> Ben's. 1:3 has 1 of 4 parts concentrate (25%). 3:5 has 3 of 8 parts (37.5%).
5. ★ Stretch: Fill in the table for 2 cups of rice to 3 cups of water, up to 10 cups of rice. Then plot it as points.
@viz coord xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=16 ystep=2 xlabel="rice" ylabel="water" keypoints="2,3;4,6;6,9;8,12;10,15"
=> (2, 3), (4, 6), (6, 9), (8, 12), (10, 15). The points line up.
:::

## Section 3: Rates and unit rates
Learning target: I can find a unit rate and use it to compare prices or speeds.

### Vocabulary
- rate :: compares two amounts with different units :: $3 for 4 apples; 240 km in 3 h
- unit rate :: a rate for ONE unit :: $0.75 per apple; 80 km/h
- per :: "for each" :: km per hour, dollars per kilogram

A **ratio** compares the same kind of thing (cans to cans). A **rate** compares different kinds of things (km to hours, dollars to grams).

::: example Worked Example 4 — Speed
The drive from Red Deer to Calgary is about 150 km. It takes 1.5 h.
Unit rate = 150 km ÷ 1.5 h = [[100]] km/h
@viz dnl top="0 h,0.5 h,1 h,1.5 h" bottom="0 km,50 km,[[100 km]],150 km" toplabel="time" bottomlabel="distance"
:::

::: explain
What does "100 km/h" mean in words?
(lines 2)
=> The car goes 100 km for every 1 hour of driving.
:::

::: misconception
WRONG: "The bigger box is always the better deal."
WHY: A bigger box costs more too. You need the price for ONE unit to compare.
RIGHT: Find the unit price of each, then compare.
:::

::: example Worked Example 5 — Best buy
Hockey tape: 3 rolls for $6.00, or 5 rolls for $9.00.
3 rolls: $6.00 ÷ 3 = [[$2.00]] per roll
5 rolls: $9.00 ÷ 5 = [[$1.80]] per roll
The [[5-roll pack]] is the better buy.
@viz bar data="3-pack:2.00;5-pack:1.80" values=1 ymin=0 ymax=2.5 ystep=0.5 ylabel="$ per roll" title="Price for ONE roll"
:::

::: explain
Why do we divide the price by the number of rolls?
(lines 2)
=> That gives the price of one roll, so both packs can be compared fairly.
:::

#### Fuel use: L/100 km
Cars in Canada show fuel use as **litres per 100 km**. A truck that uses 12 L/100 km uses 12 L for every 100 km.

::: try
a) 240 km in 3 h = [[80]] km/h
b) $4.50 for 3 kg of potatoes = [[$1.50]] per kg
c) A car uses 8 L/100 km. How much fuel for 300 km? [[24 L]]
d) Which is cheaper: 2 L of milk for $4.40, or 4 L for $6.00? Show the unit prices.
(work 2)
=> 2 L: $2.20 per L. 4 L: $1.50 per L. The 4 L jug is cheaper.
:::

::: retrieval
1. What is the difference between a ratio and a rate?
=> A ratio compares the same kind of thing. A rate compares different kinds (different units).
2. Find the unit rate: 360 km in 4 h.
=> 90 km/h
3. Find the unit price: 6 granola bars for $4.20.
=> $0.70 per bar
4. Mo says the 10 kg bag of dog food for $35 is a worse deal than the 4 kg bag for $16, because $35 is more. Is Mo right?
=> No. 10 kg: $3.50/kg. 4 kg: $4.00/kg. The big bag is cheaper per kg.
5. ★ Stretch: Why can a ratio like 1:4 be written as 25%, but a rate like 90 km/h cannot be written as a percent?
=> A percent is a part out of 100 of the same kind of thing. km and hours are different units, so 90 km/h is not "out of 100" of anything.
:::

## Section 4: Proportion problems
Learning target: I can choose a strategy (table, double number line or unit rate) to solve a ratio or rate problem.

### Vocabulary
- proportional reasoning :: using equal ratios or rates to find a missing amount :: if 2 cost $5, then 6 cost $15
- scale :: the ratio of a drawing to the real thing :: 1 cm : 50 km

@viz flow steps="Read: what is compared?|Pick: table, number line or unit rate|Solve|Check: does it make sense?"

::: example Worked Example 6 — Map scale
On a map of Alberta, 1 cm stands for 50 km. Edmonton and Calgary are 6 cm apart on the map.
Real distance = 6 × [[50]] = [[300]] km
@viz dnl top="0 cm,1 cm,2 cm,3 cm,4 cm,5 cm,6 cm" bottom="0,50 km,100,150,200,250,[[300 km]]" toplabel="map" bottomlabel="real"
:::

::: explain
Why does a double number line work for a map scale?
(lines 2)
=> Every 1 cm on the map is always 50 km, so the two lines grow together at the same rate.
:::

::: example Worked Example 7 — Unit rate strategy
A farmer earns $720 for 3 tonnes of wheat. How much for 7 tonnes?
Unit rate: $720 ÷ 3 = [[$240]] per tonne
7 tonnes: 7 × $240 = [[$1680]]
:::

::: explain
When is the unit rate easier than a scale factor?
(lines 2)
=> When the numbers don't multiply nicely (3 to 7 is not a whole-number scale factor), finding the value of ONE first makes it easy.
:::

#### What can {a/b} mean?
The same symbol can mean different things:
| {3/4} as… | Example |
|---|---|
| a fraction | {3/4} of a pizza |
| a ratio | 3 boys to 4 girls |
| a rate | $3 for 4 apples |
| a quotient (division) | 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 |
| a probability | 3 chances out of 4 |

::: try
a) 4 tickets cost $30. How much do 10 tickets cost?
(work 2)
=> $7.50 each, so 10 cost $75.
b) A recipe needs 2 eggs for 12 muffins. How many eggs for 30 muffins?
(work 2)
=> 1 egg makes 6 muffins. 30 ÷ 6 = 5 eggs.
:::

::: retrieval
1. A printer prints 45 pages in 3 minutes. How many pages in 10 minutes?
=> 15 pages per minute × 10 = 150 pages
2. The scale on a map is 1 cm : 20 km. Two towns are 140 km apart. How far apart are they on the map?
=> 7 cm
3. Give an example where {2/3} is a rate, and one where it is a ratio.
=> Rate: $2 for 3 pens. Ratio: 2 cats to 3 dogs.
4. Which strategy would you use for "5 cost $8. How much for 15?" Why?
=> Scale factor ×3 (15 ÷ 5 = 3), so $24. The numbers multiply nicely.
5. ★ Stretch: Jasmine runs 5 km in 30 minutes. At the same speed, how long for a 12 km race? Explain your strategy.
=> 1 km takes 6 minutes. 12 × 6 = 72 minutes.
:::

## Concept Reference
::: key Ratio
Compares amounts of the **same** kind. 1 to 3 · 1:3 · {1/3}
Part to part: concentrate : water = 1:3. Part to whole: concentrate : all = 1:4 = 25%.
:::
@viz tape parts="1:concentrate;3:water"

::: key Equal ratios
Multiply (or divide) **both** terms by the same number. 1:3 = 2:6 = 3:9. Never add.
:::
@viz dnl top="0,1,2,3,4" bottom="0,3,6,9,12" toplabel="concentrate" bottomlabel="water"

::: key Rate and unit rate
A **rate** compares different units: 150 km in 1.5 h. A **unit rate** is for one unit: 100 km/h.
Best buy: divide price by quantity, then compare the price for ONE.
A ratio can be a percent. A rate cannot (different units).
:::

::: key Solving proportion problems
- Scale factor: 3:1 = 12:? → × 4 → 4
- Unit rate: $720 for 3 t → $240 per t → 7 t = $1680
- Double number line or table for anything else
:::

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| 1:3 means "1 out of 3" | 1:3 is part to part; 1 out of 4 in all |
| 1:3 = 2:4 (added 1) | Multiply: 1:3 = 2:6 |
| Bigger package = better deal | Compare unit prices |
