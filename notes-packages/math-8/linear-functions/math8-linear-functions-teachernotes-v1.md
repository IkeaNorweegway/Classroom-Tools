## Overview
::: overview
**Big idea:** slope is one number that says how steep a line is and which way it goes: rise over run, up over across. Because a straight line rises the same amount for every step across, any two points give the same slope, and subtracting coordinates (m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)) does the counting for us. The sign and size of m sort lines into four kinds, and comparing m tells parallel (equal) from perpendicular (negative reciprocals). In y = mx + b the slope turns the line and b slides it; b is where the line crosses the y-axis, (0, b). In a story the slope becomes a rate of change (km per hour, $ per km, L per minute) and b becomes the starting amount.
**Sequence:** ski runs and ramps as rise and run, counting on a grid, the slope formula with integer points, points on both sides of 0 (LF1) → positive, negative, zero and undefined slope, parallel and perpendicular slopes, linear vs non-linear tables and graphs (LF2) → a table for y = 2x + 1 shows where m and b come from, changing b slides the line, changing m turns it, equation from a graph, graph from an equation, graph from a slope and a point (LF3) → x- and y-intercepts as ordered pairs, y = b and x = a, rate of change and initial value: a drive to Red Deer, a taxi fare, a draining tank (LF4).
**Materials:** grid paper (1 cm), rulers, coloured pencils, mini whiteboards with a printed grid, calculators (optional), two metre sticks and a binder or board to build a "ramp". Optional: Desmos (desmos.com/calculator) with sliders for m and b; a projected trail map of Lake Louise or Sunshine Village for the opener.
**Depth ceiling:** the slope formula uses integer points; slopes may be fractions. Parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals, compared and checked, not proved. No function notation f(x) (Grade 9), no point-slope or standard form, no solving systems. Writing an equation from a slope and a point uses the grid (step back to the y-axis), not algebra. Rate-of-change problems use whole-number or simple rates, with a sensible domain and range (Grade 7 language).
**Reading level:** student pages are written at about Grade 3–4. The math words (slope, rise, run, steepness, positive, negative, zero and undefined slope, parallel, perpendicular, negative reciprocal, linear, non-linear, y-intercept, x-intercept, slope-intercept form, m, b, horizontal, vertical, rate of change, initial value) are the learning; each has a picture beside it.
**Notation:** the notes write slope as a stacked fraction and m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); the little 1 and 2 only name the first and second point. Intercepts are always written as ordered pairs: (0, −4), not "−4". Lines are drawn across the whole grid with arrows; context graphs (distance, cost, water) are segments with a stated domain.
:::

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Slope is run over rise" | Say "up over across" every time, and estimate first: a steep line needs a big slope. If a steep line came out as 2/3, it is upside down. |
| Mixing the order in the formula (y₂ − y₁ on top, x₁ − x₂ on the bottom) | Colour the first point blue and the second red. Both subtractions start with red. Leo's explain box shows that swapping BOTH gives the same slope. |
| Losing the sign when a point is negative: 2 − (−2) = 0 | Write the brackets. Check by counting on the grid: from −2 to 2 is 4 steps. |
| "A flat line has no slope / undefined slope" | Rise 0 ÷ run 3 = 0: a slope of zero. Only a run of 0 breaks the division. Pair the words: flat–zero, straight up–undefined. |
| Reading a line right to left and calling a falling line positive | Read every line left to right, like a page. Down to the right is negative. |
| Perpendicular slope written as the reciprocal only (2/3 → 3/2) | Two steps: flip, then change the sign. Check on the grid: one goes up, one goes down. |
| "In y = mx + b, b is the slope" | m is the number times x; b stands alone. Cover b with a finger: what is left (mx) is how y grows. Then use y = 7 + ½x from the quiz to show the order can change. |
| "The x-intercept is where x = 0" | Put a finger on the x-axis: every point there has y = 0. The y-intercept is on the y-axis, x = 0. |
| Stopping the line at the plotted points | On a coordinate grid, draw the line across the whole grid with arrows. Only story graphs stop (the trip ends at Red Deer). |
| Putting the starting fee in as the slope (C = 4d + 2) | Ask "what do you pay for 0 km?" That is b. "What is added for each km?" That is m. |

## Lessons
### Lesson 1 — Slope (60 min)
DO: Pre-knowledge check (5 min). Q1 previews moving on a grid with a negative step, Q2 a negative quotient, Q3 reducing a fraction, Q4 substitution, Q5 functions (Grade 7), Q6 the constant change in a table.
SHOW: The two Lake Louise ski runs picture. "Which run is steeper? How could we prove it with numbers?"
SAY: "Steepness compares how far you go up or down with how far you go across. Rise over run. That number is the slope."
DO: Build ramps with a binder and a metre stick: same rise, longer run; same run, bigger rise. Students say which is steeper and why.
CUE: Slope on a grid (across 2, up 3). Then the slope formula and the coloured picture: rise is the change in y, run the change in x.
CUE: Worked Example 1 ((1, 1) and (4, 7), m = 2, check by counting), Worked Example 2 (Red Deer wheelchair ramp, 1/12), Worked Example 3 ((−2, 1) and (2, 3), m = 1/2).
DO: Explain box: Leo used the points in the other order. Take a vote before the answer.
WATCH: "Slope is run over rise." Misconception box: up 3 for every 2 across is 3/2.
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 1.

### Lesson 2 — Kinds of slope (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: slope through (0, 2) and (3, 8); −12 ÷ 4; solve 2x + 5 = 1; is (3, 1), (3, 4) a function?
SHOW: The four small grids (up, down, flat, straight up). Students hold an arm at each angle and say the kind.
CUE: Worked Example 1 ((0, 4) and (2, 0), m = −2: the rise is negative).
CUE: Worked Example 2 (line A flat, 0/3 = 0; line B vertical, 3/0 undefined). Use "what times 0 makes 3?" to show why.
WATCH: "A flat line has undefined slope." Misconception box.
SHOW: Parallel lines y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x − 3; perpendicular slopes 2 and −1/2. Let students check the right angle with a corner of paper.
CUE: Worked Example 3 (P and Q parallel, R perpendicular).
DO: Linear or non-linear: table A goes up 2 each time, table B goes up 1, 3, 5. Graph both on one grid.
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 2.

### Lesson 3 — y = mx + b (60 min)
DO: Warm-up interleaved: slope through (−1, 5) and (1, 1); slope of a line parallel to slope 3/4; evaluate 2x + 1 for x = 0, 1, 2, 3; solve x/3 − 1 = 4.
DO: Table for y = 2x + 1. Students graph it and find where m (up 2 each step) and b (y at x = 0) show up.
SHOW: "Change b: the line slides" and "Change m: the line turns". Optional: Desmos sliders for m and b; students predict before each drag.
CUE: Worked Example 1 (equation from a graph: read b first, then count, y = 2x − 1), Worked Example 2 (graph y = −½x + 3: plot b, then right 2, down 1), Worked Example 3 (slope 3 through (1, 2): step back to the y-axis, y = 3x − 1, check with x = 1).
WATCH: "In y = 4x + 2, the slope is 2." Misconception box.
DO: Your turn, retrieval, exit ticket Section 3. Quiz at the start of next class.

### Lesson 4 — Intercepts, rate of change and special lines (60 min)
DO: Quiz (25 min) first. Then the lesson.
SHOW: y = 2x − 4 with both intercepts. "Where is it on the y-axis? Where on the x-axis?" Write both as ordered pairs.
WATCH: "The x-intercept is where x = 0." Misconception box: (0, 3) is on the y-axis.
SHOW: y = 3 and x = −2. Students list three points on each and notice what stays the same.
CUE: Worked Example 1 (y = −1 and x = 4).
CUE: Worked Example 2 (Edmonton to Red Deer, 100 km per hour, d = 100t, domain 0 to 1.5 h), Worked Example 3 (Calgary taxi, C = 2d + 4), Worked Example 4 (60 L tank, slope −5, both intercepts in words).
DO: Explain box: the babysitter's graph starts at (0, 0), not (0, 15).
DO: Core worksheet; Challenge for early finishers. Exit ticket Section 4. The unit synthesis can be homework or the start of the next class.

## Assessment
::: box Quiz (23 marks)
LF1 (Q1–3), LF2 (Q4–6), LF3 (Q7–10). Given at the start of Lesson 4, before intercepts, special lines and rate of change (LF4). No calculator needed. Q1: rise 3 and run 4 earn the first mark even if the slope is written upside down; 4/3 earns 1 of 2. Q2: the setup with brackets earns the first mark; 10/5 left unsimplified is fine. Q4: one mark per pair, and "undefined" must be a word, not 6/0 alone. Q5: −5/2 → 2/5 needs both the flip and the sign change. Q7 b): y = 7 + ½x checks that students find m next to x, not in first place. Q9: the line must cross the whole grid. Q10 is the explain-your-thinking item: full marks need both "3 is the slope" and what the 5 means.
:::
::: box Worksheets and exits
Core (★) covers LF1–LF4 with a diagram on most questions: rise and run from a grid, the formula with a negative coordinate, two Sunshine Village ski runs, a run-over-rise error, the four kinds of slope, special-line slopes with a reason, parallel and perpendicular pairs, m and b, an equation from a graph, graphing y = ⅔x − 1, both intercepts of y = −2x + 6, and an Edmonton taxi fare. Challenge (★★) adds linear vs non-linear tables, graphing from a slope and a point, the equation through two points, a draining rain barrel, a perpendicular line with a given b, comparing two phone plans, making up a situation with slope 20 and b = 50, and a two-mistake error analysis of y = −3. Each misconception has at least one targeted question; the key names it. Exit tickets: one per section, each with a diagram and an "explain how you know" item.
:::

## Data and contexts used
- Wheelchair ramp, 1 m rise over 12 m run: the 1 : 12 maximum slope is the usual Canadian accessibility guideline. The Red Deer school is made up.
- Edmonton to Red Deer: about 150 km by Highway 2 (rounded). The steady 100 km per hour is a simplification: no stops, no speed changes.
- Calgary taxi: $4 to start and $2 per km is made up and rounded; real Calgary meter rates are a little different and also charge for waiting time. The Edmonton taxi ($5 + $3 per km) in the worksheet is made up.
- Calgary Tower elevator at about 3 m per second: a rounded figure for a ride of roughly a minute to the top. Check the current number before quoting it as fact.
- Ski runs at Lake Louise (100 m over 500 m, 300 m over 400 m, 240 m over 600 m) and at Sunshine Village (worksheet) are made up. Real runs are not straight lines; the slopes are averages for a side view.
- Phone plans ($30 + $10 per GB, and $10 + $20 per GB), the 60 L tank, the 90 L rain barrel, the $40 gift card, Ava's babysitting ($12 an hour plus a $6 bus pass) and the $15-an-hour babysitter are made up.

## Connections
- **Grade 7 functions:** input/output tables, discrete vs continuous, domain and range; Lesson 4 reuses domain and range for story graphs.
- **Proportional Reasoning (topic 3):** the constant of proportionality k is the slope of y = kx, a line through (0, 0). The draft says k is also called slope or unit rate.
- **Solving Equations (topic 5):** finding the x-intercept means solving 0 = mx + b.
- **Similarity & Dilations (topic 8):** rays from the origin in a dilation are lines through (0, 0) with slope y/x.
- **Grade 9:** function notation f(x), linear relations in more forms, and systems of two lines.
