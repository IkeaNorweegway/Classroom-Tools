## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 7-PS: Students will learn that post-secondary pathways require balancing personal goals, financial considerations, and labour market demand. |
| **Understanding** | Post-secondary pathways help prepare individuals for careers, and require thoughtful consideration of costs and opportunities. |
| **Driving question** | Is there more than one good way to reach the same career? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl7-post-secondary-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl7-post-secondary-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5 students; one recommendation per team |
| **Audience** | A panel in Session 7 (school counsellor, a teacher, and if possible someone from one of the careers) playing a family deciding with their teen |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric ("How you will be marked") on the report-card scale (4 Excellent · 3 Proficient · 2 Meets · 1 Has yet to meet), scored on Steps 1–4 and the presentation. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Do this before Session 1**
- **Book the panel** for Session 7. The school counsellor is ideal. Ask them to bring a high school course-planning guide.
- **Prepare a career list** with two real pathways each (below). Check that each school's tuition page loads on student devices.
- **Hook discussion:** there is no single right answer to "Who made the better choice?" That is the point of the project. Revisit it in Look back.
:::

### Careers with two roads (starter list)

| Career | Road A | Road B |
|---|---|---|
| Software developer | University computing science degree (U of A, U of C) | Polytechnic diploma in software or computer systems (NAIT, SAIT) |
| Chef or cook | Culinary diploma at a polytechnic | Cook apprenticeship (paid, on the job) |
| Electrician | Apprenticeship straight from high school | Pre-employment electrical program, then apprenticeship |
| Early childhood educator | 1-year certificate | 2-year diploma |
| Business or marketing | 2-year college diploma | 4-year university degree |
| Nursing (note: two different roles) | Licensed practical nurse, 2-year diploma | Registered nurse, 4-year degree |

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** The Sam and Jess hook has no single answer, which creates a real need to know about cost, time and outcomes.
- **Real numbers.** Students use real tuition and real labour-market outlooks. Financial teaching sticks best when it is tied to a real decision.
- **Equal value.** The curriculum asks students to see that all pathways can lead to good careers. Teams must say this in their product (Step 5 checklist).
- **Panel audience.** A counsellor asking "What if they don't get in?" makes the trade-offs real.

## Session plans

### Session 1 — Hook and Moment 1

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | **Hook.** Read Sam and Jess. Teams write a first answer. Take a quick vote; don't settle it. |
| 10–15 | Teams and jobs. Read the driving question. |
| 15–20 | **Need-to-know list** on a class chart. |
| 20–35 | **Moment 1.** Four pathways; the electrician example. Questions 1–3. |
| 35–55 | **Step 1.** Teams pick a career from the list (or their own, if it truly has two roads) and fill in both roads. |
| 55–60 | Self-check. |

::: misconception
**"University is the only real option."** Moment 1, Question 3 targets this. Ask: "Which pathway gets you paid from day one?" Return to it in Session 2's warm-up.
:::

### Session 2 — Moment 2 and the true cost

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) Which pathway pays you while you learn? (2) Name one difference between a degree and a diploma. (3) 3 × $1,044 = ? |
| 5–20 | **Moment 2.** The iceberg and Liam's true cost. Questions 1–3. |
| 20–50 | **Step 2.** Finders look up tuition; Math people calculate; Checkers redo the math. |
| 50–60 | **Check-in: swap** true-cost tables with another team and check the math. |

::: lookfor
Did they include **lost earnings**? Did they count months in school, not 12 months? For apprenticeships, did they note "paid while learning" (very low lost earnings)? Tuition figures should come from the school's page, with the year.
:::

### Session 3 — Moment 3 and the outlook

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) What is a true cost? (2) Name one hidden cost. (3) 8 × $2,400 = ? |
| 5–20 | **Moment 3.** Shortage and surplus seesaws; the Alberta forecast. Questions 1–3. |
| 20–50 | **Step 3.** Teams look up the outlook on OCCinfo. |
| 50–60 | **Teacher check-in** with each team. |

### Session 4 — Moment 4 and deciding

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) Shortage or surplus: 200 people apply for 3 jobs? (2) Why check the outlook again later? (3) What is a trade-off? |
| 5–20 | **Moment 4.** Priya's decision table. Questions 1–3. |
| 20–50 | **Step 4.** Teams fill in their decision table and write a recommendation and trade-off. |
| 50–60 | Check-in: does the reason match the table? |

::: lookfor
Recommendations should rest on more than cost alone. "Both, for different people" is a strong answer if they explain who each road suits.
:::

### Session 5 — Workshop and making it

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) What do programs use to choose students? (2) Name a sacrifice of a longer pathway. (3) Which road did your team recommend, and why? |
| 5–15 | **Workshop: making a comparison easy to see.** Show the three samples below; students name what makes a side-by-side clear. |
| 15–55 | **Step 5.** Choose a product, sketch, build. |
| 55–60 | Step 5 checklist. |

### Session 6 — Feedback

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Model the feedback frames. |
| 5–25 | Gallery walk; each team gets feedback from at least three others. |
| 25–55 | Make at least 2 changes. Plan answers to the panel questions. |
| 55–60 | Fill in "Who says what." |

### Session 7 — Present

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–40 | **Panel.** Each team presents (about 4 minutes) and takes 2–3 questions. The panel says whether they were convinced and why. |
| 40–60 | **Look back** page, authenticity check, **My Plan** page. |

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Side-by-side poster.** Two roads drawn as paths up a hill, with cost, time and outlook signs along each path.
2. **Comic, "Two roads."** Two characters start at grade 12 graduation, make different choices, and meet at the same job five years later, comparing what they gave up.
3. **Mini panel talk.** Each team member speaks for one road or one factor, and the team ends with its recommendation.

## When a team gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Can't find tuition | Use the school's "tuition and fees" page. For apprenticeships, use the technical training tuition per period. |
| Forgets lost earnings | Point back to the iceberg: what's under the water? |
| One road is "obviously bad" | Ask: "Who would this road be perfect for?" |
| Recommendation is only "it's cheaper" | Ask about time, interests, admission, and jobs. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- **Reading:** read scenarios and school webpages aloud; the brief's word cards cover the key terms.
- **Math:** calculators for everyone; the Math person role rotates, so all students do some calculation with a Checker's support.
- **Expression:** poster, comic, video, or a spoken panel talk.
- **Sensitivity:** family experiences of post-secondary vary widely. Don't ask students to share family education or money situations.

## Background for the teacher

- **U of A 2026–27 tuition:** $729.36 per 3-credit course (fee index × $121.56); about $7,294 for 10 courses.
- **Apprenticeship tuition** varies by school: Portage College's electrician tuition is $1,044 for each of periods 1–3 and $1,524 for period 4.
- **Alberta job market forecast 2023–2033:** registered nurses and electricians appear among occupations that may face shortages of more than 4,000 workers. Forecasts are updated; check alberta.ca's job market forecasts page.
- **Lost earnings model:** $15/hour × 40 hours × 4 weeks ≈ $2,400 a month; it is a simple estimate for comparing.
