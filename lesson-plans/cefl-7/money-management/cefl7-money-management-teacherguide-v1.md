## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 7-MM: Students explain how spending, saving, and budgeting decisions affect financial security. |
| **Understanding** | Individuals aspire to achieve financial security. |
| **Driving question** | How can I reach a money goal without losing control of my spending? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl7-money-management-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl7-money-management-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5 students. Each student makes their own budget; the team checks math and gives feedback. |
| **Audience** | A family member or trusted adult at home (family feedback card), then a short class share in Session 7 |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric ("How you will be marked") on the report-card scale (4 Excellent · 3 Proficient · 2 Meets · 1 Has yet to meet), scored on Steps 1–4 and the final product. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Do this before Session 1**
- **Make surprise cards** for Step 4 (about 15, mixed). Bad surprises: cracked phone screen $180; lost bus pass $45; friend's birthday gift $25; bike tire $35; school trip fee $40; replacing lost earbuds $30. Good surprises: extra yard job +$40; birthday money +$50; sold an old game +$20. Scale amounts to the income card: $180 is a big hit on card A.
- **Price sources** for Step 1: flyers, store websites, or a printed list of common goals with real prices (bike, headphones, a game console, a concert ticket, a gift for family).
- **Note home** before Step 7 explaining the family feedback card. Offer another trusted adult (coach, school staff) for students who need it.
:::

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** The Maya and Jake hook (same money, different results) creates the need to know what a budget does.
- **Practise the behaviour.** Financial education changes knowledge more than behaviour, so students rehearse the real task: a working budget toward a real goal, tested with a surprise.
- **Privacy by design.** Income cards mean no student has to reveal their own or their family's money. Students who want to use real income can.
- **Real audience at home.** Explaining a budget to a family member makes the plan real and brings the conversation home.

## Session plans

### Session 1 — Hook and Moment 1

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | **Hook.** Maya and Jake. Teams brainstorm what Maya did differently. Collect ideas without judging. |
| 10–15 | Teams and jobs. Read the driving question. |
| 15–20 | **Need-to-know list** on a class chart. |
| 20–35 | **Moment 1.** Ava's trade-off. Questions 1–3. |
| 35–55 | **Step 1.** Students choose a goal and find a real price. |
| 55–60 | Self-check. |

::: lookfor
Goals have a **real price and a source**, not a guess. Gently steer very large goals (a car at 12) toward something reachable in months, or let students treat it as a long-term goal.
:::

### Session 2 — Moment 2 and income

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) What is a trade-off? (2) Ava spends $45 of $60; how much is left? (3) Need or want: winter boots? |
| 5–20 | **Moment 2.** Ava's budget bar; changing a budget to reach a goal sooner. Questions 1–3. |
| 20–50 | **Step 2.** Choose an income card; list spending; label needs and wants. |
| 50–60 | **Check-in:** Checkers review need/want labels with each teammate. |

::: misconception
**"A budget means no fun."** Moment 2, Question 3 targets this. Point out that Ava's new budget still has snacks, games and gifts. Come back to it when students build their budgets: every budget should have some money for wants.
:::

### Session 3 — Moment 3 and the budget

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) What is a budget? (2) $360 ÷ $60 = ? (3) Name one need and one want. |
| 5–20 | **Moment 3.** Ty's goals and timeline. Questions 1–3. |
| 20–50 | **Step 3.** Build the budget; calculate months to reach the goal. |
| 50–60 | **Check-in: swap.** Checkers make sure every budget adds up to its income. |

::: lookfor
Budget totals equal income; savings is a line in the budget (not "whatever is left"); months = price ÷ savings, rounded up.
:::

### Session 4 — Moment 4 and surprises

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) Short- or long-term: a car? (2) $4,000 ÷ $250 = ? (3) What makes a goal clear? |
| 5–20 | **Moment 4.** Ty's two paths. Questions 1–3. |
| 20–45 | **Step 4.** Students draw a surprise card and adjust their budget; compare Plan A and Plan B. |
| 45–60 | **Teacher check-in:** does the student explain which plan gives more financial security, and why? |

### Session 5 — Workshop and making it

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) What is an emergency fund? (2) $300 ÷ $40 = ? (3) What surprise did you draw? |
| 5–15 | **Workshop: showing a budget clearly.** Show the three samples below. |
| 15–55 | **Step 5.** Build the product. |
| 55–60 | Step 6 checklist self-check. |

### Session 6 — Feedback

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Model the feedback frames. |
| 5–25 | Trade budgets in teams. |
| 25–55 | Make at least 2 changes. Prepare to share at home. |
| 55–60 | Hand out the family feedback note. |

### Session 7 — Share it

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | Students bring back family feedback cards. Pairs share one tip they got. Collect the best tips on a class chart. |
| 20–35 | Quick gallery of products. |
| 35–60 | **Look back**, authenticity check, **My Plan** page. |

::: teacher
**Timing:** students share at home between Sessions 6 and 7. If cards don't come back, students can share with a staff member during Session 7.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Poster with a budget chart.** A bar like Ava's, the goal picture with its price, and a "months to go" countdown.
2. **Savings tracker.** A thermometer to colour in each month, with the surprise marked as a dip and a note on how the plan recovered.
3. **Comic, "Saving for a bike."** Six panels: the goal, the budget, the trade-off, the surprise, the fix, and reaching the goal.

## When a student gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Budget doesn't add up | Have the Checker add each line out loud with them. |
| No savings line | Ask: "Where does the money for your goal come from?" |
| Goal takes 3 years | Show how to change the budget, choose a cheaper version, or call it long-term. |
| Surprise wipes out savings | That's the point. Talk about an emergency fund line. |
| Upset about money at home | Use income cards; never require real family money. Talk privately. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- **Math:** calculators for everyone. Students can use card A (round numbers) for simpler math.
- **Reading:** read scenarios and cards aloud.
- **Expression:** poster, tracker, video, comic, or slideshow.
- **Sensitivity:** students come from very different money situations. Income cards, sample prices, and "trusted adult" options make sure no one has to share personal finances.

## Background for the teacher

- **Card C and Ty's pay:** $13/hour (2026 Alberta student minimum wage), 20 hours every two weeks = $260 gross − EI 1.63% ($4.24) = $255.76 a pay; two pays ≈ $511 a month. Under-18s pay no CPP, and this income is below the tax-free amount. Ty in the workbook works twice as many hours ($511.52 every two weeks).
- **Research note:** financial education raises knowledge more than behaviour (about 0.2 vs. 0.1 standard deviations in a 76-study meta-analysis), and is most useful close to a real decision. Building a real budget for a real goal is the closest a classroom gets.
