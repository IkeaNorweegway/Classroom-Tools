## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 7-IT: Students develop an understanding of employment and employment income. |
| **Understanding** | Employment income is reduced by taxes and deductions, shaping take-home pay and financial choices. |
| **Driving question** | Where does my paycheque go? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl7-income-tax-workbook-v1` (4 moments) · project brief `cefl7-income-tax-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5 students, mixed, set by you. Keep teams for the whole unit. |
| **Audience** | A Grade 6 class, Session 7 (about 30 minutes) |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric in the brief ("How you will be marked"), scored on Steps 1–3 and on the final product. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Do this before Session 1**
- **Book the Grade 6 class for Session 7** now. If a Grade 6 class is not possible, use another class, families at a student-led conference, or the school's leadership group.
- Print 8–12 **real job postings** from Job Bank (jobbank.gc.ca, search your town) for teams that can't search online. Choose hourly or salaried jobs at **$27.50/hour or less** (the deduction card stops at $2,200 gross every two weeks). Include at least two jobs that hire 15–17-year-olds.
- Calculators are fine. The skill is applying the rates, not long multiplication.
- Sticky notes in two colours for Session 6; chart paper if any team picks the giant pay stub.
:::

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question and "need to know" first.** The hook (guess the take-home pay) opens a gap students want to close; each moment answers part of their need-to-know list. Lessons land better when students already want the answer.
- **Short lessons inside the project.** Each moment is 10–15 minutes and comes right before the milestone that uses it. Unguided "go research it" projects underperform; financial teaching sticks best close to the decision it supports.
- **Critique and revise at every milestone.** The check-in each session is where most of the learning gets fixed. Plan for students to change their work, not just hand it in.
- **Teams of 4–5 with roles.** Teams of 4–5 had the largest effects in the PBL research; roles keep peer talk on task and stop one student doing everything.
- **A real audience.** Grade 6 students' answers give teams honest feedback on whether their product worked.

## Session plans

### Session 1 — Hook, teams, and Moment 1

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–8 | **Hook.** Show the job ad on page 1 of the brief: "Hardware store helper, full-time, $20.00 an hour." Teams guess the take-home pay for two weeks and write it in the brief. Do not reveal the answer. |
| 8–15 | Form teams and assign Session 1 jobs (Leader, Finder, Math person, Checker, Designer; the brief's "How the project works" page). Read the driving question together. |
| 15–20 | **Need-to-know list** ("What do we need to know?" page). Teams fill in the first rows. Collect a few out loud and write them on chart paper you keep up all unit. Expect: "What gets taken off?", "Why?", "Do kids pay tax?" |
| 20–35 | **Moment 1** (workbook). Do the worked example together. Students do Questions 1–3; early finishers do the Extra practice. |
| 35–55 | **Step 1: Pick a job.** Teams choose a job ad and fill in the Step 1 page. The Checker confirms the gross pay. |
| 55–60 | Self-check in the workbook. Tick off answered items on the need-to-know chart. |

::: lookfor
**Step 1 check-in:** Is the gross pay for **two weeks** (not one week, not one hour)? For a salary, did they divide by 26? Do they have one advantage and one limitation of the pay type?
:::

### Session 2 — Moment 2 and the pay stub

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up** (no notes): (1) $16 an hour × 30 hours = ? (2) Name two ways to get paid. (3) What is 10% of $500? |
| 5–20 | **Moment 2.** Walk through Jordan's pay stub and the deduction card. **Reveal the hook:** $1,293.40. Teams compare with their guess. Students do Questions 1–3. |
| 20–45 | **Step 2: Build the pay stub.** Each student fills in the pay stub **on their own first**, then the team compares line by line. |
| 45–55 | **Check-in: swap.** Each team checks another team's pay stub with the deduction card and initials each line. |
| 55–60 | Fix any errors. Self-check. |

::: lookfor
**Errors to catch in the swap:** CPP taken from the full gross (forgot the $135); CPP taken off for a worker under 18; wrong row of the tax table; only one deduction subtracted; EI and CPP mixed up.
:::

::: misconception
**"My wage × my hours is what I get paid."** This is the unit's main misconception (workbook Moment 2, Question 3). The hook reveal is the moment to surface it: ask, "Who guessed $1,600? Why does that feel right?" Then contrast with the pay stub. Come back to it in the Session 3 warm-up.
:::

### Session 3 — Moment 3 and where deductions go

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) What is the difference between gross and net pay? (2) Ty's gross pay is $520 and he is 17. Which deductions come off? (3) 1.63% of $1,000 = ? |
| 5–20 | **Moment 3.** The flow diagram and the tax-free number line. Students do Questions 1–3. |
| 20–50 | **Step 3: Where does it go?** Teams fill in the Step 3 page and compare their worker's yearly pay to the tax-free lines. |
| 50–58 | **Teacher check-in** with each team (2–3 minutes each while others work). Use the look-fors. |
| 58–60 | Self-check. |

::: lookfor
Can every team member say, in their own words, **where CPP goes and how it comes back**? Does the team know whether their worker pays income tax, and **why** (yearly pay above or below the line)?
:::

::: misconception
**"Teenagers pay a lot of tax."** Most part-time teen workers pay **no income tax** (yearly pay below $16,452) and **no CPP** (under 18). Only EI comes off. Students are often surprised; this is good material for Path A products.
:::

### Session 4 — Moment 4 and choosing a path

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) Where does CPP money go? (2) Why does Maya pay no income tax? (3) Name one optional deduction. |
| 5–20 | **Moment 4.** The budget bar and the worked example. Students do Questions 1–3. Question 3 is the first draft for Path B teams. |
| 20–30 | **Show the three sample products** (below). Show all three, never just one: a single model narrows what students make. |
| 30–55 | **Step 4: Plan it.** Teams choose a path and product, fill in the Step 4 page, and sketch their plan. |
| 55–60 | Teacher check-in on plans; approve or redirect. |

::: lookfor
**Path A:** are their three key messages accurate and in kid-friendly words? **Path B:** does the claim have a reason that uses their pay stub, and a real trade-off?
:::

### Session 5 — Workshop and Draft 1

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up:** (1) What is the tax-free amount for Canada in 2026? (2) About what share of Alberta's spending goes to health? (3) Net pay = gross pay − ___. |
| 5–15 | **Workshop: "Make it clear for a Grade 6 reader."** Show the same pay-stub explanation two ways: a dense paragraph and a labelled visual with short sentences. Students name three differences (labels on the picture, one idea per sentence, big numbers). |
| 15–55 | **Step 5: Make it (Draft 1).** The Designer leads the layout; everyone has a part. Teams use the Step 5 checklist before finishing. |
| 55–60 | Self-check against the rubric's middle column. |

### Session 6 — Critique and Draft 2

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Model the critique frames with a made-up draft. Show one weak comment ("It's good") and one strong one ("A Grade 6 student might not know what CPP stands for; could you add a label?"). |
| 5–25 | **Gallery walk.** Each team leaves its draft out. Students visit at least three other teams and leave kind, specific, and helpful sticky notes. |
| 25–30 | Teams sort their sticky notes and choose at least **two changes**. |
| 30–58 | **Draft 2.** Record changes in the Step 6 "Feedback we got / Change we made" table. |
| 58–60 | Fill in "Who says what" on the Step 7 page. |

### Session 7 — Share and reflect

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Set up stations. |
| 5–35 | **Grade 6 visit.** Grade 6 students rotate between stations (about 5 minutes each). Path B stations run a quick vote. Each Grade 6 student fills in the three-question card from the brief. |
| 35–45 | Teams read the Grade 6 cards for their station. |
| 45–60 | **Look back** page (includes the authenticity check) and the **My Plan** page. |

::: teacher
Read the authenticity answers. If many students say "Sort of" or "No," note what they suggest; it is the best guide to improving this project next year.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 4)

These are deliberately different so students see a range. Make quick rough versions or describe them; they don't need to be polished.
1. **Comic strip, Path A.** "Where did my $1,600 go?" Six panels: Jordan gets paid; the money splits into three characters (Tax, CPP, EI) who each say where they are going; the last panel shows net pay in the bank.
2. **Giant pay stub, Path A.** A pay stub on chart paper with flip-up sticky notes over each deduction. Lift the flap to see where it goes and who it helps.
3. **Podcast debate, Path B.** A two-minute recording: one student argues for less tax using the pay stub ("$306 is almost a fifth of the pay"), another names the trade-off (hospital wait times), and the Grade 6 class votes.

## When a team gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| One student doing all the work | Re-read the team jobs. The Checker must redo the math; the Leader asks each person for one idea. |
| Stuck on the math | Point to the worked example in Moment 2 and ask them to follow it with their own numbers, one line at a time. |
| Posting doesn't fit the card (pay too high, commission only) | Have them choose a second posting, or treat it as a wage job at the posted hourly rate. |
| Product is pretty but wrong | Return to the rubric's Knowledge row. Accuracy first, then looks. |
| A team finishes early | Extend: add a second worker of a different age to the pay stub and compare, or calculate how many hours it takes to earn their My Plan goal. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- **Reading:** read the scenarios aloud; every student-facing page is written at about a Grade 4 reading level, with the curriculum words kept.
- **Calculation:** calculators for everyone. For students on an IPP, pre-fill the gross pay and the EI line, so they work on CPP, income tax and net pay.
- **Expression:** any product on the menu, including the giant pay stub (hands-on) and a podcast (spoken).
- **Language:** word cards at the back of the brief; sentence frames in the critique protocol.

These remove barriers without removing the thinking: every student still works out net pay and explains where the deductions go.

## Background for the teacher

- **Data (2026):**
  - Alberta minimum wage is $15.00, or $13.00 for students under 18 (up to 28 hours in a school week, or during school breaks).
  - Federal income tax is 14% on the first $58,523, with a basic personal amount of $16,452.
  - Alberta income tax is 8% on the first $61,200, with a basic personal amount of $22,769.
  - CPP is 5.95% of pay above $3,500 a year, starting the month after a worker turns 18.
  - EI is 1.63% of pay, at any age.
  - Re-check every figure each January.
- **The tax table is a simplified estimate:** yearly pay minus each basic personal amount, times 14% (federal) and 8% (Alberta), divided by 26. Real payroll also gives small credits for CPP and EI, so real withholding is a few dollars lower.
- **Privacy:** students should not use their family's real pay. "A job a family member has had" means the job and hourly rate only, and only if the family is happy to share.
- **Tip income** is taxable and must be reported. That is Grade 9 content, but a student may ask.
