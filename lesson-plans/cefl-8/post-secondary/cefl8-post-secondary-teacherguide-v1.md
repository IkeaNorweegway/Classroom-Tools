## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 8-PS: Students evaluate post-secondary pathways and labour market information to develop realistic, informed education and career goals. |
| **Understanding** | Career goals are most achievable when grounded in personal aspirations, finances, and an understanding of labour market realities. |
| **Driving question** | What do I need to do in high school to keep my options open? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl8-post-secondary-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl8-post-secondary-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5. Each student makes their own course plan; teams research programs and check plans. |
| **Audience** | The school counsellor or visiting high school students, Session 7 (3-minute plan conferences) |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric on the report-card scale, scored on Steps 1–3 and the product. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Before Session 1:** book the counsellor (or 3–4 high school students) for Session 7. Get your feeder high school's **course guide** (print or link); students need it for Step 2. Check that school admission pages (NAIT, SAIT, colleges, universities, apprenticeship entrance requirements on tradesecrets.alberta.ca) load on student devices.
:::

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** Jordan's missing courses (the hook) make course planning feel urgent and real.
- **Two programs, not one.** Planning for a first choice and a different backup teaches students to keep options open, the core of the outcome.
- **Real requirements and real money.** Students look up actual admission pages and awards.
- **Networking is done, not described.** Step 4 is a real conversation.

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Hook (Jordan), need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** two programs, requirements | Requirements copied from the real program page; programs are genuinely different |
| 2 | (1) What does "competitive" mean? (2) Minimum vs. admitted average? (3) Name one engineering requirement. | **Moment 2** (diploma credits) | **Step 2:** course plan with the course guide | Checker confirms diploma + both programs; -1 vs. -2 choices are deliberate |
| 3 | (1) How many credits for a diploma? (2) Why take the -1 course? (3) Name a CTS course. | **Moment 3** (scholarships, bursaries, grants, loans) | **Step 3:** tuition and two awards | Tuition has a year and source; awards are real |
| 4 | (1) Which of these is paid back? (2) What is the Rutherford Scholarship? (3) $4,800 − $1,700 = ? | **Moment 4** (networking) | **Step 4:** plan and hold the conversation (can happen before Session 5) | Every student has someone to talk to |
| 5 | (1) What is networking? (2) A good question to ask someone in a career? (3) What did your person say? | Workshop: a plan readable at a glance (three samples below) | **Step 5:** build the product | Plan shows all four parts |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Feedback, at least 2 changes, rehearse the 3-minute walk-through | Changes respond to feedback |
| 7 | — | — | **Plan conferences** with counsellor or high school students; Look back; My Plan | Advice recorded and acted on |

::: misconception
**"If I meet the minimum, I'm in."** Moment 1, Question 3. Contrast minimum vs. typical admitted averages for a competitive program. Return to it in the Session 2 warm-up.
:::

::: misconception
**"Grade 10 choices don't matter yet."** Jordan's story and the -1/-2 sequences answer this. Be careful not to imply -2 courses are "worse": they're the right fit for many pathways, and upgrading is possible.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Course map poster.** Three columns (Grade 10, 11, 12) of course "tiles", colour-coded by which program each one meets, with a cost box at the bottom.
2. **Flowchart.** "If I get into Program A → … If not → Program B → …", with the shared courses at the top.
3. **Planner page.** A one-page school-planner layout with milestones (deadlines, scholarship dates, conversation notes).

## When a student gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Picks two nearly identical programs | Ask for a second program in a different field or school type. |
| Can't find requirements | Search "[program] admission requirements Alberta"; for trades, use the apprenticeship entrance requirements. |
| Course plan misses the diploma | Checker compares against the Moment 2 diagram. |
| No one to talk to | Arrange a high school student, counsellor, or staff member. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- Read admission pages aloud; the word cards cover the key terms.
- Choice of product (poster, flowchart, video, planner).
- Some students plan to work right after Grade 12 or are unsure; a workplace-training program or apprenticeship counts as a full choice.

## Background for the teacher

- **Alberta High School Diploma:** 100 credits, including ELA 30-1 or 30-2, Social 30-1 or 30-2, a 20-level math, a 20-level science, PE 10, CALM, 10 credits of CTS/fine arts/second languages/PE 20–30/K&E, and 10 more 30-level credits. Check Alberta Education's current requirements.
- **Alexander Rutherford Scholarship:** up to $2,500 for Grade 10–12 marks (applications through Alberta Student Aid).
- **U of A 2026–27 tuition:** $729.36 per 3-credit course.
