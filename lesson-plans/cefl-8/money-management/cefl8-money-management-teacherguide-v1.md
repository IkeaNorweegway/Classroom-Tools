## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 8-MM: Students investigate strategies to achieve financial security by analyzing how costs, interest, and savings help manage personal finances. |
| **Understandings** | Financial security depends on managing fixed and variable costs so money is available for saving and investing. Interest works both for and against individuals. |
| **Driving question** | How can I make my money grow without losing control of my costs? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl8-money-management-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl8-money-management-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5. Each student makes their own plan; teams check math and give feedback. |
| **Audience** | A Money Fair (families, staff, other classes), Session 7 |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Before Session 1:** book a space and invite visitors for the Money Fair (Session 7). Check that a couple of bank or credit-union rate pages load for Step 2. Calculators (or a spreadsheet) for everyone.

**Hook answer (reveal in Session 3):** at 6% compounded yearly, Ana's $1,000 grows for 50 years to about **$18,420**; Ben's grows for 40 years to about **$10,286**. Ana has about **$8,134 more** from the same $1,000, just by starting 10 years earlier.

**Income cards:** A $180 (age 14); B $511 (age 16, 10 h/week at $13, after EI); C $767 (age 17, 15 h/week at $13, after EI). 2026 figures.
:::

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** The Ana and Ben hook creates a real need to understand compound interest.
- **Practise the decision.** Students build a budget and choose where money goes for two different timelines. Financial education changes behaviour most when it rehearses a real decision.
- **Real rates.** Students look up today's rate, so the numbers aren't pseudo-context.
- **Privacy.** Income cards mean no one shares real family money.

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Hook (Ana and Ben), need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** budget and two goals | Fixed/variable sorted correctly; goals have real prices |
| 2 | (1) Fixed or variable: groceries? (2) Ty saves $511 − $390 = ? (3) 3% as a decimal? | **Moment 2** (simple interest) | **Step 2:** real rate; simple interest | Rate has a source; percent converted to a decimal |
| 3 | (1) I = P × r × t: $500, 3%, 4 years? (2) What is principal? (3) Interest: reward or cost? | **Moment 3** (compound); **reveal the hook** | **Step 3:** compound table | Each year starts from the last year's value |
| 4 | (1) $500 at 3% compound, 2 years? (2) Why does the gap grow? (3) What did Ana and Ben show? | **Moment 4** (saving vs. investing) | **Step 4:** where each goal's money goes | Choice matches timeline; risk named |
| 5 | (1) Saving or investing: a GIC? (2) Why keep short-term money safe? (3) Your long-term goal? | Workshop: showing growth on a graph (three samples below) | **Step 5:** make it | All four parts present |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Feedback; at least 2 changes; practise the 2-minute talk | Math re-checked |
| 7 | — | — | **Money Fair**; Look back; My Plan | Visitor notes show the main ideas came through |

::: misconception
**"Simple and compound interest are about the same."** Moment 3, Question 3. True over 2–3 years, very false over 40. Use the hook reveal.
:::

::: misconception
**"Investing is just saving with a better rate."** Moment 4. Investments can lose value; the right choice depends on when you need the money.
:::

::: teacher
**Not financial advice.** Keep the investment discussion general (savings accounts, GICs, funds). Don't recommend specific products or companies.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Poster with a growth graph.** Budget pie on the left; a hand-drawn graph of simple vs. compound growth on the right; two "goal jars" labelled with where each goal's money goes.
2. **Spreadsheet with a chart.** A budget table, a 10-year compound column using a formula, and a line chart.
3. **"My money plan" booklet.** Four small pages: budget, simple, compound, and where it goes.

## When a student gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Budget doesn't add up | Checker adds each column aloud. |
| Uses 3 instead of 0.03 | Ask: what is 3% of $100? |
| Compound table restarts from the principal each year | Point to the worked example: each year starts from last year's end. |
| Picks investing for a 3-month goal | Ask: what if it dropped 10% the month before you need it? |

## Access supports (UDL)

- Calculators or spreadsheets; a formula card (I = P × r × t; value × (1 + r) each year).
- Product choice, including a spreadsheet for students who like working digitally.
- Income cards and sample costs protect privacy.

## Background for the teacher

- Rates in the workbook (2%, 3%, 3.5%, 4%, 5%, 6%) are samples. Real savings and GIC rates change with the Bank of Canada policy rate.
- The Grade 9 unit covers the other side of interest: credit cards, loans and compound interest on debt.
