## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 8-LM: Students analyze the dynamic nature of the labour market and develop an understanding of the knowledge, skills, and goals required to build career resilience. |
| **Understandings** | The labour market is dynamic, making adaptability a more reliable career strategy than job stability alone. Career resilience is built deliberately. Human capital shapes employability. |
| **Driving question** | How can I build a career that can survive change? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl8-labour-market-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl8-labour-market-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5. Each student makes their own plan; teams research and give feedback. |
| **Audience** | A Grade 7 class, Session 7 ("advice from Grade 8s") |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric on the report-card scale (4 Excellent · 3 Proficient · 2 Meets · 1 Has yet to meet), scored on Steps 1–4 and the product. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Before Session 1:** book the Grade 7 class for Session 7. Check alis.alberta.ca (OCCinfo) on student devices. **Hook answers** (accept reasoned debate): new: social media manager, drone pilot, app developer; shrinking: video rental clerk (almost gone), travel agent, bank teller; still strong: electrician, nurse.
:::

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** The hook sorting shows that jobs really do appear and disappear; the moments explain why.
- **Real Alberta example.** The 2014–2016 oil downturn (unemployment 4.4% → 9.0%) makes "the labour market is dynamic" concrete. Some students' families lived it, so handle it with care.
- **Personal plan, team support.** Each plan is personal; teams catch weak reasoning.
- **Advice for Grade 7s** is a real audience with a reason to explain clearly.

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Hook (sorting), need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** pick a career; three forces table | Each force row names a *specific* way the career could change, not "it could change" |
| 2 | (1) Name the three forces. (2) What happened to Alberta jobs 2014–2016? (3) 9.0 ÷ 4.4 ≈ ? | **Moment 2** (farm technology) | **Step 2:** tech check | Shrink and grow columns both filled; the "new skill" is specific |
| 3 | (1) One positive and one negative effect of technology. (2) Job or career security: 20 years at one plant? (3) What is human capital? | **Moment 3** (Dev and Rosa) | **Step 3:** human capital, skill ranking, network map | Ranking has a reason tied to the career; network lists roles, not full names |
| 4 | (1) Job vs. career security. (2) Name two ways to build human capital now. (3) What is networking? | **Moment 4** (Amira's milestones) | **Step 4:** plan with milestones and backup | Milestones are concrete and in order; the backup shares skills |
| 5 | (1) Short- or long-term: finish first aid this spring? (2) Why have a backup? (3) Your top transferable skill? | Workshop: a plan on one page (show the three samples below) | **Step 5:** sketch and build | Product shows all four parts |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Feedback; at least 2 changes; practise the 1-minute talk | Changes respond to the feedback |
| 7 | — | — | **Share** with Grade 7s (stations), then Look back and My Plan | Grade 7 cards show they understood |

::: misconception
**"The safest plan is one job forever."** Moment 4, Question 3 targets this. Use Dev and Rosa: Dev had job security; Rosa had career security. Return to it in the Session 5 warm-up.
:::

::: misconception
**"Robots will take all the jobs."** Moment 2, Question 3. Technology shifts tasks; it rarely removes a whole field at once. The farm shows both sides.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Infographic.** Top third: "What could change my career" with three icons. Middle: "My resilience" as a skills bar chart. Bottom: a milestone timeline with a backup branch.
2. **Letter to my future self.** A one-page letter from age 13 to age 25 that explains the plan, the backup, and what to do if the labour market changes.
3. **Short video.** A 90-second "news report from 2040" about the career, followed by the student explaining how they prepared.

## When a student gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Can't choose a career | Use the Grade 7 Career Pathway Map career, or pick one from the hook list. |
| "Technology won't affect my career" | Ask: what tools do people in it use? What would a robot or app do? |
| Milestones are vague ("work hard") | Ask: what course, club, certificate or job, and when? |
| Family job loss comes up | Acknowledge it; keep the focus on what workers can do, not blame. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- Read the scenarios and OCCinfo pages aloud; word cards are at the end of the brief.
- Any product on the menu, including the letter (writing) or video (spoken).
- The network table uses roles ("aunt", "coach"), not names, so no student has to share family details.

## Background for the teacher

- **Alberta unemployment:** 4.4% in November 2014, peaking at 9.0% in November 2016 (Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey, monthly). Annual averages are lower (about 7% for 2016).
- **Course names** in Amira's plan (Math 10C, Math 30-1, CTS) are Alberta high school courses.
- **OECD career readiness research:** teenagers who explore and plan their careers tend to have better adult outcomes (an association, not proof of cause).
