## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 8-IT: Students analyze income taxation systems and total compensation to evaluate employment options and understand the role taxes play in supporting society. |
| **Understandings** | Total compensation and tax implications help when evaluating a job offer. Taxes are how individuals collectively fund public services. |
| **Driving question** | Which job offer is really worth more? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl8-income-tax-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl8-income-tax-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5; one recommendation per team; individual tax calculations first |
| **Audience** | A panel (staff or family volunteers) playing Sam, Session 7 |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Before Session 1:** book 2–3 panel volunteers for Session 7. Calculators for everyone. Optional: make a second offer card (below) for teams that want a challenge.
:::

### Answer key: Sam's offers (2026, income tax only)

| | Offer A | Offer B |
|---|---|---|
| Salary | $45,000 | $42,000 |
| Federal: bracket tax − $2,303.28 | $6,300 − $2,303.28 = $3,996.72 | $5,880 − $2,303.28 = $3,576.72 |
| Alberta: 8% − $1,821.52 | $3,600 − $1,821.52 = $1,778.48 | $3,360 − $1,821.52 = $1,538.48 |
| Total income tax | $5,775.20 | $5,115.20 |
| Salary after income tax | $39,224.80 | $36,884.80 |
| 15% flat tax (Step 2) | $6,750 | $6,300 |
| Total compensation | $45,000 | $42,000 + $2,500 + $1,680 = $46,180 |

Offer A gives about **$2,340 more cash** after tax; Offer B has **$1,180 more total compensation**, an extra week of vacation and a training program, but a longer commute. **Either recommendation can meet the outcome** if the reasons use the numbers and the trade-off.

### Optional second offer card (software tester)

| | Offer A | Offer B |
|---|---|---|
| Salary + bonus | $68,000 + $3,000 bonus | $64,000 |
| Benefits | none | worth $3,500 |
| RRSP match | none | 5% of salary ($3,200) |
| Other | office every day | 2 remote days a week |

Key: A taxable $71,000 → federal $8,447.73, Alberta $4,054.48, total $12,502.20, after tax $58,497.80, total compensation $71,000. B $64,000 → federal $7,012.73, Alberta $3,354.48, total $10,367.20, after tax $53,632.80, total compensation $70,700. A crosses the federal bracket line at $58,523 (good for practising two brackets).

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** "Offer A pays $3,000 more" sounds obvious, so students want to find out why it might not be.
- **Brackets in a real decision.** Students calculate tax because they need it for the recommendation, not as a drill.
- **Values, not one right answer.** The panel tests reasoning, which matches the outcome's "evaluate" verb.

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Hook (Sam), need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** tax both offers (individually, then compare) | Credit subtracted; Alberta done; Checker initials |
| 2 | (1) First federal bracket rate and top? (2) Tax on $50,000? (3) What does the credit do? | **Moment 2** (three tax systems) | **Step 2:** flat vs. progressive; team view | View has a reason and a trade-off |
| 3 | (1) Name the three systems. (2) Why is GST regressive? (3) 15% of $42,000? | **Moment 3** (total compensation) | **Step 3:** total compensation and non-money factors | Ratings are from Sam's point of view, with reasons |
| 4 | (1) What's in total compensation? (2) 4% of $42,000? (3) Name a non-money factor | **Moment 4** (social contract) | **Step 4:** decide | Money reason uses Steps 1 and 3; trade-off named |
| 5 | (1) Explain the social contract. (2) Share of Alberta spending on health? (3) Your recommendation? | Workshop: a comparison readable at a glance (three samples below) | **Step 5:** make it | All five parts present |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Feedback; at least 2 changes; plan panel answers | Numbers re-checked by another team |
| 7 | — | — | **Panel** (4 minutes + questions per team); Look back; My Plan | Answers to "Is the extra tax a reason to say no?" show brackets understood |

::: misconception
**"A raise into a higher bracket can make you take home less."** Moment 1, Question 3, and the panel question in Step 7. Only the income above the line is taxed at the higher rate.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Side-by-side poster.** Two columns, each with a "pay stack" (salary, tax, after-tax, benefits) and a row of icons for the non-money factors.
2. **Letter of advice.** A one-page letter to Sam with a small table of numbers and three paragraphs: money, life, and recommendation.
3. **"Sam's choice" video.** Two short scenes of Sam a year into each job, then the team's numbers and recommendation.

## When a team gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Taxes the whole salary at 20.5% | Draw the bracket bar from Moment 1 with their salary. |
| Forgets the credit | Point to step 4 of the worked example. |
| Ignores non-money factors | Ask: which job would Sam actually enjoy? Why? |
| Team can't agree | Use the decision table: each person scores, then discuss the biggest difference. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- Calculators; a tax "recipe card" (the four worked-example steps) can stay on the desk.
- Product choice; the letter suits strong writers, the video suits speakers.
- Real postings are optional; Sam's offers keep everyone on equal footing.

## Background for the teacher

- **2026 federal:** 14% to $58,523; 20.5% to $117,045; 26% to $181,440; 29% to $258,482; 33% above; basic personal amount $16,452 (credit at 14%).
- **2026 Alberta:** 8% to $61,200; 10% to $154,259; 12%, 13%, 14%, 15% above; basic personal amount $22,769 (credit at 8%).
- The method ignores CPP and EI (and their credits) to keep the bracket idea clear; real tax is a little different.
