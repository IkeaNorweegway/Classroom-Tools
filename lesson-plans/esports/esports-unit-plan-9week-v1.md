# Esports Program — 9-Week Unit Plan
*Grades 7–9 · Once per week · Game-agnostic*
*Season 1 · Tournament + Public Showcase finale*

---

## What This Program Is

This is a **structured season**, not a gaming club. That distinction shapes everything about how it runs.

A gaming club is mostly free play. This program gives students roles, a team, a season arc, and a culminating event — the same basic structure as any school sport. Research on school esports programs shows that structure is what produces real outcomes: students show up more, perform better academically, and find belonging, especially students who do not fit traditional sport culture.

**Every student has a role regardless of gaming skill.** A student who has never played competitively can be the broadcaster, the club president, or the event organizer — and be just as important to the season as the best player on the team.

This is an **introductory program.** Students are not expected to master any domain by Week 9. The goal is to try each area, find where they fit, and finish the season knowing what esports actually involves — beyond just playing.

---

## Who This Is For

Grades 7–9. No gaming experience required for most roles. The program is designed so that students who are strong players, students who are strong communicators, students who are strong planners, and students who are strong business thinkers all find their fit.

**The Domain Sorting Survey in Week 1 places every student** — not by skill, but by personal strengths.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the season, students will:

| Area | What students will be able to do |
|---|---|
| **The program** | Explain the difference between a gaming club and a structured esports program. Recite the Pledge from memory. Describe why the points system rewards more than just winning. |
| **The Four Domains** | Name the four domains and give at least one career example from each. Identify which domain fits their strengths based on the Sorting Survey. |
| **Roles** | Describe what their production role involves and try it at least once. Explain what the IGL and the Analyst do and when each one's work happens. |
| **Conduct and wellness** | Explain what tilt is and name one way to respond to it. Describe what the Code of Conduct requires of them as a competitor. |
| **Career awareness** | Name at least one real Alberta esports career pathway and connect it to a domain. |

*These outcomes are introductory — the goal is awareness, participation, and a first experience in each domain. Students are not expected to master production or strategy skills by the end of Week 9.*

## The Premiership Points System

Points are tracked on the ladder from Week 7 onward. Culture, conduct, and preparation are worth points — not only match results.

| Category | Points | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Match win | +3 | Standard result |
| Match loss | +1 | Showed up, competed |
| Forfeit / no-show | 0 | No credit |
| On-time setup | +1 per match | Team ready at scheduled start |
| Wellbeing report — positive | +1 per week | Wellbeing Lead reports strong team cohesion |
| Quality production | +1 per week | Broadcast or coordination rated by GM (teacher) |
| Fair play deduction | −1 per incident | Code of conduct violation, confirmed by teacher |

A team that wins every match but tilts, argues, and shows up late will not top the ladder. A team with average match results but strong culture, clean setup, and good production output stays competitive. This is the structural mechanism for the culture you want — it does not require lecturing.

## Sample Role Rotation Schedule

Adjust to your team size. Every student rotates through production roles. IGL is the exception — it stays stable for 3–4 weeks to allow strategy development.

*For a 5-person team:*

| Role | Wk 3 | Wk 4 | Wk 5 | Wk 6 | Wk 7 | Wk 8 | Wk 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Broadcaster** | A | B | C | D | E | A | B |
| **Social Media Manager** | B | C | D | E | A | B | C |
| **Event Coordinator** | C | D | E | A | B | C | D |
| **Wellbeing Lead** | D | E | A | B | C | D | E |
| **Player / IGL** | E | A | B | C | D (IGL) | D (IGL) | E |

*Note: Players also rotate weekly. The IGL is assigned in Week 7 and holds the role through Week 9.*

**Publish this schedule in Week 2** so every student knows their upcoming role before the season starts. Role clarity reduces conflict and builds accountability.

## The Session Cadence

Every session follows this structure. Predictability reduces transition time and builds routine fast.

```
1.  Arrive / Sign-in / Assemble
2.  Meet & Greet / The Pledge  (whole group)
3.  Healthy Gaming Exercises / Stretching  (10 min — every session)
4.  Execute the Session Activity
5.  Debrief: What did we cover? Why does it matter? How do we use it?
6.  Wrap-up / Clean up
7.  The Pledge / Dismissal
```

**The Pledge** (recited at open and close every session):
> *"Win or lose, I pledge to play esports games as well as I know how; to obey all the rules of the game; to be a good sport at all times, and to commit to improving myself in body, mind and spirit. Good luck, have fun!"*

The teacher leads the Pledge in Week 1. Officers lead it from Week 2 onward. From Week 2, the teacher's role is **General Manager** — you support, you do not run the show.

## Week 1 — Preseason · Organizers
**Session goal:** Students understand what makes this a structured program (not a gaming club), discover the Four Domains, and begin the Domain Sorting Survey.

**Phase:** Preseason
**Roles active:** None formal yet — teacher-led
**Materials:** Projector, Domain Sorting Survey (printed or digital), Season Workbook (distribute today)

### Session Flow

| Time | Activity | Who leads | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrive, sign in, sit with assigned seat groups | Teacher | Seats are mixed — don't let friend groups self-select yet |
| 0:05–0:10 | Intro: "This is not a gaming club" | Teacher | Pose the question: *What's the difference between playing for fun and competing?* Students share one idea before you explain |
| 0:10–0:20 | Healthy Gaming Exercises | Teacher | Follow NASEF routine — stretches, wrist rolls, posture, eye breaks. This is a non-negotiable every session. |
| 0:20–0:30 | The Four Domains overview | Teacher | Use the domain diagram. Walk through each domain and its careers. Show the Alberta employer list. Key message: *There is a role for you regardless of how much you play.* |
| 0:30–0:40 | Career pathways — Alberta angle | Teacher | Name specific companies (BioWare, Beamdog, SAIT, MRU). Ask: *Which of these jobs surprises you?* Give 2 minutes for pair discussion. |
| 0:40–0:55 | Domain Sorting Survey | Independent | Students complete the 20-question self-assessment. No "right" answers — it identifies personal strengths, not gaming skill. |
| 0:55–1:05 | Introduce The Pledge | Teacher | Read it together. Break it down line by line. *What does "improving myself in body, mind and spirit" mean in this context?* |
| 1:05–1:10 | Distribute Season Workbook. Debrief. | Teacher | Three questions: *What is this program trying to do? Why does it include non-players? What domain might you fit?* |
| 1:10–1:15 | The Pledge / Dismissal | Teacher | First recitation together. Teacher leads. |

### Debrief Questions
1. What is the difference between a gaming club and this program?
2. Why does it matter that non-players have real roles, not just "support" roles?
3. Which domain caught your attention? Why?

### Assessment Touchpoint
Domain Sorting Survey results collected or submitted. Teacher reviews before Week 2 to inform team-balancing.

**Phase:** Preseason
**Roles active:** All students observe officer candidates. Officers installed at end of session.
**Materials:** Club Charter template (projected + printed), blank role rotation schedule, team formation cards or sticky notes

|---|---|---|---|

## Week 3 — Content Creator Block · Broadcasting Basics
**Session goal:** Students understand what makes effective sports commentary and practise broadcasting a live match.

**Phase:** Practice Season — Content Creators
**Roles active:** Broadcaster, Social Media Manager, Event Coordinator, Wellbeing Lead. Players compete in scrimmage.
**Materials:** Commentary station (separate screen/audio if possible), scrimmage match set up, role badges or cards

### Session Flow

| Time | Activity | Who leads | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Sign-in / Pledge / Healthy Gaming Exercises | Officers + Wellbeing Lead | Officers lead the Pledge. Wellbeing Lead runs the check-in: *One word for how you're arriving today.* |
| 0:10–0:25 | Broadcasting clinic | Teacher | What does a broadcaster do? Play a 90-second clip of esports commentary (or sports commentary). Ask: *What made that work? What was the caster paying attention to?* Teach three fundamentals: describe the action, build tension, explain significance. Demo a mock call. |
| 0:25–0:30 | Social Media Manager briefing | Teacher | Document today's session: one pre-match post (real or on paper), one in-match update, one post-match reaction. Show a template. |
| 0:30–0:35 | Event Coordinator setup | Event Coordinator | Equipment check, station ready, scoreboard ready. Teacher confirms all clear. |
| 0:35–0:50 | Scrimmage with live broadcast | Broadcaster + Players | First broadcast attempt. Broadcaster calls the match — even a few sentences is a win. Players compete. Set the tone: first tries are supposed to be rough. Social Media Manager documents. |
| 0:50–0:55 | Physical break (mandatory) | — | Students step away from screens. Walk around, stretch. No optional. |
| 0:55–1:05 | Broadcast debrief | Teacher | Play back 30 seconds of the broadcast. One strength. One improvement. Ask the class: *What was hard about describing what was happening in real time?* |
| 1:05–1:10 | Wellbeing check-out + Debrief | Wellbeing Lead | Report one word from the team. Note anything the teacher should know. |
| 1:10–1:15 | The Pledge / Dismissal | Officers | |

### Debrief Questions
1. What is the broadcaster's job — to describe what happened, or to make the audience care what happens next?
2. What did the Social Media Manager document today that would be worth sharing with someone who wasn't in the room?
3. What would make the broadcast better next week?

### Assessment Touchpoint
Wellbeing Lead submits verbal or written cohesion report. Teacher notes broadcast attempt for quality production points. No formal mark — this is Week 3.

## Week 5 — Entrepreneur Block · Mindset and Wellness
**Session goal:** Students learn tilt management and growth mindset as performance tools. Digital citizenship introduced.

**Phase:** Practice Season — Entrepreneurs
**Roles active:** All production roles rotate. Wellbeing Lead role is especially active this session.
**Materials:** Tilt scenario cards (write 4–5 realistic situations), NASEF Health and Wellness Toolkit (free at NASEF.org, co-developed with UCI Health)

### Session Flow

| Time | Activity | Who leads | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Sign-in / Pledge / Healthy Gaming Exercises | Officers + Wellbeing Lead | |
| 0:10–0:25 | What is tilt? | Teacher | Define tilt: an emotional response to a bad outcome that makes performance worse. It is not unique to gaming — ask students where they have felt something similar (sports, tests, arguments). Then bring it back to esports. *Why is tilt an Entrepreneur problem, not just a player problem? Because tilt breaks teams.* |
| 0:25–0:40 | Tilt scenario activity | Small groups | Give each group one scenario card. Groups decide: (1) What triggered the tilt? (2) What would a tilted response look like? (3) What would a regulated response look like? Share back. |
| 0:40–0:50 | Growth mindset in competition | Teacher | Fixed mindset: "We just lost because they are better." Growth mindset: "We lost that exchange — what did we do and what can we adjust?" Practice saying one growth mindset sentence about a recent loss. This is not therapy — it is performance language. |
| 0:50–0:55 | Physical break | — | Mandatory. |
| 0:55–1:05 | Digital citizenship intro | Teacher | Three questions: *What does your digital footprint say about you as a competitor? What is the difference between trash talk and competitive banter? What does a code of conduct violation cost your team on the ladder?* |
| 1:05–1:10 | Wellbeing check-out + Debrief | Wellbeing Lead | Ask: *What is one thing you will do differently next time you feel tilted?* |
| 1:10–1:15 | The Pledge / Dismissal | Officers | |

### Debrief Questions
1. What is tilt? Why does it matter to the whole team, not just the tilted player?
2. What is the difference between a fixed response and a growth response after a loss?
3. How does the points system make fair play a competitive advantage, not just a rule?

### Assessment Touchpoint
Wellbeing Lead cohesion report. Teacher notes participation quality in scenario activity (no formal mark). Season Workbook Q10 (tilt management) can be assigned as home reflection.

- IGL's two pre-game calls (things we will try): 1. _______ 2. _______

### Awards

Awards are not only for winners. Domain awards recognise contribution across the season — they make every role matter at the ceremony.

| Award | What it recognises |
|---|---|
| **Season Champions** | Premiership ladder winner (points, not just match wins) |
| **Best Broadcast** | Most consistent, engaging commentary across the season |
| **Best Analyst** | Most detailed VOD review and pre-match reporting |
| **Spirit of the Season** | Strongest fair play, culture, and cohesion (Wellbeing Lead reports) |
| **Best Brand** | Strongest team identity — logo, social presence, sponsorship pitch |
| **Outstanding Organizer** | Event Coordinator who kept the season running cleanly |
| **GM's Award** | Teacher's discretionary — a student who grew the most or contributed in ways the other awards do not capture |

Awards can be printed certificates, digital badges, or a handwritten card. What matters is the public recognition in front of an audience.

### Tournament Format

*4 teams:*
```
[Team A] ─┐
           ├─ Match 1 winner ─┐
[Team B] ─┘                   ├─ FINAL
[Team C] ─┐                   │
           ├─ Match 2 winner ─┘
```

## Assessment Overview

This program uses **performance and contribution assessment** — not tests. The Season Workbook provides formative evidence of conceptual understanding throughout the season.

| Assessment tool | What it measures | When |
