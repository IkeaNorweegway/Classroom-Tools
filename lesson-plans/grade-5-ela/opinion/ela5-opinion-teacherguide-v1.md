## At a glance

Students read a news article, two editorials that disagree, a photo essay, survey results and a model letter, all on one issue students care about: **should lunch recess be longer?** They learn to tell fact from opinion and news from editorial, find claims, reasons and evidence, judge evidence, notice fair and unfair persuasion, and consider other perspectives. Then they take a position (longer, better, a mix, or the same) and write a letter or editorial with **POW + TREE**, answer the other side, and present at a **Recess Forum** for the principal or school council.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Reading** | Fact and opinion; news article vs editorial vs photo essay; claim, reasons, evidence; perspectives; judging evidence; fair and unfair persuasion; reading survey data |
| **Writing** | SRSD with POW + TREE; counterargument and response; letter and editorial form; openings, endings, transitions |
| **Word work** | per-, com-/con-, -ance/-ence, -ity, -ment, ob-, ad-; strong words; *because / although / unless*; commas after transitions and in letters; pronoun agreement |
| **Fluency** | *Recess Radio* call-in show; repeated reading of opinion passages |
| **Oral language** | Four corners, philosophical chairs, consensus, Recess Forum |
| **Assessment** | Six exit tickets; cold reads in Lessons 14 and 27; rubric with three samples |

**Why it is built this way** (research file): SRSD's strongest results include persuasive writing with TREE (§8); writing about reading (§9); structured discussion (EEF dialogic teaching, §11); one issue across all texts so students build enough knowledge to argue (§2). The issue is real for students, which matters for motivation (§13).

**Before you start:** ask the principal (or council) whether they will listen to the students' pieces at the Recess Forum (Lesson 29). A real audience is the point of the unit. Make clear to students that the decision belongs to the adults; their job is to make the best case.

**The school, people and survey in the reader are made up.** The facts about the Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines (60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day for ages 5–17), Statistics Canada (about 2 in 5 children and youth met the target, 2019 report) and the American Academy of Pediatrics' statement on recess are real. Consider running your own class survey in Lesson 12 so students can use local data.

## The daily block and groups

Same shape as every unit. **Groups by need:** decoding (long words: *recommendation, supervisor, vigorous*), fluency (repeated reading, leveled texts), comprehension (grade-level texts with heavy support: fact vs opinion, finding reasons), on track (a second counterargument; a letter to a second audience).

## Week 1 · Facts, opinions and forms

::: lesson 1 | Hook: four corners | OL5, OL6
SHOW: Label corners: strongly agree, agree, disagree, strongly disagree. Read the three statements in Notebook L1.
DO: Students move, talk with their corner, and share one reason per corner. Notebook L1: record corners, brain dump, first opinion.
CHECK: Keep the first opinions; students compare in Lesson 30.
:::

::: lesson 2 | Fact or opinion? | TF12, CO5
SHOW: Anchor note 1. Model the "can it be checked?" test.
DO: Read Text 1 (news article). Notebook L2: fact/opinion chart; "write about it."
GROUPS: Decoding: *petition, council, moderate, vigorous, supervisors*.
:::

::: lesson 3 | How a news article is built | TF1, TF3
SHOW: Anchor note 2. Find the 5 Ws in the lead.
DO: Notebook L3: 5 Ws chart; fair vs one-sided headlines.
:::

::: lesson 4 | Inside an editorial | WR6, CO9
SHOW: Anchor note 3. Reason vs evidence.
DO: Read Text 2. Notebook L4: chart; strongest reason.
GROUPS: Fluency: Passage 1.
:::

::: lesson 5 | Summing up an argument | CO4
SHOW: Model a two-sentence summary: opinion, then reasons.
DO: Read Text 3. Notebook L5: summaries of both editorials; where they agree.
CHECK: Exit ticket 1.
:::

## Week 2 · Judging arguments

::: lesson 6 | Two perspectives | CO10, CO11, CO13
SHOW: Anchor note 4. "Whose voice is missing?"
DO: Notebook L6: compare the editorials; propose a missing perspective.
:::

::: lesson 7 | Judging evidence | WR21, TF12, CO9
SHOW: Anchor note 5: relevant, reliable, enough.
DO: Notebook L7: rate evidence; which editorial is stronger.
TALK: Pairs must disagree on at least one rating and explain.
:::

::: lesson 8 | The photo essay | TF1, TF12
SHOW: Read Text 4 aloud; ask what the photographer chose to show.
DO: Notebook L8: which side each photo supports; plan two photos for Amira. Optional: take real photos at your own recess (with permission rules for faces).
:::

::: lesson 9 | Fair and unfair persuasion | WR11, VO9, CO8
SHOW: Anchor note 6. Rewrite a name-calling sentence fairly together.
DO: Notebook L9: fair or unfair; one fair persuasive sentence.
:::

::: lesson 10 | Recess forum (philosophical chairs) | OL5, OL6, OL7, OL8
SHOW: Talk moves. Rules: one speaker at a time; you may move sides.
DO: 25 minutes of discussion; Notebook L10: strong points from the other side; a consensus proposal.
CHECK: Exit ticket 2. Observation notes for OL6–8.
:::

## Week 3 · Position and model

::: lesson 11 | Pick my opinion and audience | WR1, WR6
DO: Notebook L11: opinion (any side, including "keep it the same"), audience, evidence bank from the reader.
SAY: "You can argue any side. A strong argument for the side you don't expect is very impressive."
:::

::: lesson 12 | Cause-and-effect reasons and data | CO8, CO9, TF11
SHOW: Anchor note 7. Read Text 5 together; calculate 70%.
DO: Notebook L12. Optional: run your own two-question class survey and graph it; students may use it as evidence.
:::

::: lesson 13 | SRSD stages 1–2: the model letter and TREE | WR6, WR3
SHOW: Anchor note 8. Read Text 6; label TREE. Show the three rubric samples.
DO: Notebook L13: map the letter; set a goal.
CHECK: Exit ticket 3. Start daily mnemonic practice.
:::

::: lesson 14 | Cold read 1 | WR6, CO4, CO9, TF12, WR21, CO13, WR11
DO: Cold read 1 (garden editorial), 35 min. Edition C: read aloud first. Then POW + TREE practice.
:::

::: lesson 15 | Recess Radio and memorize | OL3, OL4
DO: Perform *Recess Radio*. Notebook L15: self-rating; POW + TREE from memory.
CHECK: Most students can write POW + TREE before you model.
:::

## Week 4 · Model and plan

::: lesson 16 | SRSD stage 3: model it | WR6, WR3
SHOW: Plan and write an opinion piece aloud on **"Our school should start fifteen minutes later"** (script below), so you don't take students' recess arguments.
DO: Notebook L16: record what you did and said; three self-talk statements.
:::

::: teacher Think-aloud script for Lesson 16 (adapt freely)
"**P:** what do I believe? 'Our school should start fifteen minutes later.' Who's my reader? The school council. **O:** TREE. **T:** I'll state it right away. **R:** why? Students would be less tired; fewer late arrivals; families have calmer mornings. Will the council believe these? The first one needs evidence. Children your age need 9 to 11 hours of sleep; that's in the Canadian guidelines too. **E:** for late arrivals, I could count late slips for a month. Which reason is strongest? Sleep. I'll put it last. **The other side:** 'The day would end later and buses would change.' That's a fair point. However, we could shorten the lunch break by five minutes and… hmm, that takes recess. Let me think of a better answer: we could ask the bus company whether the whole route can shift fifteen minutes. **E:** ending: 'Please try a later start for one term.' Do I have T, R, E, E? Yes. Good."
:::

::: lesson 17 | SRSD stage 5: we plan together | WR6
DO: Class TREE plan for "Should our class have a class pet?" You scribe. Notebook L17.
:::

::: lesson 18 | Answering the other side | WR6, CO13, OL7
SHOW: Anchor note 9. Read Leo's counterargument.
DO: Notebook L18: write a counterargument and response; test it on a partner who disagrees.
CHECK: Exit ticket 4.
:::

::: lesson 19 | Organize my notes | WR3, WR6
DO: Notebook L19: TREE plan with evidence for each reason, strongest last.
GROUPS: Conferences: is each piece of evidence relevant and reliable?
:::

::: lesson 20 | Openings, endings, transitions | WR4, WR5
SHOW: Compare Amira's opening (a picture in words) and Leo's (a direct opinion).
DO: Notebook L20.
CHECK: Exit ticket 5.
:::

## Week 5 · Draft and feedback

::: lesson 21 | Draft: opening and reason 1 | WR6, WR4
DO: Draft in letter or editorial form, skipping lines. Conferences.
GROUPS: Edition C may dictate; the letter frame is a cognitive support to fade where possible.
:::

::: lesson 22 | Draft: reasons 2–3 | WR6
SHOW: Two-minute lesson: explain the evidence ("This shows that…") instead of just dropping it in.
DO: Continue drafting.
:::

::: lesson 23 | Draft: counterargument and ending; self-check | WR6, WR7
DO: Finish; count TREE parts; graph; single-point self-check.
:::

::: lesson 24 | Peer feedback from someone who disagrees | WR7, OL7
SHOW: Model respectful disagreement: "I'm still not convinced about ___ because ___."
DO: Notebook L24 in pairs (opposite opinions where possible).
:::

::: lesson 25 | Revise | WR7, WR6
DO: Notebook L25: at least two revision moves.
CHECK: Exit ticket 6.
:::

## Week 6 · Publish and present

::: lesson 26 | Edit | WR8, CV2, CV8, CV10
DO: Notebook L26: letter form, commas after transitions, pronoun agreement, quotation marks around quoted evidence, spelling patterns.
:::

::: lesson 27 | Cold read 2 | TF1, CO4, CO9, CO12, TF12, WR6, WR21, CO8
DO: Cold read 2 (homework news report and letter), 35 min. Edition C: read aloud first.
:::

::: lesson 28 | Publish and self-assess | WR9, WR1
DO: Final copy for the audience (signed letters in an envelope to the principal; editorials for the school newsletter). Notebook L28 self-assessment. Score with the rubric.
:::

::: lesson 29 | Recess Forum | OL3, OL4, OL7
DO: Students read their pieces (or best paragraph) to the principal, council or another class. Invite the adults to ask questions and to explain the decision process honestly, including constraints like supervision and the bus schedule.
CHECK: Observation: projection, emphasis, respectful answers.
:::

::: lesson 30 | Look back | CO10, WR7
DO: Notebook L30: opinion now vs Lesson 1; what changed it; what helped as a writer. Self-check.
SAY: "Next unit we go back to fiction, into the future: science fiction, where authors ask 'what if?'"
:::
