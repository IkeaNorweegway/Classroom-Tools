## At a glance

Students read six information texts about body systems and a model report, learn how information texts are organized (topic and subtopics, main idea, cause and effect, compare and contrast, sequence) and how text features work, then research one body system or organ and write a multi-paragraph report with **POW + TIDE**. They check sources for reliability, take notes in their own words, and publish with text features. The unit ends with a **Body Expo** for another class.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Reading** | Text features, topic and subtopics, main idea and summary, cause and effect, compare and contrast, science vocabulary from context and word parts, evaluating sources |
| **Writing** | SRSD with POW + TIDE: research questions, notes, TIDE paragraphs, introduction and conclusion, transitions, text features, sources list |
| **Word work** | -al/-ial, ex-/in-, trans-/sub-, -ous/-ic, -ment/-tion; Tier 2 and Tier 3 words; commas in lists, subject–verb agreement, parentheses |
| **Fluency** | *Body Bulletin* newscast (reader's theatre), repeated reading of information passages |
| **Oral language** | Structured discussion, Body Expo talk |
| **Assessment** | Six exit tickets; cold reads in Lessons 14 and 27; report rubric with three samples |

**Why it is built this way** (`research/literacy-reading-writing-research.md`): text structure instruction (0.96 for struggling readers, §3), summary writing (0.79 at elementary, §9), SRSD (§8), and one topic for the shared texts so knowledge builds (§2). The anchor topic links to Grade 5 Science; tell the science teacher, and use their vocabulary.

**Gather before Lesson 11:** library books on the human body at a range of levels (F&P leveled non-fiction works well), and 2–3 bookmarked child-friendly websites (a children's hospital, a science centre). Students need two reliable sources each.

**Facts in the texts** are standard averages, rounded. If a student finds a different number in a book (e.g., 20,000 vs. 22,000 breaths a day), that is a good lesson: sources round differently, and that is why we cite them.

## The daily block

Same shape as every unit: warm-up (5) · mini-lesson (12) · work time with two groups and conferences (35) · exit or write-about-it (8). **Groups by need:** decoding (long-word routine on science words: *res-pi-ra-to-ry*, *cir-cu-la-to-ry*), fluency (repeated reading, leveled non-fiction), comprehension (grade-level text with heavy support; main idea and text structure), on track (extra research, a second sidebar, conferences).

## Editions

| Edition | Supports | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B | Word box per anchor page; ✎ model row in each notebook chart; sentence starters for written answers; starters in cold reads | Cognitive: fade by Week 4 (fold the word box; cross out starters) |
| C | Short retold texts (decoding/fluency); Edition A read aloud for comprehension and research; drawing and dictation; a sentence frame for the report | Read-aloud and dictation are **access** supports for comprehension and composing; not for fluency or spelling tasks |

Research is where Edition C students most need access: read sources aloud, or use text-to-speech on websites. Their report may be shorter (two TIDE paragraphs) but keeps the outcome (inform, with facts in their own words).

## Week 1 · How information texts work

::: lesson 1 | Hook: body by numbers | CO1, OL6
SHOW: Ask students to find their pulse (wrist or neck) and count for 15 seconds. "How many times does it beat in a day?" Collect guesses.
DO: Notebook L1: brain dump; body-by-numbers true/false (guess only); three questions about the body. Post questions on a "wonder wall".
CHECK: Note misconceptions to address (muscles push; heart on the left side of the chest only; "we breathe in oxygen and out nothing").
:::

::: lesson 2 | Text features | TF5, TF1
SHOW: Anchor note 1. Preview Text 1 with students: read only the title, headings, bold words, sidebar and glossary. "What do we already know about this text before reading a paragraph?"
DO: Shared reading of Text 1. Notebook L2: feature hunt, then "write about it".
GROUPS: Decoding: *organ, tissue, circulatory, respiratory, digestive*.
CHECK: Do students use headings to predict? Note who skips captions.
:::

::: lesson 3 | Topic and subtopics | TF11, CO5
SHOW: Anchor note 2. Think aloud mapping Text 4 from its headings.
DO: Notebook L3: map Text 1; explain "no system works alone" with the soccer example.
CHECK: Students who list facts instead of subtopics get a quick re-teach with the headings.
:::

::: lesson 4 | Main idea and details: the heart | CO5, CO9
SHOW: Anchor note 3. Model the main idea test on "A pump with four rooms".
DO: Read Text 2. Notebook L4: main idea and details per section; pulse experiment (one minute of running in place) and explanation.
GROUPS: Fluency: Passage 1 (the heart).
:::

::: lesson 5 | Summarizing a section | CO4
SHOW: Model a 20-word summary of one section; cross out small details aloud.
DO: Notebook L5: summaries of two sections; count words.
CHECK: Exit ticket 1.
:::

## Week 2 · Structures, words and sources

::: lesson 6 | Cause and effect: breathing | TF11, CO6
SHOW: Anchor note 4. Signal words. Model the first row of the chart.
DO: Read Text 3. Notebook L6: cause-effect chart; explain puffing after a race.
TALK: "Hand on chest, breathe in slowly. What moved? Why?" (the diaphragm).
:::

::: lesson 7 | Compare and contrast: bones and muscles | TF11, CO9
SHOW: Anchor note 5. Demonstrate biceps and triceps with your own arm.
DO: Read Text 4. Notebook L7: chart and compare-contrast paragraph; why muscles work in pairs.
GROUPS: Comprehension: build the paragraph together with the frame.
:::

::: lesson 8 | Science words | VO4, VO11, VO12
SHOW: Anchor note 6. Model the three clue types and word parts (ex-, -ous, circ-, trans-).
DO: Notebook L8; begin word-work unit words.
CHECK: Can students explain the clue they used, not just the meaning?
:::

::: lesson 9 | Can I trust it? | WR21, CO17, TF12
SHOW: Anchor note 7. The gummies example.
DO: Read Text 5 (the brain). Notebook L9: compare Source A and Source B; write which to use and why.
TALK: Pairs: "Name a website or video you trust. Would it pass our four questions?"
:::

::: lesson 10 | Notes in my own words | WR23, WR24
SHOW: Anchor note 8. Model key-word notes and "close the book and say it."
DO: Read Text 6. Notebook L10: notes on the path of food; paragraph from notes only.
CHECK: Exit ticket 2. Look for copied phrases in the paragraphs.
:::

## Week 3 · Research and the model report

::: lesson 11 | Pick and narrow a topic | WR19, WR22
SHOW: Model narrowing: "the body" → "the heart" → three questions (What is it? How does it work? How do we keep it healthy?).
DO: Notebook L11: choose a topic; three research questions; list two reliable sources (from the gathered books and bookmarked sites).
GROUPS: Conference with students choosing very broad topics.
:::

::: lesson 12 | Research notes | WR23, WR24, WR22
DO: Notebook L12: notes for each question, with sources. Students read sources and take notes; you circulate and check for copying.
GROUPS: Edition C: read sources aloud with them; they record notes as drawings or key words.
CHECK: Every note has a source.
:::

::: lesson 13 | SRSD stages 1–2: the model report and TIDE | WR3, WR4, WR20
SHOW: Anchor note 9 (POW + TIDE). Read Text 7 together; label T, I, D, E in "The heart" paragraph on the board.
DO: Notebook L13: map the model report; find TIDE in "The blood"; set a goal. Show the three rubric samples; discuss what makes each level.
CHECK: Exit ticket 3. Begin daily mnemonic practice.
:::

::: lesson 14 | Cold read 1 | TF5, TF11, CO4, CO5, CO6, CO9, VO11, VO12
DO: Cold read 1, *Your Skin* (35 min). Edition C: read the A text aloud first. Then POW + TIDE memory practice.
CHECK: Score by code; set Week 4 groups.
:::

::: lesson 15 | Body Bulletin and memorize | OL3, OL5, TF5
DO: Perform *Body Bulletin* (fluency pack); record and replay if possible. Notebook L15: self-rating; POW + TIDE from memory.
CHECK: Don't move to modelling until most students can write POW + TIDE from memory.
:::

## Week 4 · Model and plan

::: lesson 16 | SRSD stage 3: model it | WR3, WR4, WR20
SHOW: Turn your own notes on **the brain** into a TIDE paragraph, thinking aloud (script below). Write it on chart paper.
DO: Notebook L16: record what you did and said; write three self-talk statements.
:::

::: teacher Think-aloud script for Lesson 16 (adapt freely)
"My subtopic is *the three parts of the brain*. **T:** what is this paragraph about, in one sentence? 'The brain has three main parts, and each has its own job.' **I:** which facts matter most? Cerebrum: thinking and memory. Cerebellum: balance. Brain stem: breathing and heartbeat. My notes say 'cerebrum handles thinking, learning, memory, the senses'… that's almost the book's sentence. Let me say it my way: 'The cerebrum, the biggest part, does your thinking and remembering.' **D:** can I add an example so the reader can picture it? 'The cerebellum is the part that keeps you from tipping over when you ride a bike.' **E:** how do I wrap it up? 'Together, the three parts keep you thinking, moving and alive.' Do I have T, I, D and E? Yes. Good, that paragraph is done."
:::

::: lesson 17 | SRSD stage 5: we write together | WR4, WR20
DO: Class writes a TIDE paragraph on the lungs from Text 3 (you scribe; students suggest each part). Notebook L17: copy it.
CHECK: Students should name the missing TIDE part before you add it.
:::

::: lesson 18 | Hooks and conclusions | WR4
SHOW: Anchor note 10. Read the model report's intro and conclusion; notice the delivery-truck link.
DO: Notebook L18: two hooks for their own report; plan the conclusion.
CHECK: Exit ticket 4.
:::

::: lesson 19 | Organize: TIDE for each paragraph | WR3, WR4, WR20
DO: Notebook L19: a TIDE plan for each research question. Conferences: one subtopic per paragraph; details that explain.
GROUPS: Edition B/C students first.
:::

::: lesson 20 | Transitions and text features | WR5, TF6
SHOW: Anchors 10–11. Model joining sentences with transitions; sketch a labelled diagram with a caption.
DO: Notebook L20: transition practice; choose text features; sketch the diagram.
CHECK: Exit ticket 5.
:::

## Week 5 · Draft and feedback

::: lesson 21 | Draft: introduction and paragraph 1 | WR4, WR18
DO: Draft with headings, skipping lines. Conferences with 4–5 students.
GROUPS: Edition C students may dictate (composing outcome: dictation is access).
:::

::: lesson 22 | Draft: paragraphs 2–3 | WR4, WR20
SHOW: Two-minute mini-lesson: turning a number into a comparison detail (100,000 km of blood vessels → "more than twice around the Earth").
DO: Continue drafting.
:::

::: lesson 23 | Draft: conclusion; self-check | WR4, WR7
DO: Finish the draft; count TIDE parts; colour the graph; single-point self-check with the rubric.
SAY: Attribute progress to the strategy: "You have all twelve TIDE parts because you planned with TIDE."
:::

::: lesson 24 | Structured peer feedback | WR7, OL6, OL7
SHOW: Model the routine with a volunteer.
DO: Notebook L24 in pairs; author writes one change to make first.
:::

::: lesson 25 | Revise | WR7, WR5
SHOW: Revise your brain paragraph: add a comparison detail; cut a fact that doesn't fit; add a transition.
DO: Notebook L25: at least two revision moves; before and after.
CHECK: Exit ticket 6.
:::

## Week 6 · Publish and share

::: lesson 26 | Edit | WR8, CV1, CV2, CV5
DO: Notebook L26: one read per target (capitals in headings, commas in lists, subject–verb agreement, parentheses, spelling patterns, science words).
GROUPS: Subject–verb agreement mini-group (word work Week 3).
:::

::: lesson 27 | Cold read 2 | CO4, TF11, CO9, WR21, TF6, VO12
DO: Cold read 2, *Your Eyes* (35 min). Edition C: read aloud first. Early finishers: final copy.
CHECK: Compare with cold read 1 by code.
:::

::: lesson 28 | Publish and self-assess | WR9, WR18, TF6
DO: Final copy as a booklet, poster or typed pages with at least three text features and a sources list. Self-assess (Notebook L28). Score with the rubric; one written feedback point for the next unit.
:::

::: lesson 29 | Body Expo | OL3, OL5, OL6
DO: Students set up reports around the room; a visiting class (or families) circulates. Each student gives a one-minute talk: hook, three facts, the diagram, a question for the audience. Visitors write one thing they learned.
CHECK: Observation: projects voice; explains the diagram; answers a question.
:::

::: lesson 30 | Look back, look ahead | WR7, CO2
DO: Notebook L30: what helped as a researcher and writer; compare an information report with a tall tale; one question still to answer. Self-check box.
SAY: "Next unit you'll use facts as **evidence** to convince someone. That's opinion writing, with a new partner for POW: TREE."
:::
