## At a glance

Students read six information texts about Alberta's six natural regions (Grassland, Parkland, Foothills, Rocky Mountain, Boreal Forest, Canadian Shield) and a model report, learn how information texts are organized (topic and subtopics, main idea, cause and effect, compare and contrast, sequence) and how text features work, then research one region, or one special place in a region, and write a multi-paragraph report with **POW + TIDE**. They check sources for reliability, take notes in their own words, and publish with text features. The unit ends with a **Regions Expo** for another class.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Reading** | Text features, topic and subtopics, main idea and summary, cause and effect, compare and contrast, topic vocabulary from context and word parts, evaluating sources |
| **Writing** | SRSD with POW + TIDE: research questions, notes, TIDE paragraphs, introduction and conclusion, transitions, text features (including a labelled map), sources list |
| **Word work** | -al/-ial, ex-/in-, trans-/sub-, -ous/-ic, -ment/-tion; Tier 2 and Tier 3 words; capitals for place names, commas in lists, subject–verb agreement, parentheses |
| **Fluency** | *Alberta Tonight* newscast (reader's theatre), repeated reading of information passages |
| **Oral language** | Structured discussion, Regions Expo talk |
| **Assessment** | Six exit tickets; cold reads in Lessons 14 and 27; report rubric with three samples |

**Why it is built this way** (`research/literacy-reading-writing-research.md`): text structure instruction (0.96 for struggling readers, §3), summary writing (0.79 at elementary, §9), SRSD (§8), and one topic for the shared texts so knowledge builds (§2). Alberta's land suits every text structure: weather is cause and effect, neighbouring regions invite comparison, the Ice Age is a sequence. The topic links to Social Studies (geography of Alberta) and to Science (landforms, weather, living things); tell those teachers and use their vocabulary.

**Gather before Lesson 1:** a large printed **natural regions map of Alberta** (Alberta Parks publishes a free one) for the wall; students check their L1 guess about the school's region on it and use it in the Expo.

**Gather before Lesson 11:** library books on Alberta's regions, parks and animals at a range of levels, and 2–3 bookmarked child-friendly websites (the provincial and national parks sites, the Royal Tyrrell Museum, a science centre). Students need two reliable sources each. The Grassland, Rocky Mountain and Boreal Forest have the most child-friendly sources; the Canadian Shield and the Foothills have the fewest, so steer confident researchers there or let them research one place inside a region (the badlands, the Columbia Icefield, a park near the school). The model report is on the Foothills: a student may still choose it, but needs their own sources and subtopics.

**Elders and Knowledge Keepers as sources (optional, strongly worth it):** the program names Elders and Knowledge Keepers as sources of knowledge. If you can invite one to speak about the land where the school stands, follow your division's protocol for the invitation and the gift. Students may cite what they learned as a spoken source (name and Nation, with permission). Teach which Treaty area the school is on (6, 7 or 8) in Lesson 1.

**Facts in the texts** are rounded. Sources disagree: Text 3 says plainly that the famous 1962 Pincher Creek chinook is reported as "one hour" by some sources and "a few hours" by others. That is a good lesson: check two sources, and cite them. Names and spellings from First Nations languages (Niitsitapi, Áísínai'pi, Nakoda) follow common usage; check them with your division's First Nations, Métis and Inuit lead.

## The daily block

Same shape as every unit: warm-up (5) · mini-lesson (12) · work time with two groups and conferences (35) · exit or write-about-it (8). **Groups by need:** decoding (long-word routine on topic words and place names: *sub-al-pine*, *Ath-a-bas-ca*, *re-gion-al*, *Ca-na-di-an*), fluency (repeated reading, leveled non-fiction), comprehension (grade-level text with heavy support; main idea and text structure), on track (extra research, a second sidebar, conferences).

| C | Short retold texts (decoding/fluency); Edition A read aloud for comprehension and research; drawing and dictation; a sentence frame for the report | Read-aloud and dictation are **access** supports for comprehension and composing; not for fluency or spelling tasks |

Research is where Edition C students most need access: read sources aloud, or use text-to-speech on websites. Their report may be shorter (two TIDE paragraphs) but keeps the outcome (inform, with facts in their own words).

## Week 1 · How information texts work

::: lesson 1 | Hook: Alberta by numbers | CO1, OL6
SHOW: The natural regions map. "If we drove from the Montana border to the Northwest Territories, about 1,200 km, what would we see out the window?" Collect ideas. Name the Treaty area the school is on.
DO: Notebook L1: brain dump; Alberta-by-numbers true/false (guess only); guess the school's region; three questions about Alberta's land. Post questions on a "wonder wall".
CHECK: Note misconceptions to address (Alberta is all prairie; the north is all tundra; chinooks come from the south; the mountains are cold because they're in the north).
:::

::: lesson 2 | Text features | TF5, TF1
SHOW: Anchor note 1. Preview Text 1 with students: read only the title, headings, bold words, sidebar and glossary. "What do we already know about this text before reading a paragraph?"
DO: Shared reading of Text 1. Check the school's region on the wall map. Notebook L2: feature hunt, then "write about it".
GROUPS: Decoding: *region, natural, climate, landform, Canadian*.
CHECK: Do students use headings to predict? Note who skips captions.
:::

::: lesson 3 | Topic and subtopics | TF11, CO5
SHOW: Anchor note 2. Think aloud mapping Text 4 from its headings.
DO: Notebook L3: map Text 1; explain "no region stands alone" with the drop-of-water example.
CHECK: Students who list facts instead of subtopics get a quick re-teach with the headings.
:::

::: lesson 4 | Main idea and details: the Grassland | CO5, CO9
SHOW: Anchor note 3. Model the main idea test on "The rain shadow" (Text 3), then preview Text 2.
DO: Read Text 2. Notebook L4: main idea and details per section; why prairie grass survives drought and fire.
GROUPS: Fluency: Passage 1 (the Grassland).
:::

::: lesson 5 | Summarizing a section | CO4
SHOW: Model a 20-word summary of one section; cross out small details aloud.
DO: Notebook L5: summaries of two sections; count words.
CHECK: Exit ticket 1.
:::

## Week 2 · Structures, words and sources

::: lesson 6 | Cause and effect: mountains make the weather | TF11, CO6
SHOW: Anchor note 4. Signal words. Model the first row of the chart. Act out the air: students "rise" (stand up on tiptoe), "cool" (shiver), "drop snow" (flutter fingers), then "sink" (crouch) and "warm up".
DO: Read Text 3. Notebook L6: cause-effect chart; explain why the Grassland is dry.
TALK: "Who has seen a chinook arch, or watched snow vanish in a day? What happened?"
:::

::: lesson 7 | Compare and contrast: Parkland and Boreal Forest | TF11, CO9
SHOW: Anchor note 5. Show two photos side by side: an aspen grove beside a canola field, and spruce forest with muskeg.
DO: Read Text 4. Notebook L7: chart and compare-contrast paragraph; why more people live in the Parkland.
GROUPS: Comprehension: build the paragraph together with the frame.
:::

::: lesson 8 | Topic words | VO4, VO11, VO12
SHOW: Anchor note 6. Model the three clue types and word parts (sub-, -al, -ous, trans-).
DO: Notebook L8; begin word-work unit words.
CHECK: Can students explain the clue they used, not just the meaning?
:::

::: lesson 9 | Can I trust it? | WR21, CO17, TF12
SHOW: Anchor note 7. The Glacier-Power Water example.
DO: Read Text 5 (the Rocky Mountains). Notebook L9: compare Source A and Source B; write which to use and why.
TALK: Pairs: "Name a website or video you trust. Would it pass our four questions?"
:::

::: lesson 10 | Notes in my own words | WR23, WR24
SHOW: Anchor note 8. Model key-word notes and "close the book and say it."
DO: Read Text 6. Notebook L10: notes on how ice shaped Alberta; paragraph from notes only.
CHECK: Exit ticket 2. Look for copied phrases in the paragraphs.
:::

## Week 3 · Research and the model report

::: lesson 11 | Pick and narrow a topic | WR19, WR22
SHOW: Model narrowing: "Alberta" → "the Boreal Forest" → three questions (Where is it and what is the land like? What plants and animals live there? How do people live and work there?).
DO: Notebook L11: choose a topic; three research questions; list two reliable sources (from the gathered books and bookmarked sites).
GROUPS: Conference with students choosing very broad topics ("Canada") or very narrow ones ("moose").
:::

::: lesson 12 | Research notes | WR23, WR24, WR22
DO: Notebook L12: notes for each question, with sources. Students read sources and take notes; you circulate and check for copying.
GROUPS: Edition C: read sources aloud with them; they record notes as drawings or key words.
CHECK: Every note has a source.
:::

::: lesson 13 | SRSD stages 1–2: the model report and TIDE | WR3, WR4, WR20
SHOW: Anchor note 9 (POW + TIDE). Read Text 7 together; label T, I, D, E in "The land" paragraph on the board.
DO: Notebook L13: map the model report; find TIDE in "Plants and animals"; set a goal. Show the three rubric samples; discuss what makes each level.
CHECK: Exit ticket 3. Begin daily mnemonic practice.
:::

::: lesson 14 | Cold read 1 | TF5, TF11, CO4, CO5, CO6, CO9, VO11, VO12
DO: Cold read 1, *The Badlands* (35 min). Edition C: read the A text aloud first. Then POW + TIDE memory practice.
CHECK: Score by code; set Week 4 groups.
:::

::: lesson 15 | Alberta Tonight and memorize | OL3, OL5, TF5
DO: Perform *Alberta Tonight* (fluency pack); record and replay if possible. Notebook L15: self-rating; POW + TIDE from memory.
CHECK: Don't move to modelling until most students can write POW + TIDE from memory.
:::

## Week 4 · Model and plan

::: lesson 16 | SRSD stage 3: model it | WR3, WR4, WR20
SHOW: Turn your own notes on **the three zones of a mountain** (Text 5) into a TIDE paragraph, thinking aloud (script below). Write it on chart paper.
DO: Notebook L16: record what you did and said; write three self-talk statements.
:::

::: teacher Think-aloud script for Lesson 16 (adapt freely)
"My subtopic is *the three zones of a mountain*. **T:** what is this paragraph about, in one sentence? 'A mountain in the Rockies has three zones, and each one is its own little world.' **I:** which facts matter most? Montane: lowest and warmest, forests and meadows, elk. Subalpine: spruce and fir, deep snow. Alpine: above the treeline, low plants, pikas. My notes say 'the height where it becomes too cold and windy for trees to grow'… that's almost the book's sentence. Let me say it my way: 'Above the treeline, no tree can stand the cold and the wind.' **D:** can I add a comparison so the reader can picture it? 'Climbing from the valley to the peak is a bit like travelling north toward the Arctic: the higher you go, the colder it gets and the smaller the plants.' **E:** how do I wrap it up? 'From valley to peak, one mountain holds three different worlds.' Do I have T, I, D and E? Yes. Good, that paragraph is done."
:::

::: lesson 17 | SRSD stage 5: we write together | WR4, WR20
DO: Class writes a TIDE paragraph on the chinook from Text 3 (you scribe; students suggest each part). Notebook L17: copy it.
CHECK: Students should name the missing TIDE part before you add it.
:::

::: lesson 18 | Hooks and conclusions | WR4
SHOW: Anchor note 10. Read the model report's intro and conclusion; notice the giant-staircase link.
DO: Notebook L18: two hooks for their own report; plan the conclusion.
CHECK: Exit ticket 4.
:::

::: lesson 19 | Organize: TIDE for each paragraph | WR3, WR4, WR20
DO: Notebook L19: a TIDE plan for each research question. Conferences: one subtopic per paragraph; details that explain.
GROUPS: Edition B/C students first.
:::

::: lesson 20 | Transitions and text features | WR5, TF6
SHOW: Anchors 10–11. Model joining sentences with transitions; sketch a labelled map of Alberta with one region coloured, and a caption.
DO: Notebook L20: transition practice; choose text features; sketch the map or diagram.
CHECK: Exit ticket 5.
:::

## Week 5 · Draft and feedback

::: lesson 21 | Draft: introduction and paragraph 1 | WR4, WR18
DO: Draft with headings, skipping lines. Conferences with 4–5 students.
GROUPS: Edition C students may dictate (composing outcome: dictation is access).
:::

::: lesson 22 | Draft: paragraphs 2–3 | WR4, WR20
SHOW: Two-minute mini-lesson: turning a number into a comparison detail ("the Boreal Forest covers more than half of Alberta" → "it is bigger than all the other five regions put together").
DO: Continue drafting.
:::

::: lesson 23 | Draft: conclusion; self-check | WR4, WR7
DO: Finish the draft; count TIDE parts; colour the graph; single-point self-check with the rubric.
SAY: Attribute progress to the strategy: "You have all twelve TIDE parts because you planned with TIDE."
:::

::: lesson 24 | Structured peer feedback | WR7, OL6, OL7
SHOW: Model the routine with a volunteer.
DO: Notebook L24 in pairs; author writes one change to make first.
:::

::: lesson 25 | Revise | WR7, WR5
SHOW: Revise your mountain-zones paragraph: add a number detail (the 3,747 m of Mount Columbia); cut a fact that doesn't fit (the bear-safety rule); add a transition.
DO: Notebook L25: at least two revision moves; before and after.
CHECK: Exit ticket 6.
:::

## Week 6 · Publish and share

::: lesson 26 | Edit | WR8, CV1, CV2, CV5
DO: Notebook L26: one read per target (capitals in headings and place names, commas in lists, subject–verb agreement, parentheses, spelling patterns, topic words).
GROUPS: Subject–verb agreement mini-group (word work Week 3).
:::

::: lesson 27 | Cold read 2 | CO4, TF11, CO9, WR21, TF6, VO12
DO: Cold read 2, *Wood Buffalo National Park* (35 min). Edition C: read aloud first. Early finishers: final copy.
CHECK: Compare with cold read 1 by code.
:::

::: lesson 28 | Publish and self-assess | WR9, WR18, TF6
DO: Final copy as a booklet, poster or typed pages with at least three text features and a sources list. Self-assess (Notebook L28). Score with the rubric; one written feedback point for the next unit.
:::

::: lesson 29 | Regions Expo | OL3, OL5, OL6
DO: Tape a big outline of Alberta on the floor (or use the wall map) and set up reports in their regions. A visiting class (or families) "travels" across the province. Each student gives a one-minute talk: hook, three facts, the map or diagram, a question for the audience. Visitors write one thing they learned.
CHECK: Observation: projects voice; explains the map or diagram; answers a question.
:::

::: lesson 30 | Look back, look ahead | WR7, CO2
DO: Notebook L30: what helped as a researcher and writer; compare an information report with a tall tale; one question still to answer. Self-check box.
SAY: "Next unit you'll use facts as **evidence** to convince someone. That's opinion writing, with a new partner for POW: TREE."
:::
