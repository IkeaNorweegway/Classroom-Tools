**The short version:** a short explicit lesson, a long stretch of reading and writing, writing about reading every day, and needs-based groups. Each unit ends with a piece written for a real audience. (§ = sections of the literacy research file.)

## The materials

| Material | When | How to use it | Why it's there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Unit reader** | Mini-lessons and work time | Read the texts aloud or together; every student meets the grade-level text. Paragraphs and stanzas are numbered for evidence | One topic across texts builds the knowledge comprehension runs on (§2). Grade-level text for everyone; leveled books for practice (§4) |
| **Anchor notes** | The 12-minute mini-lesson | One page per skill. Model with the mentor text and think aloud; students fill the blanks as you go | Strategies and craft taught explicitly and briefly, then applied (§3). The mentor text is the worked example (§8) |
| **Reader's & Writer's Notebook** | Work time, every lesson | One page per lesson: write about the text, then plan, draft, get feedback, revise and reflect | Writing about reading improves comprehension: 0.40, summaries 0.79 at elementary level (§9). SRSD writing has the largest effect, 1.17 (§8) |
| **Word work** | Warm-ups; weekly with your WTW sorts | Teach the pattern first, sort, then spell from memory. Morphology, unit words, sentence combining, one conventions target | Explicit spelling: 0.54, and it carries into writing at 0.94 (§7). Grammar inside sentences, not on worksheets (§10) |
| **Fluency pack** | Groups 3–5 times a week; reader's theatre | Repeated reading with feedback and a goal; perform the script; score expression, not just speed | Repeated reading: 0.75 fluency, 0.73 comprehension (§5). Rehearsal gives students a reason to reread |
| **Exit tickets** | Last 5–8 minutes after each skill cluster | Sort into *got it / almost / not yet* before the next lesson; regroup and re-teach the next day | Feedback only works if someone acts on it (Principle 6) |
| **Cold reads** | Mid-unit and end of unit | An unseen text, done independently; record results by outcome code and use them to set groups | Comprehension only shows on new text; one score hides strengths (§12) |
| **Rubric + samples** | Before drafting; after the draft; before publishing | Study the three samples, self-assess with the checklist, revise, then score | Rubrics help (g = 0.45) when students use them to revise, not only for grading (rubric research) |
| **Teacher guide** | Planning | Lesson cards, think-aloud scripts, group plan, outcome map | SRSD stages: build background, discuss, model, memorize, support, independent. Move on at mastery, not by the calendar (§8) |
| **Home sheets** | Parent guide | One skill, one page, with a worked example | Short practice that families can support |

## The 60-minute block

**5** warm-up (recall, no notes) · **12** mini-lesson (anchor note + mentor text) · **35** work time: notebook and reading, plus two groups and conferences · **8** exit ticket or written response

**Groups by need, not only level:** decoding (long-word routine), fluency (repeated reading with F&P leveled text), comprehension (grade-level text with heavy support), on track (extension and conferences).

## Editions: same texts, same questions, same lesson

| | For | What's different | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Most students | Core and stretch items | — |
| **B** | Grade-level thinking, needs support to show it | Word boxes, ✎ model rows, sentence starters | **Cognitive supports: fade them** by about Week 4 |
| **C** | Reading well below Grade 5 | Short retold texts, easy-read layout, circle / draw / dictate | **Read-aloud is an access support** for comprehension and writing. Not on fluency or spelling tasks |

Choose by task, not by child. Listen to a grade-level text with the student: if they understand it heard but not read, it's a decoding or fluency gap.

::: note Don't skip
**Modelling:** think aloud before students try. **The real audience:** book the visitors early. **Mnemonic practice:** don't start modelling until most students can recite POW + the unit strategy. **Writing lines are 12 mm apart** (15 mm in Edition C); print with "Background graphics" on so the ruled lines in charts appear.
:::
