## At a glance

Students read five tall tales (three retold folk heroes, one real Canadian raftsman who became a legend, and an original Alberta tale), learn how stories are built and how characters are shown, and use the SRSD strategy **POW + WWW** to plan, draft, revise and publish their own tall tale. The unit ends with a storytelling festival for a younger class, which is the real audience for the writing.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Reading** | Story structure, character evidence, exaggeration and comparisons, summarizing, inference, theme, context and perspective, oral tradition |
| **Writing** | SRSD: background → discuss → model → memorize → support → independent. Show don't tell, dialogue, exaggeration that shows |
| **Word work** | Grade 5 affixes (-ous, -ious, -al, -ial, -ian, -ic, -ical, -ment, -ity, trans-, sub-, ex-, com-, -ant/-ent, -ance/-ence), 8 Tier 2 words, spelling from memory with WTW sorts, sentence combining |
| **Fluency** | Reader's theatre (*Paul Bunyan and the Blue Snow*), repeated reading, partner reading |
| **Oral language** | Retelling aloud, structured discussion, storytelling festival |
| **Assessment** | Six exit tickets; cold reads in Lessons 14 and 27; writing rubric with three samples; self-assessment graph |

**Why it is built this way** (`research/literacy-reading-writing-research.md`): SRSD has the largest effect of any writing approach (1.17, §8); writing about reading every lesson (0.40; summaries 0.79 at elementary, §9); explicit strategy teaching kept brief and then applied (§3); one topic for the shared texts (§2); repeated reading with feedback and reader's theatre for fluency (§5); explicit spelling with spelling from memory (§7); grammar inside sentences (§10).

## The daily block

| Min | Part |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Warm-up: retrieval from word work and earlier lessons, no notes |
| 5–17 | Mini-lesson (anchor note + mentor text + think-aloud) |
| 17–52 | Work time: reading and the notebook page; you run two groups and quick conferences |
| 52–60 | Exit ticket or notebook "write about it" |

**Groups by need** (set from the September screen, then from exit tickets and cold reads):
- **Decoding:** students who misread long words. Long-word routine (circle prefix and suffix, underline vowels, say parts, say the word) with words from the tale, then read a paragraph. 3 times a week.
- **Fluency:** accurate but slow or flat readers. Repeated reading from the fluency pack, then F&P leveled passages at about 95% accuracy. 3–5 times a week, 10 minutes.
- **Comprehension:** students who decode well but miss meaning. Reread the day's grade-level tale with you; stop to summarize and find evidence.
- **On track:** extension (compare two tales; write an extra exaggeration ladder) or writing conferences.

**F&P and Words Their Way:** keep guided reading groups, but group by need; use the reader's grade-level tales in mini-lessons and leveled books for practice and independent reading. Keep the WTW sort cycle; the word-work booklet adds explicit teaching, spelling from memory and morphology.

## The editions

All three editions use the same tales, the same lesson numbers and the same question numbers, so a student can move between them and the Edition A answer key works for all three.

| Edition | Who | What's different | Scaffold type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Most students | Core and stretch items | — |
| **B (scaffolded)** | Can think at grade level but needs support to show it | Word box per anchor page; a filled model row (✎) in each chart; sentence starters for every written answer; a story frame on the draft page | Word box and starters are **cognitive scaffolds: fade them.** Week 1–2: full; Week 3–4: fold the word box under and cross out starters you no longer need; Week 5–6: Edition A notes, starters only on request |
| **C (easy read)** | Reading well below Grade 5 (F&P Grade 1–3 range) | Easy-read layout (short sentences, support strip, line height 2); retold tales for decoding and fluency; grade-level tale read aloud for comprehension; circle / draw / dictate options | **Read-aloud and dictation are access scaffolds** on comprehension and composing outcomes (keep them). They are *not* used on fluency or spelling tasks. |

**Choose by task, not by child.** A student can use Edition A for writing and Edition C for reading. Quick screen: read a grade-level tale aloud and ask three questions. Good listening comprehension + weak reading = decoding or fluency gap (Edition C reader + fluency group, Edition A or B for comprehension work). Weak either way = language or knowledge gap (Edition B, comprehension group, extra vocabulary).

**Track B (modified outcomes)** is only for a student whose IPP team has set modified outcomes; these editions keep the Grade 5 outcomes.

## Week 1 · Meet the genre

::: lesson 1 | Hook: true or tall? | TF7, OL2
SHOW: No materials yet. Tell (don't read) the opening of *Paul Bunyan* (reader ¶1–3) as a storyteller would: straight face, slowing down for the frozen words.
SAY: "Is that true? How do you know? Who would tell a story like that, and why?"
DO: Notebook L1: brain dump (5 min, no stakes), then the true/tall sort with a partner, then two predictions about what every tall tale has.
GROUPS: None today; circulate and read the brain dumps. Note who says "a tall tale is a lie".
CHECK: Collect brain dumps; list the three most common ideas for tomorrow's warm-up.
:::

::: lesson 2 | What makes a tall tale | TF7, TF3
SHOW: Anchor note 1. Reader Tale 1 ¶1–4 on the board.
SAY: Model one feature: "In ¶2 he combs his beard with a pine tree. That's impossible, so it's exaggeration. I'll write ¶2."
DO: Shared reading of ¶1–4 (you read, students follow). Notebook L2 feature hunt with partners; "write about it" alone.
GROUPS: Decoding group: long-word routine with *whimpering, completely, measured*.
CHECK: Students check their L1 predictions. Address the "tall tale = lie" error with the anchor note's error box.
:::

::: lesson 3 | Cold write | WR14 (baseline)
SHOW: Nothing; this is a baseline.
SAY: "Write a short tall tale about any hero. This is your starting point. In two weeks we'll see how much you've grown."
DO: 20 minutes of writing (Notebook L3). Then read Tale 1 ¶5–8 aloud; quick talk about the ending.
GROUPS: Edition C students may draw and dictate a sentence.
CHECK: Don't grade. Keep the cold writes; students count their story parts in L13.
:::

::: lesson 4 | How a story is built | TF3, WR14
SHOW: Anchor note 2. Think aloud: "What goes wrong? The cold is bad, but in ¶5 the author says 'a new problem'. That's the signal."
DO: Notebook L4 story map, then "write about it": is the cold or the hunger the main problem?
GROUPS: Fluency: start repeated reading with Passage 1.
CHECK: Look for students who name the cold as the problem; correct in tomorrow's warm-up.
:::

::: lesson 5 | Summing up | CO4
SHOW: Anchor note 5. Model the John Henry gist (anchor) then do Paul Bunyan's SWBST together for the first two boxes.
SAY: "A summary is not the whole story. If it is longer than the best paragraph, cut."
DO: Notebook L5: finish SWBST, write a gist sentence, count words.
GROUPS: Comprehension group: summarize ¶5–6 together before the whole tale.
CHECK: Exit ticket 1.
:::

## Week 2 · Characters and language

::: lesson 6 | Getting to know a character | TF10, CO11
SHOW: Anchor note 3. Read Tale 2 aloud with expression (students follow).
SAY: Model one row: "She says 'Determined wins every time.' Her words show she is determined. That's *says*."
DO: Notebook L6 evidence chart, then "write about it" (the most important trait, two pieces of evidence). Flat or round?
GROUPS: Reader's theatre: hand out parts; read-through.
CHECK: Watch for body words (strong, tall) as traits; redirect to inside traits.
:::

::: lesson 7 | What does it really mean? | VO13, VO14, VO15
SHOW: Anchor note 4. Think aloud the hot-air balloon example: what's compared, what's in common, why the author chose it.
SAY: "We never stop at 'it's a simile'. What does it make you picture?"
DO: Notebook L7 table, then each student writes their own hyperbole.
GROUPS: Decoding: *mysterious, ferocious* (link to -ous in word work).
CHECK: Share three student hyperboles as tomorrow's warm-up.
:::

::: lesson 8 | Reading between the lines | CO6, CO9, CO3
SHOW: Anchor note 6: clue + what I know = inference. Model Q1 aloud with the frame.
DO: Notebook L8 questions 1–2; "monitor" box: which sentence did you reread and what helped?
GROUPS: Comprehension group: inference with the frame on Tale 2 ¶6–8.
CHECK: Look for answers with no paragraph number; teach "point to it".
:::

::: lesson 9 | John Henry: context and prediction | CO14, CO18, CO7
SHOW: Background (5 min): railroads in the 1870s; steel driving by hand; many workers were Black men freed from slavery, paid little. A photo of a tunnel entrance helps.
DO: Notebook L9: two context facts; read ¶1–4 and predict; read to the end; confirm or revise.
SAY (after reading): "The first-day line in ¶2 was a clue. Good readers go back when the ending surprises them."
GROUPS: Fluency: Passage 3 (the race).
CHECK: Handle John Henry's death respectfully; it is the heart of the theme, not a shock.
:::

::: lesson 10 | Theme and points of view | CO11, CO12, CO13, TF9
SHOW: Model the difference between a topic (courage) and a theme (a sentence about courage).
DO: Notebook L10: points-of-view chart; theme sentences; opinion with evidence plus one reason someone might disagree.
TALK: Pairs: "Was the race worth it?" Use the talk moves.
CHECK: Exit ticket 2.
:::

## Week 3 · Tales grow; getting ready to write

::: lesson 11 | Big Joe: a real person becomes a legend | OL1, OL2, CO14
SHOW: Tale 4. Map of the Ottawa River (Mattawa, Bytown/Ottawa). Mention that the stories were told in French and English.
DO: Notebook L11: the growing wagon story; real vs. legend sort; "write about it": why did raftsmen make the stories bigger?
GROUPS: Reader's theatre rehearsal.
CHECK: Listen for students who now see tall tales as an oral tradition, not lies.
:::

::: lesson 12 | Tell it out loud | OL3, OL4, OL6, OL8
SHOW: Model a 60-second retelling with deliberate voice: a pause before the biggest exaggeration, a gesture, eye contact.
DO: Notebook L12: cue card, voice plan, partner retelling and feedback. Then groups of four discuss "Is it fair to stretch the truth about a real person?" with talk moves; record a group answer.
CHECK: Observation notes for OL3, OL4 (who projects; who reads the card).
:::

::: lesson 13 | SRSD stages 1–2: background and discuss | WR3, WR14
SHOW: Anchor note 7 (POW + WWW). Read Tale 5 as the mentor text; find each WWW part together.
SAY: "Good writers plan with these seven parts. Let's see how many our cold writes had."
DO: Notebook L13: parts hunt; students count parts in their L3 cold write and set a goal. Show the three rubric samples and talk about what makes each level.
GROUPS: Edition B/C: count parts with you.
CHECK: Exit ticket 3. Start daily mnemonic practice (30 seconds, chorally, then from memory).
:::

::: lesson 14 | Cold read 1 | TF7, TF3, TF10, CO4, CO6, CO9, CO12, VO14
DO: Cold read 1 (35 min), silent and independent. Edition C: read the Edition A tale aloud first. Remaining time: POW + WWW memory practice in pairs.
CHECK: Score by code tonight; set Week 4 groups from the results.
:::

::: lesson 15 | Reader's theatre and memorize | OL3, OL4, TF18
DO: Perform *Paul Bunyan and the Blue Snow* (two casts if needed). Notebook L15 fluency self-rating; write POW + WWW from memory.
SAY: "Fluent means it sounds like a storyteller, not a race car."
CHECK: Students who can't yet write POW + WWW from memory practise again tomorrow. Don't move to modelling until most can.
:::

## Week 4 · Model and plan

::: lesson 16 | SRSD stage 3: model it | WR3, WR12, WR14
SHOW: Plan and write a new tall tale in front of the class, thinking aloud, using the script below. Write on chart paper or the board.
DO: Notebook L16: students record what you did and what you said to yourself, then write their own three self-talk statements.
CHECK: Keep your chart; it's the model for the next two weeks.
:::

::: teacher Think-aloud script for Lesson 16 (adapt freely)
"**P: Pick my idea.** What place do I know well? The hockey rink. What job could my hero have? A Zamboni driver. What's amazing? She drives so fast she can flood the whole rink in one second. OK: Zippy Zara.
**O: Organize my notes.** Who: Zippy Zara, the Zamboni driver, and the coach. When: the big game last winter. Where: our town's rink. What does she want? The ice is melting during the championship and she wants to save the game. What happens? She drives so fast the Zamboni makes a snowstorm, then she freezes the ice with her ice-cold stare… then a third thing. How does it end? The game is saved and the ice is so slippery the puck is still sliding today. How do they feel? The crowd goes wild; Zara is so dizzy she drives home backwards. Do I have all seven parts? Who, when, where, want, happens, ends, feel. Yes.
**W: Write and say more.** I'll start with when and where… I'm stuck on how to show the ice melting. Let me look at my notes. I could show it: 'Puddles spread across the ice like spilled juice.' Good, that part is done. Did I show, not tell?"
:::

::: lesson 17 | SRSD stage 5: we plan together | WR3, WR14
SHOW: The class picks a hero (vote among three ideas) and you scribe the WWW plan as students suggest notes.
DO: Notebook L17: copy the class plan; write the class's best exaggeration.
CHECK: Students should be able to say what part is missing before you add it.
:::

::: lesson 18 | Show, don't tell | WR15, WR13, WR11
SHOW: Anchor note 8; the typewriter line from Tale 5 ¶3. Model one rewrite from telling to showing.
DO: Notebook L18: three rewrites; exaggeration ladder (real → bigger → tall tale that shows a trait).
CHECK: Exit ticket 4. Watch for "very very very"; redirect to an action or sense detail.
:::

::: lesson 19 | My plan: P and O | WR3, WR14, TF20
DO: Notebook L19: brainstorm (a place I know, a job, an ability, a problem in nature or the community), pick one, organize all 7 parts. Encourage local places: a real Alberta place makes stronger tales (and connects to land and place).
GROUPS: Conference with Edition B/C students first; they use the story frame.
CHECK: No drafting until the plan has all 7 parts.
:::

::: lesson 20 | Dialogue and exaggerations | CV2, CV3, WR16, VO15
SHOW: Anchor note 9. Mentor: Tale 2 ¶7–8.
DO: Notebook L20: two lines of dialogue for their tale, three exaggerations that show, one comparison.
CHECK: Exit ticket 5.
:::

## Week 5 · Draft and feedback

::: lesson 21 | Draft: beginning and problem | WR12, WR14
SAY: Quick reminder of self-talk statements. "Skip lines. Say more."
DO: Draft (Notebook draft pages), 30 minutes. Conference with 4–5 students: one teaching point each.
GROUPS: Edition C students may dictate; the outcome here is composing, so dictation is an access support.
:::

::: lesson 22 | Draft: events | WR12, WR15
SHOW: Two-minute mini-lesson: events that build (three steps, each bigger), from the rubric Sample C.
DO: Continue drafting; conferences.
:::

::: lesson 23 | Draft: resolution and ending; self-check | WR12, WR14
DO: Finish the draft. Count parts, tick the checklist, colour the progress graph (cold write vs. draft). Self-assess with the single-point checklist.
SAY: Model attributing progress to strategy: "You went from 3 parts to 7 because you used POW + WWW."
:::

::: lesson 24 | Structured peer feedback | WR7, OL6, OL7
SHOW: Model the routine with a volunteer: author reads aloud; listener uses the checklist, then the two sentence starters.
DO: Notebook L24 in pairs; then the author writes one change to make first.
CHECK: Listen for vague feedback ("it's good") and push for "where?"
:::

::: lesson 25 | Revise | WR7, WR11
SHOW: Model one revision on your Zippy Zara draft (telling → showing; a stronger verb).
SAY: "Revising is not fixing spelling. That's tomorrow."
DO: Notebook L25: at least two revision moves; before/after sentence.
CHECK: Exit ticket 6.
:::

## Week 6 · Publish and perform

::: lesson 26 | Edit | WR8, CV2, CV4, CV12–14
DO: Notebook L26: one read-through per editing target (dialogue, past tense, capitals, my spelling patterns, joining words). Three words fixed, with the pattern.
GROUPS: Pull students who have tense slips; practise with the Week 2 word-work item.
:::

::: lesson 27 | Cold read 2 | CO4, TF10, CO11, VO13, WR15, CO6, CO9, CO12, TF7
DO: Cold read 2 (35 min). Edition C: read the A tale aloud first. Early finishers: final copy.
CHECK: Compare with cold read 1 by code; note growth for reporting.
:::

::: lesson 28 | Publish and self-assess | WR9, WR1
DO: Final copy (handwritten or typed), title, illustration with a caption. Self-assess with the rubric (Notebook L28). Score with the rubric and give one written feedback point they can use in the next unit.
:::

::: lesson 29 | Storytelling festival | OL3, OL4, OL1
DO: Students tell their tales (not read them) to a Grade 2 or 3 class in small circles, using cue cards. Arrange the partner class at least a week ahead.
CHECK: Observation checklist: projects voice; pauses for effect; uses a gesture; looks at the audience.
:::

::: lesson 30 | Look back, look ahead | WR7, CO2
DO: Notebook L30: finish the progress graph, reflect on what helped (strategy, self-talk, feedback), set a goal for the next unit, choose a tall tale for independent reading. Self-check box.
SAY: Connect the strategy forward: "In the next unit you'll learn a new POW partner, TIDE, for information writing."
:::

## Notes on the tales

**John Henry:** historians think he may have been a real worker on the C&O Railway's Big Bend Tunnel in West Virginia (about 1870–1872); no record proves it. Much of the tunnel work was done by African American workers, including convict labour.
**Big Joe Mufferaw:** Joseph Montferrand (1802–1864) was a real raftsman and strongman; the boot prints on an inn ceiling are a legend the Ottawa Valley still tells. The Stompin' Tom Connors song is a good read-aloud or listen in class (not reproduced here).
**Chinook winds** are real: warm, dry downslope winds east of the Rockies that can raise the temperature by 20 °C or more in hours. The "Chinook arch" is a band of clear sky over the mountains.
