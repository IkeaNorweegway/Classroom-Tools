## At a glance

Students read four original science fiction stories that all ask what happens when technology meets a choice: a homework robot, a weather dome, a care robot and a message from the future. They learn what makes science fiction, how third-person narration works, how to state a theme as a sentence, how setting and context shape characters, the difference between round and flat characters, and how a **flash-forward** works. Then they plan and write their own science fiction story with **POW + WWW** and a flash-forward, and read it aloud at **Future Fest**.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Reading** | Sci-fi features; third-person point of view; theme; setting and context; round and flat characters; perspectives; flash-forward; prediction and inference |
| **Writing** | SRSD POW + WWW (reviewed from Tall Tales, now with a choice, a flash-forward and a theme); showing a future world; dialogue |
| **Word work** | -ic/-ical, -al/-ial, trans-/com-, -ity/-ment, ex-/per-; tense, adverbs, dialogue, third-person pronouns |
| **Fluency** | *Signal from the Future* radio drama; repeated reading |
| **Oral language** | Robot debate; Future Fest read-aloud |
| **Assessment** | Six exit tickets; cold reads in Lessons 14 and 27; rubric with three samples |

**Why it is built this way:** students already know POW + WWW from Tall Tales, so the SRSD stages move faster here (more time on the new parts: the choice, the flash-forward and the theme). Research §8: return to a strategy and extend it, rather than starting over. Theme is taught as a sentence with evidence because "theme = one word" is the most common error (course context).

## The daily block, groups and editions

Same shape and grouping as every unit. **Decoding:** *technology, experiment, consequence, communicate, futuristic*. **Comprehension:** theme and character change with the grade-level story. **Edition B:** word box per anchor page, ✎ model rows, sentence starters; fade by Week 4. **Edition C:** retold stories; Edition A read aloud for comprehension; a story frame; dictation as access.

## Week 1 · What makes science fiction?

::: lesson 1 | Hook: invent a gadget | WR10, OL6
SHOW: "What if…" prompts on the board: …you could pause time? …your backpack talked? …houses could walk?
DO: Notebook L1: invent a gadget; what's good and what could go wrong; brain dump of sci-fi stories, movies and games.
TALK: Share gadgets in groups of four; each group picks one and asks: what choice would it force on someone?
:::

::: lesson 2 | Is it science fiction? | TF7, TF2
SHOW: Anchor note 1. Compare a tall tale and a sci-fi story on the board.
DO: Read Story 1 aloud (students follow). Notebook L2: features; tall tale or sci-fi sort.
GROUPS: Decoding: *tournament, hesitated, technology*.
:::

::: lesson 3 | Third-person point of view | TF3, WR14
SHOW: Anchor note 2. Read one paragraph in first person, then third.
DO: Notebook L3: clues; rewrite ¶12 in first person; what the narrator can tell us.
:::

::: lesson 4 | Story map and summary | TF3, CO4
DO: Notebook L4: story map and gist sentence for Story 1.
GROUPS: Fluency: Passage 1.
:::

::: lesson 5 | Theme is a sentence | TF9, CO9
SHOW: Anchor note 3. Model topic → theme with *Kiko's Grandmother* (read the ending aloud).
DO: Notebook L5: theme and evidence for Story 1.
CHECK: Exit ticket 1. Watch for one-word themes.
:::

## Week 2 · Setting, characters and time

::: lesson 6 | Setting and context | CO15, CO18
SHOW: Anchor note 4. Read Story 2 aloud.
DO: Notebook L6: setting details; why Theo's father and Mémère react differently.
:::

::: lesson 7 | Round and flat | TF10, CO11
SHOW: Anchor note 5. Sort characters from Stories 1–2.
DO: Notebook L7: chart; what causes Theo to change.
:::

::: lesson 8 | Perspectives | CO11, CO12, CO13
DO: Read Story 3. Notebook L8: how each character sees the CareBot; the makers' perspective.
TALK: Pairs argue the makers' side, then their own.
:::

::: lesson 9 | Flash-forward | TF8
SHOW: Anchor note 6. Read Story 4 aloud; stop at "Sixty years later" and ask, "What just happened?"
DO: Notebook L9: flash-forward chart; why it comes before the ending.
:::

::: lesson 10 | Predict and infer | CO7, CO6
DO: Notebook L10: predict before the last two paragraphs of Story 4; confirm; infer who sent the message.
CHECK: Exit ticket 2.
:::

## Week 4 · Model and plan

::: lesson 16 | SRSD stage 3: model it | WR3, WR12, WR14
SHOW: Plan and write the opening and flash-forward of a story aloud (script below).
DO: Notebook L16.
:::

::: teacher Think-aloud script for Lesson 16 (adapt freely)
"**P: what if?** What if your bike could ride itself anywhere you told it? Good: it would change everyday life. **What could go wrong?** You'd never learn the way yourself. **O: WWW.** Who: Noor, age eleven. When: 2075. Where: a city where self-riding bikes are everywhere. What does she want? To get to her friend's house in a new neighbourhood. What happens? The bike's map crashes halfway, and she's lost. **The choice:** wait for the bike to reboot, or find the way herself. **Flash-forward:** where should the story jump? Maybe to Noor years later, leading a hike with no map at all. What does it show? That getting lost taught her something. **Theme:** being able to find your own way matters. Let me check: is it the future? I haven't shown it yet. 'Bikes hummed past in silver lines, steering themselves around corners.' Better. Good, that part's done."
:::

::: lesson 17 | We plan together | WR3, WR14
DO: Class "what if?" vote; you scribe the plan with a flash-forward. Notebook L17.
:::

::: lesson 18 | Showing a future world | WR13, WR15
SHOW: Anchor note 8. Find showing details in Story 2.
DO: Notebook L18.
CHECK: Exit ticket 4.
:::

::: lesson 19 | My plan | WR3, WR14, WR10
DO: Notebook L19. Conferences: does the technology matter to the problem? Is there a real choice?
:::

::: lesson 20 | Flash-forward and dialogue | TF8, WR16
DO: Notebook L20: draft the flash-forward; two lines of dialogue about the technology.
CHECK: Exit ticket 5.
:::

## Weeks 5–6 · Draft, revise, publish, perform

::: lesson 21 | Draft: opening and setting | WR12, WR13
DO: Draft in third person, past tense, skipping lines. Conferences.
:::

::: lesson 22 | Draft: the problem and the choice | WR12, WR14
SHOW: Two-minute lesson: make the choice hard (something to lose either way).
:::

::: lesson 23 | Draft: flash-forward and ending; self-check | WR12, TF8
DO: Finish; count parts; graph; single-point self-check.
:::

::: lesson 24 | Peer feedback | WR7, OL6
DO: Notebook L24: the listener says what theme they think the story shows; compare with the author's plan.
:::

::: lesson 25 | Revise | WR7
DO: Notebook L25.
CHECK: Exit ticket 6.
:::

::: lesson 26 | Edit | WR8, CV2, CV4, CV11
DO: Notebook L26: third person, past tense, flash-forward time phrase, dialogue, pronouns, spelling patterns.
:::

::: lesson 27 | Cold read 2 | TF3, TF8, CO12, VO13, TF9, TF10
DO: Cold read 2, *The Seed* (35 min).
:::

::: lesson 28 | Publish | WR9, WR1
DO: Final copy for the class anthology *Grade 5 Futures*, with a cover illustration of the technology. Notebook L28. Score with the rubric.
:::

::: lesson 29 | Future Fest | OL3, OL4
DO: Read-aloud in small circles (or for another class) with one sound effect each.
:::

::: lesson 30 | Look back | WR7, CO2
DO: Notebook L30. Self-check.
SAY: "Next unit, we slow way down and look at single words and feelings: poetry."
:::
