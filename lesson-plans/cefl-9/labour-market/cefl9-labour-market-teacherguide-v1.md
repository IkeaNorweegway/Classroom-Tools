## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 9-LM: Students recognize that success in career goals requires proactive measures. |
| **Understandings** | Individuals who manage and communicate their human capital are better positioned to navigate labour market disruptions. Career success depends on identifying where the labour market is moving. |
| **Driving question** | How do I show an employer what I can do? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl9-labour-market-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl9-labour-market-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5. Each student applies for their own posting; teams coach. |
| **Audience** | Volunteer interviewers (staff, family, local employers), Session 7 |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Before Session 1:**
- **Recruit interviewers** for Session 7: aim for one per 4–5 students. Give them a one-page feedback card (strengths, one tip, would you interview this person?).
- **Two sample résumés for the hook:** one cluttered and generic, one clear and tailored (like Leo's). Make them up.
- **Postings:** print 10–15 youth-friendly postings (Job Bank, local businesses, municipal summer jobs) for students who can't search.
- **Résumé template:** a simple one-page template in your school's word processor.
:::

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** "50 applications, 10 minutes" makes the stakes of a clear résumé obvious.
- **Real postings and real interviewers.** Talking with people in the world of work is one of the strongest career-readiness experiences (OECD).
- **Labour market data first.** Students classify the industry before writing the résumé, so they apply with an eye on where work is going.
- **Coaching teams.** Practice rounds with a timekeeper make the interview less frightening.

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Résumé hook, need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** pick a posting; requirements vs. assets | Requirements and assets sorted correctly |
| 2 | (1) Automation or globalization: robots welding? (2) A job hard to automate? (3) What's an asset in a posting? | **Moment 2** (industry data) | **Step 2:** classify the industry with evidence | Evidence has a number, source and years |
| 3 | (1) Name three LMI sources. (2) Why isn't one year enough? (3) Is health care in Alberta growing? | **Moment 3** (résumé, brand statement) | **Step 3:** brand statement, match table, résumé draft | Résumé lines start with action verbs and use the posting's words |
| 4 | (1) Fix: "I did stuff at the food bank." (2) What goes in a brand statement? (3) Why tailor a résumé? | **Moment 4** (STAR, self-advocacy) | **Step 4:** STAR notes | Action step is about "I", not "we" |
| 5 | (1) What does STAR stand for? (2) Requirement or asset: "first aid an asset"? (3) An example of self-advocacy | Workshop: body language and first impressions; show three samples (below) | **Step 5:** practice rounds; finish résumé | Answers about 1 minute; eye contact |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Résumé swap; at least 2 changes | Changes add evidence or fix wording |
| 7 | — | — | **Mock interviews** (about 8 minutes each); Look back; My Plan | Interviewer cards |

::: misconception
**"I have no experience, so there's no point applying."** Moment 4. Leo's résumé is built from coaching, volunteering and a certificate. Help every student find at least three experiences.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Clean one-column résumé** (like Leo's): brand statement, skills, experience with action verbs, education.
2. **Skills-first résumé** for students with little experience: skills grouped with evidence under each.
3. **Interview clip.** A 60-second recorded STAR answer (teacher-made or from a willing past student) to model pace and eye contact.

## When a student gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| "I've done nothing" | Ask about chores, sibling care, teams, clubs, gaming communities, school roles, certificates. |
| Résumé copies the posting word for word | Ask for evidence: where did you show that? |
| Freezes in practice | Shorten to just the Action step, then build out. |
| Very anxious about the interview | Offer a smaller panel, a known adult, or a recorded answer first. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- Résumé template; a STAR card; word cards in the brief.
- Practice rounds with peers before adults.
- Privacy: students use a school or made-up email and only a city, not a full address.

## Background for the teacher

- Alberta health care employment rose about 15% from 2022 to 2025 (Canada: 9%); construction fell 3.1% and wholesale/retail rose 1.5% from May 2025 to May 2026 (Job Bank Alberta snapshot, May 2026). Update with the latest snapshot each year.
- Alberta youth employment rules: ages 15–17 can do most jobs with some limits; check alberta.ca before students choose postings.
