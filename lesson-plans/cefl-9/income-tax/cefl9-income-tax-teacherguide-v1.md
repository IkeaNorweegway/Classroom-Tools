## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 9-IT: Students recognize how registered accounts and government benefits shape personal finances. |
| **Understandings** | Understanding how income is taxed and deducted is essential to accurate financial plans. The value of employment depends on taxation, deductions, benefits and conditions. |

### Answer key: Priya (simplified 2026 method)

| Line | Without RRSP | With $1,500 RRSP |
|---|---|---|
| Total income | $18,000 + $1,500 + $2,500 = $22,000 | $22,000 |
| Taxable income | $22,000 | $20,500 |
| Federal: 14% × taxable | $3,080.00 | $2,870.00 |
| − basic personal credit | − $2,303.28 | − $2,303.28 |
| − tuition credit (14% × $4,000) | − $560.00 | − $560.00 |
| Federal tax | $216.72 | $6.72 |
| Alberta tax (8% − $1,821.52) | $1,760 − $1,821.52 → $0 | $1,640 − $1,821.52 → $0 |
| Total income tax | $216.72 | $6.72 |
| Self-employed CPP (11.9% × $2,500) | $297.50 | $297.50 |
| Total owed | $514.22 | $304.22 |
| Minus box 22 | − $700.00 | − $700.00 |
| **Refund** | **$185.78** | **$395.78** |

The RRSP saves Priya **$210** this year. **Either piece of advice can meet the outcome** if the reasons fit her situation. A strong TFSA answer: her tax rate is low now and likely higher later; she may need the money for school; a TFSA is flexible. A strong RRSP answer: $210 back now is real money, and she can still use a TFSA later. (Note: RRSP room depends on last year's earned income; assume Priya has room. Unused tuition credits can be carried forward; beyond Grade 9 scope.)

### Optional second case card: Marcus, 24, electrician apprentice

T4 box 14 **$46,000**, box 22 **$6,000**; union dues **$800** (deduction); considering a **$2,000** RRSP; no tuition this year.
- Without RRSP: taxable $45,200 → federal $6,328 − $2,303.28 = $4,024.72; Alberta $3,616 − $1,821.52 = $1,794.48; total $5,819.20; **refund $180.80**.
- With RRSP: taxable $43,200 → federal $3,744.72; Alberta $1,634.48; total $5,379.20; **refund $620.80**. RRSP saves **$440** (22%).
- RRSP vs. TFSA is closer here; good for debate.

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** Real volunteer tax clinics exist, so the role is authentic, and the hook raises the idea that people lose money by not filing.
- **One case, many ideas.** Priya's case uses T4s, tips, gig income, tuition credits, an RRSP decision and benefits, all in one real decision.
- **Advice, not just arithmetic.** The RRSP/TFSA choice needs reasons, matching the outcome's "evaluate".

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Hook (tax clinics), need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** list all income | Tips and gig income included; total $22,000 |
| 2 | (1) Box 14 vs. box 22? (2) What is a refund? (3) 14% of $15,000? | **Moment 2** (credits vs. deductions) | **Step 2:** Priya's return (individually, then compare) | Credits subtracted; tax not below $0 |
| 3 | (1) Credit or deduction: tuition? (2) What does a $1,000 deduction save at 22%? (3) Why can't tax go below $0? | **Moment 3** (RRSP vs. TFSA) | **Step 3:** redo with RRSP; advice | Tax saved = $210; reasons mention tax rate now vs. later |
| 4 | (1) RRSP or TFSA: tax-free withdrawals? (2) 2026 TFSA limit? (3) Your advice for Priya? | **Moment 4** (self-employment, tips, benefits) | **Step 4:** finish the return | Self-employed CPP $297.50; refund calculated |
| 5 | (1) Self-employed CPP rate? (2) Must tips be reported? (3) Why file to get the GST/HST credit? | Workshop: explaining money in plain words (three samples below) | **Step 5:** make it | All five parts present |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Swap with another team; reconcile any different numbers | Numbers agree with the key |
| 7 | — | — | **Tax clinic night**; Look back; My Plan | Client volunteers understood the advice |

::: misconception
**"A refund is a bonus."** Moment 1, Question 3. It's the taxpayer's own over-paid money.
:::

::: misconception
**"Tips and gig money don't need reporting."** Moment 4. All income is reportable; the CRA can reassess with interest and penalties.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Client summary sheet:** a one-page "Your 2026 return at a glance" with income, tax, refund, and three plain-language tips.
2. **Role-play script:** a short scene of the clinic conversation, with the numbers on a cue card.
3. **"Tax tips for teens" video:** 90 seconds on reporting tips, keeping receipts, and RRSP vs. TFSA.

## When a team gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Forgets tips or gig income | Ask: what money did Priya receive all year? |
| Federal tax goes negative | Remind: credits stop at $0. |
| Mixes up credit and deduction | Use the Moment 2 diagram: income box vs. tax box. |
| Advice has no reasons | Ask: what's Priya's tax rate now? When does she need the money? |

## Access supports (UDL)

- A one-page "return recipe" (income → taxable → × rate → − credits → − paid).
- Calculators; product choice, including role-play.
- Privacy: students never use their own or family tax papers.

## Background for the teacher

- **2026:** federal 14% first bracket, basic personal amount $16,452; Alberta 8%, basic personal amount $22,769; federal tuition credit 14% (Alberta has none since 2020); CPP 5.95% each (11.9% self-employed) above $3,500; EI 1.63%; TFSA $7,000; RRSP 18% of the previous year's earned income up to $33,810.
- The method ignores CPP/EI credits, the Canada employment amount and other details, so real returns differ a little.
