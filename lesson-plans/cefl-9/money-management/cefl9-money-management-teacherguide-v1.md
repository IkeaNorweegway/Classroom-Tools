## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 9-MM: Students analyze how debt and credit function and evaluate strategies for borrowing responsibly to protect long-term financial security. |
| **Understandings** | Debt can accelerate or undermine financial security. Compound interest amplifies savings and debt. A strong credit history is a financial asset. |
| **Driving question** | How can borrowing help or hurt my future? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl9-money-management-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl9-money-management-project-v1` |

| **Audience** | A Grade 8 class, Session 7 ("what we wish we knew about debt") |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test. |

### Answer key: Jordan's case

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Monthly interest | Card 2,400 × 0.2099 ÷ 12 = $41.98; phone $0; line 3,000 × 0.095 ÷ 12 = $23.75; **total $65.73** |
| Credit utilization | 2,400 ÷ 2,500 = **96%** (hurting) |
| Payment history | two late payments (hurting) |
| Debt-to-income ratio | 170 ÷ 3,000 ≈ **5.7%** |
| Avalanche order | credit card (20.99%) → line of credit (9.5%) → phone (0%): **17 months, $509.31** interest |
| Snowball order | phone ($600) → credit card ($2,400) → line of credit ($3,000): **17 months, $566.43** |
| Minimums only ($170) | **46 months, $1,712.29** |
| Car repair | payday loan $14 × 3 = **$42**; line of credit 300 × 0.095 × 14 ÷ 365 ≈ **$1.09**. Line of credit (if the limit allows), savings, or a payment plan with the mechanic are far cheaper. |

Repayment figures were computed month by month: interest added (balance × rate ÷ 12), minimums paid, extra money applied to the target debt.

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** The two signs (payday lender vs. student card) set up the whole unit: some credit builds a future, some traps people.
- **A realistic case.** Jordan's mix of a card, phone financing and a line of credit is typical for a young adult.
- **Advice for Grade 8s.** Explaining to younger students forces clear understanding, and Grade 8s are learning compound interest for savings this year.
- **Personal credit plan.** Rehearsing rules before students get their first card is the closest thing to "just-in-time" teaching for this outcome.

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Hook (two signs), need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** list the debts | Kinds of credit named correctly |
| 2 | (1) Debt vs. credit? (2) When does a credit card charge no interest? (3) One way credit helps | **Moment 2** (minimum-payment trap) | **Step 2:** monthly interest | Interest = balance × rate ÷ 12 |
| 3 | (1) One month's interest on $1,000 at 20.99%? (2) How long do minimum payments take on $1,000? (3) Why? | **Moment 3** (credit scores) | **Step 3:** credit score check | Utilization calculated (96%); late payments flagged |
| 4 | (1) Score range? (2) Name three score factors. (3) $300 of $2,000 = ? % | **Moment 4** (avalanche, snowball, payday, DTI) | **Step 4:** the plan and the car-repair advice | Recommendation uses cost **and** motivation; payday compared with an alternative |
| 5 | (1) Avalanche or snowball: which costs less? (2) Payday cost on $300? (3) What is DTI? | Workshop: explaining debt to younger students (three samples below) | **Step 5:** team product and personal credit plan | All five parts present; personal plans written |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Feedback; at least 2 changes | Clear for a Grade 8 reader |
| 7 | — | — | **Share** with Grade 8s; Look back; My Plan | Grade 8 cards show the key ideas |

::: misconception
**"Making the minimum payment means I'm keeping up."** Moment 2, Question 3. Use the 15-year chart.
:::

::: misconception
**"Avoiding credit completely gives a great score."** Moment 3. No history can mean no score.
:::

::: teacher
**Sensitivity.** Some families rely on payday loans or carry debt. Frame predatory lending as a problem with the product, not with the people who use it. Never ask about family debt.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Poster "Jordan's way out":** a debt "mountain" shrinking month by month under the avalanche plan, with a sidebar on credit scores.
2. **Podcast episode:** two hosts interview "Jordan" (a team member) and explain each step.
3. **Comic:** six panels from the payday-loan sign to Jordan debt-free 17 months later.

## When a team gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Uses 20.99 instead of 0.2099 | Ask: what is 21% of $100? |
| Forgets to divide by 12 | The rate is per year; we want one month. |
| Can't decide avalanche vs. snowball | Both work; ask which matters more to Jordan, saving $57 or a quick win. |
| Car repair advice is "use the payday loan" | Compare the $42 with the $1.09; ask what else Jordan could do. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- Calculators; an "interest recipe" card (balance × rate ÷ 12).
- Product choice, including a podcast for students who prefer speaking.
- Answer figures for the repayment plans are given in the brief, so students focus on comparing, not on month-by-month arithmetic.

## Background for the teacher

- **Typical card terms:** 20.99% purchase rate; minimum 2.5% of the balance or $10 (varies by card).
- **Credit scores:** 300–900 in Canada (Equifax, TransUnion).
- **Criminal interest rate:** 35% APR since January 1, 2025; payday loans capped at $14 per $100 borrowed under federal regulations in provinces with an approved regime (including Alberta).
