## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Outcome** | 9-PS: Students evaluate post-secondary options by weighing personal goals, financial costs, and labour market outcomes. |
| **Understandings** | Choosing a pathway requires balancing values and interests against realistic outcomes and full cost. Financial assistance can improve affordability and access. |
| **Driving question** | How do I know if a post-secondary pathway is worth the cost? |
| **Student materials** | Mini-workbook `cefl9-post-secondary-workbook-v1` · project brief `cefl9-post-secondary-project-v1` |
| **Teams** | 4–5. Each student builds their own plan; teams research and check math. |
| **Audience** | A panel (counsellor, teacher, family volunteers) playing a family deciding whether to help pay, Session 7 |
| **Assessment** | Single-point rubric on the report-card scale. No unit test. |

::: teacher
**Before Session 1:** book the panel. Bookmark the Alberta Student Aid loan estimator and repayment calculator (studentaid.alberta.ca) and ALIS OCCinfo wage pages. **Hook:** there's no single answer for Alex and Riley: Riley likely has less debt and earlier income; Alex may earn more later. Students need ROI, debt and job data to argue it.
:::

## Why it is built this way

- **Driving question first.** The Alex and Riley hook can't be answered without the ideas in the unit.
- **Real numbers.** True cost, loans and salaries come from real sources, so ROI isn't pseudo-context.
- **Contingency planning.** The outcome asks for a primary pathway, a contingency and a rationale; the panel tests all three.
- **Teachable moment.** Many students are about to choose Grade 10 courses; this project connects to real decisions.

## Session plans

| Session | Warm-up (no notes) | Lesson (10–15 min) | Project work | Check-in / look-for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Hook (Alex and Riley), need-to-know chart, **Moment 1** | **Step 1:** pathway and funding sources | Tuition has a source and year; loans listed separately from grants |
| 2 | (1) Loan vs. grant? (2) Which part of a student loan is interest-free? (3) 182.91 × 120 ≈ ? | **Moment 2** (ROI) | **Step 2:** true cost, payback | Lost earnings use months in school; salary has ALIS source and year |
| 3 | (1) What is ROI? (2) Payback formula? (3) Why might payback be longer? | **Moment 3** (work-integrated learning) | **Step 3:** co-op, internships, apprenticeships | Options are real for the pathway |
| 4 | (1) Name three kinds of WIL. (2) One benefit besides money. (3) What is credential recognition? | **Moment 4** (competitive admission, Plan B) | **Step 4:** contingency and rationale | Contingency related to the goal; rationale covers money, self and jobs |
| 5 | (1) Two things besides grades programs look at. (2) Why should Plan B be related? (3) Your rationale in one sentence | Workshop: a persuasive pitch (three samples below) | **Step 5:** make it | All four parts present |
| 6 | — | Model feedback frames | Feedback, at least 2 changes; plan panel answers | Numbers checked by another team |
| 7 | — | — | **Pitches** (3 minutes + questions); Look back; My Plan | Panel says if they were convinced, and why |

::: misconception
**"If I don't get into my first choice, my plan failed."** Moment 4, Question 3. A related contingency keeps the goal alive.
:::

::: misconception
**"Student loans are like free money."** Moment 1. The federal part is interest-free but still repaid; the Alberta part charges interest.
:::

## Sample products (show all three in Session 5)

1. **Pitch slideshow** (5 slides): goal → pathway → true cost and payback → how I'll pay → Plan B and why.
2. **One-page plan + 2-minute talk:** a table of numbers on top, the rationale in three short paragraphs below.
3. **Decision flowchart:** "Apply to RN → admitted? yes → … no → LPN → bridge later," with costs on each branch.

## When a student gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| No idea what pathway | Use the Grade 8 My High School Map or Grade 7 Two Roads career. |
| Can't find a salary | Use OCCinfo; pick the closest occupation. |
| Payback looks impossible (very long) | Check: did they use months in school? Is the baseline $31,200? Is it a part-time salary? |
| Plan B unrelated ("or I'll be a YouTuber") | Ask which pathway shares the same skills or field. |

## Access supports (UDL)

- Calculators and the repayment calculator; a payback formula card.
- Product choice, including a flowchart for visual thinkers.
- Sensitivity: students' families have very different abilities to pay. Funding plans should never require sharing family income; "family help" can be $0.

## Background for the teacher

- **Canada Student Loans:** federal portion permanently interest-free since April 1, 2023; 6-month grace period. **Alberta student loans:** CIBC prime rate. The workbook's 4.5% rate and loan split are samples.
- **ALIS 2023 averages:** registered nurses $73,823 (note: average 29.9 hours/week, so many nurses work part-time); electricians $81,919; vet techs $47,812.
- **ROI method:** payback = true cost ÷ (salary − $31,200 minimum-wage baseline). It ignores taxes and raises; it's a comparison tool, not a forecast.
