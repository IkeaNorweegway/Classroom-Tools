::: overview
**Suggested length:** ~23 classes (7–8 weeks, 3 classes a week) &nbsp;&nbsp; **Tier:** 1 &nbsp;&nbsp; **Unit colour:** Amber (Time and Place)

**Guiding question:** How was the Canadian state built after Confederation?

**Learning outcome:** Students examine the deliberate acts of state building. Local skill codes SB1–SB7 are listed in the course context file.

**Big idea for students to leave with:** Canada did not just happen. Leaders made deliberate choices: to join the colonies, take over the West, sign treaties, build a railway and bring in settlers. Each choice built the country, and each one also changed who controlled the land, often without asking the First Nations and Métis peoples who lived there.

**What this unit is really about:** Grade 7s tend to arrive with one of two simple stories: "Canada was born in 1867 and grew west" or "Canada took the land." The draft curriculum asks for something harder: seeing state building as a set of deliberate decisions, and weighing each decision from the government's point of view and from the point of view of the people it affected. Keep returning to two questions: *Why did the government do this?* and *Who was asked, and who was not?* Sections 4 and 5 (Red River, treaties, the Indian Act) carry the heaviest content and the most sensitive material, and get the most time.
:::

## Lesson Sequence

### Section 1 — British North America Before 1867 (3 classes)

#### Opening: Pre-Knowledge Check and the 1864 map
SHOW: Hand out the notes and have students complete the Pre-Knowledge Check in silence before any teaching.
SAY: "No wrong answers here. I want your first thoughts, because we're coming back to this exact page at the end of the unit to see what changed."
SHOW: The schematic 1864 map with every label covered. Ask: "Where do you think Alberta is on this map?" (It is not there: it is inside Rupert's Land.)
NOTES: Notes p. 1, Pre-Knowledge Check and Section 1 map. Feeds Workbook Q1.

#### Two empires, one continent
SHOW: A simple timeline strip: 1713 (Acadia to Britain), 1759 (Plains of Abraham), 1763 (New France to Britain).
SAY: "Every war between Britain and France moved the lines on the map. By the 1860s France was gone, and everything on our map was British, but it was still not one country."
CUE: "If the colonies were all British, why weren't they already one country?" Draw out distance, separate governments and no railway.
NOTES: Section 1 vocabulary and Retrieval Check. Feeds Workbook Q8.

### Section 2 — Why the Colonies Joined Together (4 classes)

#### Refutation first: was Canada independent in 1867?
SHOW: Put the claim "In 1867 Canada became an independent country" on the board. Have students vote agree or disagree before teaching.
SAY: "After 1867, when Britain went to war, Canada was at war too. Canada couldn't sign its own treaties with other countries. So what did it become? A Dominion: it ran things at home, but it was still part of the British Empire."
NOTES: Section 2 misconception box. Feeds Workbook Q11 and Test Q9.

#### The big neighbour and the three conferences
SHOW: The Confederation flowchart. Fill the three reasons first, then the four steps.
SAY: "Picture the U.S. in 1865: a huge army just home from the Civil War, and people saying America should own the whole continent. Now picture Nova Scotia on its own."
CUE: Role-play prompt: half the class are Nova Scotians against joining, half are Fathers of Confederation. Two minutes to argue, then switch.
NOTES: Section 2 notes and Retrieval Check, including the Stretch "justify" question (SB2). Feeds Workbook Q5.

### Section 3 — Expanding West (3 classes)

#### Refutation first: was the West empty?
SHOW: An 1890s settlement poster calling the Prairies "the last, best West." Ask: "Who is missing from this picture?"
SAY: "Cree, Blackfoot, Dene, Saulteaux, Nakota and Métis communities had lived across this land for generations. The poster was advertising land where people already lived."
NOTES: Section 3 first misconception box. Feeds Workbook Q13.

#### The Rupert's Land deal
SHOW: The transfer diagram: Hudson's Bay Company → Canada, with the "not asked" box covered until the end.
SAY: "This deal changed who claimed the land on paper. It did not change who lived there. Keep that sentence in mind for the next two sections."
NOTES: Section 3 Retrieval Check, including the benefits-and-cost Stretch (SB4). Feeds Workbook Q3.

### Section 4 — Red River and the Northwest Resistance (4 classes)

#### Red River, 1869
SHOW: A diagram of Métis river lots next to the square survey grid. Ask what a family would think if surveyors walked across their lot.
SAY: "The Métis were not against Canada. They were against being taken over without being asked. So they formed their own government to bargain."
NOTES: Section 4 notes and the 1869–1885 timeline. Feeds Workbook Q6.

#### Two points of view on Riel
SHOW: Workbook Q10's two statements about Riel, side by side.
SAY: "Historians don't vote on whether Riel was a hero. They ask what each side believed, and what evidence supports it. That's your job too."
CUE: Discuss the word choice "Rebellion" vs. "Resistance": who would choose each word?
NOTES: Section 4 Connection Prompt and Retrieval Check Stretch (SB5). Give the mid-unit quiz after this section.

### Section 5 — Treaties and the Indian Act (4 classes)

#### Refutation first: were treaties land sales?
SHOW: The two-panel treaty diagram with the right-hand panel covered. Read the written text first, then reveal Elders' understanding.
SAY: "Same agreement, two records. One was written in English by the Crown. One was remembered and passed down by people who were there. Historians use both."
NOTES: Section 5 misconception box and diagram. Feeds Workbook Q14 and Test Q12.

#### Our treaty and the Indian Act
SHOW: Your school's treaty land acknowledgement. Find your treaty area on a map.
SAY: "The Indian Act of 1876 gave Ottawa control over many parts of First Nations life. It still exists today, changed many times, and people still debate it."
NOTES: Section 5 Retrieval Check. If possible, invite a local Elder or Knowledge Keeper for this lesson (see Discussion notes below). Feeds Workbook Q9.

### Section 6 — Filling the West and New Provinces (3 classes)

#### The National Policy and the railway
SHOW: Workbook Q4's four words (railway, settlers, wheat, eastern factories) and have pairs connect them before you teach.
SAY: "Macdonald's plan only worked if all the parts worked together. No railway, no settlers. No settlers, no wheat. No wheat, no buyers for eastern factories."
NOTES: Section 6 notes. Mention Chinese railway workers here; they return in the Immigration unit.

#### Alberta joins Canada
SHOW: The provinces timeline. Ask students to find the biggest gap and explain it.
SAY: "Alberta and Saskatchewan were carved out of the Northwest Territories in 1905, because the railway had brought enough people that they needed their own governments."
NOTES: Section 6 Retrieval Check and home sheet "Canada's changing map." Feeds Workbook Q2, Q12.

### Consolidation (2 classes)

#### Unit Synthesis and the Challenge Zone
SHOW: Students complete the Unit Synthesis from memory, then reread their Pre-Knowledge Check.
SAY: "Look at what you wrote on day one about who lived on the Prairies. What would you add now?"
NOTES: Workbook S1–S3, then the unit test next class.

## Misconception Management

| Misconception | Where it is refuted | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| Canada was fully independent in 1867 | Section 2 box; Workbook Q11; Test Q9 | "Canada's birthday is when it became free from Britain" |
| The West was empty before settlers came | Section 3 box; Workbook Q13; Test Q10 | "Settlers opened up the land" |
| Rupert's Land was bought from the people who lived there | Section 3 second box | "Canada bought it from the First Nations" |
| Treaties were simple land sales, understood the same way by both sides | Section 5 box; Workbook Q14; Test Q12 | "They sold their land" |
| All the provinces joined in 1867 | Section 6 box; home sheet | "Alberta joined at Confederation" |

## Discussion and Source Activity Notes

::: lab Sensitive content (read before Sections 4 and 5)
- Teach the Red River Conflict, the Northwest Resistance, scrip, the treaties and the Indian Act factually, with First Nations and Métis as decision-makers, not only as people things happened to.
- Some students may have personal or family connections to treaties, scrip or residential schools. Tell students ahead of time what is coming, and give them a way to step out.
- Residential schools are taught in depth in Grade 9. Here they appear only as one way the Indian Act was used.
- Where possible, use First Nations and Métis sources: Treaty 6, 7 and 8 organizations, the Métis Nation of Alberta, or a local Elder or Knowledge Keeper. Follow your division's protocol for inviting Elders.
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 1 — Map-maker (best after Section 6)
- Students build a series of five simple maps of Canada: 1867, 1870, 1873, 1905, 1999.
- Each map gets a caption: what changed, and why (SB1).
- Assess with the Workbook rubric line on boundaries, or use as evidence for SB1 and SB7.
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 2 — Many voices of Red River (best after Section 4)
- Groups make museum panels from four points of view: Métis families, the Canadian government, English-speaking settlers from Ontario, and First Nations near Red River.
- Each panel must use one piece of evidence from the notes and say what that group wanted.
- Debrief: which perspective was hardest to write, and why?
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 3 — Treaty case study (best after Section 5)
- Study the treaty your school is on (6, 7 or 8): when, where, who signed, the written terms, and what First Nations understood.
- Work with a local Nation or treaty organization where possible.
:::

## Assessment Notes

::: assessment
**Formative checkpoints:** the Retrieval Checks are the fastest signal. Collect exit tickets after every section and sort them into Got it / Getting there / Not yet before the next class.

**Watch these workbook items as misconception signals:** Q11 (independent in 1867), Q13 (empty West poster), Q14 (two versions of a treaty). A student who still says "they sold the land" on Q14 needs Section 5 retaught, not just re-explained.

**What a strong extended response looks like:** on Test Q15 and Workbook S3, a strong answer explains how *control* changed, not just what happened, names specific events and covers both First Nations and Métis.
:::

::: closing
**This unit sets up the whole year.** Creating Canada's Government explains the BNA Act; Building the Economy returns to the National Policy and the CPR; Immigration picks up the settlers; Armed Forces starts with the NWMP marching west in 1874. Point back to this unit whenever those connections come up.
:::
