::: overview
**Suggested length:** ~17 classes (5–6 weeks, 3 classes a week) &nbsp;&nbsp; **Tier:** 2 &nbsp;&nbsp; **Unit colour:** Rose (Time and Place)

**Guiding question:** How can defence of peace and security play a role in the development of a nation?

**Learning outcome:** Students relate the development of armed forces in Canada to sovereignty. Local skill codes AF1–AF6 are in the course context file.

**Big idea for students to leave with:** A country that can keep order and defend itself relies less on anyone else. Canada built its own police, then its own army and navy, and its soldiers' record in the First World War earned it a voice, until in 1931 Canada controlled its own foreign affairs.

**What this unit is really about:** It is easy for this unit to become a list of battles. Keep tying every event back to one thread: *did this give Canada more control over its own affairs?* The NWMP (Section 1) connects straight back to Building the Canadian State. Section 5 is about honouring service and the cost of war, not glorifying it. The Second World War is Grade 9; mention 1939 only as the contrast with 1914.

**Timing:** this unit works well taught across Remembrance Day. If you move it to the fall, teach Section 5 the week of November 8–11.
:::

## Lesson Sequence

### Section 1 — Policing the West (2 classes)

#### Opening: Pre-Knowledge Check and the March West
SHOW: Pre-Knowledge Check in silence, then a map of the 1874 March West ending at Fort Macleod.
SAY: "Canada had claimed the West in 1870, but a claim on a map isn't the same as laws people follow. The NWMP was how Canada made its claim real."
CUE: "Who was the NWMP protecting, and who was it controlling?" Hold both answers; Workbook Q8 comes back to it.
NOTES: Section 1 notes and timeline. Feeds Workbook Q1, Q8.

### Section 2 — Building a Military (2 classes)

#### Step by step
SHOW: The staircase diagram, one step at a time, leaving the top step covered until Section 6.
SAY: "When the British soldiers left in 1871, Canada had to look after itself. Each step after that put more of Canada's defence in Canadian hands."
NOTES: Section 2 misconception box and Retrieval Check (AF2). Feeds Workbook Q3, Q7, Q10.

### Section 3 — The Boer War (2 classes)

#### Two Canadas, one war
SHOW: Two short quotes: one English-Canadian newspaper cheering for the Empire, one from Henri Bourassa opposing the war (both easy to find in Canadian archives).
SAY: "Laurier had to keep both halves of the country together. His answer was volunteers only."
NOTES: Section 3 perspectives diagram and Retrieval Check (AF3). Feeds Workbook Q5, Q11.

### Section 4 — The First World War (4 classes)

#### Refutation first: did Canada choose war?
SHOW: The date August 4, 1914, and the question "When did Canada's Parliament vote for war?"
SAY: "It didn't have to. Britain declared war, and that included Canada. Canada decided how much to give, and it gave a lot: 620,000 people out of 8 million."
NOTES: Section 4 first misconception box. Feeds Workbook Q9 and Test Q9.

#### Battles that mattered
SHOW: The war timeline. Assign groups one battle each to present in two minutes: what happened, and why it mattered to Canada or Newfoundland.
SAY: "Beaumont-Hamel and Gallipoli belong to the Newfoundland Regiment. Newfoundland wasn't part of Canada until 1949, but its history is part of ours now."
CUE: "Why did Vimy, of all these battles, become the symbol?"
NOTES: Section 4 Retrieval Check (AF4). Feeds Workbook Q2, Q12. Give the mid-unit quiz after this section.

### Section 5 — Who Served, and How We Remember (3 classes)

#### Many communities served
SHOW: Photos and short profiles: the No. 2 Construction Battalion, Francis Pegahmagabow, Buckam Singh, the 22nd Battalion.
SAY: "Many of these soldiers served a country that didn't treat them equally at home. Their stories are part of the whole history."
NOTES: Section 5 notes. Feeds Workbook Q13 and S2.

#### Reading a memorial
SHOW: A photo of your nearest cenotaph, or walk to it. Use the four questions.
SAY: "A memorial is a primary source. It tells us who a community lost and how it wanted them remembered."
NOTES: Section 5 Retrieval Check (AF5) and home sheet "Reading a memorial." Feeds Workbook Q6.

### Section 6 — From Colony to Sovereign Nation (2 classes)

#### Sacrifice earns a voice
SHOW: Uncover the top step of the staircase: 1931. Then the 1914 / 1939 comparison.
SAY: "In 1914 Canada was at war the moment Britain was. In 1939 Canada's own Parliament decided. The Statute of Westminster is what changed in between."
NOTES: Section 6 Retrieval Check (AF6). Feeds Workbook Q4, Q14 and S1.

### Consolidation (2 classes)

#### Unit Synthesis and memorial study
SHOW: Unit Synthesis from memory, then the Pre-Knowledge Check.
NOTES: Workbook S1–S3, the local memorial inquiry, then the unit test.

## Misconception Management

| Misconception | Where it is refuted | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| The RCMP always existed in its current form | Section 1 box; Test Q10 | "The RCMP marched west in 1874" |
| Sovereignty arrived all at once | Section 2 box; staircase diagram; Workbook S1 | "Canada became independent at Vimy" |
| Canada chose to enter the First World War | Section 4 box; Workbook Q9; Test Q9 | "Canada declared war on Germany in 1914" |
| Canadians only fought at Vimy | Section 4 box; Workbook Q2 | Every answer about the war is about Vimy |
| Beaumont-Hamel was a Canadian battle | Section 4 box; Test Q11 | "Canadians at Beaumont-Hamel" |

## Discussion and Source Activity Notes

::: lab Remembrance with care (read before Section 5)
- Some students have family in today's Canadian Armed Forces; some have family who fled wars or lost people in them. Keep the focus on honouring service and understanding the cost of war.
- Avoid graphic images; trench photos and letters are powerful enough.
- Good sources: the Canadian Virtual War Memorial and the Canadian War Museum, Veterans Affairs Canada learning resources, The Rooms (Newfoundland) for the Newfoundland Regiment.
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 1 — Local memorial study (best after Section 5)
- Visit or photograph a local cenotaph. Record every name from 1914–1918.
- Each student researches one name in the Canadian Virtual War Memorial: unit, date of death, age, where buried.
- Present as a class "honour roll" for Remembrance Day.
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 2 — Steps to sovereignty mural
- Groups each take one step (1871, 1873, 1899, 1910, 1914, 1917, 1919, 1931) and make one panel: what happened, and how it gave Canada more control.
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 3 — A story of courage
- Research one heroic or gallant act: a Victoria Cross recipient, Francis Pegahmagabow, a nursing sister, or a member of the No. 2 Construction Battalion. Present what they did and what it tells us about the war (Workbook S2).
:::

## Assessment Notes

::: assessment
**Formative checkpoints:** exit tickets after each section. Exit Ticket 4 (why Canada was at war in 1914) and Exit Ticket 6 (what Westminster gave Canada) check the sovereignty thread.

**What a strong extended response looks like:** on Test Q14 and Q15, a strong answer does not just list events; it explains how each step gave Canada more control over its own defence and decisions.
:::

::: closing
**Connections:** the NWMP marched west because of the expansion in Building the Canadian State. Creating Canada's Government showed sovereignty developing gradually through the courts; this unit shows the same pattern in defence. Grade 9 begins where this unit ends: Canada's independent declaration of war in 1939.
:::
