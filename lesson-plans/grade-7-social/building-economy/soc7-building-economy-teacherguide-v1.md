::: overview
**Suggested length:** ~17 classes (5–6 weeks, 3 classes a week) &nbsp;&nbsp; **Tier:** 2 &nbsp;&nbsp; **Unit colour:** Blue (Systems)

**Guiding question:** How did the Canadian government help shape the economy after Confederation?

**Learning outcome:** Students examine the Canadian government's role in developing the economy post-Confederation. Local skill codes BE1–BE5 are in the course context file.

**Big idea for students to leave with:** Government decisions shape how an economy grows, and different regions build different economies from their resources. The same decision (a tariff, a railway, equalization) can help one region and cost another.

**What this unit is really about:** Economics vocabulary is abstract for Grade 7, so every idea starts with a concrete example: a plough's price, a bushel of wheat on a train, a tiny town's GDP. The trade-off thinking matters more than the terms: who wins, who pays, and was it worth it? Grade 8 builds economic systems on this; Grade 9 takes on the Depression, post-war growth and NAFTA, so keep free trade to the idea and the 1911 debate.
:::

## Lesson Sequence

### Section 1 — Expansion and Regional Economies (2 classes)

#### Opening: what does your region make?
SHOW: Pre-Knowledge Check, then a pile of real objects or pictures (bread, a 2×4, canned salmon, a car part, a gas can). Students guess which region of Canada each comes from.
SAY: "Each region built its economy from what was in the ground, the forests and the water. The West added a whole new region: the wheat Prairies."
NOTES: Section 1 regions strip and Retrieval Check (BE1). Feeds Workbook Q1, Q8.

### Section 2 — The National Policy (3 classes)

#### Three parts, one plan
SHOW: The National Policy cycle diagram, one box at a time. After each box, ask "what does this part need from the others?"
SAY: "Tariffs protected factories in the East. The railway carried goods and people. Settlers grew wheat and bought those factory goods. Take one away and the plan falls apart."
NOTES: Section 2 notes. Feeds Workbook Q4, Q10.

#### Refutation first: did it help everyone?
SHOW: Two role cards: an Ontario factory worker and a Saskatchewan farmer, 1890. Each reads how the tariff affects them.
SAY: "Same policy, two very different stories. That's why the National Policy is still argued about."
CUE: Fill the short-term / long-term table together.
NOTES: Section 2 misconception box and Retrieval Check (BE2). Note the Macdonald balance box. Feeds Workbook Q11.

### Section 3 — The Railway (2 classes)

#### One line, two directions
SHOW: The CPR route diagram. Students trace a bushel of wheat east and a plough west.
SAY: "Calgary was a small fort until the railway came in 1883. Railways decided where towns grew."
NOTES: Section 3 Retrieval Check. Feeds Workbook Q5, Q6, Q12.

### Section 4 — Tariffs or Free Trade? (3 classes)

#### The plough problem
SHOW: The worked example on the board, step by step. Then have pairs do the jacket and bicycle versions from the home sheet.
SAY: "Who pays the tariff? Not the American factory. The Canadian buyer pays, in a higher price."
NOTES: Section 4 misconception box and worked example. Feeds Workbook Q2.

#### The 1911 election
SHOW: Workbook Q9's price table. Split the class into farmers and factory workers and hold a mock 1911 vote on reciprocity.
SAY: "Laurier lost in 1911, partly over free trade. Canada didn't sign a free trade deal with the U.S. until 1988."
NOTES: Section 4 Retrieval Check (BE3). Give the mid-unit quiz after this section.

### Section 5 — GDP and Equalization (3 classes)

#### A tiny GDP
SHOW: The tiny-town worked example, then Workbook Q3's two towns.
SAY: "GDP adds up everything a place produces in a year. It's not a bank account."
NOTES: Section 5 first misconception box. Feeds Workbook Q3.

#### Sharing between regions
SHOW: A simple bar chart of which provinces receive equalization this year (from the federal Finance Canada website).
SAY: "Alberta hasn't received equalization in decades. Is the system fair? People in Alberta disagree, and you can too, with evidence."
NOTES: Section 5 Retrieval Check (BE4). Feeds Workbook Q13 and S1.

### Section 6 — Banks and the Bank of Canada (3 classes)

#### What banks do, and a two-way street
SHOW: The GDP–banking loop. Walk one example around it: a good harvest → deposits → a loan for a tractor → a bigger harvest.
SAY: "Banks need a busy economy, and a busy economy needs banks. That two-way link is what the curriculum calls reciprocal."
NOTES: Section 6 notes. Feeds Workbook Q7, Q14.

#### The Bank of Canada
SHOW: A banknote. Find "Bank of Canada" on it.
SAY: "You can't open an account at the Bank of Canada. It was created in 1934 to look after the whole money system."
NOTES: Section 6 misconception box and Retrieval Check (BE5).

### Consolidation (1 class)

#### Unit Synthesis
SHOW: Unit Synthesis from memory, then the Pre-Knowledge Check.
NOTES: Workbook S1–S3, then the unit test.

## Misconception Management

| Misconception | Where it is refuted | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| The National Policy helped every region equally | Section 2 box; Workbook Q11; Test Q14 | "It made Canada rich" with no mention of who paid |
| A tariff is paid by the other country | Section 4 box; Quiz Q8; Test Q9 | "The Americans pay the tariff" |
| GDP is a country's savings | Section 5 box; Workbook Q3; Test Q10 | "Canada has $3 trillion in the bank" |
| Equalization gives every province the same amount | Section 5 box; Workbook Q13 | "Ottawa splits the money evenly" |
| The Bank of Canada is a bank for people | Section 6 box; Test Q12 | "My parents bank at the Bank of Canada" |

## Discussion and Source Activity Notes

::: lab Inquiry option 1 — The National Policy on trial (best after Section 4)
- Teams play prosecution and defence: "The National Policy was good for the Prairies."
- Witnesses: a Prairie farmer, an Ontario factory owner, a CPR manager, a Maritime fisher.
- Each side must use the short-term / long-term table and one tariff calculation.
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 2 — Regional economy profile
- Compare Alberta's economy with one other province: main resources, main industries, whether it receives equalization.
- Use current numbers from Statistics Canada and record the date the data was checked.
:::

::: lab Inquiry option 3 — A bank's history poster
- Research one of Canada's first banks (Bank of Montreal, Bank of Nova Scotia, RBC, CIBC, National Bank, Laurentian Bank): when it began, where, and what it did for the economy.
:::

## Assessment Notes

::: assessment
**Formative checkpoints:** exit tickets after each section; the tariff calculation (Exit Ticket 4) is the quickest check of whether students know who pays.

**What a strong extended response looks like:** on Test Q14, a strong answer covers two regions, separates short-term from long-term effects, and makes a judgement that weighs gains against costs.
:::

::: closing
**Connections:** the National Policy and the CPR appear first in Building the Canadian State and return in Immigration (settlers and Chinese railway workers). Grade 8 builds on government spending and economic systems; Grade 9 picks up the Great Depression, post-war growth and free trade with the U.S.
:::
