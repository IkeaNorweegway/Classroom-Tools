# Sub Plan — Grade 6 Science: Forces
## Lesson 1 — What Is a Force?

**Total time:** ~60 minutes
**Class:** Grade 6 Science

---

### What students will do today
1. Complete a pre-knowledge check (writing what they already know about forces)
2. Work through Section 1 of their notes package with your guidance
3. Complete 4 questions in their practice workbook independently

---

### Materials
- Student notes packages (one per student — on the desk or in the science folder)
- Student practice workbooks (one per student)
- Whiteboard and markers
- **Detailed teacher notes** are in `sci6-forces-teachernotes-v1` — use these for exact wording and board diagrams

---

### Lesson Sequence

#### 1. Pre-Knowledge Check — 10 minutes
- Hand out notes packages. Students open to **page 1: Before We Begin**.
- Write on the board: *"Write everything you think you know about forces."*
- Tell students: "This is not a test. No marks. Write honestly — you'll come back to this page at the end of the unit."
- Give 5 minutes of silent writing. Circulate — do not comment on what students write.
- Do not collect or read responses aloud.

---

#### 2. Section 1 Direct Instruction — 25 minutes
**Students open their notes to Section 1.** Work through the following sequence. The teacher notes have full board diagrams and exact phrasing for each step.

| Step | What to put on the board | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary setup** | Write the 5 terms: Force / Newton (N) / Magnitude / Direction / Force diagram | Tell students not to fill these in yet — they'll build them as you go |
| **What is a force?** | *FORCE = push or pull* + simple arrow pushing a box, arrow pulling a box | Students write the definition fill-in |
| **Magnitude** | *MAGNITUDE = size / strength (measured in Newtons)* — write **N** large | Students write the fill-in |
| **Direction** | Two arrows same length, opposite directions. Label: *10 N →* and *← 10 N* | Students write the fill-in |
| **Force arrows** | One arrow labeled for direction + short vs. long arrow labeled for magnitude | Students write the fill-in |
| **Force diagrams** | Draw a detailed box, then erase everything except the outline and add force arrows. Label: *Force diagram — forces only* | Students write the fill-in |
| **Worked example** | Box in centre, *20 N →* on left, *← 8 N* on right. Make the 20 N arrow longer. Label both. | Students complete the worked example fill-in |
| **Vocabulary fill-in** | Keep the 5 terms on the board | Students write their own definitions and examples for each term (3–5 min) |

> **One thing to address directly:** If a student says the net force is 28 N (adding instead of subtracting), stop and ask: "These two forces are pointing in opposite directions — do they add or do they partially cancel each other out?" The answer is 12 N to the right (20 − 8).

---

#### 3. Workbook Time — 20 minutes
- Students open their **practice workbook**.
- Say: *"Scan the workbook. Find 4 questions you think you can now answer. Put a tick beside each one — but don't answer yet."*
- Give 60–90 seconds for scanning.
- Ask the class which questions they found and why they connect to what we just learned. Work through the reasoning together briefly.
- Students then work independently and silently to answer those questions.
- The 4 questions are: **Q1, Q6, Q11, Q22** — students should find these on their own, but if a student is stuck after 2 minutes, you can confirm the numbers.

---

#### 4. Close — 5 minutes
- Ask 2–3 students to share one thing they wrote for Q1 (force definition).
- Students complete the **Self-check** at the bottom of Section 1 in their notes: *I could teach it / Getting there / Not yet*
- Collect nothing. Leave notes and workbooks with students.

---

### If you finish early
Students can attempt more workbook questions or re-read Section 1 and add detail to their vocabulary table.

### If you run short on time
Cut the workbook class discussion — just have students scan and work independently. The notes instruction takes priority.
