## Part 2: How Filtration Works — Layers

**Goal:** Explain what job one specific layer in a filter is doing.

> 📌 **New word: layer**
> One material stacked on top of another inside a filter. Each layer does a different job — some catch big particles, some catch smaller ones, some trap things chemically.

No single material does the whole job well by itself. That's why real filters (and yours) use more than one layer.

## Part 3: Testing, Data & Safety — the Most Important Idea in This Unit

**Goal:** Say, without being asked, why clear water is not the same as safe water.

**Common mistake:** "If I filter water until it's clear, it's safe to drink."

**Why that's wrong:** Filtering for **clarity** only removes things you can *see* — dirt, sand, plant bits. It does nothing about things you *can't* see, like bacteria and other **contaminants**. That's exactly why real water-treatment plants use filtration **plus** other steps, like disinfection. Filtration alone was never the whole solution.

**What's actually true:** Clear water can still be unsafe. Your filter solves one specific problem — clarity — not "making water safe." **We never taste or drink test water in this unit, for any reason, no matter how clear it looks.**

> 📌 **New word: clarity**
> How clear or cloudy water looks. You can measure it and compare it — against a printed chart, or by timing how long it takes to see through it.

> 📌 **New word: data**
> A number or observation you record. Not just a feeling that something "seems better."
