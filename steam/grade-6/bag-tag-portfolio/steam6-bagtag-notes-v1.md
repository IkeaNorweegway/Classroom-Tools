## Part 1: CAD — Designing in 3D on a Screen

**Goal:** Describe the basic Tinkercad moves used to design a simple object — shape, extrude, combine/subtract, resize, text.

> 📌 **New word: CAD**
> Short for "computer-aided design." It lets you build a 3D object on screen before anything physical exists.

In Tinkercad, you start from basic shapes, then change them:

- **Extrude** — stretches a flat shape into a solid one
- **Combine** — joins two shapes into one
- **Subtract** — uses one shape as a "hole," cutting through another (this is how you'll cut a keyring loop through your bag tag)

The text tool lets you personalize a design with initials or a name. Resize lets you scale anything up or down.

## Part 2: The CAD-to-Print Gap

**Goal:** Describe one specific gap between how your design looked on screen and how it came out printed.

**Common mistake:** "If it looks right on screen, it will print correctly."

**Why that's wrong:** A screen shows you a shape, not a physical object with real limits. A wall drawn too thin, a hole sized exactly to a keyring's width with no extra room, or a shape hanging over empty space with nothing under it — all of these can look completely fine on screen and still fail (or come out fragile, warped, or blocked) once printed. The computer doesn't know your printer's limits unless you tell it.

**What's actually true:** A 3D print is a real physical object that obeys real physical rules — thickness, gravity, material behavior — that a screen preview doesn't fully show you. Comparing your printed result back to your on-screen design, and noticing exactly where they differ, is how you learn what CAD design has to account for.

## Part 3: Grid, Hierarchy & Contrast — Portfolio Layout

**Goal:** Apply grid, hierarchy, and contrast to lay out a simple portfolio page.

Building a portfolio page is your first paper-to-digital step in graphic design. Three words guide the layout:

> 📌 **New word: grid**
> Lining elements up along invisible rows/columns, instead of scattering them.

> 📌 **New word: hierarchy**
> Making the most important thing — usually a title or photo — bigger or bolder, so the eye goes there first.

> 📌 **New word: contrast**
> Making different elements clearly different from each other, like a bold heading against plain body text, so nothing gets lost.

Before opening any digital tool, sketch a **wireframe** — a simple paper layout showing roughly where a photo and text will go, with no real content yet, just the plan.

> 📌 **New word: wireframe**
> A simple paper sketch of a layout, showing where things will go before you build it for real.

**Think about it:** Think of a poster, app screen, or webpage that was easy to read at a glance. Can you spot the grid, the hierarchy, or the contrast doing that work?
