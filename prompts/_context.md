# Thinking & Writing Prompts
*Getting students to show their reasoning, not just their answer*

**Pedagogical framework:** `_meta-context.md` governs how to think and design. This file governs structure and format. Read both.

---

## Purpose
A thinking prompt asks students to externalise their cognitive process — not what they know, but how they know it, what they're uncertain about, and how they connected new information to prior knowledge. These prompts are the most direct window into student thinking available to a teacher.

## Design principles
- **Ask for reasoning, not recall.** "Why do you think that?" not "What is the answer?"
- **Make uncertainty safe.** The best prompts normalise not knowing: "What are you still confused about?" is as valuable as "What did you learn?"
- **Anchor to something specific.** Vague prompts produce vague responses. "Explain your thinking about today's lesson" → "Explain why you chose that approach to solve the problem on page 3."
- **Short and frequent beats long and rare.** A 3-minute written response twice a week is more valuable than a 20-minute reflection once a month.
- **Metacognition is a skill.** Students need to be taught what it means to think about their thinking — the prompts are the training, not just the assessment.

## Prompt categories to develop
- **Metacognitive prompts** — thinking about their own understanding and process
- **Error analysis prompts** — what went wrong and why
- **Connection prompts** — linking new content to prior knowledge or real life
- **Uncertainty prompts** — surfacing confusion before it calcifies
- **Argument prompts** — taking and defending a position with evidence
- **Revision prompts** — reflecting on how thinking changed after feedback

## What a strong prompt looks like
1. **Specific anchor** — refers to a particular task, problem, or piece of content
2. **Reasoning demand** — asks for the "why" or "how", not just the "what"
3. **Low floor** — accessible to all students, including those who struggle with writing
4. **Sentence starter optional** — offer one for students who need it, but don't require it
5. **Short response expected** — 2–5 sentences is enough; length is not the point
