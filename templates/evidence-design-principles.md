# Evidence-Based Design Principles
*Apply these when building any classroom material in this workspace*

---

## The core standard

Every artifact built here should embed at least one of the Tier 1 or Tier 2 strategies below. Materials that only ask students to receive, copy, or highlight information are not meeting the design standard — those strategies have weak or no evidence behind them.

---

## Tier 1 — Non-negotiable (d ≥ 0.70)

### Formative feedback loops (d = 0.70–0.90)
Every artifact that collects student work should give the teacher — or the student — actionable information about understanding, not just completion.

**Build this in by:**
- Ending worksheets with a reflection line or self-check, not just a final problem
- Including a "teacher use" box on exit tickets for recording what to address next class
- Designing quiz items so wrong answers reveal the specific misconception (not just that something is wrong)

### Structured elaboration and justification (d = 0.82)
Students should be required to explain, justify, or connect — not just produce an answer.

**Build this in by:**
- Adding "explain your reasoning" or "show how you know" to at least one item per worksheet
- Writing thinking prompts that require a claim + evidence + reasoning structure
- Including at least one "why is this true?" question per quiz section

---

## Tier 2 — Include wherever possible (d = 0.50–0.70)

### Retrieval practice (d = 0.50–0.60)
Include items that ask students to produce information from memory — not just recognise it. True/false, matching, and multiple choice with obvious answers do not count.

**Build this in by:**
- Opening warm-up sections with short-answer recall questions on prior content
- Writing quiz items as free-recall or short-answer before adding MCQ format
- Including a "retrieval warm-up" section on notes packages (prior knowledge, no notes)

### Spaced and interleaved content (d = 0.40–0.60)
Don't let a worksheet or practice set cover only the current topic. Mix in earlier content.

**Build this in by:**
- Including a "Review" section with 2–3 items from a previous unit on every worksheet
- Writing quiz question banks large enough to draw mixed-topic sets
- Labelling stretch items so they can pull from earlier units

### Metacognitive demand (d = 0.60)
Students should be asked to monitor their own understanding, not just perform.

**Build this in by:**
- Including a confidence rating or self-check ("I can explain this without looking: yes / not yet") on any notes package
- Ending thinking prompts with "what are you still unsure about?"
- Including a "what would you do differently?" line on returned assessments

### Worked examples before independent practice (d = 0.57)
For any worksheet or notes package covering new content, a worked example must come before student practice items. Do not ask students to solve problems on content they have not yet seen modelled.

**Build this in by:**
- Structuring worksheets as: Example → Guided item → Independent items (in that order)
- Including at least one fully worked example in every notes package
- On quizzes, avoiding items on content that has only been introduced — not yet practised

## Strategies to avoid

These feel productive and are commonly included. The evidence does not support them as learning tools:

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Re-reading passages as a task | Familiarity is not retrieval — it does not strengthen memory |
| Highlighting or colour-coding as the main activity | Passive reception; does not require generation or connection |
| Copying definitions | Production without understanding; no elaboration required |
| Open-ended "research it yourself" before any instruction | Cognitive overload for novices; discovery requires a knowledge base |

---

## Quick checklist — before finalising any artifact

- [ ] Does the student have to **produce** something from memory at least once?
- [ ] Does the student have to **explain or justify** reasoning at least once?
- [ ] Is there **prior content mixed in** (not only today's concept)?
- [ ] Is there a **formative signal** — something the teacher or student can act on?
- [ ] If it covers new content, is there a **worked example before practice**?

If all five are yes: the artifact meets the evidence standard.
If fewer than three are yes: revise before finalising.
