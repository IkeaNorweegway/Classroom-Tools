# Rubric Phrase Bank
*Copy-ready wording for rubrics, success criteria and feedback comments. Evidence: `research/rubric-design-research.md`.*

Use this when writing any rubric: test extended response, project rubrics, single-point rubrics, Phys Ed skill rubrics.

**Where rubrics go:** the **student-facing** version (general wording, no answers) goes on the site, listed in the course manifest beside the practice test. Example: `public/materials/math-9/tests/rational-numbers/math9-rational-numbers-rubric-v1.html`. The **teacher** version (question-specific descriptors, model answers) stays in the test's answer key and goes into the teacher Google Drive tests folder with the tests, never on the site. The wording here is original. Level labels follow the school report card. The wording does not copy the AAC Rubric Wordsmith (reuse restricted).

---

## 1. Level labels (4-point report-card scale)

| Level | Label | What it means (teacher) | Student version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Excellent** | Outcome met with depth: explains, connects or transfers beyond what was taught | "I went further." |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Outcome met securely: accurate, complete and clear | "I've got it solidly." |
| 2 | **Meets** | Outcome met at the basic standard: the essential parts are there and correct; gaps or small errors don't change the meaning | "I've met it." |
| 1 | **Has yet to meet** | Outcome not met yet: some relevant pieces, but an essential part is missing or wrong | "Not yet. Here's my next step." |
| — | **Insufficient** | No assessable evidence: missing, blank or off-task. **Not a level.** Leads to a teacher conversation, not a score. | "I need to hand this in / try it." |

- **The bar is between levels 1 and 2.** Meets means the outcome is met. Everything from Meets up is a degree of success; Has yet to meet is the only "not met" level.
- Labels may be evaluative words. **Descriptors under them may not** (see §2).
- **Write Meets first**, straight from the outcome: the essential parts a student must show. Then write Proficient (the same parts, secure and complete), Excellent (depth or transfer), and Has yet to meet (what is present when an essential part is missing).
- **Has yet to meet** describes what the student *has* shown and names the missing essential part. Never write it as "does nothing".

## 2. Descriptor rules (check every rubric against these)

1. **Describe, don't judge.** Not "good explanation"; instead "explains how particles gain energy and spread out."
2. **Don't count.** Not "3 examples"; instead "supports the claim with relevant examples."
3. **Learning, not compliance.** Neatness, length, title page and "followed directions" go on the hand-in checklist, not in a criterion.
4. **Same attributes, same order, same sentence frame** at every level. Only the quality word changes.
5. **One kind of qualifier per row.** Use degree (thorough / clear / partial / minimal) **or** frequency (consistently / usually / sometimes / rarely), never a mix.
6. **No orphans or widows.** A key term at one level must appear at every level, and none can be dropped from just one.
7. **Say what is there.** Lower levels describe what the student did ("names one factor"), not only what's missing ("does not explain").
8. **Even steps.** Read the levels aloud; each jump should feel the same size.
9. **Excellent = depth or transfer**, not "more" or "neater."
10. **3–5 criteria, one page,** each criterion tagged with its LO code and K / U / S&P.

## 3. Quality-word ladders

Pick **one** ladder per criterion and use it on every level of that row.

| Dimension | Excellent (4) | Proficient (3) | Meets (2) | Has yet to meet (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | accurate and precise throughout | accurate | accurate on the essentials; small errors don't change the meaning | errors change the main idea |
| **Completeness** | complete, including [the subtle part] | complete | the essential parts are there; some detail missing | an essential part is missing: [name it] |
| **Clarity** | clear and concise; easy to follow throughout | clear throughout | clear enough to follow the main idea | the main idea is hard to follow |
| **Reasoning / explanation** | explains *why*, linking cause and effect across ideas | explains *why* fully for the main idea | explains *why* in a basic way; the link is stated but brief | describes *what* happens; *why* not yet given |
| **Evidence / support** | well-chosen evidence that strengthens the claim | relevant evidence that clearly supports the claim | relevant evidence that supports the claim in a basic way | evidence not yet connected to the claim |
| **Vocabulary** | precise subject vocabulary used naturally | correct subject vocabulary throughout | the key subject terms, used correctly | everyday words in place of the key terms |
| **Connections** | connects to other ideas, units or real situations unprompted | connects ideas clearly to the related idea taught | makes the connection taught | ideas treated separately |
| **Procedure (S&P)** | efficient and accurate; chooses the method and justifies it | accurate steps in a clear, workable order | workable steps; small slips don't change the result | an error or missing step changes the result |
| **Consistency** (skills observed over time) | consistently, including in new situations | consistently | usually | sometimes or rarely |

**About "with support":** wording like "independently / with occasional prompting / with frequent support" mixes achievement with how much help was given. Use it only for **process skills observed live** (lab technique, Phys Ed skills), never for written products.

## 4. Descriptor stems by outcome column

These stems are written at **Meets** (outcome met). For Proficient and Excellent, keep the sentence and change only the quality word (§3).

| Column | Meets stem | Example (Meets) |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge** | "[Identifies / defines / describes] [concept] **accurately**, using [subject vocabulary]." | "Describes the three methods of heat transfer accurately, using *conduction*, *convection* and *radiation*." |
| **Understanding** | "Explains **why** [phenomenon / result], linking [idea A] to [idea B]." | "Explains why the metal spoon heats faster, linking conductivity to particle motion." |
| **Skills & Procedures** | "[Carries out / models / calculates / constructs] [procedure] **accurately**, in a workable order." | "Builds a linear model from the table accurately and uses it to predict the cost at 30 rides." |
| **Communication** | "Presents [product] so that [audience] can follow [the reasoning / the result]." | "Presents the budget so a parent can follow where each dollar goes." |

### Do / Don't

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| Good use of vocabulary | Uses *particle*, *kinetic energy* and *thermal equilibrium* correctly |
| Includes 3 sources | Uses relevant, credible sources that support the claim |
| Excellent graph | Graph has a title, labelled axes with units, and a scale that shows the trend |
| Neat and organized | *(move to hand-in checklist)* |
| Does not explain | Names the result; the reason is not yet given |
| Some errors / Few errors / No errors | Errors change the result / One error, result still reasonable / Accurate |
| Shows effort | *(not a criterion; effort goes in comments, at the process level)* |

---

## 5. Verbs by column

| Knowledge | Understanding | Skills & Procedures |
|---|---|---|
| identify, name, define, describe, label, list, recall, recognize, classify | explain, justify, compare, interpret, predict and explain, infer, connect, evaluate, argue, critique | calculate, construct, model, measure, investigate, design, test, represent, perform, demonstrate, communicate |

Match the verb to the outcome's own verb. If the outcome says *explain*, the criterion says *explain*, not *identify*.

## 6. Student-facing wording (Grades 5–9)

### "I can" success criteria
- Start with **I can** + the outcome's verb + what, in words students use.
- One idea per statement. 2–4 statements per task.
- Add **"I can show this by…"** to make students point to evidence.

> I can explain why some materials heat up faster than others.
> I can show this by using the words *particle* and *energy* in my answer.

### Single-point rubric layout

| ✗ Has yet to meet: what's missing | ✓ Meets (the outcome) | ★ Beyond: Proficient or Excellent evidence |
|---|---|---|
| *(teacher or student writes here)* | [Meets descriptor in student words] — [LO code] | *(teacher or student writes here)* |

**Column prompts for students:**
- ✗ "The part I haven't shown yet is…" / "I'm stuck on…"
- ✓ "My evidence is on page __ / in step __."
- ★ "I went further by…" / "I connected this to…"

### Self-assessment prompts (use before handing in, and before seeing any answer key)
- "Highlight the descriptor that matches your work. Put a star where your evidence is."
- "Which criterion is your strongest? Which one will you fix first?"
- "Read the Meets box. In your own words, what does it ask for? What would make it Proficient?" (use at launch)
- "Compare your work to Sample A and Sample B. Which is closer to yours? Why?"

### Peer feedback frames (kind / specific / helpful)
- "You met [criterion] where you…"
- "To move up a level on [criterion], you could…"
- "I wasn't sure about [part]. Can you show how…?"

## 7. Feedback comment stems

Tie every comment to a **criterion name**. Aim at the process and self-regulation levels (Hattie & Timperley). One or two comments per piece, each with a next step the student does **in class**.

| Level | Where am I going? | How am I going? | Where to next? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Task** | "For [criterion], Meets means… Proficient adds…" | "Your [part] is accurate. [Part] has an error in…" | "Fix [part] using…" |
| **Process** | "The strategy that helps here is…" | "Your [step] works because… The step that broke down was…" | "Try [strategy] on [part]. Then check whether…" |
| **Self-regulation** | "Before you start, what will tell you this criterion is met?" | "How did you check [part]? What would you check next time?" | "Use the rubric to find one row you can move up. Show me what you changed." |

**Avoid:** "Great job!", "You're so smart", "Needs more effort", "Be more detailed", and a grade with no comment. Replace them with a criterion + evidence + next step:

> **Explain the meaning:** you said what the slope is (3.5). Next step: say what the 3.5 *means* for a rider, using "per ride" in your sentence.

## 8. Worked example: one criterion written three ways

**Outcome:** LO 7-3-02. Use the particle model of matter to explain the effects of adding or removing heat; explain expansion and contraction. *(Understanding)*

### Analytic (4 levels)
| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| **Excellent** | Explains expansion and contraction using the particle model, clearly linking heat added or removed to both particle speed and spacing, **and applies it to a new situation** (e.g. why bridges have expansion gaps). |
| **Proficient** | Explains expansion and contraction using the particle model, clearly linking heat added or removed to both particle speed and spacing. |
| **Meets** | Explains expansion and contraction using the particle model, linking heat added or removed to particle speed or spacing in a basic way. |
| **Has yet to meet** | Describes that materials expand when heated and contract when cooled; the particle model is not yet part of the explanation. |
| *Insufficient* | *No assessable response.* |

*Check: the same attributes on every row (heat added or removed, particle speed and spacing, the link between them), one type of qualifier (degree of explanation), positive wording, even steps. The line between Has yet to meet and Meets is the outcome: using particles to explain.*

### Single-point
| ✗ Has yet to meet | ✓ Meets | ★ Beyond |
|---|---|---|
| | I explain why things expand when heated and contract when cooled, using what the particles do (how fast they move or how far apart they are). — LO 7-3-02 | |

### Student "I can"
> I can explain why things get bigger when heated and smaller when cooled, using the particle model.
> I can show this by drawing the particles before and after heating.

## 9. Final check before a rubric is used

- [ ] 3–5 criteria, each with an LO code and K / U / S&P tag
- [ ] No criterion is about compliance (neatness, length, format)
- [ ] Meets written first, from the outcome's own verb; the 1→2 line is the outcome
- [ ] Same attributes, same order, one qualifier type per row; no counting
- [ ] Has yet to meet says what is present and names the missing essential part
- [ ] Excellent describes depth or transfer
- [ ] Insufficient kept outside the scale
- [ ] Student version ("I can" or single-point) exists if students will self-assess
- [ ] 2–3 different exemplars planned for launch
- [ ] Revision time scheduled after feedback
